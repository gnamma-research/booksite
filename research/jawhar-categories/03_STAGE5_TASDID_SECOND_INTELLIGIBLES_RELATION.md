## A. Read the argument in sequence, distinguishing Ṭūsī's “qāla” and Iṣfahānī's “aqūlu”

1. **pp186–187, rooted in Ṭūsī's lemmas:** categorical partition of the possible existent into *jawhar* vs *ʿaraḍ*; role of *maḥall* and *mawḍūʿ*; the claim **“الجوهرية والعرضية من ثواني المعقولات”** (*substantiality and accidentality are among second intelligibles*). This asserts something about **the status of the classification predicate**, not that external individuals have no reality.
2. **pp187–188, al-Iṣfahānī's argumentative critique:** multiple proposed demonstrations that *jawhar* is not a genus are tested. Some infer from a middle term required to classify a particular quiddity; some from priority relations between a body or prime matter and their individuating principles. The author challenges the inference from **knowledge by demonstration** to **not being a highest genus**; or from **existential priority** to **non-generic status**.
3. **pp188–189, further proposed proof:** a purported exhaustive disjunction relating *lā fī mawḍūʿ* to a single common substance nature is assessed and found not decisively comprehensive. The commentator distinguishes what may be a description by a concomitant (*rasm*) from what would be an essential category genus. Treat each reported proof and its reply as distinct.
4. **p189, al-Iṣfahānī's express outcome:** **“والحق أن العرض ليس بجنس للأعراض ... وأما أن الجوهر هل هو جنس أم لا؟ فلم يتحقق عندي”**. Close translation: “The truth is that accident is not a genus of the accidents … but whether jawhar is a genus or not has not become established for me.” This is an explicit historical **suspension of judgment on jawhar**, not endorsement of a definite denial. One must not conflate the commentator with the original matn or later Jurjānī glosses.
5. **pp189–191, from genus to accident's individuality:** “وحدة الحال تستلزم وحدة المحل” (unity of the inhering item entails unity of its locus), with objections concerning a single *taʾlīf* alleged to inhere in two atoms (Abū Hāshim), or the *same* adjacency/brotherhood between two individuals. The reply rejects **numerical sameness** of a single dependent inhering accident in distinct substrates; the relational determination *of A toward B* need not be the numerically same dependent determination *of B toward A*. This distinction does **not** decide all possible ontologies of relations or show that relational truths are ungrounded.

## B. Logical analysis of the relation challenge

Let A and B be distinct subjects, and denote *r(A,B)* as the determination inhering in A and referring to B, while *r(B,A)* inheres in B and refers to A. “A stands next to B” has a relational truth condition, but several ontologies remain open:

- **One numerically same accident shared between A and B:** challenged because numerical identity of inherence would require one locus in the current analysis.
- **Two numerically different directional relative accidents:** a position compatible with the objection/reply in *Tasdīd*, with distinguishable subjects yet mutually referring terms.
- **A single objective founded connection without any extra real category of accidents:** a rival ontological proposal requiring its own argument.
- **One merely stipulated/falsely attributed relation:** a separate epistemic error.

Rejecting the first **does not logically entail** the third or fourth. Also, rejecting a shared two-subject accident is different from determining whether each separate directional relation is *wujūdī* or *iʿtibārī*. The terms *nisba*, *iḍāfa*, *taʾlīf*, *jawār* and *ukhuwwa* need author-specific analyses and source layers.

This section is a **logical reconstruction by the present editor**, not a quotation of Iṣfahānī or an assertion that he endorses all options.

## C. Comparison within Arabic sources, not yet with Aquinas

- *Sharḥ al-Maqāṣid* I176–180 (JAW-001 Stage2) also poses the problem of one accident subsisting in two, the *taʾlīf* objection, accident transfer and individuating subject. This should now be aligned **argument-by-argument**, not merely by terminology.
- Earlier *Kashf al-Murād* p218 objects that the demonstration of conceptual addition of *jawhariyya* does not itself prove a second intelligible. Al-Iṣfahānī's suspension on genus is a different but parallel challenge to overconfident category epistemology.
- *Ḥikmat al-ʿAyn* p210 treats *maḥall* as wider than *mawḍūʿ* and warns that a material substantial form depends on a locus differently from an accident's inherence in a complete subject. **Thus no argument from “having a bearer” alone can identify a determination's category.**

## D. New claim ledger

- **JAW001-U** (READ-PDF-TEXT; facsimile and edition not matched): al-Iṣfahānī rejects proof that all instances of *jawhar* are species under no common genus, declares accidents do not have accident as a generic essence, and **suspends** whether jawhar is a genus.
- **JAW001-V** (READ-PDF-TEXT; same restrictions): one numerically identical dependent accidental *ḥāll* cannot, in the analyzed objection/reply, inhabit two distinct *maḥall*s; opposing directional relative determinations require separate treatment.
- **JAW001-W** (analytic conclusion dependent on V): the denial of one numerically shared accident **does not imply** denial of every objectively founded relational fact or individual relation.
- **Spiker SP-12** must be **kept I/UNPROVEN** as generalized assertion that relations *qua relations* cannot be individual extra-mental realities; this passage is not a proof of that universal claim. Neither assume real accidents in all cases.
- **SP-03/04:** the generic status of jawhar and matter/form mappings remains disputed by Arabic authors themselves. Not a single settled Avicennan translation.
