# JAW-001 · Stage 6 — Al-Dasūqī on objectively established *iʿtibārī* determinations

**Dated:** 2026-11-01. **New direct upstream/downstream witness:** Muḥammad b. Aḥmad b. ʿArafa al-Dasūqī (d.1230/1815), *Ḥāshiya ʿalā Sharḥ Umm al-Barāhīn*, Egyptian al-Ḥalabī printing transcribed on [IslamArchive p129](https://www.islamarchive.cc/ketab_content/102912/p-129) and [p130](https://www.islamarchive.cc/ketab_content/102912/p-130), read as a connected passage. Transcribed HTML, **not** original scanned-image / independent printed edition verification. Another author and genre than Bannānī's *Jamʿ al-Jawāmiʿ* gloss. This is NOT a verification of Spiker's cited Bannānī I102.

## A. Actual theological question and three authorial layers

1. **Sanūsī matn (ص):** the seven *maʿnawī* predicates, e.g. God's being powerful, knowing, alive.
2. **Sanūsī sharḥ (ش):** If *aḥwāl* are admitted, these can be counted as **separate *thubūtī* conditions** inhering in the Divine subject, neither straightforwardly existent nor sheer nullities. On denial of *aḥwāl*, they signify the actually subsisting *maʿānī* (power, knowledge etc.), without further attributes separately fixed outside intellect. Both sides affirm the underlying predicates true.
3. **Al-Dasūqī's independently labelled *ḥāshiya* (حاشية الدسوقي):** On p129 he explains the difference between *ḥāl* and a true attribution considered *iʿtibārī*. Denial of an **additional inhering condition** does not mean that the attribution arises from arbitrary opinion. He says:
   > وهذا لا ينافي أنها أمر اعتباري ثابت في نفسه بقطع النظر عن اعتبار المعتبر وفرض الفارض كالإمكان والحدوث
   Provisional translation: “This does not conflict with its being a considerational determination established in itself independently of a considerer's consideration and a supposer's supposition, as with possibility and origination.”
   This is p129, al-Dasūqī layer, not the Sanūsī base text. Distinguish *lā thubūt lahā fī al-khārij ʿan al-dhihn* as **no additional predicate-entity firmly inhering** from absence of objective truth altogether.
4. **p129→p130 spanning the page break:** the addendum distinguishes a *ḥāl* as **qārr fī al-dhāt** (firmly established in the subject, under the view that aḥwāl exist) from objective *iʿtibār* as **ghayr qārr fī al-dhāt** (not an additional abiding entity in the subject). He explicitly divides the latter:
   > قسم له تحقق في نفسه بقطع النظر عن اعتبار المعتبر وفرض الفارض ... وقسم لا تحقق له إلا في الذهن
   “One kind has actuality/obtaining in itself irrespective of a considerer's regard and a supposer's hypothesis … another kind has no actuality except in the mind.”
   Example of the **second**, falsely regarding a generous individual as miserly, with miserly quality existing only through that considerer's attribution.
5. **p130 following sentence:** al-Dasūqī moves to *taʿalluq*, relations of divine attributes to their objects, and says properly relevant *taʿalluq* belongs to the attributes *maʿānī*, not to second-order *maʿnawiyya*, on the view affirming *aḥwāl*. This guards against treating every true description as a new causal/relational attribute, and makes the ontological problem of *taʿalluq* central.

**Historical scope:** This is a later Sunni Ashʿarī *ḥāshiya*, not a transparent statement of early Ashʿarī atomism, Ibn Sīnā, al-Jurjānī or Bannānī. Spiker n145 reports a comparable twofold usage in Bannānī; that specific Arabic page, its source voices and its distinction of *khārij* remain **unverified**.

## B. Three separate questions, not one false dichotomy

- **Truth/nafs al-amr:** Is the predicate objective irrespective of this knower's opinion? For a true objective *iʿtibār*, **yes**.
- **Subject's ontological inventory:** Does its truth require a further numerically distinct property *qārra fī al-dhāt*? The anti-*aḥwāl* view says **no** for the example. The pro-*aḥwāl* view posits an intermediate condition, without conventional positive individual existence.
- **Source of objectivity:** Is this objective predicate necessarily a separately subsisting eternal archetype? **Not established** by the two-sense distinction. A predicate may be true on an already established source/attribute and a nonarbitrary analysis thereof.
- **Cognitive/semantic difference:** An objectively true propositional determination may be *iʿtibārī* in how conceived without being psychologically fictitious. Conversely a fictitious attribution remains a mental event without the claimed predicate holding in nafs al-amr.
- **Potential extension:** *imkān* and *ḥudūth* are offered as examples of genuine consideration. This does not mean either is a spatial atom, a sensory accident, a divine condition, or a Platonic form; their truthmaker needs an author-specific account.

## C. Separate Iṣfahānī, *Tasdīd* II150 (JAW-001 Stage3)

The earlier text distinguishes *iʿtibārī farḍī* (hypothetical) from *iʿtibārī ḥaqīqī*, with *yalzam taḥaqququhu bi-l-iʿtibār* under genuine consideration. In contrast, al-Dasūqī calls true consideration **established regardless of anyone's considering** (*bi-qaṭʿ al-naẓar ʿan iʿtibār al-muʿtabir*). These may be reconcilable on a difference between **actual mental apprehension** and a **counterfactually correct objective consideration**, but that is a hypothesis and not yet a demonstrated historical equivalence. Maintain independent entries in dictionary.
