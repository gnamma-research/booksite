## 1. What the fivefold classification actually classifies

**III.5–6, textual distinction.** The reported theological division identifies spatial jawhar as divisible body (*jism*) or indivisible *jawhar fard*. The reported Peripatetic division has (1) **ṣūra**, the substantial formal constituent inhering in another jawhar; (2) **hayūlā**, the receptive material constituent; (3) **jism**, their composite; (4) **nafs**, intrinsically separate in essence, but concerned in the management of a body; and (5) **ʿaql**, separate in essence and action. Three proposed division trees yield the five. The text states their premises openly: opposition to the indivisible atom, genuine matter and form, and genuinely separate soul/intellect. This is **a conditional philosophical taxonomy**, not a proof of the existence of all five.

**III.7–8, alternate classification and qualification.** The text notes a *Shifāʾ* explanatory illustration involving wood and the shape of a chair, and distinguishing a body's actuality by form from its potential material recipient. The editorial apparatus notes alternate readings (“bed”/“chair”). Crucially, **an example illustrating the analytic functions of matter and form is not an argument that carpentry produces a new specific substantial nature**. The account attributed to some ancient philosophers instead admits corporeal *jism* and incorporeal *nafs/ʿaql* without separate hylomorphic substances *hayūlā* and *ṣūra*; “matter” can then denote a body in a receptive respect and “form” an accidental property of it. Keep this reported historical alternative distinct from Taftāzānī's adopted conclusions.

**III.8–10, categorical constraint.** The page explicitly states *al-maḥall aʿamm min al-mawḍūʿ* (the inherence locus is broader than the constitutively pre-existent subject), and *al-ḥāll aʿamm min al-ʿaraḍ* (what inheres is broader than an accident). A substantial form can depend on matter but still not exist *fī mawḍūʿ* because its material recipient is not an already constituted subject independently capable of this accident. An accident can exist mediately through another accident: speed can be attributed to a moving body through its motion. This does NOT collapse the logical subject of predication into the ontological bearer. The universal substantial nature may be classified as jawhar in the **conditional** sense (“if in al-aʿyān, then not in a subject”) while its actual individual image inhering in an intellect is a particular psychic accident.

**III.10–12, transition to the kalām body.** The next argument examines whether *jism* is the mere collection of atomic components or whether a further *taʾlīf* inhering in components is required, and whether it is one accident in two substrates or individually distributed. Al-Āmidī's reading of the dispute as verbal and al-Mawāqif's as substantive are separately reported; it would be false to present a single kalām answer. Continued discussions of extension (three dimensions) are often characterizations *bi-l-khāṣṣa*, not demonstrated essential definitions.

**Result JAW001-L (READ-HTML; B pending scan):** Philosophical jawhar may include *incomplete intrinsic principles* while the atomistic scheme classifies *spatial indivisible/some divisible bearers*. The schemes are not extensionally equivalent, and even the fivefold scheme is contested internally.

## 2. Independent Ḥikmat al-ʿAyn chain — argument unit, not the Spiker edition

**Witness:** *Ḥikmat al-ʿAyn wa-sharḥuhu li-Shams al-Dīn al-Bukhārī*, searchable Mashhad edition, pp.209–214; most directly pp.210–212:
- https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/8246/210?lang=ar
- https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/8246/211?lang=ar
- https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/8246/212?lang=ar

**p.210:** A thing's locus (*maḥall*) is called *mawḍūʿ* if it is independent in constitution from the inhering state; the latter is then *ʿaraḍ*. If the locus requires its formal occupant in some respect, the two can be designated *hayūlā/ṣūra*. The possible circle of mutual dependence is rejected **when needs are in different respects**, not by declaring matter and form absolutely independent. Argument context includes the stone-plus-human as no single real composite, with an objection that the sort of unity (“actually constituted” vs “considered”) must be specified first.

**p.211:** Opening formula: “al-jawhar huwa al-māhiyya allatī idhā wujidat fī al-aʿyān kānat lā fī mawḍūʿ.” It explicitly considers universals/mental images, in which *represented quiddity* may retain a conditional categorial predicate even if *this individual cognitive state* is an accident. A seeming contradiction (“simultaneously substance and accident”) arises because the conditional criterion for jawhar has not been matched by an equally conditional criterion for ʿaraḍ.

**p.212:** It reports correction of the ambiguous fivefold tree: simply saying “something which is a locus is matter” is too broad, because an actual body is locus of accidents but not therefore prime matter. The commentary specifies the kind of formal constituent involved. Exact speaker attributions across matn/Sharḥ and editorial footnotes still need inspection of original typesetting.

**Result JAW001-M (READ-HTML; B):** “Not in a subject” is a condition on how a nature would be actual, NOT a claim that its currently occurring mental representation is itself an extramental independent substance. The presence of a real material locus does not automatically imply a prime material principle.

**Open source debt:** Spiker refers to different *Ḥikmat al-ʿAyn* pages 38, 41, 43–44, 48 including a Jurjānī gloss. These **have not** been identified through this Mashhad pagination, and this text should **not** be presented as the exact verified gloss on the bed. Carry the citation debt forward.

## 3. The crucial Tasdīd passage: genus resembles matter, but does not become matter

https://shamela.org/gen/eea1bf004328c97cd62b22182d5a7a0d_gen.pdf  
Internal textual pagination visible around vol. II, pp.144–150 (approximately PDF physical leaf 150; textual reading plus screenshot spot check). This is **not yet matched** to Spiker's cited **vol. I pp.406–407** of an identified printed edition. Other PDF layers/footnotes must not be silently attributed to the author.

### 3.1 Whole argument, in order

**a. Real one-ness and dependence (internal p.144):** The gloss distinguishes a composite truly one from independent things merely put alongside one another (*man wa-ḥajar*); relevant parts must depend in a suitable way. If they depend reciprocally it must not be in the **same respect**, to avoid a circle; matter needs form for its actual being, while the form requires matter for its individualization. Other sorts of collective form may depend on the members without being a further substance.

**b. Intellectual versus external parts (internal pp.145–146):** Parts of quiddity either have distinct external grounds or are only distinguishable as separate essential conceptual forms, such as genus and difference with respect to a species. The gloss states, *wa-l-ḥaqq anna al-jins wa-l-faṣl lā yatamāyazān fī al-wujūd al-khārijī*. It argues by *ḥaml bi-l-muwāṭāʾa*: if each were a numerically distinct extramental entity, identical predication of each of the same species would fail. It **qualifies** this: conceptual genus/difference can be extracted from actual separately characterized external principles, e.g. human bodily and psychic constituents, **or not**, as with an intellect whose logical definition can be composite though it is externally simple.

**c. Body as matter versus body as genus (internal p.147):** *jism* taken as intrinsically material part is not predicable as the complete nature; *jism* taken generally without qualification can be predicated of kinds below it. The literal word is the same while the formal consideration changes; this is not proof of two distinct body-entities inside one person.

**d. Explicit *Tajrīd* lemma and commentary (internal p.148):** The root lemma is “wa-l-jins ka-l-mādda wa-huwa maʿlūl, wa-l-faṣl ka-l-ṣūra wa-huwa ʿilla.” The answer introduced by *aqūlu* is carefully comparative: “al-jins ashbah bi-l-mādda ... wa-l-faṣl ashbah bi-l-ṣūra” — genus is **more like** matter and specific difference **more like** form. Difference counts as *ʿilla* for intellectually determining an indeterminate generic concept, not for producing a separate extra-mental genus. The decisive explicit negation reads: *wa-tawahhum kawn al-faṣl ʿilla li-ṭabīʿat al-jins fī al-khārij khaṭaʾ* (“Imagining that the difference causes the nature of the genus in external existence is mistaken”). The following reason is that genus and difference are the same extra-mentally in the relevant predicative respect; a separate cause would need to be existentially prior to its effect.

**e. *Iʿtibārī* bifurcation (internal p.150):** In an objection/reply on a hypothetical quiddity composed of coordinate parts, the *aqūlu* text distinguishes *iʿtibārī farḍī*, which need not obtain merely because considered, from *iʿtibārī ḥaqīqī*, which necessarily obtains through its relevant consideration. This is a located precursor/control for Spiker's Bannānī reference, BUT DOES NOT establish identical dependence/independence from the act of considering. Both the special sense of *yalzam taḥaqququhu bi-l-iʿtibār* and Bannānī's purported *taḥaqquq fī nafsihi bi-qaṭʿ al-naẓar ʿan iʿtibār al-muʿtabir* require full separate readings.

### 3.2 Consequences

**JAW001-N (READ-PDF textual+one screenshot spot check, B pending exact print concordance):** Genus/difference are logically distinguishable constituents that may or may not be derived from independent extra-mental constituents. Their resemblance to matter/form is not ontological identity.

**JAW001-O (same source grade):** The causehood of specific difference for genus is explicitly restricted to cognitive specification. **If read as a necessary external cause, the passage itself calls that an error.**

**JAW001-P (same source grade):** A division of *iʿtibārī* into *ḥaqīqī/farḍī* is now directly located in a pre-Bannānī philosophical commentary text; whether it exactly matches the later two-sense tradition is NOT established.

**Spiker impact:** SP-04 now has a strong contemporaneous source-level qualification, but this scan is **not** a verification of his exact page citation. SP-03 and SP-09 receive an additional reason to distinguish external unity from conditional forms. SP-10 has a newly located independent parallel, **not** a confirmation of Bannānī I102. SP-12 and SP-17 remain separate philosophical inferences, not conclusions proved by genus–difference distinction.

## 4. al-Ṭūsī and al-Ḥillī's second-intelligible dispute

**Direct Arabic witness:** al-Ḥillī, *Kashf al-Murād*, Āmulī edition, p.218:
https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/3439/218?lang=ar

The lemma's doctrine states *al-jawhariyya wa-l-ʿaraḍiyya min thawānī al-maʿqūlāt*. al-Ḥillī reports that predicating *jawhariyya* may require a middle term (demonstration) and notes that this argument proves **additionality to the subject's first bare conception**, not thereby its being a **second intelligible**. His own phrase: *yadullu ʿalā al-ziyāda lā ʿalā kawnuhu min al-maʿqūlāt al-thāniya*. Subsequent arguments distinguish levels of proof; do not assign the extensive footnotes of the modern edition to al-Ḥillī.

**JAW001-Q (READ-HTML, B):** In the Arabic tradition, **a categorical characterization of a thing** and **the thing's quiddity/actuality** are distinct ontological and logical questions. The criterion of needing demonstration does not settle whether substantiality is a second intelligible. This cannot be collapsed into the thesis that substance is “merely subjective”.
