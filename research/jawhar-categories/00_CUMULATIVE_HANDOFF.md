## 0. The research question and historical background
Do not begin by equating Arabic **jawhar** with Aristotelian/Thomistic *substantia* or with the kalām atom **jawhar fard**. First ask what each author classifies: (a) a contingent actual individual, (b) an intelligible quiddity **if it were to exist in al-aʿyān**, (c) a complete support for accidents, (d) incomplete intrinsic principles of a body, or (e) a second intelligible through which a nature is classified.

The ultimate aim is to reconstruct the Arabic concepts of **jawhar, ʿaraḍ, maḥall, mawḍūʿ, ḥāl, qiyām, ḥulūl, tarkīb, ḥaqīqī, iʿtibārī, iḍāfī, ʿayn, al-khārij, al-dhihn, shakhs, fard, tashakhkhuṣ, taʿayyun, nisba, iḍāfa, hayʾa, ṣūra, quwwa, fiʿl, nafs al-amr**. Reconstruct their definitions and internal arguments from historical source witnesses BEFORE comparing with Aristotle, Aquinas, Poinsot or the broad comparative metaphysics.

## 4. JAW-001 — Initial argument-controlled collation

### 4.1 Avicenna, Ilāhiyyāt II.1
**Source status:** direct Arabic searchable page from Anawati–Zayid print identified, plus uploaded scanned PDF pending independent image concordance.

A subject of inherence is **a thing constituted in its own nature**; the inhering accident is not a constituent part of the subject, and it does not subsist apart from that subject. **Not existing in such a subject** means jawhar. This is not equivalent to 'existing without causes' or 'standing in no relation.' A corporeal substantial form can require its material recipient yet qualify as jawhar because the recipient qua matter is NOT a pre-constituted mawḍūʿ supporting an accidental attribute.

**Claim JAW001-A:** 'Jawhar = independent being' is unsafe. More precisely, it is independent of an already constituted subject *in the categorial sense*. Evidence grade **A: direct Arabic text**, but manuscript variants not independently collated.

### 4.2 Rāzī, Mabāḥith I.137
Rāzī opens by warning that Arabic **fī** has many senses (time, place, whole, part, purpose, etc.). He specifies the technical *ḥulūl* and describes two directions of constitutive dependence:
- When the ḥāl determines/constitutes the maḥall: **ṣūra/mādda**.
- When the maḥall already supports the ḥāl as its bearer: **mawḍūʿ/ʿaraḍ**.

He treats maḥall as broader than mawḍūʿ and ḥāl as broader than ʿaraḍ. Hence *maḥall* is not synonymous with spatial location or a logical subject.

**Claim JAW001-B:** The difference is causal/constitutional, not grammatical. Grade **A** for direct transcribed Arabic page, no variant collation.

### 4.3 al-Taftāzānī, Sharḥ al-Maqāṣid I.173–176
**Voices must be kept separate:** `ʿinda al-mutakallimīn` describes a reported theological classification; `ʿinda al-falāsifa` describes a reported philosophical classification. Neither is automatically an unconditional authorial commitment.

- In the reported theological scheme created jawhar is spatially situated in its own right, whereas ʿaraḍ is dependent upon it.
- The exclusion of a separate incorporeal created jawhar from this system is connected with **absence of established proof**, with al-Taftāzānī observing weakness of a purported argument for impossibility (I.174). This is not proof that the author thinks immaterial substances logically self-contradictory.
- In the reported philosophical scheme a contingent being not in a *mawḍūʿ* is jawhar; a substantial ṣūra may require *maḥall* yet be free of *mawḍūʿ*.
- **The ten categories are derived by inductive enumeration**; al-Taftāzānī challenges any alleged demonstration that they are exactly ten, and says that *in subject/not in subject* is a **rasm bi-l-lāzim** rather than an essential *ḥadd* (I.175).
- He records competitors reducing nine accident categories to fewer higher genera, or proposing motion as another category; he raises the problem of unity, point, being and possibility not sitting straightforwardly within the ten, and some relational categories not being `aʿyān khārijiyya` (I.176).

**Claim JAW001-C:** A glossary that says 'there are ten real kinds of things' unqualified would already distort this author's inquiry. Grade **A** for direct Arabic page transcription, **B pending facsimile/edition parallel**.

### 4.4 al-Taftāzānī, Sharḥ al-Maqāṣid III.5–10 (different pagination)
He lists the theological body/indivisible *jawhar fard* and the philosophers' fivefold body, matter, form, soul, intellect. He provides **multiple competing routes of division** (receptacle/inherent/composite; separated/conjoined; extension/constituents), admits their dependence on contested theses (denial of indivisible atom, reality of form/matter, immaterial soul/intellect), and reports alternatives from older philosophers denying hylomorphic parts as independent jawāhir.

**Claim JAW001-D:** Even the fivefold taxonomy is not a deductive proof of the independent reality of all five terms. The disputed ontology must be defended first. Grade **A/B** for searchable edition; scan/page concordance pending.

### 4.5 al-Ṭūsī, Tajrīd §253–258; al-Ḥillī, Kashf pp.214–218
`§253`: `الممكن إما أن يكون موجودا في الموضوع وهو العرض أو لا وهو الجوهر`.
`§254`: intellect separate in essence and action; soul in essence; conjoined material receptacle (matter), inherent form, composite body.
`§255–257`: maḥall and mawḍūʿ, ḥāl and ʿaraḍ, differ in scope; do not flatten.
`§258`: `الجوهرية والعرضية من ثواني المعقولات` — substantiality and accidentality are second intelligibles.

**al-Ḥillī's objection (Kashf pp.217–218):** the need for an inferential middle term might show that 'substantiality' is not immediately contained in the thing as first conceived, but does not itself establish that it must be a second intelligible. Distinguish a postulated ontological quality from a second-order classificatory predicate; this disagreement is internal to the Arabic tradition.

**Claim JAW001-E:** al-Ṭūsī's doctrine is not just a restatement of Avicenna that *jawhar* is a highest genus. The status of *jawhariyya* is disputed at the logical/metaphysical boundary. Grades **A** for uploaded bilingual text and corresponding al-Ḥillī Arabic online page; **B** original Ṭūsī scan cross-check pending.

### 4.6 al-Kātibī/Mubārakshāh, Ḥikmat al-ʿAyn p.211; al-Ḥillī, Īḍāḥ p.161
The conditional formulation `الجوهر هو الماهية التي إذا وجدت في الأعيان كانت لا في موضوع` permits intellectual representations of a substantial quiddity to remain 'jawhar' by the mode of their **represented nature**, despite the act/image being presently in an intellect.

Al-Ḥillī **directly challenges** treating the *intellectual image itself* as a jawhar: the image is a *representation of* substance and not therefore substantial in its own actuality. The opposition is not about whether human or equine quiddities can be apprehended, but whether the categorical predicate belongs in the same respect to the *representation* or its *represented nature*.

**Claim JAW001-F:** Distinguish `māhiyya as represented` from `mental form qua inhering in a knower`. This is a real dispute, not simply a translation choice. Grades **A/B** for direct Arabic text of pp.210–211 and alternative commentary at p.161; precise attribution of matn, sharḥ, gloss pending critical edition.

### 4.7 Avicenna, Nafs, opening and soul-as-jawhar section
He explicitly argues that `kamāl` (completion/perfection) of a thing **does not entail jawhariyya**. A king can perfect a city and captain a ship without being their substantial form. Avicenna proceeds separately to ask whether a vegetative, sensitive or rational soul is a *jawhar* and what its constitutive relation to a body is.

**Claim JAW001-G:** From 'X organizes/completes Y' one cannot infer 'X is a jawharī form of Y' without further argument. Grade **A** for direct Arabic textual pages.

### 4.8 Tahdhīb al-Kalām
Inherited bilingual work includes `الموجود إن لم يسبق بالعدم فقديم، وإلا فحادث ... إن تحيز بذاته فجوهر` and a reported philosophical alternative. The original 1912 scan is in Library. **But the inherited editor labels this passage UNCOLLATED / OCR-derived.** Its precise authorial wording, abridgement, and the al-Sanandajī commentary are not certified. Grade **C** until facsimile verification.

### 4.9 al-Ījī/al-Jurjānī, Mawāqif, section 4 opening
The available searchable Arabic witness says `ممكن موجود لا في موضوع عند الحكماء`; a marginal voice warns that `mawjūd` cannot be read as *actually instantiated*, or else uncertain existing objects such as a mountain of ruby would be uncertain in substantial quiddity. Read as conditional: *a quiddity which if instantiated would exist not in a subject*. The marginal gloss also includes alternative viewpoints. **Do not merge the voices.** Grade **B** until four-layer Arabic facsimile review.

## 5. Preliminary critique / semantic repairs already required
- **Correction TC-001:** `tarkīb ḥaqīqī` is 'real composition' and does not by itself mean AT substantial composition. Reserve the latter for an argued comparison.
- **Correction TC-002:** `jawhar` must initially be retained untranslated in contested passages; 'substance' is a conventional but non-decisive gloss. A jawhar fard is one historically defined sub-kind of spatial jawhar, not universally every jawhar.
- **Correction TC-003:** `mawḍūʿ` can be the subject of a proposition, subject matter of science, or an already constituted ontological bearer. al-Taftāzānī himself calls out the equivocation (III.10 / I.287 in another pagination).
- **Correction TC-004:** `khārij` and `aʿyān` must be defined in each argument; do not equate 'not an independently identifiable external particular' with 'mind-dependent in every sense'.
- **Correction TC-005:** `jawhariyya` treated as second intelligible is not the same assertion as 'there are no substances'; al-Ḥillī challenges the exact proof.
- **Correction TC-006:** Definition as `lā fī mawḍūʿ` is sometimes described as a `rasm bi-l-lāzim`, not the quiddity's essential `ḥadd`; don't present a necessary concomitant as a complete ontology.
- **Correction TC-007:** Distinguish `jawharī` substantial form from `ḥaqīqī` real composition and from the artificial form `ṣūra ṣināʿiyya`. A bed's configuration does not by itself make another natural substance.
- **Correction TC-008:** The number and ontological standing of categories are not demonstrated by merely listing ten; al-Taftāzānī considers induction and counterexamples.
