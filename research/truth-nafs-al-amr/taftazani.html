What makes truth true?

Saʿd al-Dīn al-Taftāzānī, Sharḥ al-Maqāṣid I:95–96

This page is a sourced selection of six passages. A longer 17-passage Arabic–English reading supplement exists separately; this webpage does not misrepresent the excerpts as the whole work. Attributions to Ṭūsī in the reported part of Taftāzānī's text are not Taftāzānī's own endorsements.

The argument in three questions

1 · What do we mean by nafs al-amr?

It denotes a relation as it is, apart from a particular thinker’s act of considering or reporting it. “External” here need not mean independently instantiated as a concrete body: one can objectively judge even a subject that lacks external existence.

2 · Does this require the Active Intellect?

Taftāzānī reconstructs the argument for a separate intellectual bearer of truths, but rejects equating the expression nafs al-amr with that bearer. Knowing that one is half of two does not presuppose knowing the theory of the Active Intellect. The inferential distinction matters: the objection to identity does not independently refute all metaphysical truth-ground proposals.

3 · What further problems arise for the theory?

He records objections involving truth about the Active Intellect’s own knowledge, divine knowledge supposed prior to it, and knowledge of particular events. He also sets out possible replies. These belong to the argumentative genealogy, not simply to a final verdict.
