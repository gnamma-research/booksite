# Qurʾānic narrative before principial interpretation
## Source check on Nūḥ, Maryam, Ayyūb, Hūd, Ṣāliḥ, Lūṭ and Shuʿayb · Sīrah 1.6.1

**Status:** Source-focused audit of selected Qurʾānic passages and accessible tafsīr, not a full collation of the early Arabic commentary traditions. This document establishes which claims can be made directly from the Qurʾān and where later interpretive language begins.

### Nūḥ — the mission must remain the centre

**Primary evidence:** 7:59–64; 11:25–49; 23:23–30; 26:105–122; 29:14–15; 54:9–17; 71:1–28. The order of emphasis in 71:1–10 is the command to warn, call to worship of God, seek forgiveness, and endure rejection; it is not primarily a discussion about the social rank of the earliest believers.

**The chiefs and the followers.** At 11:27 the unbelieving chiefs object that Nūḥ is a human being like themselves and that those who follow him appear to be the lowest in their society. In 11:29 Nūḥ refuses to drive away those who have believed. Similar speech occurs in 26:111–115. This is important evidence for the rejection of class-based grounds of spiritual worth; it is one part of the larger proclamation rather than the entire subject of Nūḥ's prophetic life.

**The command and the Ark.** 11:37–40 commands construction of the Ark under divine instruction. This is an enacted prophetic command, requiring ordinary work in real materials as an appointed means. It is not necessary to describe it as a “technical form of obedience” or to invoke a specific cosmic analogy of paired creatures unless a verified source warrants that interpretation.

**The son.** 11:40 explicitly mentions family with an exception for those against whom God's word has already passed. In 11:42–43 the son refuses his father's appeal to board and trusts a mountain to protect him. In 11:45 Nūḥ recalls his kinship and God's true promise, in the form of a question directed to God; in 11:46 God clarifies that this son is not among the people promised rescue and commands Nūḥ not to ask about what he does not know; in 11:47 Nūḥ responds by seeking refuge, forgiveness and mercy. The Qurʾān neither portrays Nūḥ manipulating God nor declares his paternal concern itself sinful.

**Exegetical complexities.** Classical authorities disagree about the referent and grammatical construction of 11:46 (the reading *innahu ʿamalun ghayru ṣāliḥ* and readings relating the unrighteous conduct to the son), about whether the son's disbelief was hidden from Nūḥ, and about some lesser details of the surviving family. Ibn Kathīr's tafsīr explains the statement “not of your family” as not included among those promised rescue; tafsīr reports cited by al-Ṭabarī include other readings. The sound narrative paraphrase should not silently resolve these questions in favour of one contested psychology.

**Plain English conclusion:** The prophet continues calling his people to God; his son does not accept his call; the father appeals to God; God clarifies the scope of His promise; Nūḥ submits and asks forgiveness. The theological lesson is that belonging to a prophet's family does not independently guarantee deliverance or replace faithfulness to God. **What is NOT established:** that Nūḥ “tried to enlarge the promise,” or that affection itself is the reason the son drowned.

Sources: https://quran.com/11/40 ; https://quran.com/11/42 ; https://quran.com/11/45 ; https://quran.com/11/46/tafsirs ; https://quran.com/11/47 ; https://quran.com/71/1-10 ; https://quran.com/26/111-115 .

### Hūd — worship, arrogance and coercive power

**Qurʾān:** 7:65–72; 11:50–60; 26:123–140. Hūd's prophetic call centres on the worship of God without partners and turning away from rebellion. In 26:128–130 he questions the building of monuments on high places and the possession of works as if people would live forever, and accuses their exercise of force of being tyrannical. The account can illustrate pride in strength and building, but it is not the story of a prophet whose central mission is simply opposition to architecture.

**Explanatory limit:** *Maṣāniʿ* receives different lexical/exegetical interpretations—substantial structures, fortifications, waterworks—so one must not claim that a particular excavated building is directly identified by the verse.

Sources: https://quran.com/11/50-60 ; https://quran.com/26/123-140 .

### Ṣāliḥ — Thamūd's response to an unmistakable sign

**Qurʾān:** 7:73–79; 11:61–68; 26:141–159; 27:45–53. Ṣāliḥ calls his people to worship God, reminds them of divine provision and presents the she-camel as a sign. Her allotted turn at the water (26:155) creates a concrete obligation; their harm to her occurs as part of collective disobedience. The water schedule is important, but it cannot carry the full meaning of their rejection, judgment and refusal of prophecy.

**A caution about conspiracy:** 27:48–50 describes nine men causing corruption and their plot against Ṣāliḥ and his family. It should not be automatically assumed that these are the same named persons who physically hamstrung the camel without distinguishing transmitted interpretations.

Sources: https://quran.com/26/141-159 ; https://quran.com/27/48-50 .

### Lūṭ — care of guests, public wrong and divine rescue

**Qurʾān:** 11:77–83; 15:61–77; 29:28–35. The visitors' arrival exposes Lūṭ's vulnerability before a hostile group. He appeals to his people to fear God and not disgrace him before his guests. In 11:80 he expresses the wish for strength or powerful supporters; the angels then disclose their mission and tell him to leave with his household subject to the stated exceptions. The plain action is the attempted protection of guests and the rescue delivered by God. The phrase “divine promise supplies refuge beyond worldly force” conceals rather than explains that action.

**Akbarian source line:** Ibn ʿArabī's Lūṭic *Fuṣūṣ* reflects on *malakiyya*, power and human limitation; the argument is later metaphysics and requires a separate Arabic source check before importing it as an account of Lūṭ's intention.

Source: https://quran.com/11/77-83 .

### Shuʿayb — worship and truthful dealings

**Qurʾān:** 7:85–93; 11:84–95; 26:176–191. The first call is to worship God. Shuʿayb condemns false measures, misuse of people's property and public corruption. In 11:87 the people ask whether his prayer tells them how to worship or what to do with their wealth; in 11:88 he declares that he will not do himself what he forbids others. The theological significance is that devotion and just dealing are parts of obedience to God, not the abstraction that Shuʿayb “binds prayer and honest exchange.”

Source: https://quran.com/11/84-95 .

### Ayyūb — patience, prayer and the details of an oath

**Qurʾān:** 21:83–84 and 38:41–44. Ayyūb calls upon God concerning his affliction and receives mercy and restoration. In 38:42 he is instructed to strike with his foot, and in 38:44 a bundle is mentioned in relation to fulfilling an oath. **The Qurʾān does not explicitly identify in the verse whom he struck or why he swore the oath**; traditional tafsīr supplies the story involving his wife, and some available translations put that interpretation in interpolated brackets. It is a mistake to present such later narrative details as if directly in the Arabic verse.

**Theological lesson:** seeking relief from God is not necessarily contrary to patience. The Sufi reading of patient servanthood is an interpretation of these transmitted events, not evidence that suffering as such is good or that a sick person should refrain from treatment.

Sources: https://quran.com/21/83-84 ; https://quran.com/38/41-44 .

### Maryam — two scenes, no unreported hierarchy between them

**Qurʾān:** 3:37; 19:16–35. Zakariyyā finds provision with Maryam in the sanctuary (3:37). During her childbirth (19:23–26), Maryam experiences anguish, hears reassurance and is told to shake the trunk of a palm to receive dates. A spiritual reading can distinguish provision not sought by ordinary efforts from reliance on God through ordinary means. But neither 3:37 nor 19:25 declares that the act of shaking the palm proves a higher saintly station than the earlier provision, or that motherhood had degraded her prior condition. A claim of such graded realization must be explicitly sourced to an interpreter and defended rather than placed in the Qurʾānic narrative voice.

Sources: https://quran.com/3/37 ; https://quran.com/19/23-26 .

### Implications for 1.6.1 and the remaining research

1. Preserve the prophets' core calls and the actual sequence of the Qurʾānic narrative.
2. Where an Akbarian commentator offers a subtler spiritual account, cite the precise author, text and passage and distinguish the interpretive standpoint.
3. Do not force a psychological assessment of a prophet beyond what the text warrants.
4. When the episode bears a relation to fiqh, family, resource allocation or communal life, develop that **after** establishing the source's principal subject.
5. Retain disputable tafsīr or qirāʾāt issues in the scholarly apparatus, without resolving them by linguistic preference alone.
6. Future research: full Arabic comparison of al-Ṭabarī, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī and Ibn ʿArabī on 11:46, and the precise context of the *Fuṣūṣ* Nūḥ, Hūd, Ṣāliḥ, Lūṭ, Shuʿayb, Ayyūb and Maryam discussions. Their historical verification status remains explicitly open.
