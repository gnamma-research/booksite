# Editorial prose policy — Sīrah and the Making of Islamic Civilization

**Adopted 9 November 2026 · Applies to both matn and sharḥ.**

## State the history and arguments, not what the writing does

The grammatical problem is not only passive voice. A sentence can use an active verb and still describe the *reader's encounter with the text* instead of the historical subject. Remove phrases such as:

- “Those differences prepare the reader to …”
- “This chapter shows why …”
- “The narrative now turns to …”
- “The point is to understand …”
- “We should not imagine …”
- “The reader must follow …”

Replace them with **direct claims about agents, decisions, institutions, texts, evidence and consequences**.

**Before:** “Those differences prepare the reader to understand why the later Prophet’s mission is not exhausted by a single category.”

**After:** “Muḥammad proclaimed revelation, established worship, taught moral obligations and adjudicated public disputes.”

**Before:** “The contrast between Uḥud and the Trench makes the chapter more than two battle summaries.”

**After:** “At Uḥud some archers abandoned their positions; at the Trench the defenders maintained collective labour and vigilance during a prolonged siege.”

## Build explanations with mechanisms

- Name the actor and action.
- Explain the actual constraint, practice or institutional mechanism.
- State the consequence for the next historical development.
- Attribute contested interpretations to the source or interpreter. For example: “al-Ṭabarī reports …”, “al-Ghazālī distinguishes …”, “Hodgson argues …”.
- Preserve uncertainty through concrete evidence language: “The surviving reports differ on …”, not “The reader should be cautious …”.
- Avoid grand concluding flourishes and claims that an entry is “significant”, “helpful”, “a bridge” or “reveals” something without explaining what happened.

## Two layers

**Matn:** continuous narrative, active verbs where possible, actual people, dates, decisions, practices, and consequences. Do not cite Qurʾān verses or ḥadīth numbers inside the matn.

**Sharḥ:** source evidence and exact citations; competing accounts where materially important; historical causation; subsequent philosophical and spiritual interpretation. Do not use the sharḥ to praise the matn, explain what the chapter intends or restate its themes.

Distinguish historically reported conduct from later Ghazālian, Shādhilī, Akbarian and traditionalist interpretations. Use a broadly Sunni sequence, represent consequential Shiʿi perspectives accurately and proportionately, and do not make sectarian disputes the organizing backbone.

## Revisions and review

Editorial changes to the reader require synchronized updates to `data/chapters.json`, `read.html`, and the versioned complete manuscript. Preserve earlier source editions and document the scope of change. Test for self-referential expressions, links, every matn–sharḥ pair and both reading modes. A style revision does not constitute new source verification.
