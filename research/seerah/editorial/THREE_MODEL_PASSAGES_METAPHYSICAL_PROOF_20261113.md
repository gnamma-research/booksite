# Three model passages: the Muhammadan Reality, encounters, and the making of ʿumrān
### Controlled writing specimen · 13 November 2026

**Status:** proposed replacement samples for Chapters 1, 13 and 44. These remain separate from the current reader until editorial approval. The explanatory movement is *principial comprehensiveness → historically embodied act → transmission and formal differentiation → social and institutional history*. The Akbarian doctrine is attributed to its interpreters; narrated conduct and historical institutions retain their own witnesses and reasons.

## 1. Opening lesson — The names, Iblīs, and the comprehensive human

### Matn (proposed)

Every created perfection discloses a relation of dependence upon God. The divine Names name the perfections through which creatures become intelligible: life, knowledge, power, mercy, justice and beauty appear in differentiated forms according to each being's receptivity. Ādam receives knowledge of the names and a vocation to exercise *khilāfa* on earth. His spiritual dignity consists in the human capacity to know distinct realities together, understand what belongs to each and order action toward their Source.

The command to prostrate places that dignity before Iblīs. He looks upon clay, compares it with fire and makes his comparison the measure of Ādam. In the Akbarian understanding, the human being gathers a bodily form and a spiritual comprehensiveness whose unity is received through the heart. Iblīs takes the visible particular as the measure of the person and defends his judgment against the divine address. The same pattern takes another form in the Garden: Satan directs the longing for lasting life toward a created tree under the promise of immortality. Desire loses its proper direction when a finite good is expected to fulfil a longing whose end is God.

Ādam receives words of repentance and returns in dependence upon the One who guides him. Iblīs persists in self-assertion. The struggle with temptation becomes part of humanity's earthly formation: discernment is exercised, the faculties acquire their rightful order, and repentance opens the heart to divine mercy. Human life unfolds among means—work, households, land, trust, law and mutual dependence—through which spiritual responsibility acquires bodily and social expression.

The prophetic missions disclose different perfections of this vocation. In the Akbarian account, the *Muḥammadan Reality* expresses their principial comprehensiveness, while the historical Muḥammad ﷺ completes the succession with the final revealed guidance. His worship, judgment, teaching, relationships and service are integrated acts of one embodied prophetic life. The sciences and institutions of later centuries preserve and develop distinguishable aspects of that life, transmitting obligations, knowledge and the possibility of character formation to peoples living in new circumstances.

### Sharḥ (specimen)

The Qurʾān gives the defining narrative relations: Ādam receives the names (2:31–33), Iblīs objects on the basis of fire and clay (7:12; 38:76), Satan promises a tree of immortality (20:120), and Ādam receives words of repentance (2:37). The metaphysical argument concerns **what constitutes a comprehensive human subject**. In the Adamic chapter of Ibn ʿArabī's *Fuṣūṣ*, the human being functions as a comprehensive mirror of the Names. Al-Qayṣarī's *Muqaddimah*, in the exposition of Mukhtar Hussain Ali (pp. 47–49 of the supplied study), relates Iblīs's restricted perception to *al-wahm al-kullī*, the estimation of particulars. The real error occurs when that partial cognition governs the act of obedience. The spiritual heart holds together intelligible meaning, bodily knowledge and the ability to respond to a determinate claim.

The providential dimension deepens the analysis. In *Futūḥāt* I:362, Ibn ʿArabī considers how the tempter's eagerness for ruin leaves him blind to the possibility of resistance and repentance. The subsequent goods are received through God's guidance and mercy. Al-Ḥallāj's *Ṭawāsīn*, Aḥmad al-Ghazālī's *Sawāniḥ* and ʿAyn al-Quḍāt's *Tamhīdāt* explore, in different literary registers, the attachment of the self to its own chosen mode of fidelity. These are separately attributed interpretive voices. They illuminate how devotion itself can become captive to a restricted self-image.

This analysis prepares the Muhammadan synthesis. In Qūnawian and Farghānian interpretations, the comprehensive Muhammadan principle gathers differentiated prophetic wisdoms under a unity of spiritual realization. Chittick's account of the *maqām lā maqām* clarifies its human significance: perfected realization sees both the universal orientation and the particular claim of the present situation. These are two considerations of one subject, with principial priority belonging to the order of metaphysics and chronological completion to the Prophet's actual historical life. The Sīrah must therefore trace how this comprehensive vocation takes form through a child's needs, a litigant's claim, a treaty, a market, worship and other concrete relations.

## 13. Madinah — Prayer, a crying child, and the person before the rule

### Matn (proposed)

The Prophet ﷺ entered congregational prayer intending to prolong it. Hearing a child cry, he shortened the prayer because he knew the distress the child's crying caused its mother. Worship remained directed toward God, and the conduct of the prayer changed in response to the condition of one of its participants. The mother and her child belonged to the living congregation whose circumstances the imam understood and served. Through a single act, the prophetic vocation embraced devotion, attentive knowledge, authority and the welfare of another person.

A different encounter arose when Māʿiz and, later, a woman of Ghāmid approached the Prophet seeking judgment concerning adultery. Their reports preserve repeated questions, exhortations to repentance, eventual severe penalties and the public response to those punished. The woman's pregnancy and the care of her child governed the timing of proceedings and the arrangements made by others. Judicial responsibility, repentance, protection of a dependant and the education of the surrounding community entered the same field of action.

The two encounters disclose the range of the comprehensive prophetic vocation. The governing relation in each is the recognition of what is due to God, the person addressed and the other persons affected. Prayer requires a valid form and thoughtful leadership; a criminal proceeding requires evidence, competent judgment and attention to the rights of those whose lives it affects. The Prophet's conduct became available to later generations through witnesses, recollection, practice and instruction. Jurists, hadith critics, teachers and spiritual authors would preserve different aspects of these encounters through the methods of their disciplines.

### Sharḥ (specimen)

[Al-Bukhārī 709](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:709) transmits Anas's report of the Prophet's intention, the sound of crying, his knowledge of the mother's distress and his deliberate shortening of prayer. This four-part sequence supports a precise explanation: **an act already directed to God receives its appropriate measure through accurate attention to the particular persons sharing it**. The imam's realization of the mother’s condition becomes relevant to how a valid congregational prayer is conducted. Its later juridical discussion concerns lawful lightening of prayer, the duties of leadership and the limits of ritual variation.

[Muslim 1695a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1695a) supplies a contrasting judicial configuration. The report includes Māʿiz's repeated approaches, inquiries into his condition, the penalty carried out and the Prophet's commendation of his repentance. The Ghāmidī woman's pregnancy and her child's need for care shape the execution of judgment; an Anṣārī undertakes responsibility for the infant's nursing. Juristic analysis discriminates confession, proof, the authority to punish and the rights of a dependant. Ethical and spiritual analysis attends to repentance and the community's response. The episode requires the severity of the punishment to remain visible in the explanation.

Within the Akbarian interpretation, the *station of no station* supplies a formal account of the whole: the comprehensive servant realizes divine guidance through the appropriate response to distinct actualities. The claim concerns the **form of integrated action**. The transmitted reports are the witnesses to what occurred; subsequent spiritual writing offers a further interpretation of the coherence of that conduct. A jurist's account of valid prayer or criminal procedure is authoritative within its proper jurisdiction. The report also presents relational knowledge, formed character and responsive judgment in their living conjunction.

The historical movement begins with identifiable routes of transmission: the Companions' reports, their study in teaching circles, the comparison of narrations and the formation of juristic problems. Legal schools subsequently distinguish the act's normative features, while *adab* and *tazkiya* cultivate the habits through which a person becomes attentive and trustworthy. These are different modes of access to one inherited prophetic life.

## 44. Colonial law — From ʿUmar's endowment to an imperial legal controversy

### Matn (proposed)

At Khaybar, ʿUmar acquired land of exceptional value to him and asked the Prophet ﷺ how to use it. The Prophet advised him to retain the property and devote its yield to charity. ʿUmar established conditions governing its preservation and named beneficiaries among the poor, relatives, travellers, guests and others. He also permitted the administrator reasonable benefit in the course of service. Property, family responsibility, the claims of strangers, future provision and the honest exercise of administration were brought into a durable arrangement.

The arrangement supplied a formative precedent for *waqf*. Jurists studied the ownership and permanence of the dedicated asset, the founder's stipulations, the beneficiaries' claims, the manager's powers and the evidence required to establish the endowment. Over centuries, founders used these legal forms to support families, schools, worship, welfare and urban services. Different juristic interpretations and administrative settings gave the institution distinguishable forms.

A dispute in Sylhet exposed the change in the institution's political setting. Two brothers executed a family endowment deed in 1868; later transactions produced litigation among descendants and property claimants. The Privy Council in 1894 rejected the validity of the settlement because it considered the ultimate charitable benefit too remote. Muslim legal argument and political contest continued, and legislation in 1913 recognized qualifying family endowments; a 1930 measure extended that recognition retrospectively while preserving rights already accrued. A question once presented to the Prophet as the fitting use of one man's land now appeared within an imperial appellate hierarchy and a modern legislative order.

### Sharḥ (specimen)

In [al-Bukhārī 2737](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2737), ʿUmar's question concerns a particular valuable asset and the right form of giving it. The answer distinguishes its enduring substance (*aṣl*) and distributable produce. ʿUmar's stipulations then coordinate the claims of kin, the vulnerable, travellers, guests and the administrator. The original encounter exemplifies a **unified practical discernment**: one human choice joins worship, durable provision, truthful management and responsibility toward persons both present and future.

The legal abstraction concerns the structure that makes this undertaking repeatable. Earlier Ḥanafī works on endowments, notably the treatises of Hilāl al-Raʾy and al-Khaṣṣāf, investigate what constitutes an endowment, the legal force of a dedication, the meaning of founders' conditions and the claims of beneficiaries. Jurists disagreed over bindingness, ownership and certain familial arrangements. A school of fiqh thus transforms normative aspects of an exemplary act into adjudicable rules while preserving their own evidentiary and interpretive methods.

The Sylhet dispute supplies the later historical witnesses. The 1868 deed provided primarily for descendants, reserving a charitable destination after the family's extinction. The [Privy Council's judgment of 15 December 1894](https://courtkutchehry.com/judgments/abul-fata-mahomed-ishak-and-others-appellant-hash-russomoy-dhur-chowdhry-and-others-respondent) treated that ultimate charity as too remote to sustain the dedication. The [1913 validating statute](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/113875091/) and [1930 retrospective provision](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/192467222/) redefined the legal effect of relevant family endowments. Gregory Kozlowski's *Muslim Endowments and Society in British India*, chapter 5, reconstructs how a family property dispute became a contest over the authority to define Muslim law in a colonial court.

The metaphysical question remains active at this larger scale: what form of property and administration allows a received good to serve rightful human ends across generations? The juristic question asks which deeds and conditions establish enforceable rights. The historian investigates the jurisdiction, imperial procedure, creditors, legal professions and legislation through which those rights were decided. Marshall G. S. Hodgson's *Venture of Islam* provides a wider account of the differentiated religious and Islamicate orders and of modern technical-political transformation; *The New Cambridge History of Islam*, volume 5, supplies comparative histories of Muslim societies under expanding Western dominion. The local records establish this particular institutional passage from deed to court to statute.

## The derivation: four different kinds of warranted passage

| Transition | What is established | Warrant in these samples |
|---|---|---|
| Principial → human comprehensiveness | The human subject gathers differentiated faculties and can realize their rightful relations; in Akbarian metaphysics, the Muhammadan Reality expresses comprehensive prophetic perfection | Qurʾānic Adamic teaching, the Adamic and Muhammadan *Fuṣūṣ*, Qūnawī/Farghānī, al-Qayṣarī, Chittick |
| Comprehensive vocation → particular act | One situated act engages different persons, rights, purposes and constraints in an actual time and place | Al-Bukhārī 709 and 2737; Muslim 1695a; surrounding reports and textual comparison |
| Particular act → learned discipline | Reports are authenticated; normative features are abstracted and reasoned about; practices and dispositions are formed | Hadith criticism, uṣūl, fiqh, adab, tazkiya, Ḥanafī waqf treatises and schools of instruction |
| Learned discipline → historical ʿumrān | People embody and institutionalize understandings through property, teaching, administration, coercion, courts and law | Named deeds, the 1894 judgment, 1913 and 1930 statutes, Kozlowski, Hodgson and Cambridge scholarship |

**Proof rule:** the doctrinal argument explains why the historical fragments have an intelligible unity; primary reports establish the individual acts; legal literature establishes the transformation into technical norms; archives establish local and imperial institutional outcomes. Together they support a layered explanation of inherited guidance and changing ʿumrān. The doctrinal premise is specific to the Akbarian tradition and remains open to philosophical evaluation. All three examples are specimens for assessment before any replacement of the main manuscript.
