# Sīrah prose and principial fidelity audit
## Edition 1.6.1 quality pass — 12 November 2026

**Reason for revision.** The sentence “Nūḥ rejects the chiefs' contempt for poor believers, builds under revelation and learns that paternal affection cannot enlarge the divine promise” compresses three different Qurʾānic scenes into a list of loosely related moral conclusions. It disproportionately promotes a secondary detail of the chiefs' rhetoric into the centre of Nūḥ's mission and makes fatherly love appear a rival attempt to extend a promise. The Qurʾān's account is more concrete and subtle: Nūḥ called people to worship God and sought their forgiveness (71:1–10); warned against resistance and explained the mission to his detractors (11:25–31); constructed the Ark at God's command (11:37–40); called his son to board and heard him refuse in reliance upon a mountain (11:42–43); then asked about the relation of his son to the promised family and was corrected about the scope of the promise and about asking beyond his knowledge (11:45–47).

**Preferred replacement of the compressed Nūḥ sentence:**
> Nūḥ called his people to worship God and remained steadfast despite their rejection. At God's command he built the Ark. When the flood came, he called his son to join him. His son refused and was drowned. Nūḥ's subsequent appeal to God brought a clarification: belonging to a prophet's family does not, by itself, guarantee salvation. Fidelity to God takes precedence over family ties, however strong those ties may be.

Keep the details of the chiefs' objection to poorer believers in the sharḥ where 11:27–31 and 26:111–115 can be read directly. Do not claim Nūḥ himself “enlarged the promise”; the later exegetical dispute about his son's hidden faith and the referent of the reading *innahu ʿamalun ghayru ṣāliḥ* remains separate.

## Expanded literary rules

1. **Concrete narrative before derived principle.** Begin with what the person did, said, decided and experienced according to the accessible witness. Let the spiritual and civilizational significance be explained *after* the event, where appropriate, rather than replacing the event with an abstract slogan.
2. **Proportion.** Distinguish central subject (Nūḥ's proclamation of worship and perseverance, rejection, judgment and survival) from significant subsidiary detail (the chiefs' social contempt). Do not raise a minor but interesting detail into the sole defining phrase of a prophetic career.
3. **Faithful conceptual language.** The Qurʾān describes Nūḥ's appeal, his son's response, God's clarification and Nūḥ's subsequent prayer. Paraphrase that sequence without imputing psychological motives or asserting a formulation that reverses the original emphasis. Divine promise refers to God's *given word*; “larger/smaller promise” is an invented image and not a clear explanation.
4. **Do not anthropomorphize abstract nouns.** “Fidelity takes precedence” may be clear; “a promise enlarges kinship” or “authority learns to redistribute itself” does not describe an actor. Use subjects—Nūḥ, the son, a judge, a teacher, a community—rather than “jurisdiction,” “inheritance,” “continuity,” or “the covenant” as people who act.
5. **Remove telegraphic triads masquerading as interpretation.** “Shuʿayb binds prayer and honest exchange” sounds like a compressed taxonomy; say that he taught the worship of God, demanded honesty in measure and weight, and refused to make an exception for himself.
6. **Meaning before technical jargon.** Reserve *barzakh*, *maqām lā maqām*, *taʿayyun* and abstract “differentiation” for sharḥ passages where a precisely attributed doctrine is actually analyzed. They are not literary substitutes for words, decisions and deeds in the matn.
7. **Avoid compulsive qualification.** Source caution is necessary when a report is disputed, but a paragraph built entirely from “not merely,” “not simply,” “cannot automatically,” or “without becoming” has ceased to explain. State positively what evidence establishes, then briefly say where the inference ends.
8. **Prophetic respect and theological care.** A prophet's appeal to God or strong compassion toward his child should not be presented as an attempt to manipulate God. In disputed reports about prophetic failings, quote Qurʾān and attribute the interpretation; don't invent metaphysical lessons about a prophet's deficiencies.
9. **No precocious social theory.** Do not assign a seventh-century household the technical categories of a later jurisprudential or Akbarian tradition as its actors' own consciousness. Later authors may interpret it from a distinct perspective in the sharḥ.
10. **No overclaiming of historical causality.** An episode can be an exemplary precedent without being the documented founding moment of an institution. Where a later *ʿumrān* mechanism is established, name the parties, documentary connections and the conditions under which it operated.
11. **Rhythm and readability.** Vary sentence length, prefer clean idiomatic English, and reserve abstract summative sentences for the end of a genuine explanation. A prose paragraph is not a sequence of miniature conclusions.
12. **Layer discipline.** Matn offers a self-sufficient narrative. Sharḥ gives precise verses, reports, interpretive perspectives and historical argument. Editorial provenance and uncertainty logs remain in the research apparatus, not as prose about what the editor “should do.”

## Auditing protocol

- Inspect and revise **all 46 matns**, with priority on Chapter 1's compressed prophetic catalogue, then reread by book to ensure coherence.
- Inspect all **46 shurūḥ**, fixing distorted emphasis, ungainly abstractions, repetitions and editorial-address sentences without silently removing source evidence or contested historical qualifications.
- Record chapter-level changes with before/after snippets, and preserve an exact prior edition before any canonical write.
- Preserve 46 IDs, titles, ordering and links. Check parity of canonical JSON, static HTML and versioned manuscript, and test chapter links and both reading modes where the available environment permits.
- A literary/source-fidelity pass **does not certify full primary-source or Arabic witness collation**. Leave claim-level verification status in the ledger unchanged absent independent corroboration.

## Priority example corrections

- **Ādam:** Begin with Qurʾānic teaching of names, prohibition, lapse, prayer and descent; confine Guénonian/ Akbarian interpretations to the sharḥ and attribute them.
- **Nūḥ:** Centre worship, persevering proclamation, rejection, Ark, son, and divine correction; place chiefs and rank in their appropriate context.
- **Hūd and Ṣāliḥ:** Do not reduce an entire mission to monument-building or water allocation. Begin with worship, warning and response; treat arrogance and signs as concrete facets.
- **Lūṭ:** Relate the arrival of guests, their vulnerability, the people's conduct and God's rescue; avoid the obscure expression “the divine promise supplies refuge beyond worldly force.”
- **Shuʿayb:** State his call to worship God and cease fraud in weights and measures, with his pledge not to do what he forbids.
- **Yūnus:** Narrate departure, prayer, acceptance and his people's repentance rather than depicting his prophetically distinctive lesson as an abstract caution about haste.
- **Books IV–VII:** An argument about political or cultural plurality is not a substitute for the named people, offices, concrete decisions, texts, markets or courts through which it happened.


## Additional source-fidelity correction · 12 November 2026

The prose-reviewed 1.6.1 draft inadvertently placed the Ark's construction after the flood had already arrived. Qurʾān 11:37–40 gives the construction before the flood; 11:42–47 gives the son's refusal and Nūḥ's subsequent appeal. The chronological wording in Chapter 01 has been corrected accordingly. Ādam's chapter opening again states Iblīs's refusal to bow (7:11–12; 38:76), an essential part of the earlier metaphysical explanation. The reader and full manuscript were synchronized.
