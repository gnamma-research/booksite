# The Muhammadan Axis of ʿUmrān
## Prophetic integrality, the limits of disciplinary traces, spiritual inheritance, and the formation of civilization

**Gnamma · Sīrah and the Making of Islamic Civilization · Methodological research memorandum · 11 November 2026**

**Editorial status.** A proposed comprehensive framework for interpreting the seven books and 46 matns. It brings together the already established source-led historical method, the integrated prophetic exemplar, micro-to-civilizational formation, and Akbarian interpretation. It does not certify newly completed revisions of the remaining narrative chapters. Metaphysical theses are attributed to the relevant intellectual traditions rather than silently treated as historical facts.

## I. Central proposition

The Prophet Muḥammad ﷺ is not merely the biographical founder of several religious disciplines. The unity of knowledge, revelation, worship, judgment, mercy, practical deliberation, moral formation and social direction in his prophetic life **precedes the later differentiation of these functions into specialized sciences and offices**. A rule, description or discipline can preserve true aspects of this living integration, but the totality cannot be reconstructed by summing its specialized accounts.

The Qurʾān is revelation, not merely documentary information. The Prophet's character was described by ʿĀʾishah as the Qurʾān (Muslim 746a), and the Qurʾān identifies his practice as exemplary (33:21), his character as exalted (68:4), and his commission as joining recitation, purification and teaching (62:2). The familiar “walking Qurʾān” is an English metaphor for this hadith, not its literal wording. The Qurʾānic doctrine of the Book's inimitability and the Prophet's non-repeatable plenitude are related by analogy, not by an identity of proofs or modalities.

The Qurʾān remains a living source of divine guidance after the Prophet's passing: its recitation itself acts upon hearts (39:23) and the mission addresses others who would come later (62:3). Its living efficacy must not be denied by claiming that a spiritual master is always necessary to make it real.

## II. Distinguish three statements often collapsed into one

1. **Revelatory and normative proposition:** the Prophet ﷺ is the bearer of the final message and authoritative exemplar; his actual conduct joins moral, spiritual, legal and interpersonal dimensions that subsequent disciplines investigate separately. This rests on Qurʾān, Sunnah and their traditional interpretation.
2. **Akbarian metaphysical interpretation:** the comprehensive Muhammadan Reality (*al-ḥaqīqa al-Muḥammadiyya*) has principial priority, and the historical Prophet is the complete manifestation of the prophetic mode of human perfection. Ibn ʿArabī's *Fuṣūṣ*, especially its Adamic and Muhammadan chapters, and subsequent Qūnawian and Jīlīan commentaries differ in formulation. This is a specific intellectual and spiritual school, not a universally agreed doctrine.
3. **Historical explanation:** particular practices, relationships, sciences and institutions actually developed through ascertainable human transmissions, social arrangements and constraints. Their origin and function cannot be established solely from metaphysical analogies.

These three may be interpreted together without conflating them. Divine transcendence remains intact; prophetic mediation is not an incarnation or a claim of independent divinity. God's guidance, grace and causality are not displaced by the spiritual station of a creature.

## III. The original, its traces, and unequal modes of participation

The image of a **trace, shadow or silhouette** is appropriate when a transmitted account preserves one selected aspect of an integrated personal act: a juristic ruling, a statement of manners, a teaching technique, a biographical report, a model for spiritual discipline. What survives is not necessarily false, thin or unimportant; it can be entirely valid within its domain. Yet **analytical correctness does not equal existential comprehensiveness**.

An episode may involve simultaneously: public law; correction of a person's self-understanding; responsibility for a child or dependant; restraint by a judge; the emotional state of observers; patterns of mercy, truthful speech and social protection; spiritual knowledge inaccessible in full to the historian. No single analytical abstraction exhausts the whole event. And even a complete literary reproduction of all observable details would not reproduce the interior reality of the participants.

Distinguish:
- **Authenticated report** (*riwāya*): textual access to an event, with due attention to the evidentiary quality and variants of the report.
- **Formal discernment** (*fiqh*, *uṣūl*, *ḥikma ʿamaliyya*): correct judgments about its normative structure, rights, duties and conditions.
- **Imitative formation** (*ittibāʿ*, *adab*, *riyāḍa*): repetition of authorized practices reshaping understanding, desire and action.
- **Living companionship** (*ṣuḥba*): instruction and correction by a present person whose conduct can disclose aspects of wisdom not explicitly articulated.
- **Experiential recognition** (*dhawq*, *maʿrifa*, *ḥāl*, *maqām*): states, stations and understanding that the Sufi tradition describes as realized rather than simply inferred.
- **Prophetic inheritance** (*warātha*): the graded reception of works, states and knowledge according to the Akbarian reading, without assuming an inheritor becomes a law-giving prophet.

These are not ranked as though good fiqh were merely a preliminary failure and spiritual experience the only valid kind of knowledge. They concern different ends and different sorts of evidence. Experiential depth may increase the person's grasp of a revealed exemplar without conferring the competence to decide an unfamiliar legal, medical or technical question.

## IV. The Akbarian case: comprehension and non-repeatability

The Adamic *Fuṣūṣ* explains human comprehensiveness through the divine Names and the universal human's function as a polished mirror. The Muhammadan chapter treats the Prophet as culminating the line of prophetic wisdom. Richard Todd, “The Perfect Human,” in the St Andrews Encyclopaedia of Theology, distinguishes Ibn ʿArabī's own formulations from the more explicit claim in later al-Jīlī that the Prophet is the *insān kāmil* par excellence.

The unique historical and prophetic commission is not reproduceable. The possibility of saintly participation in particular aspects of his perfection is a distinct claim: *participation is not identity, inheritance is not renewed revelation, and approach to comprehensiveness is not equivalence of rank*.

Two further distinctions are necessary:
- In the Traditionalist vocabulary, **restoration of the primordial human state** and realization of trans-individual or universal perfection are not identical. Guénon distinguishes the “lesser mysteries” that restore the centre of the human state from “greater mysteries” beyond it. Do not simply equate becoming morally well-formed, restoring primordial integrity, attaining *walāya*, and embodying *al-insān al-kāmil*.
- A metaphysical interpretation of complete prophetic reality does not authorize an editor to assert full access to the Prophet's inward intention in every recorded event. Even a deeply significant episode must be reconstructed from reliable accessible testimony before its speculative or initiatic levels are discussed.

## V. The decisive epistemic problem: knowing that, knowing how, and being in a state

Al-Ghazālī's *al-Munqidh min al-ḍalāl* distinguishes discursive description of the Sufi path from **experiential tasting** (*dhawq*). Reading about generosity is not being generous; observing a wise response is not possessing the discernment that generated it; giving a definition of surrender is not surrender. The difference cannot be eliminated simply by increasing the number or detail of reports.

The Sufi literature distinguishes a **ḥāl** (often described as a divinely bestowed, transient spiritual state) from a **maqām** (a more stable station developed through discipline and grace). A **malaka** is an acquired and durable disposition or skill, and should not be identified indiscriminately with every ḥāl. These terminologies have distinct histories; investigate how each author uses them.

Living *ṣuḥba* can disclose aspects of right response that words alone do not transmit: a teacher recognizes a student's particular condition, instructs differently according to need, exposes pretence, and corrects the interpretation of an experience. In the initiatic reading, *baraka* or spiritual influence concerns more than pedagogical imitation. This stronger claim is central to certain Sufi and Guénonian understandings of transmission, but its metaphysical validity must not be passed off as an empirically established mechanism identical across traditions.

**The phrase “from original to original, heart to heart” should be qualified.** A realized person may be transformed in the presence of another, but neither ḥāl nor spiritual degree is simply a transferable object. God bestows realization; sincere companionship, correct practices, a legitimate spiritual relationship and sustained effort can be means. The later shaykh is a human inheritor, not a duplicate or infallible substitute for the Prophet. Claims of spiritual insight never remove revealed obligations or the need to establish public evidence.

Nor should the ordinary believer's access to guidance be denied. Qurʾānic recitation, worship, moral action, family instruction and reflection can genuinely form the heart even when no realized master is physically present. A mature teacher can deepen, correct and individualize these means; making access to spiritual reality exclusively dependent on one claimed elite would require stronger argument than the sources provide.

## VI. The key textual model: works, states, knowledge

William C. Chittick, “The Muhammadan Inheritance,” *Horizons Maghrébins* 30 (1996), 55–61, reports Ibn ʿArabī's three modalities of inheritance:

1. **Works**: acts embodying right conduct, worship, virtue, service and responsibilities;
2. **States**: inner experiences and spiritual dispositions, particularly what is known through realization;
3. **Knowledge**: discernment of the realities and their proper relations, not mere possession of statements.

This model is stronger than an exterior/interior dichotomy. Works, states and knowledge are reciprocally related in a complete person. The higher claim is not that a mystic's subjective feeling should override public law, but that right action is deepest when joined to an appropriate state and true understanding.

Chittick emphasizes that each prophet is associated with a differentiated paradigm of human excellence, that Muhammadan inheritance is comprehensive in relation to these paradigms, and that no human effort mechanically produces divine opening. The Prophet's comprehensive rank therefore explains why later inheritors may receive different and unequally developed aspects of one spiritual heritage.

## VII. Prophetic presence as the axial centre of civilization

The **axis** image is interpretive and geometrical; the intersection of vertical and horizontal is not a claim that the Prophet is divine or an indispensable independent intermediary between God and all created beings.

**Vertical axis:** divine dependence; ultimate end; revelation; spiritual and human perfection; graded realization; return to God.

**Horizontal plane:** historical peoples, material conditions, families, cities, economic specialization, law, scholarship, communication, political authority and the differing forms of civilization.

The **Muhammadan nexus** is the historical prophetic exemplar in whom divinely bestowed guidance became concrete in the full range of human life; within Akbarian metaphysics this is read as the outward disclosure of a more comprehensive prophetic reality.

Neither axis eliminates the other:
- Vertical principles are manifested in embodied activity, but no one empirical activity exhausts them.
- Horizontal activities have real natures and causal powers; they require geographical, economic, psychological and institutional explanation.
- A realized person acting with wisdom can hold distinct goods in proper relation without abolishing their distinctions.
- Subsequent civilizational arrangements must develop the practical competence to preserve those relations in changed settings.

The civilizational **whole** is formally relational; no single specialized profession or political organization monopolizes the prophetic inheritance. Specialization is sometimes the historical condition for preserving the inheritance at scale, not automatically a degeneration.

## VIII. From one act to an enduring ʿumrān

An integrated encounter becomes socially consequential, if it is transmitted and repeated, through the following *possible* pathways:

**Prophetic action in concrete circumstances → report, observation or companionship → correct discernment of relevant principles → imitative action and inner formation → stable personal dispositions (*malakāt*) → trustworthy relationships → repeatable practices → organizations and learned disciplines → transregional institutions and civilizational patterns.**

The arrows describe **questions to investigate**, not an empirically proven one-way chain. Institutions and cultures in turn influence each person's opportunities, habits and judgments. Material needs, ecological conditions and political pressures remain genuinely causal.

In a case of confession concerning *zinā*, for example, legal analysis distinguishes confession and evidentiary proof; ethics examines speech, protection, restraint and contempt; spiritual analysis considers repentance; social formation concerns how witnesses were corrected; and metaphysical reflection asks about justice, mercy and the standing of human beings before God. The reports about Māʿiz and the Ghāmidī woman are related but distinct and must not be silently conflated. Severe physical punishment cannot be omitted from the record. None of the analytic dimensions yields certainty about every inward state present in the event.

The claim that an encounter provides a legitimate *exemplar* is distinct from a claim that a later court, customary rule or school historically arose *because of* that encounter. Documentary verification is required for the latter.

## IX. The finite corpus and the open-ended complexity of later circumstances

A final revelation can be universal without giving one specific historical example for each conceivable future circumstance. The sheer number of hadith reports is not the proof of universal guidance, and no finite collection enumerates every future technology, psychological condition or social structure.

Sufficiency must be investigated at three different levels:
1. **Authoritative end and normative foundations:** intelligible goods, prohibitions, obligations and revealed orientation;
2. **Generative legal, ethical and intellectual methods:** qualified interpretation, distinctions among cases, *qiyās*, *maqāṣid*, accepted forms of consideration of customary practice, specialist knowledge and disciplined *ijtihād*;
3. **Transformative formation:** real capacities for discernment, virtue, spiritual attention and adjustment to particular persons and circumstances, cultivated through action, worship, counsel and (in particular paths) companionship.

The rich range of transmitted prophetic situations contributes a plurality of *exemplars* across human relationships and conditions. Their explanatory “resolution” is not only numerical detail: it is the disclosure of how goods can be properly related under diverse constraints.

Increasing complexity requires **more precise discernment and context-sensitive application**, not the fiction that one hadith necessarily supplies an exact modern answer. For example, the Prophetic inheritance can orient household ethics in a world of small families, migration or digital work; but credible judgments require knowledge of the real household's constraints, local law, economic arrangements, education and, where relevant, psychological or medical expertise.

Guénon's “dark age” is a particular Traditionalist interpretation of historical degeneration. A historian may use it as a hypothesis about qualitative loss, but cannot assume that modern people are uniformly more corrupt, or that every new institution is a mark of decline. Technologies can intensify fragmentation and also preserve archives, expand teaching, or facilitate relationships. Actual results must be studied.

**A fertile analogy:** the prophetic corpus is not merely a library of prefabricated answers, but a connected grammar of revelation, exemplarity, reasoning and formation. No analogy may be pressed so far that it makes explicit revealed rulings optional or interprets every state as independent authority.

## X. Consequences for the seven-book architecture

**Book I: Adamic and Muhammadan principles.** Explain the comprehensive human vocation and differentiated prophetic wisdom without mistaking symbolic and metaphysical time for archaeological chronology. The account of Muḥammad's final commission is anticipated as comprehensive rather than an accretion of independent earlier perfections.

**Book II: Personal and relational formation in Makkah.** Witnesses experience the Prophet's character, companionship, steadfastness, household relationships, trust, patience, moral correction and revelation. These are not merely preludes to later institutions.

**Book III: Integrated prophetic society in Madinah.** Each major episode should show which goods are held in relation within one act: worship, judgment, mercy, welfare, treaty, protection, public authority and personal spiritual formation. The micro-encounter is as important as the battlefield, constitution or treaty.

**Book IV: Differentiation without new prophecy.** The prophetic functions are inherited by partial human offices: judges, scholars, rulers, teachers, fiscal administrators, households and spiritual exemplars. Their proper relations must be investigated, not anachronistically presumed.

**Book V: Differentiated sciences and living formation.** Examine fiqh, hadith, tafsīr, kalām, adab, taṣawwuf, isnād, pedagogy, companionship and cultivated *malakāt* as overlapping media of inheritance. The vital question becomes how the sciences remain mutually ordered and how persons are actually formed rather than credentialed alone.

**Book VI: Civilizational plurality.** A common Muhammadan orientation takes different valid forms in Cairo, al-Andalus, the Persianate world, the Niger region, the Indian Ocean, Bengal, Ottoman lands, Safavid Iran and Mughal India. Distinguish unity of principles from mandatory cultural uniformity.

**Book VII: Complex modernity and renewal.** Study transformed family forms, modern sciences, law, migration, media, digital mediation, access to information and the fate of companionship. Assess whether institutions preserve or interrupt the movement from transmitted forms to genuine knowledge, character and living relationships. Renewal (*tajdīd*) may require restoration of such relationships, not merely institutional labels.

## XI. Mandatory explanatory questions for each episode

1. What is the historical record, with variant reports and the limits of accessible evidence?
2. Which specifically identifiable act of the Prophet or later participant is at issue? What was said, done, withheld, delayed or permitted?
3. What goods, rights, relationships and constraints were integrated in the act?
4. What can fiqh, ethics, tafsīr, practical philosophy, psychology and spiritual interpretation each establish within their jurisdiction?
5. Which dimensions cannot be established from reports, and what experiential claims belong to later traditions?
6. What *malaka*, *adab* or relational practice could be formed by imitation, instruction or companionship?
7. What actual channels of transmission, documented institutions, economic realities and historical mechanisms were involved?
8. Can a vertical interpretation be stated without substituting symbolism for historical causation or declaring inaccessible inner states?
9. What breaks when a discipline or institution treats its partial competence as the whole?
10. What form of integration is genuinely possible in this historical context, and what is distinctively left unresolved?

In the continuous **matn**, record the concrete human action and consequences without essayistic methodological commentary. In the **sharḥ**, reconstruct evidence, mechanism and principial relation. In separate **research dossiers**, document interpretive layers, textual disputes, metaphysical attributions and historical uncertainty.

**Canonical formulation:** *The revelation and comprehensive prophetic exemplar provide the vertical and normative centre; the transmitted forms and sciences preserve genuine, necessarily differentiated dimensions; living practice, thought, companionship and divine gift enable graded realization; relationships and institutions allow these dimensions to operate across times and cultures. The history of Islamic civilization studies how this mediation is achieved, transformed, diminished or renewed under real historical conditions.*
