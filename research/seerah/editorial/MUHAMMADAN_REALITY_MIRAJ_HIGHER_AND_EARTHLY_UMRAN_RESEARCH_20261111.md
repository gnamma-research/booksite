# The Muhammadan Reality, the Miʿrāj, and the Two Orders of ʿUmrān
## A source-led Akbarian and Traditionalist investigation of the Prophet as the living axial centre of religious civilization

**Research date:** 11 November 2026
**Project:** Gnamma · Sīrah and the Making of Islamic Civilization · Seven books, 46 matns and 46 shurūḥ
**Status:** Research memorandum, not final critical edition. Recovered Gnamma translations are often working texts and their own status registers must be consulted. The Colby volume and some named Akbarian commentators were bibliographically located but not directly collated in full. The original chapter corpus is preserved without overwriting the published reader.

## Executive argument

The Prophet Muḥammad ﷺ is the historical bearer of the final divine message and an integrated exemplar of worship, understanding, justice, mercy, instruction, bodily practice and human relationships. This integrated unity is logically prior to subsequent divisions into fiqh, taṣawwuf, kalām, tafsīr, adab, ḥadīth, governance and institutional studies. The sciences preserve genuine but disciplinary partial apprehensions; their sum is not identical with the person, and especially not with his invisible spiritual reality.

Within the Akbarian interpretation, the **Muḥammadan Reality (al-ḥaqīqa al-Muḥammadiyya)** is a comprehensive, principial locus of the divine Names. The historical Prophet is its complete manifestation in the prophetic mode of human existence. This does not entail incarnation, created divinity or independent cosmic causality. Different authors locate what they call the Muḥammadan Reality at different levels or under different considerations—first determination, comprehensive Name, Light, Spirit, Universal Human, cosmic mediation—and a sound comparison must retain this contextual differentiation. The created Prophet remains God's servant.

The Prophet's ascension and **return** are central. The Miʿrāj traverses differentiated heavenly stations, passes through encounters with previous prophets and culminates in distinctive nearness to God. Yet the comprehensive Prophet returns to ordinary life bearing the obligation of **five daily prayers**, joining the most exalted revelatory event to everyday bodily and communal acts. For Ibn ʿArabī, realization completed in the world is not identical with ecstatic absence from the world.

The projected “higher ʿumrān” is useful only as an explicitly new metaphor for the ordered spiritual worlds, presences and relationships of sainthood. **Ibn Khaldūn's technical ʿumrān is terrestrial human association**, so this metaphoric extension must not be ascribed to him. Earthly ʿumrān is not a dispensable shadow: embodiment is an indispensable locus for the disclosure of comprehensiveness. The divine orientation, living persons, spiritual companionship, duties, relationships, skills, households, crafts and institutions coexist within one historically differentiated field.

## 1. Four source registers that must never be collapsed

**Revelatory-normative:** Qurʾān (17:1; 53:1–18; 33:21; 68:4; 62:2–3; 39:23; 48:10; 57:3) and authenticated reports, such as Muslim 162a on Miʿrāj, Muslim 746a on character, Bukhārī 676 on serving the household, Bukhārī 709 on shortening congregational prayer for the crying child, and Bukhārī 6129 on addressing Abū ʿUmayr. Such accounts establish visible conduct and revealed norms, not unrestricted access to the Prophet's hidden experiences.

**Akbarian exegetical and metaphysical:** Ibn ʿArabī's Fuṣūṣ (Adamic and Muhammadan chapters), Futūḥāt Chapter 367 and the Kitāb al-Isrāʾ; al-Qūnawī's al-Nuṣūṣ; al-Farghānī's Muntahā al-madārik; al-Mahāʾimī's Mashraʿ al-khuṣūṣ; Ibn Turka's Tamhīd; and later discussions by al-Jīlī and others. These works disagree in emphases and employ different vocabularies. Their claims are attributed, not represented as unanimity of Muslim theology.

**Early Sufi devotional and experiential:** al-Sulamī's Laṭāʾif al-Miʿrāj, al-Qushayrī on knowledge, awe, shame and intimacy, the mawlid and devotional genres, and al-Būṣīrī's Burda. Poetry and mystical reports disclose interpretive possibilities and lived devotion; they are not automatically independently authenticated hadith or demonstrative metaphysical premises.

**Modern Traditionalist and scholarly reconstructions:** Guénon, Schuon, Martin Lings, William Chittick, James Morris, Richard Todd, Ismail Lala and Patrick Laude. The category of “Logos” in Traditionalist comparison is analogical and perspective-sensitive, not an uncontested lexical replacement for the classical Arabic terms. The scholar's or editor's theological conclusion must not be hidden within a purported translation.

## 2. What the historical Prophet and his higher reality mean

The Qurʾān speaks of the Prophet as the good exemplar (33:21), a bearer of exceptional character (68:4), and the messenger who recites, purifies and instructs (62:2). ʿĀʾishah's summary in Muslim 746a—his character was the Qurʾān—connects the revealed utterance with the lived, corporeal, relational human being.

The saying is not the literal expression “walking, talking Qurʾān”; that is a valid modern metaphor. Nor does the Qurʾān's own doctrine of iʿjāz logically establish that every aspect of the Prophet's life was inimitable in exactly the same sense as revealed speech. Their **analogical** relation is this: a comprehensive original cannot be reproduced merely by combining descriptions of its outward traces.

Ibn ʿArabī's Adamic Fuṣūṣ speaks of the comprehensive human mirror and its reception of the divine Names. The final Muhammadan Fuṣūṣ associates the Prophet's singular wisdom with the completion of human prophetic perfection. Richard Todd (St Andrews, 2024) notes the textual distinction between Ibn ʿArabī's descriptions of the Muhammadan spirit and later explicit identification by al-Jīlī of the historical Prophet with insān kāmil par excellence. Do not silently attribute every later developed formulation to Ibn ʿArabī.

A further distinction is essential: the **created body**, the **unique prophetic office**, the **degree of realized human perfection**, and the **principial Muḥammadan Reality** are not four interchangeable labels. They can be related within a metaphysical system while remaining distinct under their respective viewpoints. Divine attributes are manifested, not transferred as independently possessed divine powers.

## 3. Farghānī: the two Books and the balanced, enacted Qurʾān

Gnamma working critical dossier: Farghani_FULL_CUMULATIVE_CRITICAL_014.md (B2007 printed pp. 103, 109–116); current reader:
https://gnamma-research.github.io/booksite/texts/al-farghani/muntaha-al-madarik/

In the fourth foundation, p. 103, Farghānī distinguishes the complete divine disclosure in a comprehensive mirror from self-vision in differentiated manifestations. He identifies a formal or corporeal Muḥammadan manifestation in the Prophet's balanced temperament and an intelligible aspect in his all-comprehensive, Godwary heart. The balance matters: a human body is not simply an inert vessel for abstract intellect.

On pp. 110–111 he develops the cosmos as a manifest Book: differentiated beings correspond to letters, words, verses, sūras and books according to the relations they encompass. The revealed verbal Book expresses these relations in a distinct register. While earlier prophetic books express differentiated governing realities, the Qurʾān is read as articulating their undivided comprehensive equilibrium. The Prophet's sayings, actions, qualities and ethical traits express that same unity in embodied life. Farghānī explicitly appeals to ʿĀʾishah's description of his character as the Qurʾān.

On pp. 115–116, the Adamic process manifests differentiated **descent**, while the Muhammadan principle completes **ascent, return and reunion**. Earlier prophets articulate particular excellences without being restricted to a single isolated virtue. In Muḥammad, Farghānī argues, nubuwwa and wilāya meet in a distinctive equilibrium. The body and human relations are therefore not disconnected from metaphysical comprehensiveness.

This is unusually direct source evidence for the project's central thesis: **the prophetic actions are not merely cases to classify; they are the embodied articulation of a comprehensive order that cannot be exhausted by isolated classifications**. The text's Arabic transcription and proposed English rendering require further detailed independent collation; this is a research finding, not a certified word-for-word edition.

## 4. Qūnawī: restricted stations, comprehensive knowledge and the seven heavens

Gnamma source: 
https://github.com/gnamma-research/booksite/blob/master/texts/sadr-al-din-al-qunawi/al-nusus/data/parallel.json
Look up entries QUN-NU-004-A, QUN-NU-019-E, QUN-NU-019-G.

In Text Four (online pp. 238–239), Qūnawī differentiates knowledge and spiritual tasting appropriate to a **particular name or station** from his claimed comprehensive standpoint. A proposition may be valid from one determination yet not universally final in every situation. This is a vital epistemic qualification when attempting to reconstruct one prophetic action through juridical, devotional, spiritual and social viewpoints.

In Text Nineteen (pp. 256–260), Qūnawī correlates spiritual origins with the Mother of the Book, Highest Pen/First Intellect, Tablet, Throne, Footstool and imaginal degrees. In discussing the reported ascension, he does **not** treat the seven prophetic meetings as absolute, immutable celestial addresses or the total spiritual ranking of all prophets. Spiritual correspondences differ with the traits and modes of manifestation under consideration. The reported thirty-four ascensions attributed to an earlier collection still need bibliographical and hadith authentication.

This supplies a more disciplined version of the higher-dimensional metaphor. The same prophetic reality may be disclosed under multiple genuine conditions without one partial disclosure being its exhaustive definition. It does **not** follow that publicly accessible evidence of historical acts can be replaced by an inaccessible assertion of spiritual station.

## 5. Al-Mahāʾimī: from primordial reality to bodily, legal, spiritual and heart-centred inheritance

Gnamma bilingual project:
https://gnamma-research.github.io/booksite/texts/al-mahaimi/mashra-al-khusus/
Original working parallel:
https://github.com/gnamma-research/booksite/blob/master/texts/al-mahaimi/mashra-al-khusus/data/parallel.md

Printed pp. 67–70 (Gnamma entries MSH-055 to MSH-058) are especially important.

At p. 67 the heart is characterized as unitive comprehensiveness of spiritual and natural, and intellect as discerning and holding sciences. Five daily prayers correspond to a mystical pattern of reconnecting realities to their principial source. Al-Mahāʾimī distinguishes **acquiring good character** through effort (takhalluq) from more profound realization (taḥaqquq), while differentiating witnessing unity in multiplicity, multiplicity in unity, and the more complete gathering of both.

At p. 68 the Muḥammadan Reality is described through the all-comprehensive divine Name and the relation of the names to their manifestations. The Prophet is also described through knowledge and witnessing with different degrees of mediation. Read the strong language of *rabbāniyya* or lordship with p. 69, which explicitly insists that the Muhammadan principle's function is **derivative, not independent**, and the Prophet in his human aspect is a servant.

At p. 69, al-Mahāʾimī describes four aspects of the Prophet: **elemental body, religious/legal form, luminous spiritual form, and inward heart-centred reality**. Degrees of kinship with him may be physical, religious, spiritual or genuinely comprehensive; bodily lineage and full spiritual inheritance do not automatically coincide. His legacy includes knowledge, spiritual states and stations. These are very close to the user's proposed distinctions between external legal capture, ethical imitation, heart-to-heart companionship and realized inheritance.

At pp. 94–96 the commentator maintains a further limit: even the highest prophetic knowledge does not encompass the Divine Essence in its unconditioned reality. **Comprehensiveness remains creaturely and relational**, not the exhaustive divine self-knowledge of God independently possessed by a creature.

These are striking working translated passages. Some Arabic readings are flagged in Gnamma as uncertain; they require full contextual and textual review before formal academic citation.

## 6. Ibn Turka: real natural human unity versus cosmic aggregation

Gnamma:
https://github.com/gnamma-research/booksite/blob/master/texts/al-turka/qawaid-al-tawhid/data/commentary-english.md
Especially SH-039 (printed pp. 173–177), SH-050 (230–234), and SH-051 (234–237).

Ibn Turka distinguishes comprehensiveness by inclusion from determination through opposition. The human organism, united by an appropriate balance of faculties, produces acts that cannot be explained by a merely juxtaposed collection of discrete components. The macrocosm taken only as aggregate lacks this same living unity. The perfected human, by contrast, manifests a natural union of bodily constitution and spiritual intelligibility.

In SH-051, species-wide human comprehensiveness is differentiated according to individual preparedness and culminates in the personal unity of the prophetic seal. The claim about cosmic sequences is an author's metaphysical argument, not a proven biological or historical genealogy.

**Crucial methodological inference:** no compilation of separated juridical rules, ethical virtues and spiritual descriptions, however accurate, automatically becomes the integrated living person from whom those distinctions were abstracted. Their reunion requires a genuine **formal unity of living knowledge, desire, judgment and action**, not the arithmetic sum of contents. A later person might participate in aspects of that unity without equalling the sealed prophetic commission.

## 7. Ibn ʿArabī's Miʿrāj, Sulamī's early ascension sayings, and the gift of the return

Primary hadith: Muslim 162a
https://sunnah.com/muslim/1/316
The Prophet meets Adam, Jesus and John, Joseph, Idrīs, Aaron, Moses and Abraham across the seven heavens. These appearances must not be taken as exhaustive spiritual rankings. Other versions of the ascension differ in detail.

Ibn ʿArabī, Futūḥāt chapter 367, translated and analyzed by James Winston Morris:
https://ibnarabisociety.org/wp-content/uploads/PDFs/Morris_The-spiritual-ascension.pdf
This accessible work emphasizes **the return (rujūʿ)** and the prophetic heir who lives among ordinary people without public display of extraordinary inward experiences. The distinction between ecstatic absorption (*fanāʾ*) and enlightened abiding (*baqāʾ*) is indispensable: realization reaches an additional perfection through action within the world, not merely leaving it. In the encounter with Moses, experiential knowledge (*dhawq*) grounds counsel about the burden of the original prescription of fifty prayers. The descending Prophet returns with five daily prayers; a singular vertical encounter becomes recurrent human, domestic, architectural and communal practice.

Early Sufi source: Abū ʿAbd al-Raḥmān al-Sulamī, Laṭāʾif al-miʿrāj, studied and translated by Frederick S. Colby as *The Subtleties of the Ascension* (Fons Vitae, 2006, 267 pp.). This volume contains fifty-six attributed sayings of early Sufi masters. Its accessible publisher's contents identify individual discussions of **vision borne by the heart, the propriety of witnessing, divine and prophetic attributes, a secret between lovers, the benefit of ritual prayer, the ineffable limit, the purpose of ascent and the return**. These subjects show the interplay of unique rank, subtle spiritual adab, ineffability and exemplary formation.

Its 56 items were **not** all freshly read. The themes above were checked against the publisher's contents and a scholarly dissertation citing Colby's introduction, which identifies four clusters: unique station, investiture with light, divine vision and ineffability. ## 8. The Qurʾān as embodied guidance: the hidden state and the visible act

In Muslim 746a, ʿĀʾishah identifies the Prophet's character with the Qurʾān. The Qurʾān itself remains recited revelation and living address after the passing of the Prophet (39:23, 62:2–3), not an inert book requiring access to one self-proclaimed teacher to become effective.

However, the outward expression of the same text or the same legal norm in living situations can require trained awareness that a static description cannot fully communicate.

**Bukhārī 676:** the Prophet served his family and went to prayer when its time arrived. Here worship and service are not rivals.
https://sunnah.com/bukhari/10/70

**Bukhārī 709:** when the child cried, he shortened prayer, attending to the mother's distress. Worship, compassion, attention to another's particular condition and congregational leadership are integrated in a single timely decision.
https://sunnah.com/bukhari:709

**Bukhārī 6129:** he addressed a small child about his bird, entering an apparently minor human concern without dismissing it as inconsequential.
https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6129

**Muslim 2309a:** Anas's retrospective account of ten years of service without impatient reproach concerns repeated conduct, not a single grand act.
https://sunnah.com/muslim/43/70

**Muslim 1695a–b:** the reports about Māʿiz and the Ghāmidī woman are distinct, with differences of chronology and detail. Judicial questions of confession, evidence and severe physical punishment coexist with repentance, a child's independent claim and correction of contempt in observers. The reports neither require public punishment for sincere repentance nor license private violence.
https://sunnah.com/muslim:1695

The historically accessible act can be correctly reconstructed while its complete spiritual origin remains beyond the historian's access. This lack of total access is **not** a warrant to invent intentions or a hidden spiritual explanation of every decision. The Sīrah sharḥ should establish what is observable and transmitted, then carefully distinguish deeper philosophical or Akbarian interpretations.

## 9. The Burda: poetic intuition of meaning and form together

Al-Būṣīrī's Burda (third section, approximately verses 38–53) joins inward meaning and physical form, compares the Prophet's plenitude to an ocean from which the other prophets take portions, and uses sun, light, moon and overwhelming vision to express his exalted reality.

The line “He is the one whose meaning and form were perfected” (*fa-huwa lladhī tamma maʿnāhu wa-ṣūratuhu*) corresponds closely to the Akbarian distinction between formal human manifestation and deeper comprehensive meaning. The poem also proclaims the Prophet's humanity and explicitly disavows Christian-style claims of incarnation.

The poem's language concerning creation through Muhammadan Light is **poetic and doctrinal testimony**, not authenticated hadith or uncontroversial theological doctrine. Its value lies in how it perceives interior perfection through bodily beauty, ethical comportment, grace, poverty, intimacy and love. This supplies a third register beyond fiqh and speculative metaphysics: the spiritual affective apprehension of a human presence.

## 10. Guénon, Schuon, Lings: Logos and higher intellect without false equivalences

**Guénon, “Verbum, Lux, and Vita,” *Perspectives on Initiation*, ch. 47:** explicitly identifies *al-Nūr al-Muḥammadī* and *al-Rūḥ al-Muḥammadiyya* as equivalent symbols designating the principial and total form of the Universal Human and the Heart of the World. He distinguishes the divine creative *amr* from the spirit/light of first manifestation and then a further relative centre associated with the Hindu *Hiraṇyagarbha*. His symbolic metaphysics must not be cited as authentication of a popular hadith about the world's creation “for the Prophet”:
https://www.index-rene-guenon.org/index.php?Lang=FR&page=291&sigle=AI_EN

**Guénon, *Man and His Becoming According to the Vedanta*, ch. VII:** *Buddhi/Mahat* is the higher intellect, universal and formless, **but already belonging to manifestation**. It is not the unconditioned Ātman and cannot simply be identified with all uses of the “Muhammadan Reality”:
https://oeuvre-de-rene-guenon.org/textes/HDV07

**Schuon, *Understanding Islam*, “The Prophet,” printed pp. 103–125:** associates the Muhammadan Spirit with Logos, and describes the Prophet as spiritual and human norm, the harmonious integration of strength, generosity and serenity, and the point of relation between Whole Man and Primordial Man. His symbolism of the blessing on the Prophet uses hub, spokes and rim; at the highest interpretation the Prophet is the centre of a progressively expanded cosmic community. Schuon explicitly denies incarnation. The precise Logos comparison is contextual: it can relate to divine Word, universal Spirit or human symbolic manifestation according to the perspective.
Accessible copy:
https://traditionalhikma.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/02/Understanding-Islam-by-Frithjof-Schuon.pdf

**Martin Lings, *The Book of Certainty*, ch. II, printed pp. 24–25:** the different prophets appearing in the heavens express spiritual degrees or characteristics without delimiting their highest reality. The Prophet's vision and the Muhammadan Light belong in a higher spiritual plenitude. Chapter on the chains of the Sufi paths also associates living lineage with transmission of spiritual influence, not solely a preserved manual. A Library copy was inspected at the cited pages.

**Comparative hierarchy:** The terms “Logos,” “Universal Intellect,” “Buddhi,” “first determination,” “Universal Spirit,” “Muḥammadan Light” and “Perfect Human” cannot be arranged in a single timeless dictionary of exact synonyms. Guénon's Buddhi is formless **manifested** intellect; some Akbarian authors treat the Muhammadan Reality at a more principial rank than the first originated intellect; Schuon's term Logos can be used across several levels. Patrick Laude, *Pathways to an Inner Islam* (2010), ch. 4, explains this functional, perspective-relative usage. Defensible conclusion: **Muhammadan Reality is analogous to Logos as universal mediation; its direct identity with Buddhi is not warranted without specifying the exact consideration.**

## 11. The two ʿumrāns: a proposed analogy with an essential correction

**The “higher ʿumrān”** is our proposed metaphor for the differentiated yet integrated order of the unseen realms, presences, ranks, spiritual inheritance, duties, relationships and modalities of realization. Technical sources more commonly speak of *ʿālam al-ghayb*, *malakūt*, *jabarūt*, *ḥaḍarāt*, *marātib* or *maqāmāt*; “higher ʿumrān” is **not** a standard label in Ibn Khaldūn.

**Earthly ʿumrān** retains the historically specific relations of households, habitats, ecology, kinship, labour, contracts, law, crafts, institutions, states and traditions. It is lower in the sense of ontological conditioning and material mediation, not simply valueless or unreal as a created domain.

One cannot deduce a court's procedure, a family's division of labour or a harbour's commercial networks directly from the hierarchy of the divine Names. Separate historical, psychological and material causes remain necessary. The higher order supplies orientation, ultimate intelligibility, spiritual possibility and legitimate analogy; it does not supply a substitute archive.

**Important Akbarian correction:** Ibn ʿArabī's spiritual ascent culminating in return and *baqāʾ* does not permit treating embodied human life as a merely discarded husk. The comprehensive human's distinctive perfection can consist precisely in joining the ordered divine Names with an embodied life in which difference remains real. Ibn Turka's analysis of human natural unity and the later Akbarian claim about corporeal completeness strengthen this point.

The Prophet is not the second independent creator of the earth. The claim of cosmic dependence upon Muhammadan Reality refers, within the Akbarian cosmogony, to a principial/final causal and mediating order of divine manifestation; God remains the one self-sufficient Creator. Eternal divine knowledge and temporal creation are not identical. The historical person is neither a material pre-Adamic biological entity nor an incarnate divine hypostasis.

## 12. The tesseract and the limits of projection: a controlled model

Let **W** stand for a living integrated reality, which includes actually related knowledge, will, spiritual states, decisions, habits, corporeal acts and circumstances. A specialist discipline *Dᵢ* applies a limited projection or abstraction **πᵢ(W)**: valid legal rules, ethical qualities, chronological events, language, psychology, politics or mystical interpretation. These projections may be correct in their own domains without exhausting W.

Even several correct projections **π₁(W), π₂(W), ...** do not necessarily allow unique reconstruction of W: relational coherence, the actuality of a state, the manner of choosing in real time, and the original subject's precise relation to God may not survive disciplinary abstraction.

The “four-dimensional object projected into three dimensions” is a **non-invertible projection metaphor**, not a claim that the Muhammadan Reality literally occupies one extra Euclidean spatial dimension. Indeed a tesseract is still a mathematical object and its projections can sometimes be reconstructed from sufficient data; a spiritual state is not a hidden physical dimension. The strongest analogical point is the difference between **the unity of a lived form and the sum of its analytical appearances**, not a formal mathematical proof of religious metaphysics.

Ibn Turka's argument that the human can be a genuine natural unity where the macrocosm is an aggregate provides an Akbarian philosophical grounding stronger than the geometrical image alone.

## 13. A principial methodology for living history and future complexity

Distinguish the following nonidentical operations:
(1) primary historical and hadith criticism;
(2) normative legal and ethical discernment;
(3) psychological and social interpretation of the concrete encounter;
(4) lived imitation and cultivation of stable capacities (*malakāt*);
(5) companionship and spiritually transformative realization (*ṣuḥba*, *dhawq* and *maʿrifa* in their relevant traditions);
(6) recurring practice, specialized disciplines, institutions and long-run ʿumrān.

The arrows between them must be reconstructed through actual people, acts, means and organizations: no automatic claim of institutional descent from a single hadith.

The comprehensive Muhammadan model does not require a separate transmitted case for every future circumstance. Universality rests on **revelatory ends, concrete exemplars, disciplined methods of *ijtihād* and case application, and the continuing possibility of intelligent and spiritual formation**. A finite corpus is not an encyclopaedia of every possible technological, institutional or psychological problem.

Neither “all experts combined” nor “the hidden state of one spiritual shaykh” guarantees adequate judgment in every specialist field. A valid legal judgment, authentic medical diagnosis, economic analysis or technical competency has its own object and evidentiary standards. Realized companionship may deepen the apprehension of moral and spiritual reality without conferring expertise on unrelated material facts.

Likewise, Guénon's distinction between restoration of the primordial human state and supra-individual realization should not be compressed into one scheme of ordinary salvation versus higher liberation without theological qualifications. The ordinary believer can receive genuine guidance through the Qurʾān, Sunnah, prayer, family and practice even without recognized initiatic access to a realized spiritual master.

## 14. Operational rule for all 46 matns and shurūḥ

For each substantial Prophetic encounter, record the actual human **subject** and context: who is involved, what is explicitly reported, what is asked or withheld, what actual rights or goods conflict, how the Prophet acts and what changes for other participants.

Then reconstruct what a **fiqh reading**, an **ethical and character-formation reading**, a **relational reading**, a **spiritual-state reading**, and an **ʿumrān reading** can validly say—and what each cannot establish. The aim is not five mini-essays beside every episode; it is one integrated explanation that retains the specific historical event.

At the civilizational level distinguish transmission of **words**, transmission of **methods and practical skills**, transmission of **virtuous dispositions**, and transmission of **spiritual realization**. Investigate when these cooperate, fragment or are institutionally reconstructed. Avoid treating all differentiation as decline or all political success as moral evidence.

Keep the metaphysical centre present without writing speculative metaphysics over every market transaction or treaty. The more concrete the episode, the more essential careful source criticism and actual social mechanisms become.

### Application by book

**I:** Adamic comprehensiveness, differentiated prophetic wisdom, the world as book, Muhammadan gathering and finality.  
**II:** The real human reception of a living prophetic presence in households, trust, suffering, counsel and prayer in Makkah; account for the Miʿrāj and its return.  
**III:** The fullest historically documented interpenetration of worship, compassion, judgment, law, community and everyday relationships.  
**IV:** Differentiation of the non-reproducible prophetic commission into partial successor offices, with clear limitations of jurisdiction and authority.  
**V:** Formation and partiality of fiqh, tafsīr, hadith, kalām, adab, taṣawwuf, spiritual lineages and scholarly methods.  
**VI:** Real plurality of cultural and political embodiments without equating unity of spiritual principle with uniformity of institutions.  
**VII:** Modernity and digital mediation as altered conditions of *knowledge becoming character*, of relationship, attention and social responsibility—not a predetermined one-way decline.

**Unresolved central questions:** Distinguish the first determination and the first created Universal Intellect in each author; verify the Arabic of Farghānī pp. 103–116 and Mahāʾimī pp. 67–70; collate al-Sulamī's 56 sayings from Colby's Arabic text; identify exactly which versions of the ascension and vision of God are used; audit hadith often cited in this metaphysics, especially “but for you,” “I was a prophet while Adam was...,” and sayings of exclusive moments; examine the interrelations between Sufi prophetic light doctrine, love-poetry, actual traditions of companionship and differentiated political institutions. Claims of full-text access and confirmed isnād authenticity must not be fabricated.

## Consolidated explanatory thesis

**The Prophet Muḥammad ﷺ is the uniquely commissioned, embodied comprehensive prophetic exemplar. In the Akbarian interpretation his historical manifestation discloses the more principial Muḥammadan Reality, the universal nexus in which differentiated divine Names attain a complete human form without displacing divine transcendence. His Miʿrāj is a paradigmatic movement of ascent and return: access to the higher spiritual orders culminates, not in abandonment of humanity, but in transformed service through bodily worship, discerning relationships and communal duties. The revealed Book and transmitted Sunnah preserve genuine aspects of this integrality; the disciplines specify partial but valid structures; practice, knowledge, companionship and divine grace permit graded forms of inward realization. Historical ʿumrān is the differentiated field in which this inheritance acts through persons, households, crafts, social relations and institutions. Its material particularity is neither independently ultimate nor a dispensable illusion; it is the created setting in which the orientation of human life toward God can become manifest.**

This thesis is principial and interpretive. Its unfolding into actual Sīrah narrative must remain source-led and avoid importing unverified inner states, symbolic chronologies or causal historical genealogies.
