# The Muhammadan Integrality of Prophecy and the Civilizational Sciences
## Revised Akbarian explanatory framework for the seven-book Sīrah

**Research edition: 12 November 2026.** This is a methodological and source-critical synthesis meant to govern narrative rewriting. Its authors and texts must not be made into an undifferentiated “Akbarian consensus.” The historical report, juristic judgment, later metaphysical commentary, and our own synthesis are separate modes of evidence.

## I. Correct the architecture of the question

The Sīrah cannot move by a simple sequence of “metaphysics → character → institutions,” as if a completed metaphysical doctrine were cast downward into successive inferior replicas. Historical causation is more specific, human beings learn and act under different conditions, and mystical insight cannot directly establish chronologies or institutional origins.

The **centre is the concrete, comprehensive prophetic act**: a person answering God and responding to another person, with particular bodily, moral, intellectual, legal and spiritual relations simultaneously present. The divine and cosmic order cannot be dissolved into the human; the human is neither divine nor a mere disposable shadow of a higher principle. The creature's utmost station remains servanthood.

The governing research question is: **How do the Prophet's historically attested expressions of a comprehensive vocation enable transmission of authoritative guidance, graded moral and spiritual formation, and enduring but limited human relationships and institutions under changing conditions?**

Answering requires six concurrent investigations:
1. **Historical and transmitted:** what precisely happened, what is authenticated, and what varies among reports?
2. **Normative:** what obligations, rights and evidentiary thresholds govern the case?
3. **Personal and relational:** what knowledge, capacities, affections, words, silences, dependants and duties were actually involved?
4. **Spiritual and metaphysical:** what can the passage illustrate within an explicitly attributed Akbarian interpretation? What cannot be known about an inaccessible inner state?
5. **Practical and institutional:** how are insights imitated, trained into malakāt, communicated by ṣuḥba, written, disputed, specialized and organized?
6. **Historical-civilizational:** which documented mechanisms (power, ecology, economy, scale, institutions, transmission, geography) explain the later outcomes?

These must inform *one explanation*, not six unconnected paragraphs appended automatically to every episode.

## II. Station of no station: the decisive Akbarian improvement

Ibn ʿArabī's *maqām lā maqām* (station of no station), interpreted by Chittick in discussions of the Muhammadan Station, describes comprehensiveness that is **not defined and constrained by any one station**. Specific names, prophets, virtues and stations disclose true but conditioned relations; the Muhammadan mode integrates their relevance without making each equally operative at all moments. See Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, “Ibn ʿArabī,” §6.1; Chittick, “Qūnawī's Role in the School of Ibn ʿArabī” and “The Religion of Love Revisited.”

This is not arbitrary flexibility or the ability to ignore law. It is **responsive, proportionate realization**: the trained recognition of what truth, obligation and mercy demand under an actual determination.

For the historian the analogy is instructive but constrained. A report that the Prophet shortened a prayer when a child cried permits an explanation of particular rights under the demands of worship; it does not license inventing the Prophet's otherwise inaccessible state. A jurist, Sufi, teacher, psychologist or public administrator may recognize different valid aspects of the same event. Their competence is partial in domain, not necessarily defective in truth.

Qūnawī, *al-Nuṣūṣ*, Text Four (Gnamma QUN-NU-004-A), distinguishes the station-relative tasting of one divine Name from a comprehensive standpoint claiming greater universality. The claim remains Qūnawī's spiritually grounded self-description; it is not identical to an ordinary claim about the historical record.

**Critical implication:** Comprehensiveness is not a total inventory of professions or an accumulation of maximum attributes. It is the correct ordering of differentiated goods, a capacity for true relations under changing circumstances.

## III. Metaphysical rank and the non-identity of its names

Keep these considerations separate:
- **God's Essence:** intrinsically transcendent and independent, not a creature or hypostasis produced by cosmic history.
- **Principial comprehensiveness:** the *first determination*, *aḥadiyyat al-jamʿ*, Reality of Realities, or comprehensive divine Name in certain later Akbarian usages.
- **Universal manifestation:** Universal Spirit, First Intellect, Pen, light and cosmic mediations; equivalences vary by author and standpoint.
- **Complete human manifestation:** *insān kāmil*, the living comprehensive mirror, an actual human soul/body relation—not the whole cosmos as an external aggregate.
- **Unique historically commissioned Prophet:** Muḥammad ﷺ, last law-giving prophet, characterized by perfected servanthood and historically particular acts.

*Al-ḥaqīqa al-Muḥammadiyya* may be used from more than one of these considerations. It does not have a universal fixed numerical “position” without specifying the author and stage. A comparison with **Logos** is strongest at the function of principial mediation and comprehensiveness. Guénon's **Buddhi/Mahat** belongs to formless manifestation and is not a satisfactory unqualified translation of an explicitly more principial Akbarian first determination. Schuon's uses of Logos can range between levels; he explicitly denies divine incarnation. Consult Richard Todd, *The Perfect Human* (St Andrews Encyclopaedia of Theology) on the Qūnawian and later Jīlīan development.

The claim that reality is cosmically rooted in the Muḥammadan Principle is an Akbarian *ontological or final-causal* interpretation of divine self-disclosure—not evidence of a pre-Adam biological body, a second creator, or divinity independently possessed by the Prophet. Distinguish principial priority from temporal chronology. Avoid treating unsupported forms of the “were it not for you” saying as authenticated hadith.

## IV. The book, the body and the heart: evidence in our ongoing editions

**Farghānī, Muntahā al-madārik, fourth foundation, Beirut 2007 pp. 103, 110–116.** The cosmos is a manifest Book whose realities correspond at different levels to letters, words, verses and sūras. The Qurʾān is a comprehensive verbal Book. The Prophet's acts, words, traits, and character constitute their embodied comprehensive expression. Farghānī distinguishes Adamic **descent and differentiation** from Muhammadan **return and comprehensive completion**. He describes a correspondence between the purified Muḥammadan temperament and the all-comprehensive heart; *nubuwwa* and *wilāya* have a distinctive equilibrium. Working Gnamma critical translation FAR-014; not an independently finalized manuscript collation.

**Al-Mahāʾimī, Mashraʿ al-khuṣūṣ, printed pp. 67–70 (MSH-055 through MSH-058).** The Muḥammadan Reality is connected to the all-comprehensive divine Name, while the Prophet's humanity remains under servanthood. The Prophet's **bodily, legal, luminous spiritual and inward heart-centred** aspects are distinguishable without constituting four separate persons. His kinship and inheritance may be bodily, religious, spiritual or more comprehensive. Importantly, effortful *takhalluq* and realized *taḥaqquq* are differentiated, as are the seeing of unity in multiplicity and multiplicity in unity. Textual readings flagged uncertain in this working translation must remain flagged.

**Qūnawī, al-Nuṣūṣ, Text Nineteen (QUN-NU-019-E and G).** The prophet encounters in the Miʿrāj are disclosures of differentiated correspondences, not an absolute fixed census of which prophets have the highest posthumous ranks. Different divine names, human states and relations mediate distinct loci of manifestation. This prevents reducing the ascent to a literal cosmological ladder. Reports of multiple ascents require separate authentication.

**Ibn Turka, Tamhīd commentary, SH-039, SH-050 and SH-051.** The embodied human can possess a **genuine natural unity** of bodily and spiritual faculties not possessed by the cosmos considered simply as an aggregate. The completing prophetic seal has individual comprehensiveness distinct from species-wide human potential. Thus the earthly body can be a necessary locus of manifestation, not a merely degenerate shell discarded on ascent. His metaphysical claims about levels and earlier sages are not substitutes for modern historical evidence.

**Ibn ʿArabī, Fuṣūṣ, Muhammadan chapter.** Love of women, perfume and prayer illustrates that spiritual fulfilment need not be restricted to withdrawal. Ibn ʿArabī makes the embodied, relational reality of creaturely beauty central to his contemplation of the divine Names. Modern close readings: Ismail Lala, “Ibn ʿArabī and the Spiritual Sīrah” (2023), and “Asceticism as Renouncing and Embracing the World” (2023). The gendered cosmic symbolism belongs to Ibn ʿArabī's text, not a blanket modern norm for sex roles or evidence about any particular relationship.

## V. Miʿrāj as graded disclosure and a completed return

Use reliable narration first: Qurʾān 17:1 and 53:1–18, with distinct interpretive questions concerning the vision; Muslim 162a for the sequence of seven heavenly encounters, counsel of Moses and the reduction of the original prayer prescription. Compare variants rather than harmonizing all into one remembered script.

**Sulamī**, *Laṭāʾif al-Miʿrāj*, early Sufi sayings (Colby, *Subtleties of the Ascension*): four clusters concern rank, light, vision and ineffability. **Full 56-item direct collation remains outstanding**. **Ibn ʿArabī**, *Futūḥāt* chapter 367 in James Morris's translation and commentary, treats ascent through differentiated spiritual realities and the significance of **return**. Attaining the high is not completed by perpetual absence from the low: the prophetic and realized return to action involves *baqāʾ*, discernment and service. This is a spiritual interpretation, not a way to bypass concrete legal history.

The five prayers are especially revealing. A uniquely vertical episode results in recurring acts of bodily standing, bowing and prostration, organized through family time, congregational relations, public space and later institutions. **The vertical becomes effective in the horizontal without being exhausted by the horizontal practice.** The returning Prophet's ordinary activities must not be described as demotions from his ascensional state.

In Martin Lings, *The Book of Certainty*, ch. II, the seven heavenly prophets denote particular spiritual characteristics, not the limit of each prophet's complete rank. The Light of Muḥammad is treated as higher plenitude in the Garden of the Spirit; this is Lings's spiritual exegesis, not a substitute for hadith.

## VI. Love, intimacy and the ordinary deed

A central insight follows from the *station of no station*: the Prophet's response in a particular human interaction can make visible multiple goods without being reduced to any single one.

The Sunnah of serving one's household (Bukhārī 676), shortening the prayer when a child cries (Bukhārī 709), speaking affectionately with a child about his bird (Bukhārī 6129), and correcting observers who insult the repentant in Muslim 1695 show different ways that worship, care, obligation, authority, vulnerability and dignity operate together. Every event has its own transmitted context; a generic rhetoric of kindness is inadequate.

The *Burda*'s poetry on the completion of outward form and inward meaning expresses devotional perception of the Prophet's plenitude, not a proven cosmogony. In the Sufi concept of *uns* (intimacy), love and spiritual companionship must remain joined to awe (*hayba*), humility, restraint and normative responsibility. A living teacher can communicate trained judgment and receptivity not conveyed by a written proposition; however, spiritual transmission is not a mechanical transfer of an identical state, nor an exclusive monopolization of access to God.

## VII. The modalities of inheritance are different types of knowledge

William C. Chittick, “The Muhammadan Inheritance” (1996), distinguishes **works, states and knowledge**. They are not mutually exclusive jurisdictional spheres. Fiqh is not only works, nor Sufism only states; both can concern all three under different methods. The later sciences can be genuine and necessary but each is a **partial angle** on the living prophetic exemplar.

Distinguish at least:
- authenticated text (*riwāya*) and contextual evidence;
- binding norm and disciplined legal application (*fiqh*, *taḥqīq al-manāṭ*);
- outward imitation (*ittibāʿ*, *adab*, *riyāḍa*) and habit formation (*malaka*);
- counsel and companionship (*ṣuḥba*) with appropriate safeguards;
- realized witnessing (*dhawq*, *maʿrifa*) in attributed Sufi epistemology;
- professional and institutional reproduction (*isnād*, teaching, school, court, waqf, guild, market, administration);
- independent historical causation (*ʿumrān*), which cannot be deduced from analogy.

The Qurʾān's continuing power must not be reduced to that of an inert textual archive requiring one mystical teacher's physical presence to activate it. The saint's inward witnessing does not confer independent prophetic legislation or technical competence in unfamiliar medicine, finance, engineering or historical criticism.

## VIII. The personal–relational–institutional–civilizational mediation

Study the **micro-event** before generalising: person, bodily circumstances, intentions *as testified*, evidence, conflicting legitimate goods, counsel, correction, timing, expressed action, effects on bystanders and responsibilities.

Then describe what the action could form:
**encounter → recognition → intention and imitation → repeated conduct → malaka → trustworthy relations → shared practice → institution → transregional civilizational form.**

These arrows represent *possible paths of transmission*, not historically proven linear necessity. A particular school or judicial procedure cannot be said to have originated in a particular hadith unless sources establish the causal transmission. The relations also run in reverse: authorities, economic pressures, political structures, gender arrangements, curricula and media shape human character for good or ill.

Classical practical philosophy distinguishes *tahdhīb al-akhlāq*, *tadbīr al-manzil*, and *siyāsat al-mudun*, but household and city are not exhaustive: friendship, neighbourhood, partnership, teaching circles, healing relationships and spiritual companionship mediate them. Ibn Khaldūn's *ʿumrān* investigates ecological, economic, solidaristic and political processes with their own causal principles. The term **higher ʿumrān** is our interpretive metaphor for the ordered unseen ranks and spiritual communities; it is not Ibn Khaldūn's technical label and must not be substituted for it.

## IX. What the final dispensation enables in later difficult conditions

Finality and universality are not established by the sheer number of hadith items, nor does a finite corpus predict every future household or institution. The guidance provides: **authoritative ends and norms, varied historical exemplars, legitimate methods for generating judgments, and ongoing practices of formation and companionship**. These operate at different depths and require contextual knowledge.

Distinguish **restoration of primordial human integrity**, **salvation in relevant theological doctrines**, **sanctification through spiritual disciplines**, and **trans-individual realization in Guénonian terminology**. Do not make one identical ladder or treat ordinary believers as deprived of meaningful divine guidance without initiation. Guénon's account of the dark age is an interpretive claim that cannot replace investigation of social and material history.

When later technologies or institutions expand access, test their effects on comprehension, durable character, mutual responsibility and spiritual formation. Neither “everything new is decline” nor “more information is more wisdom” is explanatory.

## X. Writing protocol

1. Preserve the continuous **matn** as historical narrative in active voice. A strong matn embeds the relationships between goods in what persons *actually did*, not abstract lectures about “the method.”
2. Develop the **sharḥ** as a connected, evidence-led explanation. Where appropriate, distinguish outward rule, ethical/relational demand, witnessed state and mystical interpretation; do not turn every section into a ritual seven-part checklist.
3. Keep primary citations, variants, linguistic disputes, metaphysical comparison and unresolved claims within the sharḥ or separate dossiers. Do not push editorial status updates into the reading stream.
4. Preserve independent mechanisms: attribution, economics, scarcity, technical crafts, enforcement, scale and political incentives.
5. Never identify political success with spiritual legitimacy; don't infer inaccessible intentions or a providential explanation of victims' suffering.
6. Recognize the distinctive problems of each book: I primordial vocation and historical worlds; II living companionship and moral resistance; III prophetic integration of worship and public responsibility; IV non-prophetic succession and public administration; V specialized sciences and living learning; VI plural regional realizations; VII transformed media, statehood and character formation.
7. Maintain **four records per substantial revision**: matn, sharḥ, source/claim ledger, and continuity note to the next chapter. The text files, canonical JSON and generated reader must remain synchronized; book-level methods are not a substitute for chapter-level work.

### Consolidated thesis

**The Muhammadan Reality, as interpreted within the Akbarian school, provides a principial account of comprehensive prophetic mediation without displacing divine transcendence. The historical Prophet ﷺ is its uniquely commissioned embodied exemplar: the person whose ascent and return, worship and companionship, judgments and intimate care manifest rightly ordered relations under diverse circumstances. The Qurʾān, Sunnah and subsequent sciences preserve genuine but necessarily selective aspects; actions, states and knowledge are inherited in different degrees through texts, counsel, discipline, companionship and divine grace. Civilizational ʿumrān is the historically contingent field in which those aspects become personal dispositions, actual relations, recurring practices and institutions. Its material specificity is neither autonomous from higher meaning nor a dispensable illusion. The task of the Sīrah is to explain both the vertical principle and the real horizontal mechanisms, without mistaking an analogy for proof of an event or institution.**
