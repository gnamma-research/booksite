# Sīrah · Metaphysical Restoration and Forward Development
## Editorial reconstruction from the approved 1.4.1 / expanded Matn-1 research

**13 November 2026 · Restoration programme and evidence limits**

### The error being corrected

The earlier matn began with divine plenitude, manifestation, the Names, Adamic human comprehensiveness, the Fall and the role of Iblīs. The 1.6.1 revision replaced that beginning with a largely chronological narrative. Edition 1.7 then attached brief metaphysical parallels to its chapters. The original architecture had made metaphysical intelligibility the basis of interpretation, with historical episodes supplying the specific human forms in which the principles become operative.

The restoration takes **edition 1.4.1** as its textual baseline. The strongest detailed exposition is the earlier working document *Sīrah and the Making of Islamic Civilization — Explanatory method, a revision plan for all 46 matns, and a new Matn 1 with sharḥ*, especially its sections on the Garden, Iblīs and divine providence. The 1.4.1 manuscript also preserves extensive exegesis of the individual earlier prophets. This inherited material has a continuing claim upon any new revision.

### The governing insight

**An integrated spiritual whole becomes intelligible through differentiated acts.** God is the Source of created perfection; the human vocation receives and rightly orders diverse creaturely realities. A partial capacity becomes disordered when it claims the role of the whole. The Garden, temptation, repentance, labour and prophecy reveal the conditions of restoration. The comprehensive Muhammadan Reality provides the Akbarian principial horizon in which differentiated prophetic excellences are gathered; the historical Prophet ﷺ enacts final guidance through real human relationships.

The movement of explanation runs in two directions. The vertical gives meaning and ultimate orientation to the historical event. The historically particular event gives the interpreter a reality to understand—people, duties, skills, circumstances, language and consequences. Later disciplines emerge through valid acts of attention to different dimensions of the Prophet's life. A jurist, teacher, hadith critic, spiritual guide, administrator or parent can preserve a true element of that inheritance through a genuine practice. Its full integration remains a question of rightly ordered action and spiritual realization.

### The Iblīs complex

The Qurʾānic refusal has priority: Iblīs judges by fire and clay, rejects the command and continues in opposition. The metaphysical and epistemological readings then enrich what is being disclosed:

- **Restriction to the particular:** al-Qayṣarī's account, available in Mukhtar Hussain Ali's study of the *Muqaddimah* (pp. 47–49), uses *al-wahm al-kullī* to explain the incapacity to comprehend Ādam's spiritual universality.
- **The self-confirming devotion:** al-Ḥallāj's *Ṭawāsīn* and Aḥmad al-Ghazālī's *Sawāniḥ* raise the paradox of an attachment that protects its preferred self-understanding even against a divine command. Their literary treatments carry distinct claims and warrants.
- **Providence and divine *makr*:** Ibn ʿArabī, *Futūḥāt* I:362, explains how the tempter's urge to ruin another blinds him to the possible good of resistance and repentance that God bestows. Read at https://www.futmak.com/english/maymaniya/page.php?id=362.
- **Concealment and manifestation:** ʿAyn al-Quḍāt's *Tamhīdāt* and Guénon's symbolic account of opposition and complementarity offer differentiated metaphysical interpretations. Ethical agency remains answerable to the Qurʾānic command.

These readings belong in a detailed sharḥ with exact attribution. They furnish conceptual explanations of cognitive fixation, will, desire and providence. The matn carries the principial narrative in sustained, intelligible prose.

### What happens to the Muhammadan Reality

Muhammadan comprehensiveness is developed **from** the Adamic constitution and the differentiated prophet stories. Ādam receives the Names, the prophets exhibit varied wisdoms and the final Messenger gathers their excellences in a comprehensive revealed dispensation. Ibn ʿArabī, Qūnawī, Farghānī, Jāmī and al-Mahāʾimī use different language for the various considerations of the comprehensive reality; source-critical translation retains those distinctions. The human Prophet remains God's servant, and his actual worship, judgment, love and teaching mediate his final mission within history.

The Miʿrāj is a particularly important transition. The return with the five prayers joins spiritual ascent to recurring embodied worship. From this one living original, later communities acquire distinct sciences and forms of formation. Their distinctive domains are real: fiqh judges legal relations; taṣawwuf cultivates states and realized knowing; ḥadīth safeguards reports; medicine learns bodily conditions; political offices govern real affairs; education forms *malakāt*.

### The style of the reader

The text begins by stating **what a thing is, how it acts, why it matters and what it makes possible**. Every term earns its place through an actual explanatory relation. Negations are reserved for material source disputes, exact logical distinctions or an assertion that would otherwise be misleading. The habitual device of listing what a thing “is not” will be removed during chapter editing.

The matn sustains the original metaphysical narration and develops the examples in continuous prose. The sharḥ makes textual evidence, commentary and conceptual interpretation clear. Edition status, editorial qualifications, variant catalogues and research logs stay outside the continuous reading body.

### The seven-book development programme

| Book | Principial question | Historical material that develops it |
|---|---|---|
| I | How do plurality, limitation, temptation, repentance and the human heart relate to the Source? | Ādam, Iblīs, earlier prophets; material arts and the first differentiated civilizations |
| II | How is the comprehensive prophetic presence received and recognized by different persons? | Khadījah, early believers, Hira, Aksum, Taʾif, Miʿrāj and ʿAqabah |
| III | How do distinct goods become rightly ordered within one embodied prophetic act? | Prayer with a crying child, family life, Māʿiz and Ghāmidiyya, market, treaties, famine, battle and pilgrimage |
| IV | How do obligations persist through differentiated authority after the final Prophet? | Succession, provinces, fiscal offices, government, contested legitimacy and the caliphal dynasties |
| V | How is differentiated knowledge received, made teachable and brought toward realization? | Ḥadīth, fiqh, uṣūl, adab, philosophy, kalām, medical learning, ṣuḥba and spiritual schools |
| VI | How do multiple peoples and forms of life participate in a common orientation? | Andalusian, Maghribi, Persianate, Ottoman, West African, Malay, Bengal and other histories |
| VII | How do altered conditions of mediation affect knowledge and the possibility of renewal? | Colonial law, print, states, industrial production, mass schooling, migration, archives and digital research |

Each chapter needs its own generating question and a source-led historical reconstruction. The metaphysical account will develop through the work rather than recur as an attached formula. Later archival and research improvements from editions 1.5–1.7 remain preserved for controlled reintegration, with their established and provisional witness statuses visible.

### Version policy

The 46-chapter 1.4.1 edition forms the restored reading baseline; the newly recovered Chapter 01 provides the proof of the recovered method. Other revised chapters are identified individually. The 1.7.0 reader and chapter JSON are preserved in edition-specific archives, together with all earlier 1.4 and 1.6 source-critical records. A successful mechanical consistency check establishes publishing integrity only. Primary Arabic witness collation, independent scholarly review and historical claim verification remain open.
