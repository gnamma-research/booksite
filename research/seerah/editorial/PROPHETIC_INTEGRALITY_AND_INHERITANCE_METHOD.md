# Prophetic integrality and differentiated inheritance
## Micro-level character, relational formation, disciplines, and civilizational history

**Sīrah research methodology · 11 November 2026 · Applies to the seven books and 46 matns/shurūḥ.**

**Status.** This file records a governing method for future source-led revisions; it does not claim the remaining chapters have already been rewritten, or that primary sources have been exhaustively collated.

### 1. The integrated prophetic exemplar is prior to the divided scholarly disciplines

The Prophet Muḥammad ﷺ is not best explained by adding together later categories such as jurist, spiritual guide, educator, judge, ruler and exemplar of character. Within his prophetic commission, knowledge, intention, worship, mercy, judgment, instruction, relationships and delegated authority were integrated in particular speech, actions and decisions. The functions remain distinguishable without becoming independent compartments.

Qurʾān 33:21 calls him an exemplary pattern, Qurʾān 68:4 praises his character, and Qurʾān 62:2 joins recitation, purification, instruction in the Book and instruction in wisdom. ʿĀʾishah describes his character as the Qurʾān in Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim 746a. These sources ground an integrated model of guidance, but do not in themselves settle every later metaphysical interpretation.

The **whole cannot be recovered by adding the separately abstracted disciplines together**. Their integration concerns the right relationship among ends, persons, acts and duties in actual situations, not a simple aggregation of subject areas.

### 2. The legitimate disciplines are partial perspectives, not necessarily mere shadows

Fiqh and uṣūl attend to normative acts, authoritative evidence, rights, duties and conditions of judgment. Taṣawwuf and tazkiya attend especially to sincerity, the inner life, self-correction, spiritual states, companionship and acquired character. Hadith and Sīrah preserve and examine reports of actions, words and their historical circumstances. Tafsīr investigates revealed meaning. Kalām explicates and defends doctrinal claims. Adab and education investigate conduct, teaching, imitation and practical discernment. Social and political studies concern authority, common life, wealth and institutions.

These are **differences of attention, not watertight partitions**. Fiqh includes intentions and moral purposes; taṣawwuf remains answerable to revealed norms; teaching is simultaneously ethical, relational and intellectual. A valid disciplinary finding can preserve genuine and sometimes binding truth. The limitation of the “silhouette” metaphor is that a partial perspective is not necessarily an inaccurate one.

Do not classify the Qurʾān and the authoritative Sunnah as merely two more human disciplines. Do not attribute the entire later technical vocabulary of any discipline to the Prophet's historical speech. Do not imply that any single discipline, institution or teacher alone represents the full prophetic inheritance.

The finality of law-giving prophethood remains intact. No combination of sciences, saintly attainments or scholarly practices reconstitutes the Prophet's unique commission or grants later persons independent prophetic legislative authority.

### 3. Differentiation can preserve or obscure integration

Specialization is not itself evidence of decline. Larger communities require distinct competencies, recognized procedures, textual preservation, qualified juristic reasoning and institutions of teaching. The problem arises when a specialized form mistakes its remit for the total end, denies the relevance of neighbouring forms of knowledge, or confuses the continued existence of an institution with the living formation of persons.

Later Islamic sciences also possess distinctive technical histories. Their relation to the prophetic model may involve preservation, interpretation, extension through legitimate reasoning, adaptation to historical needs, and sometimes distortion. These relations must not be collapsed into a single claim of continuous direct descent.

### 4. Different modes of prophetic inheritance

Identify at least five overlapping but non-identical modalities:

1. **Textual:** revelation, transmitted reports, wording, variant recensions, attribution and context.
2. **Normative:** commandments, permissions, limits of authority, principles of discernment and legitimate reasoning.
3. **Relational:** companionship, advice, correction, example, protection, trust, teaching and mutual obligation.
4. **Dispositional:** formation of understanding, sincerity, practical wisdom, adab, stable malakāt and inward responsiveness.
5. **Institutional:** households, scholarly circles, courts, markets, learned professions, endowments and networks that preserve or reshape earlier modalities.

Textual availability does not equal comprehension. Normative correctness does not alone establish stable virtue. A valid institution does not guarantee its operators have attained its proper purpose. These distinctions are central to the civilizational chapters.

**Akbarian specification:** In Ibn ʿArabī's account of prophetic inheritance, the modalities of works, states and knowledge identify distinguishable dimensions of following a prophetic paradigm. William C. Chittick's “The Muhammadan Inheritance” (1996), pp. 55–61, explains this tradition's claim that the Muhammadan model encompasses differentiated prophetic excellences. This is an attributed Akbarian interpretation; it is not a consensus statement of all Muslim theology. Investigations of the Muhammadan Reality and saintly inheritance must preserve distinctions between metaphysical, experiential, normative and historically evidential claims.

### 5. The individual and relational levels constitute the civilizational level

Human civilization is not only a collection of virtuous or vicious individuals, nor only a superstructure of states and organizations. Its enduring substance includes determinate relationships: giving and receiving counsel, promising and fulfilling, protecting and being protected, teaching and learning, contracting and exchanging, commanding and being accountable.

A disposition of honesty belongs to a person; a trustworthy transaction also requires an actual relation of disclosure and reliance. A teacher's knowledge is not identical with the relation through which a student becomes competent. A just intention alone does not produce a sound judicial procedure. Relations and institutions have distinctive formal and causal features while remaining constituted by the actions of real people.

Use the linked sequence as a research heuristic, not as an automatic chronology:

**Prophetic action → recognition of relevant goods → observation and imitation → cultivated malaka → interpersonal relationship → recurring social practice → institutional form → large-scale ʿumrān.**

Effects also run in reverse: family practices, offices, reward structures, courts, teaching forms and media alter which qualities people can readily cultivate. Avoid false claims that a particular institution can be genealogically traced to one episode simply because its values resemble those expressed there.

### 6. One prophetic episode: several dimensions of an integrated action

Distinguish the transmitted accounts of Māʿiz b. Mālik and the Ghāmidī woman in Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim 1695a–b. The reports concern self-accusation and repentance, the Prophet's initial redirection or withholding of immediate punishment, judicial conditions, the protection of a dependent child, a grave physical punishment and the correction of observers who spoke contemptuously of a repentant person. Do not omit the historical punishment of stoning, merge variant reports indiscriminately, or infer that public punishment was required for sincere repentance.

A juristic inquiry legitimately investigates confession, proof and authority. An ethical inquiry considers restraint, speech, human dignity and the claims of dependants. A spiritual inquiry considers repentance and inward standing with God. A relational inquiry considers how the Prophet's conduct corrected bystanders and formed communal habits of judgment. None alone exhausts the whole transmitted situation.

Historical documentation is still necessary before claiming a later court, law school or institution actually grew out of that specific encounter. The integrated reading is an explanation of the episode, not an invented chain of social influence.

### 7. The vertical and horizontal are concurrent, not separate histories

Every substantive unit should investigate:

- **Vertical:** dependence on God, the human vocation, revelation, wisdom, justice, worship, formation and the ultimate end of conduct.
- **Personal:** perception, intention, knowledge, desire, deliberation, action and malaka.
- **Relational:** the actual rights, duties, power, mutual trust and encounters of persons and small communities.
- **Institutional:** repeated patterns, roles, rules, professional disciplines and organized means.
- **Horizontal and historical:** ecology, labour, material resources, authority, ʿaṣabiyya, economic exchanges, language, migration, scale, technology and historical contingencies.

The vertical does not replace causal inquiry; the horizontal does not render final ends dispensable. Symbolic resemblance is not proof of historical derivation. Divine providence cannot be used to excuse perpetrators or infer a victim's inward condition. The integration of multiple goods must be demonstrated through the actual acts and relationships, not asserted as an abstract moral.

Ibn Khaldūn contributes large-scale causal analysis of ʿumrān. Classical practical philosophy distinguishes individual ethics, household management and civil politics; the research should expand its intermediate forms to include companionship, apprenticeship, neighbourhood, contracts and small-group teaching. Neither body of theory can substitute for the concrete record of the prophetic action being explained.

### 8. Required research questions for each matn and sharḥ

1. **Recover the whole historical event.** What was said and done? What is reliably transmitted, disputed, missing or variant?
2. **Identify goods and duties.** What is the proper object of each action? Which demands conflict or constrain one another?
3. **Recover integrated judgment.** What did the Prophet say, permit, prohibit, ask, delay or decline to make public; and what can properly be inferred from it?
4. **Track personal formation.** What understandings, dispositions, skills, intentions and practical forms of adab are actually evidenced?
5. **Track relations.** What changed between teacher and student, ruler and subject, spouse and spouse, buyer and seller, complainant and judge?
6. **Distinguish disciplinary readings.** What do fiqh, taṣawwuf, tafsīr, ethics, legal reasoning and historical analysis each illuminate? Which source has actually been checked?
7. **Identify mechanisms of transmission.** How could the action or lesson become a recurrent habit, expectation or practice? Do we have records of that historical process?
8. **Explain institutional and large-scale causes.** Specify actors, resources, procedures, rules, historical constraints and consequences without reducing them to symbolism.
9. **Test distortion and alternatives.** Differentiate outward compliance from realized excellence, political success from legitimacy, and formal institutional survival from effective teaching.
10. **Compose without editorial intrusion.** The matn narrates directly and concisely. The sharḥ supplies evidence, argument and interpretive distinctions. Keep extended source disputes and work logs in the critical apparatus, not in continuous reading mode.

### 9. Seven-book continuity

**Book I:** Adamic comprehensiveness and differentiated earlier prophetic wisdom establish the vocation without imposing a single global historical chronology.

**Books II–III:** Meccan and Medinan encounters display the integration of purification, teaching, social relations, authority, mercy, law and worship in the Prophet's living conduct. Preserve micro-episodes as substantive units, not decorative anecdotes.

**Book IV:** After the Prophet, governorship, scholarship, public adjudication and fiscal administration are inherited by necessarily non-prophetic offices. Study their distinct authority and limits.

**Book V:** Different sciences of religious learning and spiritual formation develop expertise, methods and institutions. Investigate both the value of differentiation and the relationships needed for recombination in a living religious education.

**Book VI:** Common religious ends appear in plural linguistic, political and economic settings. Integrity need not entail institutional or cultural uniformity.

**Book VII:** Modern statehood, education, print, digital communication and changing economic orders can enlarge access while transforming the conditions of stable understanding, trust and character. Evaluate actual evidence rather than assuming universal progress or necessary decline.
