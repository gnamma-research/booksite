# The Muhammadan Reality and the Integrated Human Exemplar
## A source-controlled Akbarian prolegomenon for the Sīrah, edition 1.6 research pass

**12 November 2026 · Source-led research, not an independent critical edition of every Arabic witness.**

### The problem

The Sīrah is concerned with a person who receives revelation, embodies worship, teaches and judges, responds to persons, receives counsel, protects others, and establishes the conditions of communal life. He is historically situated and fully human; yet Islamic belief identifies him as God's final Messenger, and the Akbarian tradition assigns his **Muḥammadan Reality** a comprehensive, principial role in the world's dependence upon the divine Names.

A rigorous explanation requires neither making historical actions into exhaustive proofs of metaphysical schemes nor making the metaphysical station an ornamental gloss upon historical actions. The object is a **single embodied person under genuinely distinct considerations**: revealed commission, human faculties, actual conduct, spiritual knowledge, and principial comprehensiveness. The latter cannot be identified with one more observable psychological quality.

The historian has access to reports, the interpreter to arguments and transmission, the jurist to normative classifications, and the spiritual author to a graded discourse on realization. The first three do not entitle one to announce certainty about the fourth. Equally, a claimed private disclosure cannot authenticate an uncertain hadith, determine a battlefield's casualty count or license independence from binding revealed norms.

### 1. Ontological order without a false ladder of synonyms

The following are distinctions of **consideration**, not necessarily six serially created entities:

1. **The Divine Essence in absolute independence:** the Real is not one cosmic part among others. There is no claim here that the created Prophet is an incarnate divinity or independent creator. Neither the “Infinite” nor Total Possibility can serve as a transparent synonym for the revealed God without argument.
2. **Names and relations of manifestation:** created realities possess different capacities to show life, knowledge, power, mercy, justice and beauty in their appropriate limits. A divine Name is not a creature's private property.
3. **Principial comprehensiveness:** in later Qūnawian and Farghānian formulations the first determination, first comprehensiveness or comprehensive Name may be associated with the Muhammadan Reality, as the principle through which differentiated realities are gathered intelligibly. Authors differ on the level under consideration.
4. **First created spiritual orders:** Universal Spirit, First Intellect, Pen, Tablet, various heavenly or imaginal worlds and the Presences are not always interchangeable labels; their locations and relative statuses vary among the philosophical and Akbarian authors. *Buddhi/Mahat* in Guénon's Vedantic comparison belongs to formless manifestation and must not simply be identified with an Akbarian first determination beyond that level.
5. **The *insān kāmil* as comprehensive *barzakh*:** the created human being can possess, through divine bestowal, an integral capacity for receiving and acting through otherwise differentiated realities. “Between” here is ontological and epistemological, not an intermediate spatial position in a line drawn between two physical objects.
6. **The historical Muḥammad ﷺ:** the complete and uniquely commissioned human Prophet of the final revelation; his human body, temperament, heart, family life, public acts and historical limitations must not be erased by principial language. His unique *nubuwwat al-tashrīʿ* cannot be reproduced by the saints.

A given author's “Muḥammadan Reality” may refer to a comprehensive principial determination, the Muhammadan Light or Spirit, the Perfect Human in a cosmic role, or the inward reality whose full historical manifestation appears in the Prophet. **One cannot force a unique numerical metaphysical address on all such usages.**

**Primary comparison anchors:** Farghānī, *Muntahā al-madārik*, fourth foundation, Beirut 2007, pp. 103, 110–116 (Gnamma cumulative critical translation); al-Mahāʾimī, *Mashraʿ al-khuṣūṣ*, pp. 67–70 (Gnamma MSH-055–058); Ibn Turka, *Tamhīd*, SH-039 and SH-050–051. Their Gnamma English editions have explicit textual caveats and are not a completed independent witness collation.

### 2. Comprehensiveness: not collection but a higher formal unity

An inventory of virtues is not the same as the integrated perfection of the one possessing them. Courage, mercy, restraint, generosity, knowledge and worship differ in their objects and appropriate uses; a great quantity of each does not automatically make their conjunction right.

Ibn Turka distinguishes a **genuine human unity** from an aggregate in which unrelated qualities merely coexist. His account of a balanced bodily constitution joined to spiritual receptivity is especially important: the body is not merely a defective copy of a higher subject, but an actual locus of differentiated unity. In SH-050, a living human whole can produce integrated acts that the cosmos as a mere collection of components cannot originate through an equivalent one-subject natural form.

Farghānī's fourth foundation develops a related but distinct account: the universe is a manifest Book, composed of realities whose relations correspond to letters, words, verses and higher groupings; the revealed Qurʾān is the all-comprehensive verbal Book. The Prophet's speech, actions, character and bodily constitution represent, in Farghānī's hermeneutic, a **living, enacted comprehensive form**. “His character was the Qurʾān” (Muslim 746a) becomes a formal correspondence rather than merely a slogan for moral goodness. The correspondence remains his interpretation; the hadith itself should not be made to assert Farghānī's entire cosmological system.

The crucial implication for writing a Sīrah: an episode of practical wisdom is neither a snapshot of a generic virtue nor full empirical access to the Prophet's inward spiritual disclosure. It is **one real expression** of a person whose historical and normative integrality must be recovered as fully as the transmitted evidence permits.

### 3. The station of no station: two eyes, not mere adaptability

Chittick's “The Central Point – Qūnawī's Role in the School of Ibn ʿArabī” explains Ibn ʿArabī's *maqām lā maqām*: specific stations are genuine modalities of perfected human being, but the Muhammadan station encompasses modes without being restricted by one such form. Its inheritors see with **two eyes**: universal realization and the real demands of each particular moment. This is not ordinary “good leadership,” situational ethics or an invitation to relativize every law. It is an ontological-experiential claim from which context-sensitive right judgment can follow.

Qūnawī, *al-Nuṣūṣ*, Text Four (Gnamma QUN-NU-004-A), distinguishes a tasting (dhawq) under a particular Name or station from what he claims as the all-comprehensive standpoint. That station is not a psychological power that any observer can verify from the outside. A competent historian can reconstruct the Prophet's outward conduct and reflect upon how it accords with this metaphysical form without claiming to know what he “tasted” at each instant.

The **two eyes** also correct two opposing reductions: (i) treating spiritual knowledge as escape from the claims of other persons, and (ii) treating the comprehensive station as only the ability to compromise among worldly duties. A bearer of true realization, on this account, does not lose the multiplicity of creaturely rights; neither does he confuse them with self-sufficient ultimate ends.

### 4. The barzakh as connective separation

In Ibn ʿArabī a *barzakh* joins **while distinguishing**. The soul is neither sheer spirit nor sheer body; imaginal existence is not simply physical matter and not the nonexistence of a mere invented picture. The Perfect Human is intelligible as a comprehensive meeting point of the Real and creation under a relation of dependence; the Real does not become an enclosed substance inside a creature.

The Sīrah may be read by analogy through that distinction: an action addresses a finite, particular person and can at the same time have a higher normative and spiritual significance. But the action's matter, manner and effects remain particular and do not yield an exhaustive map of all divine Names. This is a **principial analogy**, not a historical efficient cause.

Qūnawī and al-Mahāʾimī clarify that the Prophet's creaturely aspect remains *ʿabd* even when the Muhammadan Reality is described through cosmic mediation and the comprehensive Name. Al-Mahāʾimī (pp. 68–69) expressly distinguishes bodily form, religious/legal form, luminous spiritual form and inward heart-centred form. These dimensions cannot be turned into four separately historical persons, nor does delegated lordship mean divinity independently possessed.

### 5. Relations of witnessing, knowing, acting and serving

The Sufi account distinguishes **knowing about an act**, **acting correctly**, **having the inward disposition**, and **knowing through realized witnessing**. The distinctions require care:
- *Riwaya*: an evaluated report of words and acts.
- *Fiqh*: normative discrimination of rights and obligations under the relevant evidence.
- *Takhalluq*: acquired virtues and repeated morally purposeful conduct.
- *Malaka*: durable acquired competence or disposition; it is not every transient feeling or spiritual state.
- *Hal*, *maqam*, *dhawq* and *shuhud*: spiritual experience, stable station, experiential knowing and witnessing, though authors differ on their exact boundaries.
- *Tahaqquq*: deeper realization, not identical with effortful imitation.
- *Suhba*: the formative relation of teacher and learner, potentially including a more specific claim of spiritual influence and blessing in an initiatic tradition.
- *Waratha*: inherited works, states and knowledge in the Akbarian analysis summarized by William Chittick, “The Muhammadan Inheritance” (1996).

A disciple's learning in the presence of a teacher may communicate forms of attention and practical correction not available through a textbook. The stronger doctrine that a realized spiritual state is mediated through *baraka* is an explicitly spiritual explanation, not an empirically measured transfer of an identical object from one body to another. Nor does reliance on living teachers make the recited Qurʾān inert without them. It remains active divine address and a source of formation wherever recited and received.

### 6. From higher perception to embodied return

The Miʿrāj provides a test and image of the complete movement: differentiated heavenly encounters; greater nearness; limitations of ordinary speech and vision; Moses's insight about the burden of repeated prayer; return with five daily obligatory prayers. Ibn ʿArabī's *Futūḥāt* chapter 367, in James Morris's translation, describes the return (*rujūʿ*) as indispensable to highest realization. A saint whose insight is complete may have no public signs of spiritual exceptionality, because the realized witness returns to actual relationships, duties and service. See https://ibnarabisociety.org/wp-content/uploads/PDFs/Morris_The-spiritual-ascension.pdf .

The connection is **not** that prayer is chiefly a technique of time management or civic cohesion. It is worship of God; its bodily, temporal and communal effects are real mediations through which believers receive its formative action. A worshipping subject can be ordered inwardly by a discipline practiced among actual people.

### 7. The disciplinary traces and their limits

After the final Prophet's death, no one later individual reproduces the comprehensive prophetic commission. Hadith critics, jurists, teachers, physicians, rulers, household members and spiritual guides inherit different responsibilities and competencies. Each may possess true partial knowledge. A verified legal judgment is not a degraded “mere shadow”; a mature Sufi state is not a new permission to overrule binding revealed law; a political victory is not proof of universal moral right.

The **trace metaphor** is strongest in epistemology: knowledge received through a selection of observable features does not automatically produce the actuality of the spiritual state from which a perfected act proceeded. Nevertheless, texts, rites, teaching and duties are genuine modes of access and can be fully valid in their domains. Some participation may become more comprehensive through purification, worship and realized understanding, but no one simply regains the seal's unique law-giving office.

### 8. Historical ʿumrān: a genuine order, not a cosmetic shadow

*ʿUmrān*, in Ibn Khaldūn's disciplinary sense, concerns the organization and transformation of terrestrial human life through ecological conditions, labour, power, solidarity, industry, settlement, law and learning. The phrase “higher ʿumrān” is a *new analogical expression* for the differentiated spiritual Presences and the relational life of realized persons; it is **not** an established synonym in Ibn Khaldūn.

A household with shared obligations, a market with transactions, or an educational institution with trained teachers is constituted through actual relations; a state cannot simply be spiritually labelled to replace its revenue, water, transport or administrative causes. Created human life is lower in the ontological order of dependence, but it is not worthless or dispensable. The Prophet's integrated, embodied realization makes that distinction central, not secondary.

Practical constitution follows *possible*, evidenced pathways:
**encounter → recognized obligations → imitative act → malaka → reliable relationship → recurring practice → teaching or institutional form → wider civilizational order.** The process reverses too: institutions shape the occasions and incentives for forming character. A particular later institution's origin cannot be “proven” by resemblance to one hadith. Every arrow requires its own evidence.

### 9. Comparative Logos and Guénon–Schuon

Guénon's *Perspectives on Initiation*, “Verbum, Lux, and Vita,” identifies Muhammadan Light/Spirit with the principial form of Universal Man; his *Man and His Becoming According to the Vedanta*, chapter VII, places *Buddhi/Mahat* within formless **manifestation**, not the unconditioned Self. Schuon's *Understanding Islam*, “The Prophet,” relates the Muhammadan Spirit to Logos, and interprets spiritual norm, generosity, strength and serenity in the Prophet. Neither author warrants an exact one-word dictionary equation **Muḥammadan Reality = Logos = Buddhi** without context. Schuon expressly rejects a literal Islamic incarnation of God.

The *Burda* speaks of the Prophet's meaning and form together as part of devotional poetics. Its imagery is relevant as a witness to intimacy, love and the perceived depth of embodied perfection; it is not a critical-historical source for the temporal creation of the world.

### 10. Revisions dictated by this ontology

**Book I:** Distinguish Qurʾānic and historical claims from Akbarian/Traditionalist metaphysical synthesis; the opening matn's unsupported universal formulation about “the Infinite” needs attribution/qualification in its sharḥ.

**Books II–III:** Give connected prophetic encounters narrative space. Read the highest ascent and its return together; include the mother's distress, the household's needs, the repentant offender's dignity, counsel, treaty and public justice. Do not invent hidden motives.

**Book IV:** Distinguish law-giving nubuwwa, spiritual inheritance, public political succession and doctrinal claims about the Imamate. No synthesis can predetermine a contested historical verdict.

**Book V:** Study specialist disciplines not just as directories of authors but as actually different ways of forming knowledge, habits, and spiritual states; distinguish their evidentiary limits and mutual corrective responsibilities.

**Book VI:** Research particular persons, witnesses and institutions demonstrating how shared ends become real in diverse languages and livelihoods.

**Book VII:** Modern state, law, print, digital tools and clinical/technical knowledge change the mediations through which guidance is applied; do not expect a specific hadith for every future case or infer a cycle of inevitable decline from symbolism.
