One text, different questions

Both editions are generated from the same recovered reader corpus. Edition I retains the legal inquiry; Edition II adds the other commentary layers. A shared primary-text record contains the Arabic, working translation, edition and locator. Published English page images remain distinguished from the new short working translations.

Legal authorization, knowledge of the practical object and final explanation are different inquiries. Later classifications are controls, not evidence for Ibn Rushd’s historical vocabulary. Alchemical language concerns proposed effects of applying law; it does not name an alternative legal inference.

What remains open

  • Position-by-position checks and independent school-source controls across the eight studies.
  • Direct Qurʾān/hadith commentary chains and print collation of electronic witnesses.
  • The English Volume I funeral pagination gap and the Arabic II397 homicide reading.
  • The preserved differences at Arabic II201 (ثمن معلوم) and Arabic I8 (Aḥmad versus the English witness’s Ḥammād).
  • REP-43: check initiation versus release from interdiction and the priority of Qurʾān 4:5–6. The existing discussion is preserved pending review.
  • Historical support for institutional effects and the conditions under which practice forms virtue.
