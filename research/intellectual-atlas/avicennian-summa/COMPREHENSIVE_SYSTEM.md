# A mature Avicennian Summa — twenty connected investigations

**GNAMMA Research | Systematic synthesis 0.2 | 30 October 2026**

This dossier corrects an earlier overconcentration on the Giver of Forms and infinite regress. The question is the **entire mature Avicennian philosophical system** after kalām, Akbarian metaphysics, Illuminationism and Ṣadrā, compared issue by issue with Aristotelian–Thomistic metaphysics. This is a systematic **research synthesis**, not a claim that the late commentaries have been fully collated or translated.

## The logical order: what each part presupposes

1. **Logic and epistemology:** distinguish the first notions of being, thing, necessity and quiddity, the object of knowledge, mental existence, and the epistemic warrant for metaphysical demonstration. Without this, later debates about *nafs al-amr* and existence become equivocal.
2. **Ontology:** distinguish quiddity as conceived, essence as instantiated, existence as actuality, modal contingency, and the shared concept of being. This is prior to asking whether reality is numerically one or graded.
3. **Substance and nature:** analyse matter, form, potency, act, individuation and four causes. Determine whether the Giver of Forms is a universal explanatory requirement or a separable cosmological hypothesis.
4. **Motion and causal power:** test substantial motion and natural generation against the persistence of a subject. Distinguish causes of transformation from existential sustaining causes.
5. **The soul and intellect:** the Active Intellect's epistemic role must be separated from its role in sublunary form-bestowal; soul-origin disputes cannot be inferred from ordinary material generation.
6. **First philosophy and theology:** necessity, contingency, causal order, divine simplicity, knowledge, will, providence and creation. The regress arguments belong here, not as the organising principle of the entire summa.
7. **Cosmology, teleology and practical life:** assess the order of intellects, celestial mediation, prophecy, resurrection, final causality, ethics and the human good.

## Five findings that are more important than a school label

**A. The post-Avicennian tradition is plural.** It is misleading to speak of a single 'latest Avicennism'. Lāhījī, Narāqī, Jilwa and the Indian tradition do not automatically agree, even where they oppose Ṣadrā. Distinguish an author's direct doctrine, a commentary on another's doctrine, and a later editor's synthesis.

**B. An objection may strengthen a doctrine without changing it.** Rāzī's critique and Ṭūsī's reply can improve demonstrations and technical distinctions. The intellectual history must track changes in *proof*, not only changes in conclusions.

**C. The essence/existence issue cannot be reduced to an accident added to a complete essence.** Ibn Sīnā's account is modal, metaphysical and explanatory; later debates about primacy and gradation are further theses. A full AT comparison must treat existence as actuality, real composition, predication and causation separately. See [Lizzini, SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ibn-sina-metaphysics/) and [Essence and Existence in Arabic and Islamic Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/arabic-islamic-essence/).

**D. Continuing dependence does not abolish secondary causality.** Narāqī's causal argument concerns the ultimate reason a contingent existent is sustained. It does not by itself settle whether a natural agent or the Active Intellect confers a substantial form. See [the Narāqī working dossier](primary-translation-causation.md) and [the four-causes analysis](four-causes-and-forms-research.md).

**E. Ontological unity, gradation and conceptual commonality are not the same claim.** An argument for a common concept of existence does not entail metaphysical monism; an argument for real dependence does not abolish numerical plurality. Ṣadrā's stronger synthesis and Narāqī's selective appropriation must be reconstructed from complete argument units.

## Required comparative apparatus for every domain

For each of the twenty domains above, produce seven independently attributed columns: **(1) Ibn Sīnā, (2) Rāzī, (3) Ṭūsī and adjudicators, (4) later Tajrīd/Mawāqif/Maqāṣid commentators, (5) Ṣadrā and Akbarian/Illuminationist challenges where relevant, (6) late independent Avicennian responses, (7) Aquinas and later AT commentators.** Within each column retain exact Arabic/Persian/Latin lemmata, edition and page, argument premises, objections, replies, technical terminology, and the difference between doctrinal change and demonstrative repair.

## Primary reading backbone

- Ibn Sīnā: *Shifāʾ* (logic, physics, *De anima*, metaphysics), *Ishārāt*, *Najāt*, *ʿUyūn al-ḥikma*.
- Critical reception: Rāzī's *Sharḥ al-Ishārāt* and *Mabāḥith*, Ṭūsī's *Ḥall mushkilāt*, Quṭb al-Dīn al-Rāzī's *Muḥākamāt*.
- Kalām: *Tajrīd*, *Sharḥ al-Mawāqif*, *Sharḥ al-Maqāṣid*, *Shawāriq al-ilhām*, later glosses.
- Sadrian: *Asfār*, *Taʿlīqāt al-Shifāʾ*, with differentiated Akbarian and Illuminationist contributions.
- Late Peripatetic: Khwānsārī, Lāhījī, Narāqī, Jilwa, Khayrābādī and their distinct source witnesses.
- AT: Aristotle's *Physics*, *Metaphysics*, *De anima*, *Generation and Corruption*; Aquinas' *De ente*, *De principiis naturae*, *De potentia*, *Summa theologiae*, *SCG*; later Thomistic commentaries where doctrinal clarification matters.
