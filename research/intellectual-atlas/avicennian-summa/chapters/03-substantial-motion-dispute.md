# Book II, Chapter 1 — Substantial motion: the persisting subject versus continuous existence
**A direct comparison of al-Ḥillī, Lāhījī, Ṣadrā, Ibn Sīnā and the AT analysis of change**

**Research state:** One *complete bounded argument unit* of Lāhījī (*Shawāriq* I:53, sixth question, through before the seventh question) rendered in connected English below; the Arabic original is given in selected, source-located lemmata, not reprinted in its entirety. Ṣadrā's principal counterargument in *Asfār* IV:85–87 is translated in connected portions with explicit omissions. The earliest al-Ḥillī witnesses are a source-located preliminary stage, not a complete translation of his many proofs. Single digital editions have been checked; manuscripts and textual variants remain outstanding.

**Primary source links:** [Lāhījī I:52](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/9309/52?lang=ar), [I:53](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/9309/53?lang=ar); [Ṣadrā, *Asfār* IV:85](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/1606/85?lang=ar), [IV:86](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/1606/86?lang=ar), [IV:87](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/1606/87?lang=ar); [al-Ḥillī, *Nihāyat al-marām* IV:399](https://najafdesertlibrary.com/book/%D9%86%D9%87%D8%A7%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%B1%D8%A7%D9%85-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D8%B9%D9%84%D9%85-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%83%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%85/v/4/p/399), [IV:410](https://najafdesertlibrary.com/book/%D9%86%D9%87%D8%A7%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%B1%D8%A7%D9%85-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D8%B9%D9%84%D9%85-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%83%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%85/v/4/p/410).

## I. The older, not merely reactive, Peripatetic problem

Before Ṣadrā, the general Aristotelian–Avicennian position distinguished:

- **Accidental motion**, such as the same body becoming hotter or changing place;
- **Substantial generation/corruption**, in which one substantial form ceases and another begins, ordinarily not analysed as continuous motion through an infinity of actual intermediate substantial species.

This distinction is explicitly argued in al-Ḥillī's *Nihāyat al-marām*, IV, discussion of the impossibility of motion in substance.

**Arabic — al-Ḥillī IV:399**

> اعلم أن الذي يقع الاشتباه فيه من الجواهر وأنه متحرك في الجوهر إنما هو الصور الجوهرية ... والمدّعى هنا بيان أن الصور الجوهرية إنما يمكن حدوثها على الوجه الأول دون الثاني.

**Translation**

Know that the question susceptible of confusion among substances, whether something moves in substance, concerns substantial forms. The thesis to be established is that substantial forms can come into existence in the first of two ways—at once—and not in the second, namely gradually.

**Arabic — IV:410**

> أن هذا التبدل والتغير في الصور الطبيعية لا يمكن أن يحدث على التدريج، بل إنما يحدث دفعة واحدة.

**Translation**

Such replacement or alteration of natural forms cannot occur gradually; it occurs all at once.

**Important source distinction:** The web edition of IV:399 **interleaves a modern editor's note** about Ṣadrā and Ṭabāṭabāʾī with al-Ḥillī's primary prose. That note cannot be translated as if al-Ḥillī, who predated Ṣadrā, were discussing Ṣadrā's later theory. Its provenance belongs to the apparatus, not the matn.

The later disagreement is therefore more precise than “philosophy was static until Ṣadrā.” Many accidental movements, natural developments, and even substantial coming-to-be were already recognised; the disagreement concerns **the ontological category in which gradual change occurs** and whether the substance itself is an intrinsically flowing existence.

## II. Lāhījī — sixth question on movement in existence (*Shawāriq* I:52–53)

**Boundary:** from the heading «المسألة السادسة في أن الماهية لا تتحرك في الوجود» on p. 52 through the immediately preceding argument before «المسألة السابعة» on p. 53.

### L1. Motion presupposes a subject stable relative to its changing determination

**Arabic — p. 53**

> والدليل على ذلك هو أن الحركة في كل شيء معناها أن يكون المتحرك متقوماً وموجوداً من دون ذلك الشيء وباقياً بعينه ويتوارد عليه في كل آن من الآنات المفروضة في زمان الحركة فرد من أفراد ذلك الشيء.

**Translation**

The argument is that motion in respect of something means that the thing moving is constituted and exists without that respect in which it moves, remaining the same individual while a different instance of the relevant determination comes upon it at each posited instant during the motion.

In change of place, the body already exists without possessing this specific place and acquires successive positions. In heating, the same water persists while particular intensities of heat succeed one another.

### L2. Essence cannot remain actual while one existence is exchanged for another

**Arabic**

> وظاهر أن مثل ذلك التبدل والتوارد لا يمكن في الماهية بالقياس إلى الوجود، فإن الماهية لا يتصور كونها متقومة وموجودة وباقية من دون الوجود ليتصور توارد الوجود عليها.

**Translation**

Plainly, such exchange and succession cannot occur to a quiddity with respect to existence, since a quiddity cannot be conceived as constituted, existent and persisting **without existence**, so that successive existences could come upon it.

In the same way, prime matter cannot undergo a motion *in* substantial form if it cannot be established without some form; the allegedly stable subject is not available in the way required by the definition of motion.

The inference extends to substance, essence and what is essential, and more generally to anything without which the subject cannot exist as a constituted actual thing.

### L3. Gradation of different existents does not itself prove movement of one existent

Lāhījī grants the possibility of gradational comparison (*tashkīk*) among existing beings while resisting the inference from such comparison to the progressive intensification of **one** quiddity's existence. Different instances can be stronger and weaker without it following that the **same actual individual** changes continuously from one instance of existence into another.

**Arabic**

> وأيضاً إن الوجود ليس له فرد قائم بالماهية بمعنى كونه صفة موجودة ممتازة عن الماهية، بل هو مجرد كون الماهية.

**Translation**

Moreover, existence does not have an instance subsisting in a quiddity as though it were an actual property distinct from the quiddity; rather, it is simply the quiddity's being.

This links the substantial-motion problem directly to [the critique of an attached existence-property](01-mental-existence-and-quiddity.md).

### L4. The first objection: perhaps existences can replace one another instantaneously

**Arabic**

> فإن قيل ... فيجوز أن يتوارد على الماهية وجودات متعاقبة بحيث إذا انتفى عن الماهية وجود في آن حصل لها في ذلك الآن بعينه وجود آخر أشد أو أزيد أو أضعف أو أنقص، وكذلك في الهيولى.

**Translation**

Someone might object that the quiddity needs only *some* instance of existence, just as matter needs some instance of form. Successive existences might therefore come upon it: at the very instant one instance ceases, another stronger, greater, weaker or smaller instance obtains. Likewise successive forms might come upon matter.

**Answer**

> قلت وجوب بقاء الموضوع في الحركة بعينه يستدعي بقاء وجود بعينه مع الماهية بالضرورة، وإلا لكانت الماهية مع كل وجود فرداً آخر منها فلا تكون باقية بعينها.

I reply: the requirement that the subject of motion remain numerically the same necessarily requires the persistence of **the same existence** belonging to the quiddity. Otherwise, with each instance of existence the quiddity would be a different instance of itself and would not persist numerically unchanged. The same applies to matter in relation to its form.

**Core pressure:** identity is not guaranteed by the formal claim that “the same essence” is being predicated at each stage. Lāhījī thinks the identity of the *actual subject* is required for a continuous physical motion.

### L5. The second argument: do infinitely many intermediate states exist actually or potentially?

Lāhījī presents a further response. In continuous motion the indefinitely many individual positions or qualitative stages traversed between two limits are not all individually actual as discrete, instantaneously occupied states. If a moving thing really occupied an actual determinate state at every supposed instant, either it would remain at one state long enough to interrupt motion, or a succession of actually existing indivisible instants would follow, which cannot form a continuous time.

Applied to movement *in existence itself*, this analysis threatens to leave the quiddity actually nonexistent between posited stages, since its instances of existence would be merely potential.

**Arabic (selected)**

> فلو تحركت الماهية في الوجود لزم أن يكون جميع أفراد وجودها في زمان الحركة بالقوة فيلزم كون الماهية حال الحركة في الوجود موجودة بالقوة لا بالفعل.

**Translation**

If a quiddity moved with respect to existence, all its instances of existence during the time of motion would have to be potential, and the quiddity during that motion would exist potentially rather than actually.

### L6. Objection from motion in place, and answer

An interlocutor objects that the body cannot be deprived of an actual location. Lāhījī answers that what is needed is not its occupation of each discrete **terminal** location at each instant; the actuality of **being between** the extremes can account for motion while the individual points are potential divisions.

A final objection proposes that an actual specific state exists at each hypothetically chosen instant, without successive instants: between any two posited instants lies time. Lāhījī replies that the quiddity would then lack actual existence during that intervening time if the relevant actual instances were restricted to each posited instant.

**The unit closes with an instruction to reflect** (*fa-tadabbar*), not a full rejection of all concepts of continuous individual existence.

### Critical evaluation of Lāhījī's premises

His case rests on a **subject-and-determination model of motion**:

1. The moving subject exists actually.
2. The determination in which it moves must be distinct from what makes that subject actual.
3. A numerically identical subject persists during the reception of successive determinations.
4. Existence and constitutive form cannot be treated as determinations that a subject already actual *without them* receives.
5. Therefore movement in existence and in constitutive substance cannot occur in the same manner as movement in place or heat.

The vulnerable premise is **(2)**: must all continuity be analysed as an already actual subject bearing different ontologically derivative features? Ṣadrā answers **no**.

## III. Ṣadrā's specific rebuttal: the continuity belongs to existence (*Asfār* IV:85–87)

These passages are *direct source* for Ṣadrā, but they are **not** evidence that Ṣadrā is personally answering Lāhījī's exact wording. They are a philosophical juxtaposition of independently located arguments.

### S1. One flowing individual can possess potentially infinite determinations

**Arabic — IV:85**

> فيمكن اشتداده واستكماله في ذاته بحيث يكون وجود واحد شخصي مستمر متفاوت الحصول في شخصيته ووحدته الجوهرية بحيث ينتزع منه معنى نوع آخر بالقوة في كل آن يفرض.

**Translation**

It can therefore become more intense and reach further perfection in its own essence, while being **one continuous individual existence**, differing in its mode of obtaining within its individuality and substantial unity, such that a different specific determination could potentially be abstracted from it at each posited instant.

**The difference:** Ṣadrā does not require innumerable separate **actual** substantial kinds at every instant. The differentiated substantial meanings are merely potential boundaries or analyses of a numerically continuous existence.

### S2. The classic objection: either the species persists or a new species constantly replaces it

**Arabic — IV:85**

> أما أن يبقى نوعه في وسط الاشتداد مثلاً أو لا يبقى، فإن كان يبقى نوعه فما تغيرت الصورة الجوهرية في ذاتها ... وإن كان الجوهر لا يبقى مع الاشتداد مثلاً فكان الاشتداد قد أحدث جوهراً آخر ...

**Translation**

Either its species remains while it becomes more intense, or it does not. If it remains, its substantial form has not changed in itself, but only in an accident; if it does not remain, the intensification has produced another substance, and each posited instant would introduce a further new substance.

Ṣadrā reports this as the objection of Ibn Sīnā and earlier philosophers, **not as his own conclusion**.

### S3. Reply: continuity of one existence is not persistence of a fixed specific meaning

**Arabic — IV:86**

> إن أريد ببقائه وجوده بالشخص فنختار أنه باق ... لأن الوجود المتصل التدريجي الواحد أمر واحد زماني والاشتداد كمالية في ذلك الوجود.

**Translation**

If by its persistence they mean that the **individual existence** persists, we accept that it does: one continuously developing existence is one temporal actuality, and intensification is a perfection belonging to that existence.

**Arabic**

> فلا محالة يتبدل عليه صفات ذاتية جوهرية ولم يلزم منه وجود أنواع بلا نهاية بالفعل، بل هناك وجود واحد شخصي متصل له حدود غير متناهية بالقوة.

**Translation**

Essential and substantial determinations inevitably change within it, but that does not entail an infinity of actual species. Rather, there is **one continuous individual existence** with infinitely many possible boundaries.

**Editorial qualification:** The online page contains interleaved glosses and note markers (*s rah* etc.). Only Ṣadrā's running argument is translated; those glosses belong to a separate editorial stratum and have not been silently incorporated.

### S4. Does movement need an enduring *substrate*?

**Arabic — IV:87**

> الأصل في كل شيء هو وجوده والماهية تبع له ... وموضوع كل حركة وإن وجب أن يكون باقياً بوجوده وتشخصه إلا أنه يكفي في تشخص الموضوع الجسماني أن يكون هناك مادة تتشخص بوجود صورة ما ...

**Translation**

The fundamental reality of each thing is its existence, while its quiddity follows upon it. Although the subject of every movement must indeed persist in its existence and individuation, it suffices for a bodily subject's individuation that there be matter individualised through the presence of some form ...

This is a radical reformulation of what **persists**. Ṣadrā can preserve continuity while treating the continuous actual **existence** as the principle of identity, rather than taking an unchanged specific quiddity as a subject to which substantial states are attached.

### What this rebuttal does and does not establish

- **Established in Ṣadrā's own text:** a continuous individual existence can support substantial intensification without an actual infinity of simultaneously or successively separate species.
- **Not yet established:** that every natural substance in ordinary physical science must be understood this way, or that the proposed continuity is demonstrated independently of Ṣadrā's account of existence.
- **Key philosophical objection still live:** can **individual unity of existence** itself be specified non-circularly without presupposing the very continuity whose possibility is under dispute?

## IV. AT comparison: not all “substantial change” is the same

Aristotelian–Thomistic philosophy permits **substantial generation and corruption** and genuine powers of development within living substances. It ordinarily denies that one numerically identical substantial form is simply a continuous series of different substantial species in the same sense Ṣadrā proposes.

But the following are distinct:

1. **Accidental alteration of a persistent substance** (a body becomes hotter).
2. **Growth and maturation of one organism** (same natural kind, developing powers and accidents).
3. **Substantial generation/corruption** (loss or acquisition of constitutive substantial form).
4. **Ṣadrian substantial motion** (the existence of a material substance intrinsically flows toward different degrees of actuality).

A comparison that calls (2) “substantial motion” because it involves real development would **equivocate**. A stronger comparison would ask whether the unity of a changing organism's life admits degrees of actuality that can be understood without converting a substance into a succession of numerically different substances.

### The new philosophical synthesis to test

**Proposition:** An individual may have real developmental continuity, with varying determinations of actuality, while retaining substantial identity.

- Lāhījī supplies the **identity constraint**: changes presuppose a numerically identifiable subject.
- Ṣadrā supplies the **continuity possibility**: numerical unity may belong to one flowing existence, not merely to an unchanging kind.
- AT supplies an account of **act, potency, form and intrinsic powers**, including the distinction between what it is to retain the same substantial nature and what it is to undergo substantial generation.

There may be a useful new synthesis around **analogical grades of actuality within enduring substances**, but this is a **proposed philosophical development**, not a position to be attributed to Ibn Sīnā, Lāhījī or Aquinas without further texts.

## V. What actually changed in the tradition?

**A. From Ibn Sīnā and al-Ḥillī to Lāhījī:** the traditional claim that substantial form is not acquired through continuous motion is preserved, but Lāhījī develops an explicit argument from **persistence of the existence of the subject** and meets a specific objection about replacing instances of existence at the same instant. That is more than slogan-level conservatism.

**B. Ṣadrā's real innovation:** the very individuation of a material thing can be its **continuous existential flow**, with different substantive determinations only potentially rather than actually distinct. The debate is therefore about individuation, existence and how the continuum is analysed, not merely about whether material beings change.

**C. Later Peripatetic response:** a historian cannot infer that *every* late Avicennian rejects substantial motion. In particular, the nineteenth-century Jilwa is reported as the author of a printed *Ithbāt al-ḥaraka al-jawhariyya*, [Tehran 1313 AH print noted in al-Ṭihrānī's *al-Dharīʿa*](https://almntqim.com/%D9%A3%D9%A3%D9%A6%D9%A6_%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B0%D8%B1%D9%8A%D8%B9%D8%A9-%D8%A2%D9%82%D8%A7-%D8%A8%D8%B2%D8%B1%DA%AF-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B7%D9%87%D8%B1%D8%A7%D9%86%D9%8A-%D8%AC-%D9%A1/%D9%A8%D9%A9_%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B5%D9%81%D8%AD%D8%A9). Its **title and physical printing** are documented, but its thesis, precise argument and relationship to Sadrian substantial motion **must be inspected directly**. The previous reflexive classification “Jilwa = opponent of substantial motion” is retracted.

### Apparatus requirements for a definitive critical edition

- Collate the full Lāhījī sixth question with additional editions; particularly his distinction between *tashkīk* among instances and *motion* of one individual.
- Retrieve the relevant parts of Avicenna's *Physics* and *Metaphysics*, and Rāzī's objections, rather than relying on Ṣadrā's report of their position.
- Keep al-Ḥillī's **matn** and modern editor's footnotes separate.
- Collate Ṣadrā's running text IV:85–87 with its glosses (e.g. Sabzawārī), separately attributed.
- Locate and inspect the 1313 AH Jilwa print: *Ithbāt al-ḥaraka al-jawhariyya*, and distinguish it from other writings titled *Taḥqīq al-ḥaraka fī l-jawhar*.
- Compare the exact Latin *De principiis naturae* and Aristotle's *Physics* V before declaring an AT consensus about the indivisibility of substantial generation.

**Completion statement:** Lāhījī's sixth question is translated as a connected unit, with Arabic lemmata; Ṣadrā's principal reply is translated in selected connected portions; al-Ḥillī's two historical claims are translated from their loci. This is a substantive philosophical collation, **not a multi-manuscript critical edition** or proof of Jilwa's doctrinal allegiance.
