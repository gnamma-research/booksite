# Book I, Chapter 1 — Mental existence, quiddity, and the meaning of *wujūd*
**A post-classical Avicennian argument in full, with a critical comparison to Thomistic intentionality**

**Editorial state:** **Translation of one complete bounded argument unit** in Lāhījī's *Shawāriq al-ilhām*, vol. I, printed p. 52 (fifth question, through but not including the heading of the sixth). The associated mental-existence discussion from pp. 50–52 is represented by selected lemmata, **not** a complete translation. One digital print transcription has been checked; no handwritten witnesses or alternative editions have been collated.

**Primary sources:** [Lāhījī I:50](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/9309/50?lang=ar), [I:51](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/9309/51?lang=ar), [I:52](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/9309/52?lang=ar); [Aquinas, *Summa theologiae* I q. 85 a. 2](https://www.corpusthomisticum.org/sth1084.html).

## I. The problem

When the intellect understands a horse, is the horse's **quiddity** present in intellect, or only a distinct representation? How can a mental content representing a substance be an accidental **quality** in the knower? And is existence a separate property added to an already constituted quiddity?

These are connected but not identical questions. Lāhījī's solution turns on an exact differentiation between **what is represented**, **the representation's mode of being in a knower**, and **the representation as an actual psychological event**. His subsequent criticism of existence as a real adjunct to a prior quiddity prevents an overly literal use of the essence–existence distinction.

### A. A substance known and the cognition as a quality

**Arabic — Lāhījī I:50 (selected contiguous passage)**

> فهو من حيث هو معلوم بالذات وجوهر أي ماهية جوهر وكلي وموجود في الذهن، ومن حيث خصوص الوجود الذهني علم، ومن حيث القيام بالذهن الشخصي والمحفوفية بعوارض ذلك الذهن جزئي وعرض من الكيفيات النفسانية وموجود خارجي بالمعنى المقابل للوجود الظلي.

**Translation**

Considered in itself, it is something known primarily, a substance—that is, it has the quiddity of a substance—a universal, and something existing in the mind. Considered with regard to the specific mode of its mental existence, it is knowledge. Considered insofar as it inheres in a particular mind and is surrounded by that mind's individual conditions, it is a particular, an accident belonging to the psychological qualities, and an externally existing thing in the sense opposed to derivative or shadowy existence.

**Critical note 1.** *Mawlūm bi-l-dhāt* must not be collapsed into the accidentally cognised extramental individual, *maʿlūm bi-l-ʿaraḍ*. That the mental act is an accidental quality does not imply that the object signified thereby is a quality.

**Critical note 2.** The word "external" in the last clause means **the act of cognition as an actual state of the really existing knower**, not that the horse represented exists extramentally inside the mind. Lāhījī explicitly differentiates these descriptions.

### B. The apparent contradiction is equivocation between respects

**Arabic — I:50 (selected)**

> ولا منافاة بين كون الشيء جوهرا ذهنيا وعرضا خارجيا ... وإنما المنافاة بين كون الشيء جوهرا ذهنيا وعرضا ذهنيا أو بين كونه جوهرا خارجيا وعرضا خارجيا.

**Translation**

There is no contradiction in something being a mental substance and an extramental accident. What would be contradictory is its being both mental substance and mental accident, or both extramental substance and extramental accident, *in the same consideration*.

Here "mental substance" means that the content represents something whose extramental mode of being is not inherence in a subject; "extramental accident" means that the representing psychological state actually inheres in a knower.

### C. The knowing image itself is actual in the knower

**Arabic — I:51 (selected)**

> ومما يجب أن يعلم أن وجود الصورة العلمية في الذهن وقيامها به إنما هو وجود ذهني للمعلوم الذي هو ذو الصورة وليس هو وجودا ذهنيا لنفس هذه الصورة ... فإن الذهن أعني النفس الناطقة موجودة في الخارج وقد قامت بها تلك الصورة وترتبت على تلك الصورة آثارها ولوازمها.

**Translation**

One must understand that the existence of the cognitive form in the mind, and its inherence there, constitutes the mental existence of what is known—the thing represented by that form—not the mental existence of the form considered as an object in its own right. For the mind, that is, the rational soul, really exists outside the mind as an actual being; the cognitive form inheres in it, and the form's own effects and concomitants belong to it.

**Source caveat.** The sentence includes material omitted by the ellipsis; it has been broken out as an *explanatory excerpt*, not a continuous translation of every word on p. 51.

### D. Why a likeness is not necessarily a substitute object

Lāhījī reports a customary distinction between ancient advocates of mental "images" resembling objects and later writers who speak of the quiddities themselves obtaining mentally. He argues that the word "shadow" can designate a quiddity's **different mode of being**, without implying a fundamentally different object merely matching some of its accidents.

**Arabic — I:52 (selected)**

> فإن الحكم على شيء إنما يستدعي وجود ذلك الشيء وثبوته لا ثبوت أمر مغاير له وإن وافقه في بعض الأعراض.

**Translation**

For judging something requires the being or establishment of that very thing, not the establishment of something numerically or essentially different from it merely because the latter resembles it in certain accidents.

**Limitation:** This does not yet answer all problems of intentionality, nor settle whether the known nature is numerically identical across mental and external instantiations. The relevant *Shifāʾ* and logical discussions must be collated.

## II. COMPLETE BOUNDED TRANSLATION: *Shawāriq* I:52, the fifth question

**Boundary:** from «المسألة الخامسة [في أن الوجود ليس أمرا ينضم إلى الماهية]» through the final sentence immediately before «المسألة السادسة». Arabic orthography and punctuation below are lightly normalised; the underlying online print transcription has not been collated.

### Arabic base passage

> **المسألة الخامسة:** في أن الوجود ليس أمراً ينضم إلى الماهية فيتحقق به الماهية، بل هو نفس تحقق الماهية وحصولها وكونها كما أشرنا إليه فيما سبق، وإليه أشار المصنف بقوله: «وليس الوجود معنى به يحصل الماهية في العين بل الحصول».
>
> وهذا ردّ لما ذهب إليه جماعة من أتباع المشّائين من كون الوجود صفة موجودة في الخارج منضمة إلى الماهية وكون الماهية موجودة بها؛ فعندهم المجعول بالذات والصادر من الجاعل بالحقيقة إنما هو الوجود، والماهية من حيث هي غير مجعولة ومن حيث الاتصاف بالوجود مجعولة بالعرض. وهذا المذهب بظاهره سخيف جداً، وقد أبطله شيخ الإشراق في كتاب الإشراق وغيره بما لا مزيد عليه.
>
> وحاصله أن ثبوت الصفة العينية للموصوف يتوقف على ثبوت الموصوف في العين، فيلزم كون الماهية موجودة في الخارج قبل وجودها في الخارج. وأيضاً لو كان الوجود موجوداً في الخارج فإما بوجود غيره فيلزم التسلسل في الوجودات، وإما بوجود هو نفسه فيلزم أن لا يكون حمل الموجود على الوجود وغيره بمعنى واحد، إذ مفهومه فيه أنه نفس الوجود وفي غيره أنه شيء له الوجود، والحال أنه لا يطلق على الجميع إلا بمعنى واحد.
>
> ومستندهم هو أن الماهية إن لم ينضم إليها أمر من الجاعل فهي على العدم. وجوابه أنكم فرضتم ماهية في الخارج ثم يضم الجاعل الوجود إليها، وهذا باطل؛ لأن الماهية إذا كانت أولاً فهي موجودة، إذ لا يفهم من الوجود إلا كون الماهية، فأي حاجة إلى ضم الوجود إليها؟ بل نفس الماهية إنما صدرت من الجاعل فكانت في الخارج، فكان كونها في الخارج بعد الصدور هو وجودها.
>
> والمصنف أشار بهذا الكلام إلى أنه لا حاجة في إبطال ذلك المذهب إلى هذه المؤونة، بل يكفي أن يقال: إنا لا نعني ولا نفهم من الوجود إلا كون الماهية وحصولها بالضرورة، لا أمراً ينضم إلى الماهية فتصير موجودة؛ فلو انضم إليها أمر من الجاعل لا يكون ذلك الأمر وجوداً بالمعنى المفهوم للكل.
>
> ولا ينبغي أن يتوهم المنافاة بين ما ذكر هاهنا وبين ما أشرنا إليه سابقاً وسيأتي تحقيقه من كون الوجود عند الحكماء ذا أفراد حقيقية، إذ ليست تلك الأفراد عندهم ما ينضم إلى الماهية فتصير موجودة به، بل إن هي إلا أكوان خاصة للماهيات المتخالفة، كل منها نفس كون ماهية مخصوصة بحسب الخارج، كما ستعرفه إن شاء الله تعالى.

### English — complete translation of the argument unit

**The fifth question: Existence is not something that attaches to a quiddity such that the quiddity comes to be established through it; rather, it is the very establishment, obtaining, and being of that quiddity.**

We have already indicated this, and it is what the author of the base text signifies when he says: “Existence is not a meaning through which a quiddity obtains in extramental reality; rather, it is the obtaining itself.”

This replies to the position of a group among the followers of the Peripatetics: according to them, existence is a really existing attribute that is joined to a quiddity outside the mind, such that the quiddity becomes existent through it. On their account, what is made in itself, what genuinely proceeds from the productive cause, is existence; the quiddity, considered in itself, is not made, while considered as qualified by existence it is made incidentally. In its apparent sense this position is extremely unsatisfactory. The Master of Illumination has refuted it in the *Ḥikmat al-ishrāq* and elsewhere in a way that leaves little to add.

The substance of the refutation is as follows. For a real attribute to belong to a subject, that subject must already be established in reality. It would therefore follow that a quiddity is existent outside the mind **before** it exists there. Moreover, if existence itself were something existing outside the mind, either it would exist through another existence, in which case an infinite regress of existences would result; or it would exist through an existence identical with itself, in which case predicating “existent” of existence and of something else would not have the same meaning. For in the former case it would mean “existence itself,” and in the latter “something possessing existence,” whereas the expression is employed with one common meaning.

The ground invoked by advocates of that position is that a quiddity remains non-existent unless something coming from the productive cause is added to it. The reply is that you have first posited a quiddity outside the mind and then had the producer add existence to it. That is false: if the quiddity is already there in the first place, it is already existent. For by existence we understand nothing other than the quiddity's being; what need, then, would there be to adjoin existence to it? Rather, the quiddity itself proceeds from the productive cause and is thereby outside the mind; its being outside the mind upon proceeding from that cause **is its existence**.

The author of the base text indicates that this more elaborate effort is unnecessary for refuting the disputed position. It suffices to say that by existence we necessarily mean and understand nothing but the quiddity's being and obtaining, not something that is joined to it and thereby makes it existent. Even if something were adjoined to it by the producer, that addition would not be existence in the meaning commonly understood by everyone.

Nor should anyone imagine a contradiction between the present account and the view already mentioned, which we shall examine further, that for the philosophers existence has **real instances**. For those instances are not, on their account, things that join a quiddity and thereby make it exist. They are nothing other than **particular modes of being of different quiddities**, each being precisely the extramental actuality of a determinate quiddity, as you shall learn, God willing.

### Critical notes on this translated unit

**[E1] Attribution.** The terse sentence introduced by «قال المصنف» is the *Tajrīd* lemma being expounded. The criticisms involving Suhrawardī, the explanatory regress, and the final reconciliation of particular existences are **Lāhījī's commentary**. They must not be merged into a single authorial voice.

**[E2] Scope of criticism.** The target is an **extrinsically joined, independently actual property** called existence. The argument does *not* simply deny that there can be a genuine metaphysical distinction between essence and *esse*, and it does not establish the Scotistic or Thomistic status of that distinction.

**[E3] The apparent contradiction resolved.** He denies “existence is a second actual item joined to a previously actual essence” while affirming “there are different particular existences.” That is consistent if the latter are the *ways in which different things actually are*, not a stock of detachable existence-properties.

**[E4] What follows concerning Ṣadrā?** Neither the primacy of existence (*aṣālat al-wujūd*), nor a single all-inclusive reality of existence, follows merely from this critique of the extrinsic-property account. A separate ontology is needed.

**[E5] Editorial uncertainty.** The online copy's transcription has occasional spelling and typographical irregularities. No manuscript variants are being reported or invented; the Arabic here is a normalised reading of that one printing.

## III. The Thomistic comparison: *species qua*, not *species quod*

Aquinas distinguishes the intelligible form as **that by which** the intellect understands from the extramental nature as **what** the intellect primarily understands. He rejects the thesis that knowledge of nature is restricted to cognition of one's own mental representations. See *Summa theologiae* I q. 85 a. 2, [Corpus Thomisticum](https://www.corpusthomisticum.org/sth1084.html), response and replies.

**Latin lemma:**

> *Species intelligibilis non est quod intelligitur actu, sed id quo intelligit intellectus.*

**Translation:**

The intelligible species is not what is actually understood; rather, it is that by which the intellect understands.

This is **not automatically** equivalent to Lāhījī's distinction. The latter employs an elaborate system of “mental existence of what is known” and “external existence of the mental act,” whereas Aquinas's formal/objective distinction involves a theory of intentional assimilation, abstraction, and the act's term. The comparison must preserve these technical differences.

### Argument graph

1. The intellect can consider **the quiddity** of an extramental substance.
2. The mental act is itself a **particular quality** belonging to a particular intellect.
3. It is not a contradiction that the quiddity **represented** belongs to the category substance while the **act representing it** belongs to the category quality.
4. Therefore the existence of a representation must not be mistaken for an extramental horse or other substance somehow lodged in the intellect.
5. In the linked ontology, “existence” means the actual being/obtaining of a thing and need not be pictured as an additional actual item glued to it.
6. Neither (1)–(5) proves the numerical identity of nature across instantiations, nor settles whether existence is really or only conceptually distinct from essence.
7. Both the Avicennian and Thomistic accounts therefore require a further analysis of **intentionality and metaphysical composition**.

### New result for the mature-summa project

Lāhījī is **not** simply a conservative repeater of a naive essence-plus-existence-as-accident theory. His text already integrates a theory of intentionality, the critique of object-substitution, differentiated existence, and a refined interpretation of making. The next complete argument unit, [*On what a cause makes*](02-making-and-causal-explanation.md), shows why that last topic cannot be detached from the foregoing distinctions.
