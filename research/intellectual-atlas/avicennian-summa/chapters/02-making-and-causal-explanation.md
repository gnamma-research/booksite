# Book I, Chapter 2 — *Jaʿl*: what does the cause actually make?
**Lāhījī, *Shawāriq al-ilhām* I:158–161; al-Taftāzānī, al-Ījī and al-Jurjānī; comparison with Ibn Sīnā and Aquinas**

**Scholarly status:** A connected critical translation of the central **objection–reply sequence** on printed pp. 158–161. All specifically translated Arabic extracts are identified below; the page contains additional technical discussion of the classification of simple and complex essences, which is **not** represented as exhaustively translated. Arabic checked against the named searchable print transcription only. No source-manuscript variants established.

**Primary sources:** [Shawāriq I:158](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/9309/158?lang=ar), [I:159](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/9309/159?lang=ar), [I:160](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/9309/160?lang=ar), [I:161](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/9309/161?lang=ar). ## Thesis and the prior question

The dispute over whether quiddities are “made” often mixes up at least four questions:

1. Does **an existent thing** owe its existence to a producer?
2. Does a thing's **quiddity count as the kind of quiddity it is** only through an act of production?
3. Does a **complex** quiddity have parts and thus a kind of internal dependence not shared by something simple?
4. What is really one outside the mind but analysed into distinct concepts—**particular existent, its quiddity, and its existence**?

The disputants can agree on (1) while disagreeing about (2)–(4), or appear to disagree only because they use “made” differently.

## I. Three reported positions — *Shawāriq* I:158

**Arabic (the reported schools)**

> وكما يتحقق الحاجة إلى جاعل في المركب فكذا في البسيط. اعلم أن العقلاء بعد الاتفاق على أن وجود الممكن بالفاعل اختلفوا في ماهيته. قال شارح المقاصد: فذهب المتكلمون إلى أنها بجعل الجاعل مطلقاً، أي بسيطة كانت ومركبة، وذهب جمهور الفلاسفة والمعتزلة إلى أنها ليست بجعل الجاعل، بمعنى أن شيئاً منها ليس بمجعول، وذهب بعضهم إلى أن المركبات مجعولة دون البسائط.

**Translation**

Just as there is a need for a maker in the case of something composite, so there is in the case of something simple. Understand that, although those who employ reason agree that the **existence** of a contingent thing comes from an agent, they disagree about its **quiddity**. The commentator on the *Maqāṣid* [al-Taftāzānī] states: the theologians maintained that it comes about through the producer's making, whether simple or composite; the majority of the philosophers and the Muʿtazilites maintained that it does not come about through the producer's making, in the sense that none of it is made; and some maintained that composite quiddities are made but simple quiddities are not.

**Editorial qualification:** These are historical *position ascriptions in this dispute*, not a blanket agreement among all theologians or all Muʿtazila. The strong Muʿtazilī view about the nonexistent as a thing must be checked school by school.

### A. The missing distinction: simple and composite making

**Arabic**

> وأما وجه ذهاب الفلاسفة إليه فهو أنهم نفوا الجعل المركب، أعني جعل الماهية ماهية، على ما يدل عليه دليلهم كما يأتي. وأما الجعل البسيط، أعني كون الصادر عن الفاعل نفس الماهية ...

**Translation**

The reason for attributing that view to the philosophers is that they deny **composite making**: making a quiddity be that very quiddity, as their proof will indicate. As for **simple making**, meaning that the very quiddity is what proceeds from the agent ...

**The passage continues on p. 159.** The ellipsis above indicates omitted text, not a completed paragraph. The real distinction is between “making something exist” and “making humanity be humanity” or “making blackness be blackness.”

## II. The first reported position: its three arguments and Lāhījī's replies — I:159

### Objection 1: possibility is universal

**Arabic**

> ثم إن مستند المذهب الأول أمور: الأول أن علة الحاجة هي الإمكان وهي عامة.

**Translation**

The first position rests on several considerations. First, the ground of causal dependence is possibility, which is common [to the simple and the composite].

**Answer**

> والجواب عن الأول بأن معنى حاجة الممكن إلى الجاعل أن وجوده منه لا ماهيته.

The answer to the first is that the need of the contingent being for a producer means that **its existence comes from that producer**, not that its quiddity as such comes from him in the sense under dispute.

### Objection 2: if the quiddity is unmade, making disappears altogether

**Arabic**

> الثاني أنه لو لم يكن الماهية مجعولة ارتفع المجعولية؛ لأن ما فرض كونه مجعولاً من الوجود أو موصوفية الماهية به فهو أيضاً ماهيته في نفسه، والمفروض أن ما هو ماهية ليس بمجعول.

**Translation**

Second, if quiddity were not made, making as such would disappear. For whatever is assumed to be made—existence or the quiddity's being qualified by existence—also has a quiddity of its own, and the assumption is that whatever is a quiddity is not made.

**Answer**

> وعن الثاني أن المجعول هو الوجود الخاص وما هو من أفراد الوجود، لا ماهية الوجود ومفهومه.

In reply to the second: what is made is **particular existence**, something that belongs to the instances of existence—not the *quiddity* or *concept* of existence.

**Critical distinction:** Lāhījī's reply permits particular actuality to be produced while rejecting the proposition that the **meaning** of existence has to be made a meaning. Compare [Chapter 1](01-mental-existence-and-quiddity.md) on why existence is not an independently actual property adjoined to an already actual quiddity.

### Objection 3: without making, a quiddity would possess independent external establishment

**Arabic**

> الثالث أنه لا تقرر للماهية في الخارج بذاتها، لما مر في نفي ثبوت المعدومات، فيكون تقررها بالجاعل.

**Translation**

Third, the quiddity has no independent establishment outside the mind, as already explained when denying the established reality of non-existent things. Its actual establishment must therefore come from a producer.

**Answer**

> وعن الثالث أنه ليس تقرر الماهية إلا وجودها.

In reply to the third: the quiddity's external establishment is **nothing other than its existence**.

**Result:** His thesis does not posit a quiddity established outside the mind awaiting a separate act of existential attachment. It is precisely this possible misconception that must be removed in comparing him with Aquinas.

## III. The opposing proof from essential identity — I:159

**Arabic (reported proof and counter-reply)**

> ومستند المذهب الثاني أن كون الإنسان إنساناً مثلاً لو كان بجعل الجاعل لارتفع بارتفاعه فيلزم سلب الشيء عن نفسه على تقدير عدم الفاعل وهو محال. وأجابوا عنه بمنع استحالته على طريق السلب دون العدول. وردّ بأن السلب الصادق على الإنسان حال ارتفاعه هو أن المعدوم ليس بإنسان لا أن الإنسان ليس بإنسان، والحق أن استحالة سلب الشيء عن نفسه ضرورية.

**Translation**

The argument supporting the second position is that, if a human being's being human, for example, resulted from an agent's making it so, then it would cease to be human if that productive activity were removed. This would entail denying of a thing what it itself is, on the hypothesis of the agent's absence—which is impossible.

The reply offered to this says that there is no impossibility here when the denial is taken as **negative predication** rather than as the affirmation of an opposite property [*salb* as opposed to *ʿudūl*]. But this is answered as follows: the true negation when the human is absent is “the non-existent is not a human,” not “the human is not human.” The impossibility of denying of something what it itself is must be regarded as necessary.

**Apparatus:** The distinction between *salb* and *ʿudūl* is a technical logical distinction, not evidence that one can coherently predicate “not human” of an actually existing human. The objection guards the invariance of an essence's defining content under hypothetical non-instantiation.

## IV. A third position: composites but not simple quiddities — I:159

**Arabic**

> ومستند المذهب الثالث أن شرط المجعولية الإمكان وهو يستدعي نسبة وهي تستدعي اثنينية وهي غير متصورة في البسيط دون المركب. والجواب أن الاثنينية التي تستدعيها النسبة اللازمة للإمكان إنما هي باعتبار الماهية والوجود وهي شاملة للبسيط والمركب.

**Translation**

The third position rests on the claim that the condition of being made is possibility; possibility requires a relation, which in turn requires a duality, and such duality cannot be conceived in the simple, though it can be conceived in the composite.

The reply is that the duality required by the relation involved in possibility is the distinction **in consideration between quiddity and existence**. It applies to both the simple and the composite.

**Key inference:** Dependence is not restricted to internally composite essences; an externally dependent simple being can still be contingent. This matters when drawing analogies to separated intellects or incorporeal created substances.

## V. Al-Taftāzānī: clarify the *real* subject of disagreement — I:159

Lāhījī quotes the commentator on the *Maqāṣid*. We reproduce the argument as **reported in Lāhījī**; independent collation against *Sharḥ al-Maqāṣid* is outstanding.

**Arabic**

> فإنه معلوم أن ليس للفاعل تأثير وجعل بالنسبة إلى ماهية الممكن وآخر بالنسبة إلى الوجود حتى تكون الماهية مجعولة كالوجود، وأن ليس للماهية تقرر في الخارج بدون الفاعل حتى يكون المجعول هو الوجود فقط، بل أثر الفاعل مجعولية الماهية بمعنى صيرورتها موجودة.

**Translation**

For it is known that the agent does not exercise one act of influence and making with respect to the contingent being's quiddity and a **second** act with respect to its existence, such that the quiddity is made just as existence is made. Nor does the quiddity enjoy extramental establishment without the agent, such that only existence is made. Rather, the agent's effect is that the quiddity becomes actual—that is, it is made in the sense of **coming to exist**.

**Critical insight:** Taftāzānī does not present essence and existence as two separately manufactured external items. This is an important point of contact with an AT analysis of the composite's dependence on a cause.

## VI. Al-Ījī and Jurjānī: two senses of dependence — I:159–160

Lāhījī next relays the author of the *Mawāqif* and the commentator on it. Do not attribute all the following distinctions directly to al-Ījī.

**Arabic — reported in I:159**

> المجعولية قد يراد بها الاحتياج إلى الفاعل وقد يراد بها الاحتياج إلى الغير على ما يعم الجزء.

**Translation**

“Being made” may mean needing an **efficient agent**, or it may mean needing **another** in a broader sense that includes needing a constitutive part.

A simple contingent existent can need an efficient agent without being composed of internal parts; a composite quiddity additionally depends upon its constituents. Treating these as the same need would make the disagreement appear larger than it is.

**Arabic — I:160, reported *Sharḥ al-Mawāqif***

> والصواب أن يقال معنى قولهم الماهية ليست مجعولة أنها في حد ذاتها لا يتعلق بها جعل جاعل وتأثير مؤثر ... فلا مغايرة بين الماهية ونفسها حتى يتصور توسط جعل بينهما ...

**Translation**

The correct way to state the meaning of “the quiddity is not made” is that, taken in itself, it is not the terminus of a producer's making or a cause's influence. There is no difference between a quiddity and itself such that one could conceive an act of making that intervened between the two.

This does not mean the **existent** is independent of its creator. It means a causal act is not required to make what humanity means *be* humanity.

### The dyer analogy — I:160

**Arabic — reported *Sharḥ al-Mawāqif***

> فإن الصباغ مثلاً إذا صبغ ثوباً فإنه لا يجعل الثوب ثوباً ولا الصبغ صبغاً بل يجعل الثوب متصفاً بالصبغ في الخارج ...

**Translation**

When a dyer dyes a garment, he does not make the garment be a garment, nor dye be dye; he makes the garment qualified by the dye outside the mind.

**Lāhījī's criticism**

> أقول وهذا كلام لا غبار عليه إلا أنه ينبغي تحقيق معنى جعل الماهية متصفة بالوجود لئلا يتوهم أن الماهية والوجود كليهما حاصلان بلا جعل وأثر الجاعل أنه يجعل أحدهما متصفاً بالآخر.

I say: this is a sound account, **except** that we must clarify what is meant by making a quiddity qualified by existence, lest someone imagine that quiddity and existence are both already actual without being made and that the producer's effect is merely to join one to the other.

The dyer analogy is pedagogical, not an ontological model of two already established substances being combined. That further clarification is Lāhījī's central corrective.

## VII. Lāhījī's own adjudication — I:161

**Arabic**

> بل المراد أن الصادر من الجاعل إنما هو أمر واحد يحلله العقل إلى أمرين ويجعل أحدهما متصفاً بالآخر، فهذا هو معنى جعل الماهية متصفة بالوجود كما مر غير مرة.

**Translation**

Rather, the intention is that what proceeds from the producer is **one thing**, which the intellect analyses into two considerations and regards one as qualified by the other. **That** is the meaning of making a quiddity qualified by existence, as has already been explained more than once.

**Arabic**

> ثم أقول لكن هذا الكلام أيضاً يرفع النزاع فلم يحصل معنى يصلح للخلاف، بل الصواب كما أشرنا إليه أن يجعل النزاع في الجعل المركب للماهية مع قطع النظر عن الوجود، فإن الاحتياج إلى الجاعل في الوجود موضع الوفاق كما عرفت والجعل البسيط للماهية دقيق لا يفهمه الأكثرون ...

**Translation**

I add that this account, too, removes the disagreement: no meaning remains that would properly support a dispute. The correct course, as we indicated, is to locate the disagreement in **composite making of the quiddity while abstracting from existence**. For the need of a producer in respect of existence is admitted on all sides, as has been shown, while the simple making of a quiddity is a subtle matter not understood by most.

He goes on to explain why some theologians, unfamiliar with simple making, treated making as composite; others, notably those who accepted pre-existential quiddities, denied the need to make those quiddities; philosophers denied the same compound making as unintelligible; and advocates of a simple/composite distinction reasoned from the imagined internal division of a composite. These are **Lāhījī's explanatory diagnoses of the schools**, not independent verification of what every author held.

**Arabic — concluding interpretation**

> فالماهيات الممكنة سواء كانت مركبة أو بسيطة محتاجة إليه على السواء ومجعولة بالجعل البسيط بالمعنى الذي عرفت.

**Translation**

Contingent quiddities, whether composite or simple, need the producer equally and are made through **simple making** in the sense already explained.

### The major repair to the prior argument

The productive act does not connect two already actual parts called "essence" and "existence." It makes one **particular existent**, which the intellect legitimately considers under both the terms **quiddity** and **existence**. This is why Lāhījī's earlier claim that particular existence is what is made is consistent with rejecting a separately joined existential attribute.

This is a real **post-classical argumentative refinement**: it integrates the ontological problem with the disputed meanings of *jaʿl*, the logic of essential identity, and the three theological schools' senses of dependence.

## VIII. Where this places the AT comparison

Aquinas, in [*De potentia* III.8](https://www.corpusthomisticum.org/qdp3.html), says that what is produced in natural generation **per se** is the composite, while the form is that *through whose acquisition* something becomes actual; he denies that a natural substantial form must be individually created as an independent item.

This is not an identity of doctrines with Lāhījī. Yet both insist on a useful principle:

**Do not multiply independently existing objects simply because an adequate explanation distinguishes several metaphysical principles.**

The points requiring independent investigation are:

- Does the maker confer an act of existence really distinct from essence in Aquinas's sense, or is the essence–existence contrast in Lāhījī primarily a legitimate conceptual analysis of one actual existent?
- Is a distinct natural **generator** the proximate cause of the composite's substantial form, or are proximate material agents merely dispositive with respect to the form's reception from a separate intellect?
- How can the distinction between existence and quiddity be real **without** reifying both as prior existing items?
- Does a simple created intellect have a composition of essence and existence without the material composition of parts?
- How does the objection to real, separately added existence interact with Ṣadrā's *aṣālat al-wujūd* and *tashkīk*?

### Dependency summary

**Knowledge and quiddity → distinguishing mental and extramental modes → meaning of existence → particular existence as made → denial of an extrinsically attached existence-thing → simple making of one actual existent → real causal dependence without dependence on internal parts → generation and conservation.**

This is one continuous philosophical route, not a list of unrelated school positions.
