After Ṣadrā: an Avicennian Summa

A critical reconstruction of Avicennism after its encounters with kalām, Illuminationism and Sadrian philosophy: not a falsely attributed single historical text, but a problem-indexed synthesis grounded in identifiable primary passages.

Editorial status

This is a genuine initial edition, not a completed translation or an authenticated composite system. The Arabic excerpts below were checked against specific online print transcriptions, and translations are new working drafts. Ellipses mark omitted material. Manuscript and critical-edition collation is outstanding. Comments, classifications and philosophical syntheses are editorial, not words of Ibn Sīnā, Lāhījī or Narāqī.

Important correction: Narāqī's substantial published Sharḥ Ilāhiyyāt al-Shifāʾ is reported unfinished in the editor's introduction (ed., p. 24). Nor is Lāhījī's Shawāriq a complete commentary on every part of the Tajrīd.

Thirty-two questions across six books

I. First philosophy & logical method

  1. What is the subject of metaphysics: being as being, God, or first causes?
  2. Are existence, thing, and necessity primitive concepts?
  3. How should essence, existence, thubūt and nafs al-amr be distinguished?
  4. Do quiddities have reality independently of existence?
  5. Does the doctrine of mental existence follow from knowledge?
  6. Can Avicennian demonstration survive later epistemological objections?

II. Possibility, causation & God

  1. Why does a contingent being require a cause?
  2. Does a cause explain both origination and continued existence?
  3. Are efficient causes vertically ordered and how do they involve the four causes?
  4. Does an effect become necessary through its complete cause?
  5. Can an infinite causal regress be admitted in any sense?
  6. What precisely does the Proof of the Truthful establish?
  7. Divine simplicity, attributes, knowledge and the plurality of effects.

III. Nature, matter & change

  1. Hylomorphism: matter, form, body and substantial unity.
  2. Is substantial motion coherent without a persisting subject?
  3. Are gradation of being and intensification of substance different theses?
  4. Motion, continuity, time and modern conceptions of nature.
  5. Nature's causal powers, chance, and anti-atomist arguments.

IV. Soul & cognition

  1. Is the soul immaterial at origination or corporeal in origination?
  2. Abstraction, intelligibles, unity of intellect and intelligible.
  3. Knowledge by presence, mental existence and the object of thought.
  4. Imagination, estimation, bodily faculties and survival of the soul.

V. Creation, theology & metaphysical plurality

  1. Temporal origination, essential dependence and dahrī origination.
  2. Are God and contingent beings genuinely distinct existents?
  3. Does aṣālat al-wujūd entail waḥdat al-wujūd?
  4. Graded existence, analogical predication and the unity of a common concept.
  5. Divine agency, secondary causes, providence and evil.
  6. Resurrection, prophecy, and the limits of philosophical reasoning.

VI. Cross-tradition adjudication

  1. Rāzī, Ṭūsī, post-classical kalām and the Avicennian proofs.
  2. Suhrawardī, Dāmād, Ṣadrā and their divergent ontological commitments.
  3. Akbarian aʿyān thābita and their relation to the status of quiddity.
  4. Jilwa, Khayrābādī and the possibility of a modern Avicennian reconstruction.

A1 · The subject of metaphysics

Ibn Sīnā · al-Shifāʾ, Ilāhiyyāt I.1, passage on the first subject

English · working translation

But if causes are considered insofar as they are existents, and in respect of what belongs to them under that aspect, then the primary subject must be the existent insofar as it is existent.

العربية · من النص المطبوع

وأما إن كان النظر في الأسباب من جهة ما هي موجودة وما يلحقها من تلك الجهة فيجب إذن أن يكون الموضوع الأول هو الموجود بما هو موجود.

Translation note & evidence status

The first cause is something to be investigated within metaphysics; it is not simply presupposed as the subject whose existence that science cannot question. This is an editorial explanation, not part of the translation.

A2 · Primitive conceptions

Ibn Sīnā · al-Shifāʾ, Ilāhiyyāt I.5, p. 29–30

English · working translation

The meanings of 'existent', 'thing', and 'necessary' are impressed upon the soul in a primary fashion; that impression does not need to be brought about through things better known than themselves. ... Were every conception to require a preceding conception, the process would either proceed to infinity or turn in a circle.

العربية · من النص المطبوع

إن الموجود، والشيء، والضروري، معانيها ترتسم في النفس ارتساما أوليا، ليس ذلك الارتسام مما يحتاج إلى أن يجلب بأشياء أعرف منها. ... ولو كان كل تصور يحتاج إلى أن يسبقه تصور قبله لذهب الأمر في ذلك إلى غير النهاية، أو لدار.

Check the Arabic source and locator

Translation note & evidence status

Two non-contiguous excerpts have been joined by an ellipsis. This argues for primary intelligibility; it does not on its own settle whether the concept of existence corresponds to an independently existing universal.

B1 · Why substantial motion is disputed

ʿAbd al-Razzāq Lāhījī · Shawāriq al-ilhām, vol. I, p. 53

English · working translation

The proof is this: motion in respect of anything means that what moves is constituted and exists apart from that thing, remains the very same subject, and receives, at each posited instant during the time of motion, a different instance of that thing. ... Plainly, a succession of this kind cannot occur in a quiddity with respect to existence: a quiddity cannot be conceived as constituted, existent and persisting without existence, such that successive existences could come upon it.

العربية · من النص المطبوع

والدليل على ذلك هو أن الحركة في كل شيء معناها أن يكون المتحرك متقوما وموجودا من دون ذلك الشيء وباقيا بعينه، ويتوارد عليه في كل آن من الآنات المفروضة في زمان الحركة فرد من أفراد ذلك الشيء. ... وظاهر أن مثل ذلك التبدل والتوارد لا يمكن في الماهية بالقياس إلى الوجود، فإن الماهية لا يتصور كونها متقومة وموجودة وباقية من دون الوجود ليتصور توارد الوجود عليها.

Check the Arabic source and locator

Translation note & evidence status

Lāhījī's objection turns on the identity and persistence of the subject of change. It should not be reduced to 'he denies all change of substance': the exact scope of motion and the Sadrian reply require comparison with the Asfār. The ellipsis separates non-contiguous clauses.

Arabic checked against a searchable transcription of the cited page; edition/witness collation pending.

B2 · What the maker brings about

ʿAbd al-Razzāq Lāhījī · Shawāriq al-ilhām, vol. I, p. 159

English · working translation

In answer to the first: for a possible being to need a producer means that its existence comes from that producer, not its quiddity. In answer to the second: what is brought about is particular existence, that which is an instance of existence—not the quiddity or concept of existence. In answer to the third: a quiddity has no established actuality other than its existence.

العربية · من النص المطبوع

والجواب عن الأول بأن معنى حاجة الممكن إلى الجاعل أن وجوده منه لا ماهيته؛ وعن الثاني أن المجعول هو الوجود الخاص وما هو من أفراد الوجود، لا ماهية الوجود ومفهومه؛ وعن الثالث أنه ليس تقرر الماهية إلا وجودها.

Check the Arabic source and locator

Translation note & evidence status

The three replies answer three earlier objections in a debate about 'making' (jaʿl), so they should never be circulated as an unqualified standalone declaration of every doctrine held by Lāhījī. 'Particular existence' translates al-wujūd al-khāṣṣ.

One continuous extract of numbered responses; contextual objections not yet translated.

C1 · Contingency and continued dependence

Muḥammad Mahdī al-Narāqī · Jāmiʿ al-afkār wa-nāqid al-anẓār, vol. I, pp. 38–39

English · working translation

Before entering into its proof, we must explain why things need an efficient cause. We say: the correct position is that what accounts for a possible being's need for a cause is its possibility, in agreement with the majority of philosophers. A group of theologians has maintained that the explanation is temporal origination; others have proposed possibility and origination as two constituent factors, or have treated them as conditions. ... Once it is established that possibility explains the need, it follows that a possible being, while continuing to exist, still needs its cause. Possibility belongs necessarily to the essence of a possible being and does not depart from it while it persists; consequently its result—namely dependence—also obtains in that condition.

العربية · من النص المطبوع

وقبل الخوض في بيانه لا بد من بيان علة احتياج الأشياء إلى المؤثر. فنقول: الحق أن علة احتياج الممكن إلى المؤثر هو الإمكان، وفاقا لجمهور الحكماء. وذهب جماعة من المتكلمين إلى أن العلة فيه هو الحدوث؛ وقيل: الإمكان مع الحدوث شطرا؛ وقيل: شرطا. ... وإذ ثبت أن علة الحاجة هي الإمكان يلزم منه أن يكون الممكن الباقي حال بقائه أيضا محتاجا إلى المؤثر، لأن الإمكان لازم لماهية الممكن ذاتي لها لا ينفك عنها حال البقاء أيضا، فيوجد معلوله ـ أعني الاحتياج ـ أيضا في تلك الحال.

Check the Arabic source and locator

Translation note & evidence status

The ellipsis omits Narāqī's intermediate proofs, which distinguish possibility from temporal origination. 'Possibility' means metaphysical contingency, not statistical probability. This passage does not establish that a secondary cause is unreal or that continuous divine causation displaces horizontal causes.

Two non-contiguous excerpts; Arabic matched against pp. 38–39 of the text; longer argument untranslated.

Provisional theses — expressly editorial

1. Being and first principles

What is first in conception is not automatically a single independently existing object; a reconstruction must distinguish the meaning of existence from its instances and the precise role of quiddity.

2. Perpetual dependence

Narāqī's argument from possibility gives the persistence of contingent beings a causal explanation, not merely their origination. Whether this establishes the exact Thomist account of primary and secondary causes remains a question for argument, not an assumed equivalence.

3. Change requires identity

Lāhījī's argument makes the enduring subject of motion a central test of Ṣadrā's substantial-motion thesis. The competing Sadrian account of continuity through intensification remains to be translated and tested directly.

4. No premature monism

Accepting that existence is causally bestowed or metaphysically fundamental does not by itself prove that finite existents are merely manifestations of one absolute being. Multiple distinct senses of unity require separate demonstrations.

Critical method

For each question the working edition will preserve four separate layers: (1) the established base passage, (2) verbatim Arabic extracts from each relevant commentary with edition/page/manuscript locator, (3) a faithful English rendering with a lexical apparatus, and (4) an explicitly labelled comparative adjudication. A later thinker responding to a claim is not treated as the author of a new 'definitive' historic summa. Textual dependence, a reported position and demonstrated agreement are distinct evidence categories.
