# Late-commentator fascicle 08 — Lāhījī, *Sarmāya-i īmān*: preference, continual existence and the builder

**31 October 2026 · independent Persian theological work → new English translation**

**Source:** ʿAbd al-Razzāq al-Lāhījī, *Sarmāya-i īmān dar uṣūl-i iʿtiqādāt*, Persian text, [p.37](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/17348/37) and [p.38](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/17348/38); [p.39](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/17348/39) concludes a separate theological argument. This is **an independent work** from *Gawhar-i murād* and *Shawāriq al-ilhām*, not another copy of the same wording. The translation is focused on the contiguous two-page argument on preference, causality and conservation; the page-38 excerpt closes mid-proof at the p.39 transition.

## p.37 — *awlawiyya* cannot explain an actual contingent effect

> الشيء ما لم يجب لم يوجد

**Translation of the argument:** Suppose an existence is preferable to nonexistence, whether that preference derives from the possible being's essence or from another cause. The contingent thing still cannot exist until its cause renders its existence *necessary*. If, given the preference, nonexistence is no longer possible, the alleged preference is really necessity and should be named as such. If nonexistence remains possible, the actual occurrence of existence requires another determinant; otherwise an equally possible alternative occurs without sufficient explanation. Introducing a second, third, or infinitely many “preferences” does not help. Either the totality of these makes nonexistence impossible, in which case necessity has been reached, or nonexistence remains possible and the difficulty recurs. In the hypothetical-syllogistic formulation: if preference alone sufficed for occurrence, non-occurrence would be impossible; but non-occurrence is possible in the case of a contingent thing. Therefore preference alone does not suffice. **“A thing does not exist until it has become necessary.”**

## p.38 — why need persists as long as the contingent thing exists

> ممكن ... در بقاء وجود نيز محتاج باشد به علت

**Translation:** Once it is understood that the ground of dependence is contingency (*imkān*), not origination (*ḥudūth*), and that contingency is inseparable from a possible quiddity even after the thing exists, it follows that the contingent being needs a cause both at the beginning and throughout its continuance. Indeed existence flows into it afresh, instant after instant, from the cause: **persistence is nothing but continued existence**. At every instant in which existence occurs it must proceed from its cause. Without the true principle's giving existence, the whole world would fall away into complete nonbeing. 

**The builder objection and reply:** Some who make temporal origination the ground of need imagine a completed house or pot should disappear as soon as builder or potter dies, if continuing existence depended on a cause. But craftspeople are not the true existential efficient causes of houses and pots: they cause movements which **prepare** materials to receive form and existence from the bestower of forms and being. The absence of a preparatory cause does not imply the disappearance of the already constituted effect. Moreover, denying need during continuance damages the argument for the Necessary Existent: an allegedly beginningless contingent could terminate an explanatory chain without any cause.

## Apparatus and comparisons

1. **Important conceptual subtlety:** Lāhījī's imagery that existence is continually renewed “instant after instant” should not automatically be construed as Ṣadrā's **substantial motion**, or as Ashʿarite **re-creation of every accident**. In context the point is existential dependence; the mode of substantial persistence is a further question.
2. **Two works, one coherent doctrine:** this independent Persian argument agrees with [*Gawhar-i murād* Chapter 7](LATE_FASCICLE_02_LAHIJI_GAWHAR_TEMPORAL_CAUSATION.md) about contingency and continuance and [Chapter 6](LATE_FASCICLE_01_LAHIJI_GAWHAR_ACTIVE_INTELLECT.md) about a distinct bestower of forms. A historical author may formulate the same principle differently in a short creed and a long philosophical treatise.
3. **Comparison:** Avicenna [*Ilāhiyyāt* VI.1](PRIMARY_TRANSLATION_FASCICLE_E_AVICENNA_VI1.md) already states the argument; Lāhījī provides a Persian pedagogical exposition, not its first invention. Aquinas [*ST* I q.104](PRIMARY_TRANSLATION_FASCICLE_F_AQUINAS_ST104.md) likewise differentiates a builder's *fieri* agency and an existential conserving source, though his divine freedom doctrine diverges.
4. **Document state:** direct searchable printed text consulted; modern print transcription, no manuscript variants established; p.37–38 translated as a bounded passage, not a full edition of the creed.

**Result:** independent Persian corroboration from Lāhījī that preserving a contingent being is a **continuous act of dependence**, not something once brought about by a physical builder.
