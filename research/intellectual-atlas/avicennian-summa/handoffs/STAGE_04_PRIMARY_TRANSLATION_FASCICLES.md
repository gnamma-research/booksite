## 1. Genuine new translation achievements

### 0. Avicenna, *Ilāhiyyāt* VI.1

- **Full continuous chapter translation**, printed pp.257–263: four/five causes and the material subject; the form as constitutive and partially mediating the causal actualization of matter; metaphysical giver-of-being versus physical mover; “after nonexistence” as an accidental predicate rather than the proper object of efficient causality; the explicit proof that a contingent effect needs its existential cause **for as long as it exists**.
- This source demonstrates that continuing dependence is already present in Ibn Sīnā's own metaphysics and cannot be treated as Narāqī's late discovery.
- Base witness: [VI.1 start](https://lib.rafed.net/view.php?b_id=1128&page=257&type=c_fbook) and [continued chapter to p.263](https://lib.rafed.net/view.php?b_id=1128&page=264&type=c_fbook). No independent manuscript collation.

### A. Avicenna, *Ilāhiyyāt* VI.2

- Complete chapter movement: objection from father/builder/fire → essential versus preparatory causes → potentially unbounded succession of motion-conditioned causes → continuity of motion resolves mistaken simultaneous infinitude → existence through a perpetually accompanying cause → distinction between essential and temporal priority → terminology of *ibdāʿ* and *takwīn* → the natural agent and its powers.
- Important philosophical safeguards: an artisan does not necessarily confer the continuing being of a house; natural powers are nonetheless genuinely efficacious; a chain of successive preparatory states differs from an infinite simultaneous essentially ordered hierarchy. “Necessary through its complete cause” is compatible with being contingent in itself. “Creation” cannot silently conflate emanative *ibdāʿ* with a first temporal instant.
- Base: edited searchable Ibn Sīnā text [pp.263–267](https://lib.rafed.net/view.php?b_id=1128&page=264&type=c_fbook) with reported print variants; no new manuscripts collated.

### B. Avicenna, *Ilāhiyyāt* IX.5

- Complete chapter movement: why the celestial order terminates in generable/corruptible elements → shared matter and differentiated forms → separate intellect as giver in cooperation with celestial motions → preparatory *istiʿdād* and *takhṣīṣ* → fiery form succeeding watery form → matter constituted only with *some* form and superior principles → celestial influences → critique of a friction-based account of elemental origins → the direct phrase “receives the form from the Giver of Forms.”
- Critical result: the natural agent's true preparing of matter, the form's intrinsic constitution of the composite, and the separately bestowed form are three different claims. Avicenna does not describe inert matter or deny all natural agency; Aquinas nevertheless locates substantial generation differently. The translation contains corrections against confusing “this form” with “every form.”
- Base: edited searchable Ibn Sīnā text [pp.409–413](https://lib.rafed.net/view.php?b_id=1128&page=410&type=c_fbook), with selected reported variants. Not a collated *Avicenna Latinus* witness.

### C. Lāhījī, *Shawāriq* I:90–93

- Two complete consecutive issue units (23 and 24): from neutrality of intrinsic possibility to the dispute over *awlawiyya dhātiyya*, Taftāzānī's three meanings, a distinction between merely conceivable weak preference and actual sufficient preference, Lāhījī's necessity and causation replies, and the argument that extrinsic preponderance itself cannot complete the causal explanation unless it reaches necessity.
- Jurjānī and Dawwānī are translated **as embedded in Lāhījī's report**. They have not been independently recollated. The p.92 electronic transcription appears damaged in a negation critical to the argument, and the translation flags this rather than making a silent critical correction.
- Base: *Shawāriq*, printed vol I [pp.90](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/9309/90?lang=ar)–[93](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/9309/93?lang=ar).

### D. Aquinas, *Summa theologiae* I q.104 aa.1–2

- The complete article sequences on all creatures' need for divine conservation and whether that conservation is immediate have been freshly translated from the Latin; all four+three objections and their replies are included.
- Aquinas establishes that God remains the principal conserving cause while **created intermediaries can also conserve**, sometimes without initiating new changes. Aquinas and Avicenna therefore agree more extensively on a layered account of conservation than a caricature suggests.
- Latin source: [Corpus Thomisticum](https://www.corpusthomisticum.org/sth1103.html), §32757–32774.

## 2. Integrated philosophical consequences (not automatic author attributions)

1. **Complete cause** is not a synonym for prior event. Ibn Sīnā's differentiated essential, incidental and preparatory causes bear on Lāhījī's proof that insufficient preference does not account for actual occurrence.
2. **Continuing dependence** follows from the contingency of an effect even when an initiating generator has vanished, as Ibn Sīnā already argues in VI.1–2. Do not treat Narāqī as the originator.
3. **Vertical mediation** is itself differentiated: a separate intellect bestows form, celestial alterations prepare matter, proximate material agents bring about transformations, intrinsic forms constitute natural composites. “Vertical divine causality” does not settle the generation dispute with Aquinas.
4. **Matter is not an independent lump** awaiting form: IX.5 explicitly states that matter cannot exist without a form and that this *particular* form can replace a prior one.
5. **Conditional necessity** does not establish theological determinism by itself: distinguish an effect becoming necessary given its complete cause from the necessitation of the First's free willing or cosmic procession.
6. **The four causes crosswalk** must include a fifth explanatory coordinate: *hierarchical location* of an otherwise legitimately recognised efficient cause.
