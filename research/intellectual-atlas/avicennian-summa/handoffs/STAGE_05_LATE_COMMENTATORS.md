## 3. Five substantive historical findings

### 3.1 Lāhījī explicitly retains natural form-bestowal by the tenth Active Intellect

*Gawhar* II.3.6, pp.301–303 assigns **rational souls and the species forms of composites** to the tenth intellect while differentiating individual things by material dispositions. The same discussion says there is no need for a separately subsisting horizontal “lord of species” for every form: the tenth intellect suffices under many causal respects. This is a direct positive historical answer to an earlier project question, though it is not universal across all late thinkers.

### 3.2 A necessary distinction between mediation and independent creative efficacy

At *Gawhar* p.293, Lāhījī explicitly states that intermediaries **do not independently bestow existence**, but mediate its arrival from the true First Cause. Thus “the tenth intellect bestows forms” must be read within a **hierarchy of secondary/mediatory causality**. Distinguish (i) original existential efficient cause, (ii) the separate form-bestowing mediator, (iii) celestial/physical preparatory conditions, and (iv) the intrinsic substantial form constituting the composite.

### 3.3 Temporal specificity is not the same explanatory problem as existential dependence

*Gawhar* II.3.7 pp.304–306 differentiates existential/conserving cause (*ʿilla mubqiya*) from a time-specifying originating cause (*ʿilla muḥditha*), assigning gradual matter/subject preparation and classical celestial motion to the latter. *Sarmāya-i īmān* independently affirms continuous dependence; the builder is not a self-sufficient existential cause. Compare Ibn Sīnā *Ilāhiyyāt* VI.1–2, IX.5, and Aquinas *ST* I q.104 with precision.

### 3.4 A plural late Persian debate over existence, rather than a uniform “essence-only” tradition

Narāqī's *Lamaʿāt* pp.72,82 explicitly treats particular existence as what is made and quiddity as **conceptually distinct but externally identical** to a finite actuality whose limitations ground essential abstraction. This is not a mere synonym for full Ṣadrian doctrine; nor is it equivalent without proof to Aquinas' real essence–esse composition. Lāhījī's *Shawāriq* likewise refuses the simplistic claim that only essences are produced. A complete historical summary must track each author's position separately.

### 3.5 The Indian and post-*Tajrīd* scholastic network contains live argumentative disagreements

Tahānawī explicitly names Mīr Zāhid on *jaʿl*, preserving a systematic Indian reception of the debate. Astarābādī's later compilation cites Qūshjī, Khafrī and **Āqā Jamāl** al-Khwānsārī over whether necessity through will can secure genuine choice. This is not merely the repetition of Avicenna's formula that a contingent effect becomes necessary through its complete cause. Different commentators contest how the modal respect of agency relates to divine **will**, **time**, and alternate possibilities.

## 4. Source and attribution corrections

- **Āqā Jamāl al-Khwānsārī** in Astarābādī's reported controversy must not be confused with his father **Āqā Ḥusayn al-Khwānsārī**, who wrote separate Avicennian glosses.
- The title **al-Sayyid al-Sanad** generally denotes **al-Sayyid al-Sharīf al-Jurjānī**, not Ṣadr al-Dīn al-Dashtakī; maintain the correction from the separate Tajrīd witness project.
- The two reported Mīr Zāhid Indian printed editions (Delhi 1874, Lucknow 1876) and public-domain **1856 scan** are distinct bibliographical witnesses; neither can be silently converted into a collated primary text merely by reading Tahānawī.
- The digitally interleaved Siyālkūtī/Ḥalabī marginal lines are unattributed at lemma level; the text is a composite physical edition, **not** an authenticated standalone Siyālkūtī work.
- A source may be critical of Ṣadrā without rejecting all of his ontology, and a printing titled *Ithbāt al-ḥaraka al-jawhariyya* does not by itself reveal the true argument or attribution of Jilwa's alleged treatise.

## 5. Important leads that remain uncollated

1. **Mīr Zāhid:** [public-domain 1856 scan (106 pages)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Hashiya_Ala_Sharah_Al_Muwafiq_Umoor_E_Aamma_(IA_in.ernet.dli.2015.324001).pdf); [1874 Delhi edition](https://kutuphane.osmanlica.com/kitap/5250986); [1876 Lucknow edition](https://search.worldcat.org/title/alhashyh-lmyr-zahd-ala-shrh-almwaqf/oclc/978479874). Exact `jaʿl` passages need transcription and line-to-image verification.
2. **Indian successor:** Muḥammad ʿAbd al-Ḥaqq al-Khayrābādī, [*Sharḥ al-ḥāshiya al-Zāhidiyya* (1879–80)](https://dar.bibalex.org/en/BookDetail/BookDetail.aspx?keyword=DAF-Job%3A166828&queryParam=id). This is **not** identical to Faḍl-i Ḥaqq's separate physics work.
3. **Faḍl-i Ḥaqq al-Khayrābādī:** [*al-Hadiyya al-saʿīdiyya* Cairo 1904](https://ddl.ae/book/95961) exists as a scan, but the Arabic physical-philosophy pages needed for form-generation, active intellect and motion have not been reliably extracted. No fabricated translation is included.
4. **Qūshjī**, *Sharḥ al-Tajrīd* pp.310–311; **al-Khafrī** marginal manuscript fol.5; separate original Khwānsārī gloss and Āqā Jamāl's own note are not yet compared with the Astarābādī report.
5. **Khwānsārī–Sabzawārī** competing Shifāʾ glosses and later counterglosses remain important; the accessible bibliography is not a substitute for their original arguments.
6. **Jilwa** Shifāʾ and *Ishārāt* marginalia and the motion treatise remain witness-acquisition targets. Explicitly mark doctrines unknown until direct reading.
7. **Dawwānī–Dashtakī, al-Qūshjī, al-Jurjānī, al-Lāhījī, al-Siyālkūtī** require independently tracked gloss lines and manuscript versions for a truly exhaustive *Tajrīd* stemma.

## 6. What is complete, what is not, and why

**Finished in Stage 05:** 11 new source-identified edition fascicles; 8 distinct directly accessed works across Persian and Arabic; an integrated searchable/printable HTML edition; page-precise source URLs; stated translation limits and witness uncertainty; a machine-readable witness register with 11 unresolved acquisition/mediation records; preservation of earlier six primary fascicles and ten thematic chapters.

**Still open:** exhaustive translation of every page of these works; manuscript comparison and full apparatus; physical-source access for Khayrābādī, Mīr Zāhid, Khwānsārī and Jilwa; independently sourced Qūshjī and al-Khafrī objections; full ethics, psychology, providence and afterlife coverage by each author; publication deploy/browser verification.

**Do not turn these 11 fascicles into a claim of eleven independently collated manuscripts.** Three chapters of one Persian work and two excerpts of one Arabic work count as multiple **translation units**, not independent witnesses.

It is **not a direct collation of the 1904 printing**, is not included among the eleven direct-access source fascicles, and does **not** settle whether Khayrābādī changed the ontology of the Giver of Forms. The appendix provides page locations, original short Arabic anchors and a fresh English rendering with interpretive cautions.
