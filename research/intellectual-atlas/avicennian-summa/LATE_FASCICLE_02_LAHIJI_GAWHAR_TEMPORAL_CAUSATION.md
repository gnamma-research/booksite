# Late-commentator fascicle 02 — Lāhījī, *Gawhar-i murād*, II.3.7: how temporal events depend on an eternal cause

**31 October 2026 · Persian primary source → new working English translation**

**Work**: ʿAbd al-Razzāq al-Lāhījī, *Gawhar-i murād*, Article II, Part 3, **Chapter 7**, “On the cause of the origination of events and the connection of temporal events with the eternal.” **Witness**: Nashr-i Sāya Persian text pp. **304–306**: [304](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/17438/304?lang=ar), [305](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/17438/305?lang=ar), [306](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/17438/306?lang=ar). Printed notes record variant sigla A, B, C but no manuscript images have been collated here.

## p.304 — two different grounds of causal dependence

> سبب حاجت ممكن به علت ، امكان است ... نه حدوث

**Translation:** “The cause of the contingent being's need for a cause is its contingency, not its temporal origination.”

As already established, the ground of a contingent being's need for a cause is possibility—this is the doctrine of most philosophers and the critical investigators among theologians—not temporal beginning, as another group of theologians maintains. “Cause” in this first claim means what **bestows existence** and makes existence prevail over nonexistence. In truth, the character of possibility is the ground of that dependence, whether the possible being begins in time or not. By a “temporally originated thing” here we mean something whose being is preceded by a time in which it did not exist, as in everyday events. By an “eternal” possible thing we mean something not so preceded, even if its being follows absolute nonbeing in an order other than time, as in the traditional example of the celestial sphere. This pair of terms must not be confused with the earlier discussion of origination and eternity of the world: what is “eternal” in the present temporal sense can nonetheless be originated in the earlier essential sense. All possible things are originated in that latter sense, but only some in the present temporal sense. Hence all possible things, temporal or not, need the being-giving cause equally. A temporal event has an **additional** need.

## p.305 — *mubqiya* and *muḥditha*; why motion matters

Its additional need is for a cause of origination (*ʿilla muḥditha*), meaning what accounts for the fact that the existence-bestowing cause favors its existence at this particular segment of time, rather than an earlier segment. Otherwise this time would be selected without a selector, just as without a cause bestowing existence, being rather than nonbeing would prevail without a determinant. A possible being with no preceding time needs no such additional cause.

Scholars therefore call what makes existence itself prevail the **conserving cause** (*ʿilla mubqiya*), since the contingent thing needs it throughout its continuance, and what determines the specific time the **originating cause** (*ʿilla muḥditha*), needed only for the event's temporal inception. The temporally determining cause must itself become operative at the event's time; otherwise the effect would fail to accompany its complete cause. Repeating the question for every new cause threatens an infinite regress of newly arisen causes. There must therefore be something that is new in one respect yet stable in another: **motion**, which is stable with respect to its own nature but constantly renewed in relation to the boundaries of its course. Here Lāhījī's model presupposes a motion without a temporal beginning in this specific sense.

**Printed variants**: B omits `چون` at p.305 n.1, B,C omit `گويند` n.2, A has `نماند` versus `باقى نمانده` n.3. These do not alter the explicit distinction between conserving and time-specifying causes.

## p.306 — the gradual acquisition of preparedness

In this cosmological account the required continuous motion is celestial motion. It is by that motion that a temporally originated thing can be the effect of the eternal Necessary Existent without the effect being delayed after a completed sufficient cause; thus temporal things are connected with the eternal. Celestial movement specifies the time of an event when the event has a **matter or underlying subject** whose preparedness for it grows gradually through celestial movements and configurations. When that matter or subject has become fully ready, the event's existence necessarily flows to it from the **giver of existence**. Complete preparedness is thus the proximate cause determining the *time* of its occurrence, while celestial motions and configurations are remoter causes. Accordingly, a temporal event is preceded both by motions/configurations and by matter or subject. This explains the philosophers' maxim that every originated thing is preceded by **matter and duration**. “Duration” means time measured by celestial motion; “matter” is used broadly, embracing both prime matter (*hayūlā*) and an underlying subject of accidents.

**Printed variants**: A omits `به` (p.306 n.1), reads `حادثى` for `حادث` (n.2), `استعدادى` for `استعداد` (n.3); B `موضوعى` for `موضوع` (n.4), `تخصيص` for `تخصّص` (n.5), and omits `از مادّه به معنى` (n.6). The n.5 change makes the proximate cause's act of **selecting** the event's time more explicit; either reading preserves the distinction.

## Philosophical apparatus

1. **Three causal tasks must be distinguished:** (i) giving a possible thing existence, (ii) conserving it while it exists, and (iii) specifying the exact moment when a *temporally beginning* event comes to be. Lāhījī's p.305 *mubqiya/muḥditha* pair refers to (i)/(ii) versus (iii), **not** to a denial of real natural effects.
2. **Crucial relation to Avicenna:** compare [*Ilāhiyyāt* VI.1–2](PRIMARY_TRANSLATION_FASCICLE_E_AVICENNA_VI1.md) and [VI.2](PRIMARY_TRANSLATION_FASCICLE_B_AVICENNA_VI2.md). Lāhījī adopts a structured Avicennian resolution of temporal novelties under an eternal source; he does not simply replace the natural world with instantaneous creation.
3. **Link to form-bestowal:** [*Gawhar*, Chapter 6](LATE_FASCICLE_01_LAHIJI_GAWHAR_ACTIVE_INTELLECT.md) explicitly assigns natural forms to the tenth intellect. Chapter 7 specifies how a new form appears at **this time**: material preparedness is completed and an existential gift arrives. The combined account is strikingly close in structure to Ibn Sīnā *Ilāhiyyāt* IX.5.
4. **Historical limits:** the celestial mechanics belongs to premodern cosmology and is not empirically vindicated by translation; the metaphysical distinctions of temporal determination, existential dependence and causal priority can be analysed independently.
5. **Source boundary:** this is a continuous, short Chapter 7 as printed on pp.304–306, translated with selected reported print variants; it is **not** an edition of the entire Persian work or an original manuscript collation.

**Result:** Direct Persian evidence for a mature later Avicennian account of the **relation of temporal effects to eternal causes**, with a precise distinction between existential and temporal causal explanation.
