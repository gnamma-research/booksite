## 1. What the author is answering

**Earlier lemma (pp. 290–293):** From the absolutely One no more than one effect proceeds directly in the same respect. The first effect is an intellect; plural **respects of this first effect** permit mediated diversity without an extra simultaneous effect coming directly from the First. Matter, form and the sublunary world occur at later stages. Lāhījī distinguishes a **mediate causal channel** from the one true giver of existence.

**Problem carried into pp. 294–297:** Is the proposed procession identifiable with Islamic descriptions of the first creation? Must the chain contain precisely ten separate intellects? Can the limited respects in the early intellects explain the immense numerical multiplicity of fixed stars? The last is an objection attributed to **Shaykh al-Ishrāq (Suhrawardī)**. The conclusion on p.297 is a reply to that objection, *not* a freestanding assertion about the number of stars.

### [p. 294] The First Intellect as intellect, spirit and pen

In certain traditions the first created reality is named “my intellect,” and in others “my spirit”; other reports describe the Pen as the first of created things. Lāhījī argues that these varying expressions need not contradict one another if they all allude to the First Intellect. He also recalls his earlier account of the Prophet's existential rank as related, in the returning series of being, to the First Intellect in the descending series. The identification is explicitly an **interpretation of reported traditions**, not a further physical demonstration.

The same principle supplies an analogy for the Pen: the First Intellect mediates the effusion of knowledges and perfections into lower receptivities as a pen impresses characters on a tablet. An external receptive field and the plane of *nafs al-amr* can, in that analogy, be called the clear book, while levels of simple and composite existents are the script inscribed through intelligible mediation. The Divine Power is the ultimate agency: a suitable metaphorical “pen” for it is a separate intellect. Lāhījī also mentions an interpretation of Qurʾān 68:1 involving intelligences or immaterial angels and introduces Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī's explanation from the *Tafsīr al-kabīr*. **These reports belong to the exegetical subsection and must not be treated as identical to the earlier demonstrative premise.**

*Original-language control:* «و نيز چون عقل اوّل واسطهء فيضان علوم و كمالات است». [Source p.294](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/17438/294?lang=ar).

### [p. 295] The number ten is an explanatory lower bound, not an impossibility proof against eleven

Continuing the report attributed to Rāzī, Lāhījī notes traditions identifying the first creature as an intellect, a pen, or a certain simple substance, and construes their subject as a single underlying referent. He then makes a qualification indispensable to an accurate account of later Avicennism: **the philosophers do not profess to have demonstrated that more than ten separate intellects are impossible**. The standard count is advanced because the arguments discussed have reached that number.

Accordingly there may be additional vertically ordered intellects between the Necessary Existent and the intellect responsible for the first celestial sphere. Avicenna's *Ishārāt* speaks **indefinitely** of “an intellectual substance” from which another intellectual substance and a heavenly body follow, not of a uniquely demonstrated numerical first intellect directly producing a uniquely demonstrated first sphere. Lāhījī then reports **Ṭūsī's commentary** as explicitly treating that formulation as noncommittal on whether the very first intellect is the direct principle of the first sphere: there is no demonstration fixing that identity.

On an older cosmological identification of the first sphere with the sphere of the fixed stars, its actual diversity might exceed the explanatory aspects of the absolutely first intellect. The cause of that sphere might therefore be a **later** intellect. Lāhījī attributes this caution to Ṭūsī and draws the consequence that the second intellect could potentially be the source of the fixed stars.

*Original-language control:* «قول به تعيين عدد عشره در عقول مجرّده ، مجزوم به حكما نيست». [Source p.295](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/17438/295?lang=ar).

### [p. 296] Reply to the fixed-star plurality objection: twelve possible effects

Lāhījī now develops the response to the objection that the early intellects lack enough distinct aspects to explain the fixed stars. Take the absolutely One as **A** and its sole immediate effect as **B**. At the second causal level we can have **C** proceeding from A through B, as well as **D** from B itself. Thus already two effects can coexist at the second level without violating the rule that only one immediate effect proceeds from A. If we also allow the lower principle B to operate in a more complicated mediation involving the higher source, the range of possibilities increases.

Retaining only C and D at the second level, twelve effects are distinguishable at the third level through different ordered or combined channels: **six** grounded in A by means of C, D, C+D, B+C, B+D, and B+C+D; **three** grounded in B through C, D, or C+D; and **three** proceeding from C alone, D alone, or C and D together. This enumeration explains why multiple derivative respects and mediating configurations may yield far more effects than the very small number of immediately produced separate intellects would suggest.

If further orders of mediation—such as the operation of a lower source through a higher source—are admitted, the range is greater still. Thus the numerical objection cannot simply infer that the first intellect needs one intrinsically distinct aspect for every observed star. **This is a combinatorial possibility argument, not a newly verified celestial count or a proof that all listed channels actually occur.**

*Original-language control:* «در مرتبهء سوّم دوازده معلول متحقق تواند شد». [Source p.296](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/17438/296?lang=ar).

### [p. 297] Conclusion: the objection of Shaykh al-Ishrāq

Even the simplified third-level enumeration can be augmented by taking into account additional causal respects in the first intellect and differing orderings of intermediaries. On those suppositions the numerical possibilities become too numerous to list exhaustively. Lāhījī therefore concludes that the criticism attributed to **Shaykh al-Ishrāq**, that the second intellect has insufficient plurality to account for the many fixed stars, is answered.

This is the **end of Section IV**. The following heading on p.298 introduces Section V concerning **elemental forms and mixtures**. The two sections are logically connected: IV answers how mediated multiplicity is possible; V addresses how sublunary matter takes different forms. Neither is an independent assertion about substantial generation.

*Original-language control:* «پس مندفع شد تشنيع شيخ اشراق بر مشّائين». [Source p.297](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/17438/297?lang=ar).

## 2. Textual and attribution apparatus

- **Boundaries:** Section IV is pp.290–297. This continuation renders pp.294–297; the existing prior fascicle renders pp.290–293. A split file must not be misrepresented as a separate fourth/fifth chapter.
- **Voices:** Lāhījī is the narrator; *reported ḥadīth* and a passage attributed to **Rāzī's *Tafsīr*** provide interpretive correspondence; **Ṭūsī** supplies the quoted reading of Avicenna's *Ishārāt*; **Suhrawardī** is the opponent whose plurality objection is answered. The report of any earlier author here is **not** an independent collation of that author's own book.
- **Printed textual differences:** p.294 marks omissions of «عقل» and «صيغه» in selected lettered witnesses; p.295 reports «اعظم» for «اول» in the sphere discussion; p.296 records «معلول» instead of a phrase identifying a second effect; p.297 reports «ترتب» for «ترتيب». These are **editor-reported readings**, not variants established by this project.
- **Critical logical distinction:** “Not demonstrated that ten must be the maximum” is **not** the thesis that the series is actually infinite. The twelve-effect illustration demonstrates explanatory *possibility* rather than an observed count.
