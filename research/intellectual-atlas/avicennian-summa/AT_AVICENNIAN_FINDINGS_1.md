# Mature Avicennism and Aristotelian–Thomism: findings and unresolved textual tests
**GNAMMA Summa, synthesis 1.0 · 30 October 2026**

## Executive finding

There is **substantial agreement about the four causes, intrinsic substantial forms, and the need for a continuing source of contingent existence**, but a potentially decisive disagreement about **which efficient cause generates a new substantial composite**. Classical Ibn Sīnā assigns the *bestowal* of sublunary forms to the last separate intellect, while natural agents bring matter into the relevant disposition. Aquinas insists that natural agents genuinely produce the **composite** through the eduction of form from matter's potency. These are not mere verbal alternatives. Yet it would also be wrong to call Avicennian natural causes inert or to identify the bestowal of form with the denial of intrinsic form.

### Strong evidence for the classical baseline

- Olga Lizzini, [*Ibn Sīnā's Metaphysics*, SEP, §6.2](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ibn-sina-metaphysics/): precise primary locators *Ilāhiyyāt* IX.4–5, including IX.5, pp. 411 and 413; the Giver of Forms/Agent Intellect and the preparation of matter through natural processes.
- Jon McGinnis, [*Ibn Sīnā's Natural Philosophy*, SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ibn-sina-natural/): matter, corporeal form, and potency; prime matter is not a fully constituted passive subject without corporeal form.
- Peter Adamson, [“Natural, Artificial, and Organic Forms in Avicenna” (2023)](https://academic.oup.com/book/46780/chapter-abstract/413395731): different natural, artificial and organic forms and their causal accounts. **Do not** extrapolate from a house, fire, or seed to every form without this differentiation.
- Boris Hennig, [“Avicenna's Agent Intellect as a Completing Cause”](https://www.borishennig.de/texte/2023/ai.pdf): a recent argument for understanding the Agent Intellect as a completion of natural/formative processes. Interpretive secondary study, not a new Avicennian primary text.
- Aquinas, [*De principiis naturae*, ch. 3](https://scholathomistica.com/works/de-principiis-naturae?lang=en): natural matter/form/privation are not sufficient without efficient and final causes.
- Aquinas, *De potentia* q.3 a.8, as cited in William E. Carroll, [“Aquinas on Creation and the Metaphysical Foundations of Science”](https://repositorio.uca.edu.ar/bitstream/123456789/12668/1/aquinas-on-creation.pdf): the **composite** is produced *per se*, not an independently subsisting form; forms are educed from matter's potency.

## Four causes: an exact comparison

| Level | Ibn Sīnā | Aquinas | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Matter | Receptive underlying potency, constituted with corporeal form | Potency toward substantial actuality | Strong structural agreement; fine-grained disagreement possible |
| Form | Intrinsic principle of the natural composite | Intrinsic principle of the natural composite | Substantial agreement |
| Natural efficient agent | Produces dispositional changes preparing matter | Causes generation of the composite by actualising matter's potency | **Real dispute about proximate efficient causality** |
| Separate intellect | Giver of substantial forms in classical sublunary explanation | Not the universal bestower of forms in natural generation | **Real dispute about mediation** |
| End | Explains the natural process and the order of perfections | Final cause directs natural agency | Strong structural agreement; cosmic teleology differs |
| Primary existential cause | Contingent effects remain dependent upon ultimate source through cosmic hierarchy | God immediately sustains the being and operation of creatures | Agreement on dependence, possible disagreement on mode of mediation |

**Critical qualification:** “Natural agents only prepare” means their causal contribution is **preparatory with respect to the substantial form's ultimate bestowal**, not that they lack all real efficacy. Conversely, “Aquinas' natural agent produces form” must be formulated carefully: Aquinas says the **composite**, not a form treated as a freestanding thing, is what is generated.

## The mature tradition: what changed and what is demonstrable

**1. Ṣadrā's change is genuine and profound.** The primacy and gradation of existence and substantial motion revise the explanatory basis of substance and generation. A static subject undergoing merely accidental change is no longer the universal model of natural persistence. Source: [SEP, Mullā Ṣadrā](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/mulla-sadra/) and [Ibrahim Kalin, “Between Physics and Metaphysics”](https://jis.cis-ca.org/jis/article/view/16). This does **not** establish that all later Avicennian philosophers accepted Ṣadrā's theory.

**2. Lāhījī represents a significant counterargument, not mere repetition.** The surviving working translations of *Shawāriq* I:53 articulate an objection to substantial motion from the persistence of the subject; I:159 distinguishes **particular existence as what is produced** from the general concept of existence. That complicates the idea that Peripatetics defend “only quiddity is produced.” The larger objections/replies must still be translated before final attribution.

**3. Narāqī tightens the argument for continuing dependence.** The translated *Jāmiʿ al-afkār* I:38–42 explicitly distinguishes *imkān* from *ḥudūth*, the explanatory from the temporal order, and the builder's preparatory agency from an existential cause. It does not establish that Narāqī abolished the separate Giver of Forms in natural philosophy.

**4. Unity of existence requires three distinct propositions.** A shared **concept** of existence, gradation of existence, and the strong thesis that finite existents are only manifestations of one reality are not logically equivalent. One can accept some Sadrian ontological arguments while rejecting stronger monistic conclusions. Source: [SEP, Arabic and Islamic Philosophy of Religion](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/arabic-islamic-religion/) and [Rizvi, “An Avicennian Engagement…”](https://doi.org/10.1163/18778372-04801008). The exact extent of Narāqī's acceptance and rejection remains a primary-text question.

**5. The nineteenth-century continuation is not a single system.** Jilwa's annotations and Khayrābādī's *al-Hadiyya* show later Peripatetic engagement, but no source presently verified here establishes a uniform revised doctrine of natural form-production across both. The [1904 Khayrābādī printing](https://ddl.ae/book/95961) is an acquisition lead, not an inspected or collated text.

## What a coherent AT–Avicennian synthesis can and cannot say

**Defensible synthesis:** The four causes explain a natural composite at different levels; the composite has an intrinsic form, natural agents exercise genuine causal powers, and the contingent being's actuality remains ultimately dependent. This is a coherent *framework for comparison*.

**Not yet a historical agreement:** Ibn Sīnā's separate Active Intellect and Aquinas' natural eduction of form are not automatically equivalent. Nor is Avicenna's necessary emanation simply Aquinas' freely willed creation.

**Promising reformulation:** The Giver of Forms may be interpreted as a *completing* cause that explains the actuality of a properly prepared composite without nullifying the natural preparation. This narrows the conflict, but Aquinas' critique of an extrinsic *dator formarum* still bites if the natural generator is denied a true role in generating the composite. The reformulation is a philosophical proposal, not evidence that Jilwa or Narāqī adopted it.

**Important exception:** Rational soul formation is not an ordinary case of matter's production of a material substantial form in either system; it needs its own dossier.

## Edition status and integrity

This is a **finished analytical synthesis of the evidence presently established**, not a completed Arabic critical edition. It includes no invented historical quotations. The late Peripatetic primary-text question — **did any named later author abandon or significantly modify the Giver of Forms?** — is **unresolved** until the relevant natural-philosophical texts are transcribed and translated. Do not call it resolved merely because the research programme has been written.


## Synthesis 1.1 — integrated philosophical decision tree (30 October 2026)

The twenty-domain project should be read as a connected explanatory system, not twenty independent essays. The following dependencies clarify what counts as a change to Avicennism.

1. **Ontology → causality.** First distinguish the shared *concept* of existence from its extra-mental instantiation, from gradation in reality, and from a strong unity-of-being thesis. None entails the next without additional premises. Only then ask what precisely is made (*majʿūl*) by an efficient cause: a quiddity, a particular existence, or the composite. Lāhījī's distinction at *Shawāriq* I:159 makes a simple “Peripatetics produce essences, Ṣadrā produces existence” contrast untenable without qualification.
2. **Dependence → generation.** The proof that a contingent existent requires an existential cause while it exists does not settle whether a natural agent generates a substantial composite. Narāqī's distinction between *imkān* and *ḥudūth* establishes the first kind of dependence, not a resolution of the second. Thus Avicenna and Aquinas may converge on sustained dependence yet disagree on the division of labour between proximate natural agent and separate intellect.
3. **Natural form → substantial motion.** If substantial form is intrinsic to the composite, this does not by itself determine whether substance undergoes continuous essential renewal. Ṣadrā's substantial motion alters the persistence model; Lāhījī's subject-persistence objection targets this further claim, not necessarily intrinsic form as such.
4. **Cosmological mediation → divine action.** Separate intellects, the Active Intellect, and the Necessary Existent belong to distinguishable explanatory levels. To say that God is always causally operative does not establish that Avicenna means immediate divine concurrence in the same sense as Aquinas. Their accounts also differ over the necessity or freedom of cosmic procession.
5. **Epistemology → metaphysics.** Mental existence, abstraction, *nafs al-amr*, and the status of universals should be compared with the external-existence theses without treating logical universality as an independently existing universal thing. This is the bridge to the Tajrīd, Mawāqif and post-Rāzian logical traditions.
6. **Relations → divine and created order.** A relation may be conceptually described, extra-mentally founded, or construed as a distinct real accident; the precise options must be source-collated before comparing with Thomist real relations or proposing an ontology of social structures.
7. **Akbarian integration → non-equivalence.** A single common concept of *wujūd*, Ṣadrian graded existence, and Akbarian manifestation are three separate theses. A commentator's use of *tajallī* or *ẓuhūr* is insufficient by itself to prove acceptance of the strongest unity thesis.

### Author-by-author evidence discipline

| Author or corpus | Established interpretive contribution | Still requires direct collation |
|---|---|---|
| Ibn Sīnā | Intrinsic form, natural preparation, separate bestowal, continuing contingent dependence | Generation/corruption passages against *Ilāhiyyāt* IX.4–5 |
| Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī and Ṭūsī | Major argumentative interlocutors in post-Avicennian metaphysics | Exact objections and responses on matter, form, causal efficacy |
| Ṣadrā | Primacy/gradation of existence and substantial motion | Specific form-bestowal passages and treatment of intellects |
| Lāhījī | Objection to substantial motion; particular existence as made | Full *Shawāriq* objection-and-response context |
| Narāqī | Possibility, not temporal origination, grounds continuing existential dependence | Whether he revises the Active Intellect's natural role |
| Khwānsārī, Jilwa, Khayrābādī | Historically relevant later continuations | No definitive doctrine on form-bestowal can yet be assigned |
| Aquinas | Eduction of form through natural generation of composite; divine conservation | Exact Latin loci and distinctions between creation and generation |

### Publication and completion criterion

Each of the twenty domains must eventually include (i) a question and conceptual distinctions, (ii) primary passage with edition/page or folio, (iii) checked transcription and translation, (iv) objections and replies across named authors, (v) comparison with Aquinas where applicable, (vi) evidence-based verdict with confidence level, and (vii) cross-links. The current synthesis meets the analytical integration criterion but **does not** meet the full primary-text collation criterion. Never mark those twenty dossiers as source-complete without that work.
