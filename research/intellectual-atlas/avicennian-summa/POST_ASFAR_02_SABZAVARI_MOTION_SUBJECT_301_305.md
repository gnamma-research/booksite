# After the *Asfār* — Sabzavārī and the problem of a subject for substantial motion

**Layered working commentary dossier, 1 November 2026 — not a full original critical edition**

**Location:** Mullā Hādī Sabzavārī (d. 1873), *Sharḥ al-Manẓūma / Ghurar al-farāʾid*, **the part on natural philosophy** (*al-maqṣad al-rābiʿ fī l-ṭabīʿiyyāt*), **section / ghurar no. 111**: «غرر في تعيين موضوع هذه الحركة ودفع ما قالوا من عدم بقاء الموضوع» (on determining the subject of substantial motion and answering those who deny that the subject persists), in the **five-volume printed edition annotated by Ḥasan Ḥasanzāda Āmulī**, volume IV, pp. **301–305**.

**Directly inspected online printed pages:** [301](https://najafdesertlibrary.com/book/شرح-المنظومة/v/4/p/301), [302](https://najafdesertlibrary.com/book/شرح-المنظومة/v/4/p/302), [305](https://najafdesertlibrary.com/book/شرح-المنظومة/v/4/p/305). **Important incompleteness:** the edition's intervening pp. **303–304 have not been independently accessed in this pass**, so the passage below is **not** represented as a continuous translation of the entire section. The physically printed edition **interleaves Sabzavārī's verses/authorial commentary and Ḥasanzāda's much later annotations**. Exact attribution at every printed sentence is therefore **provisional until the page image and a different edition are compared**.

## 1. Which question is this section answering?

The **immediately preceding** ghurar no. 110 asks **how many Aristotelian categories admit motion**. Earlier sections no. 94–103 analyse body, prime matter, substantial form and their relations; nos. 104–109 introduce definitions and kinds of motion.

The question now is an explicit objection to **Ṣadrā's substantial motion**: **Every motion supposedly requires a persisting subject distinct from the change. But if substance itself is continually changing, where is that persisting subject?** The Peripatetic denial of motion in substance depends on this difficulty and on an assumption about the sudden coming-to-be or corruption of a substantial form.

This is substantially **more specific** than the general Tajrīd sourcebook's Questions 17–18 (matter, form, potency, movement). Tajrīd remains the baseline; this dossier treats a **nineteenth-century response** to the inherited objections and directs the reader back to Sabzavārī's own natural-philosophy sequence.

## 2. Verses at the opening of no. 111 — p. 301

> حلت بصورة مع الهيولى ... موضوعها بشخصه له البقا ... مبقيه اعلمنه المفارقا

**Working sense, with caution about punctuation in the digital verse:** “The motion is posited in a form together with matter, resembling growth and decline; its subject is preserved in its numerical individuality, and what preserves it must be recognised as a separate cause.” The following verses appeal to persistence in a gradually continuous reality, a flowing form with successive phases, and the apparent identity of an aged person with the youth and child from whom he developed.

The transmitted online metrical text contains compressed or potentially corrupted spacing; a **line-by-line definitive translation cannot be certified from this HTML alone**. It is included here to establish the *authorial problem* of the section rather than to manufacture perfectly restored poetry.

## 3. The substantial subject as the existence of a flowing nature — p. 302

> اعلم أن موضوع الحركة في الجوهرية هو وجود الطبيعة الجوهرية، وحيث إن وجود تلك الطبيعة جوهر سيال فموضوع الحركة الجوهرية نفس الحركة الجوهرية، ففي الحركة الجوهرية لا حاجة إلى موضوع خارج عن الحركة، فالجوهر الطبيعي حركة ومتحركة في نفسه.

**Translation:** “Know that the subject of substantial motion is the existence of the substantial nature. Since the existence of that nature is a **flowing substance**, the subject of substantial motion is itself the substantial motion. There is therefore no need for a subject **external to the motion**: natural substance is motion and is moved in its own reality.”

**The targeted older argument:** The passage contrasts this account with the Peripatetics, who admit motion only in accidents and require an enduring subject external to the accidental change. Projecting that same requirement onto the substance, they demand a distinct, unchanged subject for it, and so deny substantial motion.

## 4. The explicit Avicenna passage to which the text replies — p. 302

The printed edition cites **Ibn Sīnā, *al-Shifāʾ*, Physics, Book II, Chapter 3** on the categories in which motion occurs. This is not simply a mention of “movement”; it introduces a two-branch argument that the later doctrine must answer.

> أما الجوهر فإن قولنا: إن فيه حركة هو قول مجازي فإن هذه المقولة لا تعرض فيها الحركة ...

**Working English, first premise:** “As for substance, when we say that there is motion in it, that expression is metaphorical. Motion does not apply in this category.” Avicenna's stated reason is that when a substantial nature is corrupted, it is corrupted **all at once**, and when produced, it comes about **all at once**, without an intermediate perfection between pure potency and completed substantial actuality.

He then challenges the proposal that substantial form gradually intensifies. Two alternatives are considered:

1. **The original specific nature remains the same during intensification.** Then what intensifies is an accident of that nature, not its substantial form. This is **alteration**, not the production of a new substance.
2. **The original specific nature does not remain.** Then the intensification brings about a new substance at each supposed stage; the prior substantial form is lost. The result seems to require a potentially limitless plurality of intermediate substantial species, incompatible with the intended account of a single persistent specific nature.

**Conclusion of the Avicennian passage:** substantial forms therefore come and go at once; continuous motion has a mediating actuality between potency and act, which this kind of substantial generation does not possess.

This quotation is the real **objection** targeted by the later proof, not an uncontextualized slogan that Avicenna “denied change.”

## 5. The later reply and its limits — printed pp. 302 and 305

The later commentary replies by rejecting the assumption that the subject of a substantial motion must be **another stable substantial form behind the flow**. If the actual existence of a natural substantial nature is flowing, the motion is not an accidental state inhering in a numerically distinct stable thing: **the very existing nature is in successive actualisation**.

It is therefore crucial to distinguish:

- the **subject** in the categorial logic of *accidental movement*;
- a material receptive condition present through **substantial generation**; and
- the **individuated flowing existence** of a substantial nature in the post-Ṣadrian account.

A further printed discussion on **p.305** distinguishes the natural universal “form as such” from the particular individual form which is always in some concrete existence. It argues that successive formal determinations may be received in matter continuously while that matter depends on some constitutive form, as in an ordinary account of generation and corruption. It then asks:

> إن قلت: الشيء ما لم يتشخص لم يوجد ... فكيف تكون صورة ما علة لوجود الهيولى؟

**Translation:** “If you object: ‘A thing has no being before it is individuated … how can *some form* [in an indeterminate sense] be a cause of prime matter's existence?’”

The reply distinguishes the actual numerically one transcendent causal principle from the general consideration of formal nature. It cautions that the general description “form as such” cannot by itself be the **individual** sufficient cause of a numerically one particular portion of matter.

**CRITICAL LAYER WARNING:** The p.305 digital text contains explicit **«(ح.ح)»** marks identifying **Ḥasan Ḥasanzāda Āmulī's twentieth-century annotations**. That material includes a reference back to *al-Asfār* and a defence of gradual generation/corruption; it must **not** be silently presented as Sabzavārī's nineteenth-century authorial voice. The paragraph boundaries in this edition have not yet been independently resolved from a pre-Ḥasanzāda witness.

## 7. What genuinely differs from the Tajrīd baseline

The **Tajrīd reader** handles the shared logical and metaphysical vocabulary of movement, substance, potency and actuality. This dossier asks what happens **after Ṣadrā** to the specific requirement that motion have a **numerically persisting subject**. It supplies the target from *al-Shifāʾ* Physics II.3 and distinguishes a post-Ṣadrian answer about flowing existence from the earlier theory of abrupt substantial generation.

Do **not** treat the modern annotator's arguments as Sabzavārī's own reply. Do **not** treat a successful reply to the *need for a separate stationary subject* as a completed theory of identity through time. And do **not** claim the whole no.111 has been translated until pp.303–304 and both authorial layers have been checked.
