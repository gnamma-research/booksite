# Late-commentator fascicle 10 — Narāqī, *al-Lamaʿāt al-ʿarshiyya*: existence, quiddity and the priority of actuality

**31 October 2026 · Arabic primary source → new English translations**

**Direct witness:** Muḥammad Mahdī al-Narāqī (d. 1795), *al-Lamaʿāt al-ʿarshiyya*, publicly readable scholarly edition. Two **distinct bounded argument units**, printed [p.72: objection to primacy of existence](https://najafdesertlibrary.com/book/%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%84%D9%85%D8%B9%D8%A7%D8%AA-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B9%D8%B1%D8%B4%D9%8A%D8%A9/v/1/p/72) and [p.82: how quiddity unites with existence](https://najafdesertlibrary.com/book/%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%84%D9%85%D8%B9%D8%A7%D8%AA-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B9%D8%B1%D8%B4%D9%8A%D8%A9/v/1/p/82). They are **not adjacent continuous pages**; this is a paired selection, not a complete chapter. The online edition's contents distinguishes origination of quiddities, possibility within particular existence, and unity as separate topics; do not treat them as one continuous lemma.

## I. An objection to the primacy of existence and Narāqī's reply (p.72)

> اتصاف الماهية بالوجود أمر عقلي

**Translation of the objection:** Suppose existence is said to be primary and quiddity secondary. How does existence attach to a quiddity? If it precedes the quiddity, a predicate would exist before its subject; if it follows the quiddity, the quiddity would already exist and existence would be superfluous or require a further existence, causing regress; if existence and quiddity coexist independently, quiddity would not be existent *through* it, and another existence would again be needed.

**Reply:** The quiddity's being qualified by existence is a **consideration of the intellect**, not a physical attribution like blackness attaching to a body, as though quiddity and existence were two distinct external things with separate subsistence. They are externally one: existence is actual through itself and quiddity exists through its identity with that actuality. The agent produces the **particular existence of the thing**, which is itself the referent to which the quiddity is truly predicated. Thus neither temporal precedence, temporal posteriority nor a separate simultaneity applies between two external entities. The meaning of “existence is prior to quiddity” is that it is fundamental in actuality and production, and is the reality grounding predication of essential notions. This priority is analogous to what is primarily vs derivatively true, not cause preceding effect or receptor preceding received thing.

**Further implication:** particular contingent existences contain real respects of limitation and deficiency relative to absolute true being; from these real limits external quiddities can be abstracted. Quiddities therefore are not merely arbitrary fictions of the mind, but their intelligible form expresses the way contingent actuality is limited.

## II. Contingent existence and the possibility of abstraction (p.82)

> فكل وجود ممكن متضمن جهة عدمية يقتضيها بمحض حقيقته

**Translation:** Every contingent particular existence includes a privative or limiting respect that belongs to it precisely as the kind of finite existence it is. The intellect abstracts a quiddity from that limiting respect. Without such an element no finite quiddity could be abstracted, as in the case of the Necessary Existent. Hence every existence from which a quiddity can be abstracted has composition or differentiation in this sense. Once we accept that each possible being's existence and quiddity are **identical in concrete reality**, we must nevertheless deny that their *concepts* are identical. Otherwise “existence” and “human being” would be synonymous, predicating existence of humanity would teach us nothing, and one could not think of one while abstracting from the other—all plainly false. Therefore existence and quiddity are conceptually distinct, while externally identical as the one individuated reality.

The remaining problem, Narāqī remarks, is to explain the way quiddity is attributed to existence **within intellectual analysis** without reintroducing a false external relation between distinct things.

## Critical commentary

1. **Not a simple contrast “Avicenna teaches essences, Ṣadrā teaches existences”.** Here a later non-monolithic Avicennian scholar adopts terminology characteristic of the *primacy of existence* and specifies the external unity of existence and quiddity. Do not assume he therefore accepts every further Sadrian thesis.
2. **What is made (*majʿūl*)?** At p.72 the agent directly produces the **particular existence**, not an independently real generic concept of being. Compare [Lāhījī's *Shawāriq* debate](chapters/02-making-and-causal-explanation.md) and the [Indian Tahānawī/Mīr Zāhid gloss](LATE_FASCICLE_05_INDIAN_TAHANAWI_MIR_ZAHID_JAL.md).
3. **What is real in limitation?** Narāqī does not claim an externally separable entity “nonbeing” is added to a piece of existence. The limit is an actual grounding respect (*ḥaythiyya wāqiʿiyya*) by which quiddity is intelligibly abstracted. This qualifies both crude nominalism and reification of quiddities.
4. **Relation to Aquinas:** the real/nonreal distinction and priority of actuality may be compared with Aquinas' *actus essendi*, but Narāqī's external identity of particular existence with quiddity is **not automatically** Aquinas' account of a real composition of essence and esse. The analogy requires metaphysical argument.
5. **Source control:** two directly inspected edited Arabic pages, with short original Arabic anchors and new English translations. They are discrete original arguments; no claim of contiguous edition of the intervening pages or manuscript-level variants.

**Result:** primary evidence for selective appropriation of existence-first metaphysics in a late Persian scholar, going materially beyond the old shorthand “all later Avicennism remained quiddity-first.”
