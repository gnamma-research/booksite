# Post-Avicennian System: first verified cross-domain primary-text collation
**GNAMMA | 30 October 2026 | Working translation 0.1**

This is a **passage-level comparison across three distinct metaphysical problems**, not a claim that the entire late Avicennian tradition has been critically edited. The source excerpts are taken from searchable print transcriptions. The English translations below are new. Manuscript variants have **not** been established.

## 1. Intrinsic possibility versus necessity through another
**Lāhījī, *Shawāriq al-ilhām*, printed p. 88.** [Arabic source](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/9309/88?lang=ar)

**Arabic excerpt**

> واعلم أنه ظاهر ان الوجوب السابق مختصّ بالممكن لكونه حاصلا باعتبار العلة واما الوجوب اللاحق فالظاهر أنه أيضا كذلك لأنه بإزاء الوجوب السابق فلا يتصوّر الّا حيث يتصور فيه الوجوب السابق

**English**

Know that it is evident that antecedent necessity is restricted to a contingent being, since it obtains through consideration of its cause. As for consequent necessity, it appears that it is likewise so restricted, since it stands opposite antecedent necessity and therefore cannot be conceived except where antecedent necessity can be conceived.

**Analytical note.** This passage distinguishes a possible being's intrinsic modality from necessity conferred by its cause and from the necessity of a proposition once existence is included in its terms. That helps clarify Avicenna's dictum that a possible being does not exist until it becomes necessary through its cause. It does **not** establish that God's causing a possible thing is itself necessitated in the Thomistic sense.

## 2. Why possibility rather than temporal origination explains causal dependence
**Lāhījī, *Shawāriq*, printed p. 89.** [Arabic source](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/9309/89?lang=ar)

**Arabic excerpt**

> فذهب الحكماء والمحققون من متأخري المتكلمين إلى أنها الامكان والقدماء منهم إلى انّها الحدوث وبعضهم إلى أنها الامكان مع الحدوث شطر أو بعضهم إلى أنها هو معه شرطا

**English**

The philosophers and the critical investigators among the later theologians held that the ground [of the possible being's need for a cause] is possibility, whereas the earlier theologians held it to be temporal origination. Some maintained that it consists of possibility together with origination as constituent parts; others that origination is a condition alongside possibility.

**Arabic excerpt**

> وذلك لجزمه بالبديهة بان أحد المتساوين لا يمكن ان يترجح الّا لمرجّح وذلك المرجح هو المراد من العلة هاهنا

**English**

For [the intellect] judges as self-evident that one of two equally balanced alternatives cannot prevail except through a determinant; and that determinant is what is meant here by a cause.

**Arabic excerpt**

> قلنا الامكان انما هو كيفية النسبة بين الماهيّة ومفهوم الوجود من حيث هو متصور لا بين الماهية والوجود الحاصل لها

**English**

We reply: possibility is the character of the relation between a quiddity and the *concept* of existence insofar as that concept is conceived, not between the quiddity and the existence that it actually possesses.

**Comparison with Narāqī.** Narāqī's *Jāmiʿ al-afkār* I:38–42 follows the same broad argumentative route: dependence grounded in intrinsic possibility, not temporal origination; the distinction between a quiddity's conceptually possible existence and its actual existence; and the consequence that causal dependence continues. The close parallel is now **documented at the argument level**, but direct textual dependence or borrowing is not yet established. See [Narāqī translation dossier](primary-translation-causation.md).

**Critical implication for AT.** This gives a strong shared framework for contingency and conservation. But the inference from intrinsic contingency to an external determinant is philosophically distinct from the doctrine that a free First Cause creates without necessity. Agreement on the former does not prove agreement on the latter.

## 3. Substantial unity, matter and the loss of form
**Lāhījī, *Shawāriq*, vol. II, printed p. 185.** [Arabic source](https://najafdesertlibrary.com/book/%D8%B4%D9%88%D8%A7%D8%B1%D9%82-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A5%D9%84%D9%87%D8%A7%D9%85-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D8%B4%D8%B1%D8%AD-%D8%AA%D8%AC%D8%B1%D9%8A%D8%AF-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%83%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%85/v/2/p/185)

**Arabic excerpt**

> فأما مع القول بها، فالوحدة الزائلة هي وحدة الصورة الزائلة. وكما زالت وحدتها، زال وجودها. والباقي ليس إلا المادة المتصفة بالوحدة بالعرض.

**English**

But if matter is admitted, the unity that passes away is the unity of the form that passes away. Just as its unity has disappeared, so too has its existence. What remains is nothing but matter, to which unity belongs accidentally.

**Arabic excerpt**

> والأشبه أن الوجود الخاص، والوحدة والتشخص، متحدة بالذات، متغايرة بالاعتبار

**English**

It is more fitting to say that particular existence, unity, and individuation are identical in reality but distinct in consideration.

**Why this is important.** The first excerpt is a concrete treatment of **substantial form, matter and persistence**, not merely a general debate about *waḥdat al-wujūd*. The second states an important qualified identity between particular existence, unity and individuation. This directly complicates any crude narrative in which later Avicennian thinkers regard existence as a merely extrinsic conceptual label while Ṣadrā alone grants it metaphysical weight.

**Limits.** This is a short passage within a longer dispute. It does not establish Lāhījī's full theory of substantial generation or that he rejects the Giver of Forms.

## 4. Natural causation and the separate Giver of Forms: what is securely established
The [Stanford Encyclopedia article on Ibn Sīnā's natural philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ibn-sina-natural/) explicitly identifies *Physics* I.10, *De anima* II.1, and *Metaphysics* VI.2 and IX.5 as primary loci: parents and other natural agents cause preparatory accidental changes; a separate immaterial Giver of Forms imparts the species form. The [metaphysics article](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ibn-sina-metaphysics/) identifies *istiʿdād* and *istiʿdād khāṣṣ* as crucial distinctions in the receptive potency of matter.

**AT test:** Aquinas' account of natural generation must be compared with this allocation of efficient agency, not with a caricature denying all Avicennian secondary causation. An Avicennian substantial form is still **intrinsic** to the composite even when bestowed by an extrinsic intellect.

**Late-history status:** The primary texts examined above do not yet determine whether Narāqī, Jilwa or Khayrābādī retained the universal form-bestowing function. A definitive answer requires their actual natural-philosophical passages.

## 5. Wider system: interconnected findings and open questions

| Domain | Verified advance | Remaining textual test |
|---|---|---|
| Modal ontology | Lāhījī distinguishes intrinsic possibility, antecedent causal necessity and consequent necessity | Compare Avicenna's own precise modal account and Aquinas on necessity |
| Causal dependence | Lāhījī and Narāqī share the argument from possibility rather than temporal origination | Collate full objections and replies, not just excerpts |
| Substance and individuation | Lāhījī distinguishes the passing form's unity from the remaining matter and relates particular existence to individuation | Compare hylomorphic substantial unity with Aquinas and Ṣadrā |
| Natural agency | Classical Giver of Forms model supported by identified primary loci and specialist scholarship | Establish later authors' actual modifications |
| Motion | Lāhījī's substantial-motion objection is independently documented in scholarly research | Translate complete *Shawāriq* proof and Sadrian response |
| Existence and unity | Distinguish unity of concept, individuation, graded existence and metaphysical monism | Full Narāqī and Ṣadrā comparison |
| Soul and intellect | Active Intellect's epistemic role must be distinguished from its form-bestowing role | Primary passages in *De anima*, *Asfār* and late commentaries |
| Theology and creation | Causal necessity and continuous dependence are distinct from divine freedom | Compare first cause, will and emanation directly |
| Mental existence | Lāhījī explicitly distinguishes mental and external predicative domains (*Shawāriq* I:197) | Translate argument and connect with *nafs al-amr* |
| Ethics/politics | No new primary passage established in this collation | Separate research, not inferred from metaphysics |
