# Avicennian Summa · Primary-source translation dossier 1.1
## Narāqī on origination and continuing dependence
**Working critical translation — 30 October 2026.** Base: Muḥammad Mahdī al-Narāqī, *Jāmiʿ al-afkār wa-nāqid al-anẓār*, I:38–41. Online Arabic text: https://lib.rafed.net/view.php?b_id=728&page=38&type=c_fbook and https://lib.rafed.net/view.php?b_id=728&page=41&type=c_fbook.

**Editorial policy.** These are consecutive opening sentences and selected subsequent passages, **not** a falsely claimed complete translation of the whole pp. 38–41. Arabic quotations are checked against the searchable transcription; manuscript collation is pending. The intervening reasoning is explained as editorial synopsis, not disguised as translation.

### I:38 — The ground of causal dependence

**Arabic**

> وقبل الخوض في بيانه لا بدّ من بيان علّة احتياج الأشياء إلى المؤثّر. فنقول : الحقّ انّ علة احتياج الممكن إلى المؤثّر هو الامكان ، وفاقا لجمهور الحكماء ؛

**English**

Before embarking upon its demonstration, we must explain the ground of things' need for an efficient cause. We say: the correct position is that the ground of a contingent being's need for an efficient cause is its contingency, in agreement with the majority of philosophers.

**Arabic**

> وذهب جماعة من المتكلّمين إلى أنّ العلّة فيه هو الحدوث ؛ وقيل : الامكان مع الحدوث شطرا ؛ وقيل : شرطا.

**English**

A group of theologians, however, held that the ground of that need is temporal origination. It has also been said that contingency together with origination constitutes that ground, either as constituent parts or as conditions.

**Arabic**

> لنا : انّ العقل يحكم بأنّ الممكن ما يتساوى وجوده وعدمه ، وانّ ما يتساوى وجوده وعدمه فهو محتاج إلى مرجّح مغاير للممكن مرجح احد طرفيه المتساويين على الآخر ـ لبطلان ترجّح احد المتساويين على الآخر ـ ، فيعلم منه انّ علّة الحاجة في الواقع هي الامكان.

**English**

Our argument is that the intellect judges a contingent being to be that for which existence and nonexistence stand on an equal footing. Whatever stands equally related to existence and nonexistence requires a determinant other than the contingent being itself, determining one of its equally balanced alternatives over the other—for the spontaneous preponderance of one of two equal alternatives is impossible. From this we know that the real ground of the need is contingency.

### I:38–39 — Against temporal origination as the ground

**Arabic**

> وممّا يدلّ على أنّ الحدوث ليس علّة الافتقار إلى المؤثر انّا نتصوّر حدوث الممكن ولا يحصل لنا العلم باحتياجه إلى المؤثّر ما لم يلاحظ امكانه

**English**

Among the considerations showing that temporal origination is not the ground of dependence upon an efficient cause is that we can conceive the temporal origination of a contingent being without thereby knowing that it needs an efficient cause, unless we also attend to its contingency.

**Editorial synopsis of the next argument (not translation):** Narāqī argues that origination is a characteristic of existence as preceded by nonexistence. Existence is posterior in explanatory order to production, production to need, and need to its ground. Making origination the ground would therefore make it prior to itself through a sequence of explanatory stages. He explicitly distinguishes this priority from ordinary chronological succession.

### I:39 — The objection from possibility as a relation

**Arabic**

> فان قيل : الامكان صفة للممكن بالقياس إلى الوجود ـ لانّه عبارة عن مساوات نسبة الوجود والعدم إلى الماهية الممكنة ـ ، فيكون متأخّرا عن الوجود فلا يكون علة للاحتياج المتقدّم على الوجود بمراتب ؛

**English**

If it is said: Possibility is a qualification of the contingent thing in relation to existence—since it consists in the equal relation of existence and nonexistence to the contingent quiddity—and must therefore be posterior to existence, so that it cannot be the ground of dependence which is prior to existence by several stages—

**Arabic**

> قلنا : الامكان وإن كان متأخّرا عن الماهية نفسها وعن مفهوم الوجود أيضا لكنّه ليس متأخّرا عن كون الماهية موجودة بل مقدّم على اتصاف الماهية بالوجود

**English**

We reply: Although possibility is posterior to the quiddity itself and also to the concept of existence, it is not posterior to the quiddity's actually being existent; rather, it is prior to the quiddity's being qualified by existence.

### I:39 — The inference to continuing dependence

**Arabic**

> واذ ثبت انّ علّة الحاجة هي الامكان يلزم منه أن يكون الممكن الباقى حال بقائه أيضا محتاجا إلى المؤثّر لانّ الامكان لازم لماهية الممكن ذاتي لها لا ينفكّ عنها حال البقاء أيضا ، فيوجد معلوله ـ أعني : الاحتياج ـ أيضا في تلك الحال.

**English**

Once it has been established that the ground of need is contingency, it follows that a contingent being which continues to exist requires an efficient cause even during its continuance. For contingency is intrinsic to the quiddity of a contingent being, inseparable from it even while it persists; consequently its effect—namely dependence—also obtains during that state.

### I:40–41 — The builder objection

**Arabic**

> وتمسّكوا في ذلك ببقاء البناء بعد فناء البنّاء ، ولم يعلموا انّ الكلام في العلّة الموجدة ، وليس البنّاء موجدا للبناء وانّما هو علّة لحركات الآلات واللبنات والأخشاب بحركة يده

**English**

They appealed, in support of this, to the persistence of a building after the builder has perished. But they failed to realise that the discussion concerns the cause which bestows existence. The builder does not bestow existence upon the building; rather, through the movement of his hand, he is the cause of the movements of the tools, bricks and timbers.

**Arabic**

> فان قيل : تأثير المؤثّر في الباقي محال ، لانّه إن افاد الوجود الّذي كان حاصلا فيلزم تحصيل الحاصل ؛ وإن افاد امرا آخرا متجدّدا لم يكن التأثير في الباقي ، بل في هذا الأمر المتجدّد ؛

**English**

If it is said: The action of an efficient cause upon something that persists is impossible. For if it confers the existence that was already present, this entails acquiring what has already been acquired; whereas if it confers some other newly arising thing, its action would not concern the persisting thing, but rather that newly arising thing—

**Arabic**

> قلنا : تاثير المؤثّر في استمرار الوجود وبقائه ، فانّ الحاصل بالحدوث ليس إلاّ الوجود في زمانه وحصول شيء في زمان لا يوجب حصول استمراره وبقائه ، فابقاء الوجود وادامته من المؤثّر ، وهو غير حصول نفس الوجود.

**English**

We reply: The efficient cause acts in respect of the continuation and persistence of existence. What is obtained through origination is only existence at its own time, and a thing's obtaining at one time does not entail the obtaining of its continuation and persistence. Thus the preservation and prolongation of existence come from the efficient cause, and this differs from the original obtaining of existence itself.

### Analytical comparison — editorial, not translated primary text

**Avicenna:** Contingency grounds the requirement for an existential cause even where the contingent effect has already been produced. The hierarchy of causes is explanatory, not reducible to earlier events.

**Lāhījī:** In *Shawāriq al-ilhām* I:159, his reply about *jaʿl* identifies what is produced as particular existence, not the concept or quiddity of existence. This should be compared with the above before treating Lāhījī as an uncomplicated opponent of Ṣadrā's primacy-of-existence doctrine. Source: https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/9309/159?lang=ar

**Ṣadrā:** The proof of continuing dependence is not by itself distinctively Sadrian. His stronger innovations concern the mode of existence and substantial change. Precise passages of *Asfār* I and the *Shifāʾ* annotations must be collated before a claim of agreement or contradiction can be made.

**Aquinas:** Conservation and secondary causation provide a promising comparative framework. But the differences concerning free creation, necessitation, emanation and the status of substantial forms cannot be resolved from Narāqī's argument alone.

## Supplement — full explanatory-priority argument and modal objection (I:38–39)

**Arabic**

وأيضا لو كان الحدوث علّة الاحتياج لزم تقدّم الشيء على نفسه بمراتب. بيانه : انّ الحدوث كيفية الوجود ووصف له ـ لانّه عبارة عن مسبوقية الوجود بالعدم ـ ، فتأخّر الوجود المتأخّر عن الايجاد المتاخّر عن الاحتياج ، لانّ الشيء إذا لم يكن محتاجا إلى المؤثّر لم يتصوّر تأثيره فيه ـ كما في الواجب والممتنع ـ ، فالايجاد فرع الاحتياج والاحتياج متأخّر عن علّته ـ لوجوب تقدّم العلّة على المعلول ـ ، فلو كان الحدوث علّة للاحتياج أو جزء لها أو شرطا لها وجب تقدّمه على الاحتياج المتقدّم على الايجاد المتقدّم على الوجود المتقدّم على الحدوث ، فيلزم تقدّمه على نفسه بأربع مراتب ـ على تقدير العلّية والشرطية ـ ، وبخمس مراتب ـ على تقدير الجزئية ، لوجوب تقدّم جزء العلّة عليها ـ. وأمّا الامكان فلا ريب في انّه مقدّم على الاحتياج بالذات لعدم تصوّر الاحتياج بدون تصوّر الامكان.

**Translation**

Moreover, were temporal origination the cause of dependence, a thing would have to precede itself by several ranks. Origination is a mode and attribute of existence, since it means that existence is preceded by nonexistence. Existence is therefore posterior to the conferral of existence, which is posterior to dependence: if a thing did not require an efficient cause, the cause could not be conceived as acting upon it, as in the cases of the necessary and the impossible. Thus conferring existence presupposes dependence, and dependence is posterior to its cause. If origination were the cause of dependence, part of that cause, or a condition of it, origination would have to precede dependence, which precedes conferring existence, which precedes existence, which precedes origination. It would thus precede itself by four ranks if it were the cause or a condition, and by five if it were a constituent part, since a part of a cause precedes that cause. Possibility, by contrast, is prior to dependence in the order of essence, since dependence cannot be conceived without possibility.

**Arabic**

فان قيل : الامكان صفة للممكن بالقياس إلى الوجود ـ لانّه عبارة عن مساوات نسبة الوجود والعدم إلى الماهية الممكنة ـ ، فيكون متأخّرا عن الوجود فلا يكون علة للاحتياج المتقدّم على الوجود بمراتب ؛ قلنا : الامكان وإن كان متأخّرا عن الماهية نفسها وعن مفهوم الوجود أيضا لكنّه ليس متأخّرا عن كون الماهية موجودة بل مقدّم على اتصاف الماهية بالوجود ، ولذا يوصف الماهية ووجودها بالامكان قبل اتصاف الماهية بالوجود. وامّا الحدوث فلا يتصف الماهية ولا وجودها به إلاّ إذا صارت موجودة ؛ على انّ تاخّر الحدوث عن الايجاد ممّا لا ريب فيه ، ولذا صحّ أن يقال : أوجد فحدث ، وبذلك ثبت المطلوب سواء قلنا انّه متأخّر عن الوجود أيضا أم لا.

**Translation**

If it is objected that possibility is a property of the contingent thing in relation to existence—since it means that existence and nonexistence stand in an equal relation to the contingent quiddity—and must therefore be posterior to existence, so that it cannot be the cause of dependence, which is prior to existence by several ranks, we reply: although possibility is posterior to the quiddity itself and also to the concept of existence, it is not posterior to the quiddity's actually being existent. Rather, it is prior to the quiddity's being qualified by existence. Hence the quiddity and its existence may be described as possible before the quiddity is qualified by existence. Temporal origination, however, cannot qualify the quiddity or its existence unless it has become existent. Moreover, the posteriority of origination to the conferral of existence is beyond doubt: hence it is correct to say, 'He brought it into existence, and so it originated.' The conclusion is established whether or not we also say that origination is posterior to existence itself.

**Critical note:** The ranks are explanatory, not chronological. The objection distinguishes the concept of existence from the quiddity's actual existence. The online edition identifies this as the fourth preliminary; its navigation inconsistently says third. Source: https://lib.rafed.net/view.php?b_id=728&page=39&type=c_fbook . This addition still does not constitute a full critical collation of the preliminary or the matching Avicennian/Sadrian texts.
