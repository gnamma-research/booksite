# Late-commentator fascicle 06 — al-Taftāzānī, *Sharḥ al-Maqāṣid*, the three objections to mental existence

**31 October 2026 · Arabic primary source → working English translation**

**Direct source:** Saʿd al-Dīn al-Taftāzānī (d. 792/1389), *Sharḥ al-Maqāṣid fī ʿilm al-kalām*, printed ed. 1401/1981, pp. **78–79** as transcribed at [Usul p.78](https://usul.ai/ar/t/sharh-maqasid/78) and [p.79](https://usul.ai/ar/t/sharh-maqasid/79). **Boundary:** a connected **argument dossier** from the discussion of proofs and counterarguments for *al-wujūd al-dhihnī*, not the entire work or full surrounding chapter. The entire three-objection-and-answer sequence of p.79 is translated below in distinct units. The long preparatory discussion on p.78 is selectively translated with explicitly bounded sentences.

## I. Arguments for mental existence — p.78 (selected connected unit)

> أن الكلي مفهوم وكل مفهوم ثابت ضرورة تميزه عند العقل

**Translation:** “The universal is conceived, and everything conceived has some establishment, necessarily, by virtue of its being distinguishable for the intellect.” But a universal as universal cannot be a singular thing in extra-mental reality, where everything is individuated; hence the kind of establishment in question is mental. Likewise certain genuinely universal affirmative judgments must have intelligible subjects despite lacking any external instance, as in the claim “Every phoenix is an animal.” Their subject cannot be restricted to extant particular birds.

**The opponent's objection:** Predication does not require an externally or mentally *existent* subject: in predicating we may only mean that whatever answers to a subject term answers to the predicate; recognition of the terms is not yet proof of an additional mode of existence. The supposed three proofs collapse into the fact that we conceive what lacks an external instance. If the defender simply assumes that conception occurs through a thing's form being present in the intellect, the disputed thesis has been smuggled into a premise.

**Reply:** There must be a relation between knowing intellect and object known, whether cognition is conceived as an acquired form, a special relation, or a relational quality. Relation to absolute nothingness is impossible; therefore the object known must possess at least some determinate standing. Since universals, absent things and impossibilia do not exist externally, their intelligible determination must lie elsewhere.

## II. The rival location hypotheses — pp.78–79 (selected)

Some propose independently subsisting Platonic forms, an imaginal world, or forms imprinted in a separate Active Intellect. Taftāzānī answers that what is absolutely impossible cannot subsist externally as an individual form or be imprinted in a separate intellect as a self-standing impossible individual. What that intellect could possess is **the conception** of the impossible, itself bringing the explanation back to representation. Yet the exact inference from **objectivity of conception** to a *form inhering in this knower* is contested; al-Taftāzānī reports that even the *Mawāqif* formulation may be disputed at precisely this point.

## III. The deniers' three objections — p.79

> الأول لو كان تصور الشيء مستلزما لحصوله في العقل

**First objection, translation:** If imagining a thing requires its presence in the intellect, then conceiving heat and cold would make the intellect hot and cold. That is impossible, since it would combine contraries and attribute to the intellect properties of bodies.

**Second objection:** The heavens, with all their vastness, would have to be present in the intellect when conceived, and in imagination when imagined. This is evidently false.

**Third objection:** Conceiving nonexistent things would make them exist externally, since they exist in an intellect that itself exists outside the mind, and what exists *inside* a thing existing outside the mind is supposed to exist where that thing is—just as water in a jug in a house is in that house.

## IV. The answer — p.79

> الفرق بين الوجود المتأصل ... والوجود ... الذهني

**Translation:** All three objections fail to distinguish **rooted, extra-mental existence**—through which a thing has its concrete individuality—from the non-rooted existence of a **mental form**. The thing that is literally hot is a subject in which the extra-mental reality of heat subsists, not one that possesses its mental form; the opposition of heat and cold concerns their external realities, not their representations. What plainly cannot be contained within an intellect or imagination is the *actual cosmic reality* of the heavens, not the universal or particular form through which they are grasped. Finally, the inference from an interior to an enclosing exterior applies when both are independently existing concrete realities (water, jug, house), not when an absent object's **form** has mental being while the mind has external being. The mental representation and the external individual differ in their necessary concomitants: the former may be universal, abstract and causally inactive, the latter individuated and productive of effects. Their *correspondence* means that a quiddity actualised externally would be that external reality, and stripped of individuating attachments could be considered as the intelligible quiddity.

## V. Editorial and philosophical apparatus

1. **The direct voice is Taftāzānī.** A nested report of al-Ījī's *Mawāqif* is identified as such, and an objection to it remains Taftāzānī's presentation. We do not count it as independently collated al-Ījī text.
2. **Key terms:** `wujūd aṣīl / mutaʾaṣṣil` is extra-mental, concrete actuality; `wujūd dhihnī` is mental representational actuality; `ṣūra` need not be a second physical individual; `hawiyya ʿayniyya` is extra-mental individuated reality. The source's `ʿāriḍ` argument should not be paraphrased as “ideas are physical heat.”
3. **Cross-commentary test:** compare [Jurjānī's *Sharḥ al-Mawāqif*](LATE_FASCICLE_07_MAWAQIF_JURJANI_GLOSSES.md) and [Lāhījī's *Shawāriq* mental-existence argument](chapters/01-mental-existence-and-quiddity.md). The logical difference concerns what is **represented** versus the representing act's ontological inherence.
4. **Real issue not settled automatically:** one can reject the opponents' crude physical objections and still debate whether the intelligible species is formally identical with the represented nature, whether conception entails mental *wujūd* or a merely intentional relation, and what constitutes *nafs al-amr* for true judgments about impossible objects.
5. **Boundaries:** p.79 provides the direct sequence of three objections and the connected answer, translated without eliding a substantive objection. The surrounding discussion p.78 is selective; no manuscript witnesses or variant readings were checked.
