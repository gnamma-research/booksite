# Late-commentator fascicle 01 — Lāhījī, *Gawhar-i murād*, II.3.6: the tenth intellect, souls and natural forms

**31 October 2026 · Persian primary source → new working English translation**

**Author**: ʿAbd al-Razzāq al-Lāhījī (d. 1661/2). **Work**: *Gawhar-i murād* (گوهر مراد), Article II, Book/Part 3, Chapter 6. **Witness**: searchable Persian edition, Nashr-i Sāya, Tehran, print pp. **301–303**, [p.301](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/17438/301?lang=ar), [p.302](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/17438/302?lang=ar), [p.303](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/17438/303?lang=ar). ## Heading, printed p.301

> فصل ششم ... در بيان آنكه علّت فيضان نفوس ناطقه و ساير صور نوعيّهء مركبات ، عقل عاشر است

**Translation:** “Chapter Six, on showing that the cause of the emanation of rational souls and of the other specific forms of composite things is the tenth intellect, also called the Active Intellect.”

### p.301 — why a single species has multiple individuals

Rational souls, as already established, are many in number but agree in specific reality. On account of their number they cannot all emanate immediately, at one level, from the Necessary Existent, who is an absolutely true One. And on account of their agreement in species, they cannot emanate in a sequence in which some souls are causes of other souls, for that would introduce a gradation in an essential determination: if the quiddity is the same in all individuals, causal priority among them would introduce an essential priority that is not present in the species. The situation differs for intellects that are distinct in their realities. Rational souls likewise cannot emanate from a celestial body, for the soul is more noble than a body, and the nobler is not the effect of the baser. Nor can they emanate from the first effect: if its many respects differed in reality, what issued through each would differ in specific reality; if those respects were of one reality, they could not account for many individuals without matter.

**Apparatus**: at p.301 n.1, `صدورشان` (“their procession”) has `صدورش` in B and C. This does not change the argument's contrast between one species and multiple rational souls.

### p.302 — preparedness and individuating bodies

Many individuals of a single species come about only through matter and material accidents, which are absent from the first effect. The same problem excludes the other separate intellects preceding the tenth. Although each of these intellects is associated with a sphere containing matter, celestial matter does not receive generation and corruption and therefore cannot be responsible for a numerical plurality within one species. The tenth intellect, by contrast, is the source of the matter of generated things. Thus a single respect in it can produce the species of rational soul while its individuals multiply through matters with different dispositions arising from different temperaments. The source of the rational soul must accordingly be an intellect also responsible for sublunary matter. The individuality of each soul is derived from matter made ready by a particular disposition—that is, the body. If the existence of an intellectual principle for each sphere is otherwise established, this principle will be the tenth intellect. Lāhījī asks whether the present argument itself can establish the entire number of intellects and says that, on close examination, it can, as Ibn Sīnā argued in *al-Mabdaʾ wa-l-maʿād*.

**Variants**: p.302 n.1 B,C read `معلولات اوّل` for `معلول اوّل`; n.2 witness A omits `نيز`; n.3 A has `باشند`; n.4 C adds `كه`; n.5 B,C omit `امّا`.

### p.303 — a hierarchy, not separate species-lords

The different dispositions of the matter of generated things, which account for the numerical multiplication of rational souls, arise with the assistance of celestial motions and mixtures. The corresponding intellect must therefore be placed at the level of the lunar sphere, the last of the spheres. It cannot be the first effect, since other spheres precede it; nor can it be the product of the sphere, since it is nobler than the sphere. It must proceed from a further intellect, and the same argument may be repeated until it reaches an intellect ordered with the first sphere and proceeding, along with that sphere, from the first intellect. The familiar ordered series of ten intellects follows.

Once the tenth intellect has been established as the source of rational souls, it is likewise the source of the different species of generated things, through its different causal respects and the different dispositions of receptive matter. **Specific diversity belongs to diverse causal respects; numerical diversity within a species belongs to diverse dispositions.** Since the rational soul is the noblest of generated species, it derives through the noblest respect; other composites proceed through other respects. What the Illuminationists call a “lord of the species” (*rabb al-nawʿ*)—the source that oversees and pours out each species' perfections—can thus be referred to the **tenth intellect** under its various respects. There is no need to posit additional horizontally arranged separate intellects, one for every natural species.

**Variants**: p.303 notes 1–5 record B `براى` for `بنابر`, A `اوّل` for `معلول`, A `و` for `پس`, B omission of `انواع`, and B `به ترتيب` for `به تربيت`. The last variant affects whether “ordering” or “nurturing” is the stated relation, so the main translation retains the base's stronger nurturing formulation.

## Editorial adjudication

**What the text demonstrates:** a named late Safavid post-*Tajrīd* philosopher **explicitly retains** the separate tenth intellect as the bestower of human rational souls and **other natural specific forms**, and uses matter's differentiated *istiʿdādāt* to explain distinct individuals. He further *rejects the need* to multiply species-lords horizontally. This is a first-hand textual answer to the earlier open question whether Lāhījī had abandoned a separate Giver of Forms: **he had not, at least here**.

**What it does not show:** that the rational soul is intrinsically material, that every late commentator agrees, that Lāhījī denies real natural agent efficacy, or that the ten-intellect astronomy is empirically credible. In particular, later [Chapter 7](LATE_FASCICLE_02_LAHIJI_GAWHAR_TEMPORAL_CAUSATION.md) distinguishes sustaining existence from time-specific material preparation.

**Coverage**: the source's entire Chapter 6 is short and spans exactly printed pp.301–303; this translation follows its argument paragraph by paragraph, with selectively reported printed variants, **not manuscript autopsy**. The edition is a new English translation, not an independently established Persian critical text.
