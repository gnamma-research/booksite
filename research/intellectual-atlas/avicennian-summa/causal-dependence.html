An Avicennian Summa · Book II · Working chapter 1 · 30 October 2026

On Contingency and Continuing Causation

A developed test of the Peripatetic claim that a contingent thing needs a cause not merely when it begins to exist, but throughout its existence—and what that claim does, and does not, establish about natural causes, divine agency and post-Sadrian philosophy.

5 original Arabic theses3 sourced translation lemmata5 argument stagesEditorial reconstruction, not historical attribution

How to read this chapter

The five Arabic theses below are newly composed editorial propositions, followed by precise English translations of that newly composed Arabic. They are not presented as lost passages of Ibn Sīnā, Narāqī or Lāhījī. Separately, the source-translation section gives three short genuine Arabic lemmata from Narāqī and new English translations, with direct links and footnotes. A full critical translation of pp. 38–42 is still outstanding, as are the variant readings of the available printed edition.

The chapter is a working argument: it proposes a synthesis, explicitly indicates its evidentiary basis, and records unresolved objections rather than hiding them.

I. A proposed matn: five theses

1. On contingency

English · translation of new editorial Arabic

A contingent quiddity, considered as such, demands neither existence nor nonexistence. When one of these alternatives is actualised, the determination cannot derive from the quiddity's mere possibility; it therefore requires something that determines it.

المتن المقترح · من إنشاء المحرّر

الماهية الممكنة من حيث هي لا تقتضي الوجود ولا العدم؛ فإذا تحقّق أحد الطرفين لم يكن تعيّنه من نفس إمكانها، بل افتقر إلى ما يعيّنه.

2. On origination and dependence

English · translation of new editorial Arabic

Temporal origination differs from intrinsic possibility. Origination describes an existence preceded by nonexistence, whereas possibility describes a quiddity's relation to the alternatives of existence and nonexistence, whether the quiddity exists now or has yet to exist.

المتن المقترح · من إنشاء المحرّر

والحدوث الزماني غير الإمكان الذاتي؛ فإنّ الحدوث يصف وجوداً سبقته حالة العدم، أمّا الإمكان فيصف نسبة الماهية إلى طرفي الوجود والعدم، سواء أكانت موجودة الآن أم لم توجد بعد.

3. On the persistence of dependence

English · translation of new editorial Arabic

A contingent thing does not become necessary in itself when it exists. Being actualised through its cause does not remove its intrinsic contingency. What may be asked concerning the cause of its initial existence may also be asked concerning its continuing existence, while keeping the distinction between first origination and conservation.

المتن المقترح · من إنشاء المحرّر

ولا يصير الممكن واجباً بذاته إذا وجد؛ فإنّ تحقّقه بعلّته لا يزيل عنه وصف الإمكان لذاته. فما صحّ أن يسأل عن سبب وجوده في الابتداء يصحّ أن يسأل عن سبب دوامه، مع التمييز بين الإيجاد الأول والإبقاء.

4. On the two uses of efficient causality

English · translation of new editorial Arabic

The term 'agent' is used in different ways. A natural agent may move matter, arrange components, or prepare a subject to receive a form. None of that alone establishes it as an independent principle sustaining the effect's very existence. We must therefore distinguish causes of natural transformation from the cause of existence considered as existence.

المتن المقترح · من إنشاء المحرّر

ولفظ «الفاعل» يقع على أنحاء؛ فالفاعل الطبيعي قد يحرّك مادّةً أو ينظّم أجزاءً أو يُعدّ موضوعاً لقبول صورة، وليس يلزم من هذا وحده أن يكون هو المبدأ المستقلّ الذي به تقوم حقيقة وجود المعلول. فيجب التمييز بين أسباب التحوّل في الطبيعة وبين علّة الوجود من حيث هو وجود.

5. On concurrence and the limits of assimilation

English · translation of new editorial Arabic

An effect's continuing dependence upon its cause does not entail the abolition of proximate causal activity. Neither does it establish agreement among philosophers about how actions proceed from the First Principle. Sharing an argument for dependence is one thing; agreeing about will, emanation, and the orders of intermediary causes is another.

المتن المقترح · من إنشاء المحرّر

فلا يلزم من دوام افتقار المعلول إلى علّته إبطال فعل الأسباب القريبة، كما لا يلزم منه الاتفاق بين الفلاسفة في كيفيّة صدور الأفعال عن المبدأ الأوّل. فاشتراك برهان الافتقار شيء، والاتفاق في الإرادة والفيض ومراتب الوسائط شيء آخر.

II. Translation from Narāqī: three lemmata

From Muḥammad Mahdī al-Narāqī, Jāmiʿ al-afkār wa-nāqid al-anẓār, vol. I, the fourth premise on the need of a contingent being for a cause at its origination and in its continuance (printed pp. 38–42). Orthography is lightly normalized; no editorial apparatus is reproduced. These are deliberately selected lemmata and are not a translation of the intervening pages.

1. Jāmiʿ al-afkār I, p. 38

New English working translation

The ground of a contingent being's need for a cause is possibility.

Arabic lemma · original source

علّة احتياج الممكن إلى المؤثّر هو الإمكان

Translator's note: This is the main contention against making temporal origination the primary ground of causal need. Check source text and surrounding argument.

2. Jāmiʿ al-afkār I, p. 41

New English working translation

The builder is not the cause that bestows existence upon the building.

Arabic lemma · original source

وليس البنّاء موجدا للبناء

Translator's note: The word mujid is stronger than the everyday sense of 'builder'. Narāqī proceeds to discuss movements of instruments and arranged materials as preparatory causes. Check source text and surrounding argument.

3. Jāmiʿ al-afkār I, p. 42

New English working translation

Arabic lemma · original source

فإبقاء الوجود وإدامته من المؤثّر

Translator's note: The immediate context answers the objection that sustaining what already exists would be a pointless acquisition of something already attained (taḥṣīl al-ḥāṣil). Check source text and surrounding argument.

III. Reconstructed argument and its objections

Possibility is an intrinsic modal status

For a contingent quiddity, neither existence nor nonexistence follows simply from what the thing is. This is not the claim that a possible thing is in fact hovering between existence and nonexistence. It is a claim about what the thing's essence, considered in itself, suffices to establish. We must distinguish the logical grasp of possibility from any hypothesis about the extramental reality of a non-existent essence.

Actuality therefore calls for an explanatory determinant

If a contingent thing is actual, the fact that its essence was neutral between existing and not existing cannot by itself explain why it has actuality. Narāqī appeals to the rejection of an ungrounded preference of one equal alternative over the other. This is the metaphysical principle at issue; its justification is not secured merely by restating it. One should compare Ibn Sīnā's arguments about possible existence with later Rāzian objections to necessitation.

Temporal origination is not the basic explanation

Coming to be after not being is a temporal characterization of an actual existence. It is not automatically an explanation of why a quiddity requires a cause. Narāqī argues that taking origination as the ground of that need reverses an order of explanation: the need for a cause is conceptually prior to causal production, which in turn is prior to the resultant created existence. Whether all kinds of temporal creation require this precise explanatory ordering remains a separate question.

Continued being presents a renewed explanatory question

A contingent being does not stop being intrinsically contingent when it becomes actual. Narāqī distinguishes giving a thing existence at an initial moment from causing its existence to continue. An objection says a continuing cause would merely confer something already possessed. The response is that what was possessed previously was existence at that previous time; its persisting later is not thereby explained. This is causal dependence, not necessarily repeated temporal creation of numerically new substances at each instant.

Natural production and conferral of existence are not interchangeable

A builder's death does not make a house disappear, but the builder is not the kind of cause being discussed. His activity moves and arranges materials into a structure. That activity can be a genuine proximate cause while not independently explaining why the resulting natural reality has existence at all. The exact ontological status of the enduring composite, its form, and the hierarchy of efficient causes must be addressed rather than assumed.

V. Points requiring critical adjudication

Does “the existence of a thing now” differ intrinsically from “its existence a moment ago”? A conservation argument can become circular if it treats a new moment of existence as a fresh entity without justification. The more promising version asks why the same contingent substance remains actual, given that its essence does not suffice for its actuality. That distinguishes a sustaining ground from a temporal series of repeated productions.

Is the causal dependence formally identical in Avicenna and Aquinas? No such identity has yet been established here. Their agreements may be deeper than common textbook contrasts suggest, especially on simultaneous or hierarchical causal orders; nevertheless, divine willing, emanation, the mode of created esse, the First's knowledge, and the ontology of substantial forms could yield genuine differences. The comparison must be conducted from their own causal analyses, not merely from the labels 'vertical' and 'horizontal'.

What exactly did Ṣadrā add? A claim about continuous dependence may already be fully articulated in Avicennism. Ṣadrā's distinctiveness must be tested in his account of the way substances change and the relation of quiddity to existence, not assumed to lie in being first to say that creatures remain dependent.
