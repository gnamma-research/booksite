# Twenty-domain critical synthesis: what the primary texts actually establish
**GNAMMA Avicennian Summa — source-graded research edition, 30 October 2026**

## Scope and outcome

This register is an **audit of achieved evidence**, not a list of intended research steps. A bibliography of works is not their translation; a published edition's reported variant is not a manuscript collation; a commentarial quotation of another philosopher is not itself an independently collated witness to the quoted philosopher. Those distinctions govern every entry below.

**The essential historical outcome:** later Avicennism survives as several competing philosophical programmes, not one univocal system; the strongest work is to identify which **proof**, **ontology**, **causal allocation**, or **interpretive method** a later author changed. The source-based chapters now demonstrate some such changes directly rather than merely asserting them.

## Nine earned substantive conclusions

### 1. Later Peripatetic ontology is *not* essence plus an independent existence-object

Lāhījī's fifth question (*Shawāriq* I:52) refutes a crude position in which a quiddity first exists outside the mind and a second actual existent called “existence” is thereafter attached to it. He then affirms *individual instances of existence*. This is an important late-scholastic discrimination: **the unity of an actual thing** does not invalidate a distinction in analysis. It does not automatically prove the Thomistic real composition of essence and *esse*. [Chapter 01](chapters/01-mental-existence-and-quiddity.md).

### 2. The very question “what is made?” had been refined by later kalām

In *Shawāriq* I:158–161, the reports from Taftāzānī and Jurjānī and Lāhījī's own adjudication distinguish (a) a quiddity **being what it is**, (b) a quiddity **actually obtaining**, and (c) the **concrete individual existent** thereby produced. To make humanity “be humanity” by a second act of production is incoherent; to make **a human being actually exist** is intelligible. The debate is not settled by simply labelling one party “theologians” and the other “philosophers,” as their statements often target different senses of *jaʿl*. [Chapter 02](chapters/02-making-and-causal-explanation.md).

### 3. The strongest evidence of real philosophical change: substantial motion

Lāhījī's analysis demands a numerically persistent actual subject in which **some other determination** can vary. Ṣadrā's response is not that no continuity is needed. It is that **the very individual existence** can be continuous and intensify without a collection of independently actual successive forms. This directly reforms the ontology of identity and change, and connects natural philosophy with the psychology of bodily identity. [Chapter 03](chapters/03-substantial-motion-dispute.md).

### 4. Avicenna's four causes include a crucial natural/metaphysical distinction

*Ilāhiyyāt* VI.1 itself—not just a modern handbook—says the **natural efficient** cause concerns a mode of movement whereas the **metaphysical efficient** cause confers being. The elements and their preparation for a Giver of Forms appear in IX.5. Aquinas, *De potentia* III.8, explicitly recognises Avicenna's **last intelligence** as form giver but rejects the division under which natural generators fail to produce the **composite**. This is a real causal disagreement, not necessarily one about the form's being **intrinsic** after generation. [Chapter 05](chapters/05-four-causes-form-bestowal.md).

### 5. The Active Intellect's epistemic role is different from its cosmological form-bestowing role

Ibn Sīnā *al-Nafs* V.5 argues from potency to actuality in intellectual learning, with sensations and reflected demonstrations *preparing* reception. A disposition (*malaka*) that lets one recover knowledge is not the uninterrupted actual contemplation of every known proposition. These are philosophical insights a Thomist can accept while replacing the cosmological/external intellect with different causal operations of the human agent intellect. The later Qajar and Indian authors have yet to be proven to accept or reject those functions independently. [Chapter 04](chapters/04-intellect-soul-and-identity.md).

### 6. Agreement on continuous existential dependence is compatible with real disagreement about God willing the cosmos

Narāqī's *Jāmiʿ* I:38–42, Lāhījī's modal explanations and Avicenna IX.1–4 support contingency, causal dependence, immutable First and an intelligently ordered cosmos. Aquinas's *De potentia* III.16 nevertheless criticises necessary **one-effect** emanation and denies that the **choice of this specific world** follows necessarily from God's nature. A genuinely coherent AT synthesis must say which Avicennian claims it retains and which it actually **changes**. [Chapter 06](chapters/06-divine-agency-necessity-and-plurality.md).

### 7. Narāqī's positive account of unity is neither simple monism nor simple repudiation

Narāqī *Jāmiʿ* II:166–167 explicitly rejects (i) placing God at the top of a common graded existential series and (ii) identifying the purified existence of finite things with God's numerically identical existence; he preserves instead **complete existential causation** and **God's self-knowledge as more perfect knowledge of what proceeds from him**. This response poses a problem also confronted by AT analogy: affirm participation without making God one member of a genus. But the two philosophies differ regarding necessary concomitants and willing. [Chapter 07](chapters/07-unity-plurality-and-divine-knowledge.md).

### 8. Quiddity's being considered in itself does not create a third universe of essences

Ibn Sīnā *Ilāhiyyāt* V.1: what horseness **means** does not itself include the conditions of mental being, external actuality, universality or singularity. The *meaning's* neutrality is not proof of actual uninstantiated horses. Lāhījī's distinction of mental being from the real act of understanding provides the next needed step; AT *species qua/quod* supplies a related but not identical epistemological account. [Chapter 08](chapters/08-universals-quiddity-nafs-al-amr.md).

### 9. Later kalām sharpened both hylomorphic and epistemological demonstrations

The *Mawāqif* commentary and later glosses VII:34–35, 45 reconstruct an argument for prime matter from the reception of connection and separation, then contest it with the possibility that **corporeal form itself** is receptive of varying magnitude. The theological trilemma insists that purported matter must either be an independently spatial body, a spatially dependent attribute, or something with no intelligible relation to extension. This is not a slogan-level repudiation but a precise challenge to the **demonstrative status** of prime matter.

Taftāzānī's *Sharḥ al-Maqāṣid* pp.78–79 similarly distinguishes the meaning of a universal from an actual extramental instance and replies to three objections to mental existence: representation of heat need not physically heat the intellect; representation of the heavens need not place immense bodies inside the brain; and thinking the nonexistent does not make it an extramental real substance. The distinction between **primary external actualisation** and **intellectual representation** supplies an indispensable stage between Ibn Sīnā's *Ilāhiyyāt* V.1 and Lāhījī's refined psychology of intellectual forms. [Chapter 09](chapters/09-mawaqif-maqasid-hylomorphism-mental-existence.md).

**Historical qualification:** Matn, commentary and later glosses are interleaved in this particular *Mawāqif* printing. No unverified sentence is assigned to Siyālkūtī or Ḥalabī merely because the copy includes their glosses.

## The fundamental test for philosophical coherence with AT

We can now state the problem more sharply than “Are Ibn Sīnā and Aquinas basically the same?”:

**Shared core to explore:** actual substances possess intrinsic formal principles; potency is actualised through causation; there are material, formal, efficient and final explanations; the existence of finite contingent reality cannot be explained merely by the intelligibility of its quiddity; the First is not a material thing inside the cosmos; knowledge can reach real natures, not merely a private set of mental objects.

**Genuine friction:** the created separate Active Intellect's division of causal labour; whether human abstraction requires that intellect's separate activity; whether the First's eternal self-knowing necessarily determines this very universe; whether nature's changing substance can be one intensifying actual existence; and whether simple First/diverse creature relations require univocity, analogy, graded existential participation or metaphysical unity.

**Testable reformulation:** retain the demonstrative strength of contingency and fourfold causality and make the **concrete composite** the proper terminus of natural substantial generation; retain genuine ontological dependence upon the First while disentangling it from a necessitated tenth-intellect cosmology. This yields a promising *proposed synthesis*, but it should be labelled **a new philosophical reconstruction**, not attributed wholesale to Lāhījī, Narāqī, Jilwa, Aquinas or Ibn Sīnā.

## High-priority missing primary witnesses — quantified accurately

1. **Post-Rāzian philosophical kalām:** complete argument units from Rāzī's *Sharḥ al-Ishārāt* and *Mabāḥith*, Ṭūsī's reply, Qūshjī, Jurjānī's *Sharḥ al-Mawāqif*, Taftāzānī's *Sharḥ al-Maqāṣid*, and others. The Lāhījī chapter contains **reported citations**, not newly collated originals of all those works.
2. **Late natural philosophy:** full relevant text of Khwānsārī, Jilwa, Khayrābādī, Narāqī's *Sharḥ Ilāhiyyāt* and the print known as Jilwa's *Ithbāt al-ḥaraka al-jawhariyya*. A **bibliographical title** cannot determine its precise doctrine.
3. **Akbarian and Illuminationist originals:** Ibn ʿArabī, Qūnawī, Suhrawardī and their later interpreters: the language of divine containment/manifestation must be established textually, not inferred through Narāqī's reported school.
4. **Psychology and theology:** complete intellect/soul-origin arguments, knowledge of particulars, resurrection, providence and evil, *nafs al-amr* and practical philosophy, each with full cross-translation and apparatus.
5. **True critical edition:** transcription of manuscript witnesses (or at least complete independently examined edited versions), comparison of substantive variants, and provenance of each interpretive layer.

These gaps are real. A **critical comparative edition of dozens of multi-volume works cannot truthfully be declared complete** merely because eight chapters have been written. The present research demonstrates real material progress in full bounded units, explicit objections and replies, cross-source Latin comparisons, and some published textual variants.
