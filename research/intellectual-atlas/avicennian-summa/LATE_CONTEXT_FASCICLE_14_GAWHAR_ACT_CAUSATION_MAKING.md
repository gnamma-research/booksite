# New independent post-Ṣadrian source fascicle — Lāhījī, *Gawhar-i murād*, Article II, Book III, Sections I–II, pp. 278–282

**Working close English rendering with exact source boundaries · 31 October 2026**

## Editorial placement and why the full question matters

**Work:** ʿAbd al-Razzāq al-Lāhījī, *Gawhar-i murād* (Persian). **Division:** Article II (مقاله دوم), Book III (باب سوم), **Section I: the meanings of fiʿl, ījād, takwīn, ṣanʿ, khalq, jaʿl, ibdāʿ, ikhtirāʿ (pp.278–279)**; **Section II: whether what proceeds from God is quiddity, existence, or their attribution (pp.280–282)**. Print and transcription: Nashr-i Sāya / Ahlulbayt Library [278](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/17438/278?lang=ar), [279](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/17438/279?lang=ar), [280](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/17438/280?lang=ar), [281](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/17438/281?lang=ar), [282](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/17438/282?lang=ar).

**Immediate context:** the preceding Book II addresses the Necessary Existent and divine attributes. Book III now asks what it *means* for God to cause other things and, more exactly, *what* is produced. Its Section III immediately afterwards discusses the nature of procession, and Section IV the number and hierarchy of intellects. Consequently the question of **what is made** must be read before the cosmological mechanism of making.

This is **not** another translation of Lāhījī's separate *Shawāriq al-ilhām* gloss on the *Tajrīd*. The *Tajrīd* reader owns its independent lemma collation. Here the evidence is an original, independent Persian work following a different explanatory order.

## Section I, pp.278–279 — the meanings of production

## Page 278: what counts as giving existence?

**Context and working translation.** It was established earlier that a contingent thing needs a cause for the preponderance of its existence, and that the chain of causes depends upon the Necessary Existent. Thus all contingent existents ultimately depend on God, whether immediately or through intermediaries. *Ījād* means bestowing existence; in one usage *takwīn* means the same. Existence bestowed by a cause is broader than existence preceded by temporal nonbeing: the relevant priority can be **essential** rather than temporal. Hence even if an effect were temporally eternal, it would remain caused in this sense.

Lāhījī notes a difference of usage. Some theologians confine “acting,” “crafting,” or “creating” (*fiʿl, ṣanʿ, khalq*) to producing something after temporal nonbeing, whereas philosophers do not require temporal beginning in every such use. The resulting terminology should not settle the substantive question of eternal dependence by fiat.

**Short original anchor:** «وجود مفاد ، اعمّ است از اين كه مسبوق باشد به عدم زمانى يا به عدم ذاتى».

**Doctrinal function:** The distinction between temporal and essential beginning is the presupposition of the following *jaʿl* controversy. A contingent being cannot cease needing its cause simply because it has existed for some time.

## Page 279: *ibdāʿ*, *takwīn*, *iḥdāth* and simple/compound making

**Working translation.** The philosophers reserve *ibdāʿ* for bestowing existence without prior **matter or duration**, treating it as the most eminent mode of giving being. Bringing into being through prior matter can be called *takwīn* in a narrower sense, and existence after prior duration *iḥdāth*. On that usage separate intellects are produced by *ibdāʿ*, material things by *takwīn*, and daily temporal events are *muḥdath*. Some theologians further include awareness and will in the notion of action, thus withholding it from spontaneous natural occurrences such as the heating of fire or illumination of the sun. Others deny that a cause must necessitate its effect and therefore resist applying “cause” to God under the philosophers' stronger definition.

*Ikhtirāʿ* is likened to making without an exemplar taken from outside. **Compound making** is making a thing be qualified by existence, like a dyer making a garment coloured. **Simple making** is producing the very thing—or, in one debated formulation, the thing's existence—so that after its production the thing is said to exist.

**Short original anchor:** «و اما جعل بر دو گونه است : مركّب و بسيط».

**Connection:** This is a carefully staged semantic distinction. When the subsequent section says “the made thing is a quiddity,” it does not mean merely that the *concept* of an essence is physically manufactured. The subject is the actual thing issued by a maker.

## Section II, pp.280–282 — what is made: quiddity, existence, or attribution?

## Page 280: the three alternatives and Lāhījī's stated preference

**Question heading rendered:** “Whether what really proceeds and is made by the Most High is the quiddity of things, the existence of things, or the quiddity's qualification by existence.”

**Working translation.** Each of these three alternatives has its advocates. Lāhījī explicitly describes the first as the view of the **critical investigators**, a position that must be reported as his own *here*. Existence in the sense of a general concept abstracted by intellect is a mental consideration; it is not a self-subsisting external entity. By contrast what is originally made must be some actual, externally established reality. Therefore the actual produced quiddity, rather than an independently existing concept of being, is what proceeds from the maker. The intellect subsequently abstracts from its external actuality the common conception “exists.”

He then asks the advocate of making *existence* whether “existence” names that merely mental concept or a supposed actual reality answering to it. If the former, it cannot be the primary external made object. If the latter, that actual ground must be identified: in contingent things it is the **quiddity as actually produced and related to its maker**. If this is all that “making existence” means, the dispute over its name approaches a **verbal disagreement** rather than the assertion of a second separately produced entity.

**Short original anchor:** «احتمال اوّل ، مذهب محققين است».

**Important interpretive limit:** Calling an actual quiddity the thing produced is not equivalent to asserting that essence exists independently *before* its production. And “existence as abstract concept” is not equivalent to saying that nothing real exists.

## Page 281: why simply making the attribution leads to regress

**Working translation.** Lāhījī continues: if someone says that a real actuality distinct from quiddity is what is called existence, yet ultimately means only the same actual produced quiddity, the dispute may collapse into differing names. But if a third candidate—*the quiddity's attribution to existence*—is what is made in itself while neither quiddity nor existence is made in itself, several problems arise.

If the two relata occur without being made, they are already actual, and in that respect independent of the cause; their contingent existence becomes unintelligible. Suppose, as an attempted reply, that each relatum is only accidentally produced through the origination of the attribution. Then the attribution itself would have to be an actually realised thing issued from a maker, exactly the sort of entity Lāhījī calls a “quiddity.” If that attribution must in turn be constituted by a further attribution to its existence, infinitely many further created quiddities would be required in every supposedly simple case. Moreover, when one thinks one has made a given thing, it would always be another thing—the existence attribution—that is truly made.

The chapter closes this chain by identifying a fourth conceivable possibility: a **compound making of a quiddity's identity itself**, for example making a human being be human. That further possibility is directly addressed on the next page.

**Short original anchor:** «اگر مجعوليّت اين ماهيت نيز كه اتّصاف است ... ترتّب ماهيات غير متناهيّه».

**What is answering what:** The regress is aimed at the third **attribution-first** thesis. It is not a blanket denial of intellectual distinction or of relations grounded in realities.

## Page 282: “the maker did not make the apricot apricot”

**Working translation.** The fourth proposal is that the maker makes a quiddity acquire precisely the quiddity it already is—for example, makes a human a human. Its defect is obvious. Lāhījī appeals to Avicenna's response to his student Bahmanyār: “The maker did not make the apricot an apricot; rather he made the apricot exist.” Yet Lāhījī warns that this should **not** be interpreted as Avicenna endorsing a compound creation of an essence plus a distinct existence-attribute, as an attribution-first theory would demand.

His own reading is that the maker produces the actual essence simply; being actual is not a separately made appendage superadded to the production of that essence. Once the essence issues from the maker, it is necessarily established outside the mind, and **that establishment** is what we predicate as its existing. Thus the quoted expression means that the apricot's identity is not produced by a prior apricot identity being modified; rather, the apricot itself is brought about.

**Original Arabic phrase quoted within the Persian witness:** «الجاعل لم يجعل المشمش مشمشا ، بل جعل المشمش موجودا».

**Attribution:** This is **Avicenna as quoted and interpreted by Lāhījī**. The independent Avicennian source and exact wording must be checked before it is counted as a separate direct witness to Avicenna.

## What follows and why this is a distinctive late source

Section III (pp.283–289) immediately moves from **the meaning of production** to **how effect proceeds from cause**, reporting and critically comparing Akbarian/Sufi, philosophers' and philosophical-mystical expressions of existence. Section IV (pp.290–297) then develops the order of intellects, Section V (pp.298–300) explains elemental forms and mixtures, and Sections VI–VIII explain the Active Intellect, time-specific causal dispositions and divine agency.

A reader who begins at the tenth intellect in Section VI, or who extracts just the apricot line, misses the systematic movement from *what is made* → *how procession is conceived* → *how mediated causes are ordered* → *how matter becomes prepared* → *which source bestows forms*.

- **Full five-page bounded chapter sequence:** Sections I (pp.278–279) and II (pp.280–282) are translated here as connected **working English argumentative renderings**. They are not a word-for-word Persian critical edition. The historical sections' boundary and order are checked against the digital Persian text.

- **Major potential tension to investigate:** In independent *Gawhar* II.3.II the primary created object is the actual **quiddity** under a specific construal of being; Narāqī's *Lamaʿāt* (pp.72,82) speaks of **particular existence** as fundamental. Lāhījī's position must not be silently replaced by the label “all late Avicennism is existence-first,” nor uncritically collapsed into his separate *Shawāriq al-ilhām* treatment of *jaʿl*.
- **No duplicate Tajrīd edition:** The *Tajrīd* project already contains extensive *Shawāriq* material. This separate Persian source is the **new original witness**. The relation is a philosophical comparison between *two works of the same author*, not a claim of two manuscript copies of one text.
