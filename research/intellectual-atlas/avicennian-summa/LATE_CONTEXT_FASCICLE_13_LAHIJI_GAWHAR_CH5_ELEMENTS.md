## I. The question and the reported rival explanation — p.298

**Question:** If the lower world shares a common material basis, why do the four elements possess *different* substantial forms? By what processes do the elements combine into mixed bodies disposed for the forms of more complex substances?

**Rival account, explicitly reported from Ibn Sīnā's *Shifāʾ*, not endorsed by Avicenna:** Certain people professing natural philosophy maintained that circular motion of the celestial sphere brings it into frictional contact with an initially stationary body inside it. The contact heats the outer part of that body until it becomes **fire**. The remote central part remains relatively motionless, becomes cold and dense, and becomes **earth**. Between them, the part closer to the fire is less intensely hot and moist, yielding **air**, while that closer to earth is colder and moist, yielding **water**. Thus differences in heat, density, distance and contact allegedly explain the substantial coming-to-be of the four elements.

This account, if read without the introductory attribution and subsequent criticism, could be falsely represented as **Lāhījī's** or **Avicenna's adopted theory**. It is instead **the view targeted by the ensuing objection**.

*Original text anchor:* «شيخ ابو على ابن سينا نقل كرده در شفا از جماعتى منتسبين به علم حكمت». [Printed p.298](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/17438/298?lang=ar).

## II. Avicenna's hylomorphic objection and the separate form-bestower — p.299

**Reported Avicennian reply:** After relating the friction-and-cooling hypothesis, Avicenna judges it unsound. The hypothesis tacitly requires that a body first exist with no **specific substantial form**, and only later acquire fire, earth, air or water through movement and rest. Such a wholly indeterminate body, possessing merely generic corporeality but no specific form, is impossible. Corporeality in the abstract is like a **genus**; specific elemental forms stand to it as determining **differentiae**. Just as an actual genus does not subsist apart from any differentia, matter cannot remain as an actual body without any such constitution.

**Positive alternative attributed to Avicenna and endorsed in Lāhījī's exposition:** Both the material basis of the elements and their differing substantial forms proceed through the **tenth intellect**, with cooperative causal roles assigned to the celestial spheres. That cooperation may involve **four spheres**, groups of celestial spheres or one sphere under four distinct relations. The rival explanation of heat and cold may still describe **why bodies occupy or are conserved in particular places**; it does *not* explain the original conferral of their specific forms.

**Further citation to *al-Mabdaʾ wa-l-maʿād*:** As elemental bodies undergo generation and corruption, their proximate principles must include something receptive of alteration. An intellect by itself cannot be their sole proximate natural principle. The shared matter is correlated with a common celestial character, identified in the historical cosmology with circular motion, while the diversity of elemental forms requires diversified causes and motions. This **real causal role of physical change** must not be deleted when discussing the higher source of form.

*Original text anchors:* «و بعد از نقل اين كلام حكم كرده به عدم صحّت اين كلام»; «صور عناصر نيز فايض شود ... از عقل عاشر به معاونت افلاك». [Printed p.299](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/17438/299?lang=ar).

## III. Common matter, material actualisation, and mixtures — p.300

The different directions and rates of celestial motions, as understood in this historical account, differentiate the relevant causal preparations. **Matter cannot subsist without some form**, so its constitution cannot depend only on the common nature of the celestial spheres. On the other hand, since the matter persists across the replacement of this or that elemental form, no *single particular* currently existing form can, by itself, be the sole permanent ground of matter. Thus its persistence is accounted for by **both** the higher common causal nature and some constitutive form; Lāhījī does not posit bare unformed matter as an actually existing intermediary.

He then classifies causes of **mixture** into two types:

1. Differences in the relations between terrestrial elements and celestial influences. By his example, the sun's alignment with a region gives illumination, illumination gives heat, heat produces rarefaction or upward displacement, that displacement removes a body from its usual place, and bodies thereby mix.
2. The natural powers and configurations that proceed, in his cosmology, from the heavens into the natures, forms and souls of bodies. These powers move their own matter and other matter—for example, nutritive powers—and consequently bring about mixtures and temperaments.

Different quantities and qualities of the constituent elements produce **different temperaments**, hence diverse degrees and kinds of material preparedness. Finally, **different specific forms of compounds** are said to emanate from the Giver of Forms in correspondence with that differentiated preparedness. These final sentences provide the immediate premise of **Section VI**, where Lāhījī argues that the responsible separate source for the rational soul and all species is the tenth intellect.

*Original text anchor:* «به حسب استعدادات مختلفه ، صور مركّبات متخالفه از واهب الصور فايض شود». [Printed p.300](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/17438/300?lang=ar).

## Notes on the textual edition

- The *Shifāʾ* reference printed in Lāhījī's edition is **Ilāhiyyāt IX.5** (the cited edition includes p.536 in its own pagination); the existing [continuous working English translation of IX.5](PRIMARY_TRANSLATION_FASCICLE_D_AVICENNA_IX5.md) gives the larger Avicennian context. **Do not regard these as one edition with a shared page numbering**.
- The printed edition at p.298 records omission of the word «مركبات» in A/B/C and several minor differences about bodily movement and temperature; p.299 records «صور» for «صورت» (B/C), «تخصص» for «تخصيص» (C), and the absence of «مستديره» (C); p.300 records «نسيب» for «نسبت» (B/C). These are **reported editorial variants**, not independently inspected manuscripts.
- **The argument's four levels are separate:** (i) intrinsic form constituting the composite; (ii) natural physical action and mixture; (iii) celestial influences in medieval cosmology; (iv) tenth-intellect mediated bestowal. These distinctions allow meaningful comparison with Aquinas while respecting the historical account.
- **Exact completion boundary:** This document supplies a connected working English rendering of the **three-page complete Section V**, reconstructed from its page-ordered original Persian paragraphs. It does **not** constitute a bilingual line-by-line diplomatic edition or independent Persian manuscript stemma, nor does it independently collate the *Shifāʾ* and *Mabdaʾ wa-l-maʿād* citations.
