## The issue

The doctrine of **latent fire** (*kumūn al-nār*) seeks to explain heating as the entry or manifestation of previously concealed fiery substance. Khayrābādī challenges it by arguing that heating can arise through the **transformation** (*istiḥāla*) of an already present material constitution, not merely by ingress of invisible preexisting fire. Five phenomena are offered; the argument is a piece of nineteenth-century Indian scholastic **natural philosophy**, not yet evidence of his full position on the Active Intellect.

### Five transmitted objections — new English rendering

1. **Heat generated by violent motion.** Dry solids become hot when rubbed without a detectable foreign fire entering; analogous changes occur in rarefied air through forced compression/expansion and in violently agitated liquids. If every rise of temperature required imported fiery substance, such cases would be difficult to explain.
2. **The permeability test.** When comparable liquids are heated in two vessels, one dense and the other porous, the latent-fire theory predicts that the porous vessel should admit the fiery influence faster. The observed outcome, he argues, does not follow that prediction.
3. **The sealed vessel test.** A carefully closed vessel can become intensely hot although it lacks the entrance and exit paths supposedly required for a substantial influx of fire replacing other contents.
4. **The bursting vessel.** When a tightly sealed flask containing water is heated, pressure develops until it may burst. The production of heat and pressure within the enclosure, without a commensurate exchange of imported fire and departing water, indicates internal transformation and generation. Khayrābādī notes that this argument and the preceding one overlap but are not logically identical: one establishes transformation, the other transformation **together with new generation**.
5. **Ice cooling the region above it.** The colder parts do not naturally rise upward on their own, and no extraneous forcing agent is present; the cooling is better described as a transformation in the affected medium rather than a migration of a latent cooling substance.

**Primary Arabic anchor phrases reproduced very briefly:** `الحركة العنيفة` (“violent movement”); `الاستحالة والكون معا` (“transformation and generation together”); `فإنه هو الاستحالة` (“for it is transformation”). The complete nineteenth-century passage was quoted in the linked modern study; this annex does not republish that modern book's extended quotation.

## Critical analysis: what is demonstrated and what is not

- **A meaningful Indian scholastic transformation debate exists.** The five arguments are an extended attempted refutation of an occult-substance interpretation of heating. They are empirically styled counterexamples within an Aristotelian–Avicennian taxonomy of forms, elemental natures, motion and qualitative alteration.
- **Do not overinterpret their success.** Modern thermal physics cannot be inferred from this argument; frictional heating and phase change require mechanisms different from those Khayrābādī's inherited Aristotelian framework offers. The argument establishes the structure of his position, not its scientific truth.
- **Disambiguate two changes.** Heating may be an *accidental qualitative alteration*; his reference to *kawn* in the bursting vessel goes further toward substantial generation. Neither is yet evidence that he rejects a separate intellect as the ontological giver of species forms. Such a conclusion requires the original natural-philosophical chapters on substantial forms.
- **Manuscript and printing discrepancy:** modern catalogues give Cairo 1904 as the printing; the cited 2023 book refers to 1322 AH. Neither printing was directly consulted here. The original work's composition is often placed in the 1840s and must be distinguished from publication.
- **Source-layer honesty:** the direct readable witness is **a 2023 modern quotation of a historical Arabic primary text**, with its original page references. The 1904 Arabic original remains uncollated; this is not one of the eleven directly translated Stage05 fascicles.
