## Question, previous step and source voices

**Immediately previous question (Section II, pp.280–282):** Is what is made an actual quiddity, an independently real existence, or the attribution of one to the other? Lāhījī argues that simple making of an actual quiddity suffices and that common existence is mentally abstracted, while invoking an Avicennian phrase through his interpretation.

**This Section III asks:** What is the *relation* of an effect to the First Cause? Does production require a substantial part of the First to turn into the effect, a separate act of making, an essentially ordered dependence, or a more subtle mode of manifestation? The subject is **causal procession and the truth of unity**, not a demonstration of the number of celestial intellects.

**Voices must remain separate:** (1) *Sufis* as Lāhījī reports their metaphysical claims; (2) some philosophically inclined exegetes offering three conceptual considerations; (3) Lāhījī's objections to those reconstructions; (4) a view described as the *taste of the mutaʾallihīn*; (5) the Peripatetic-philosophical framework of a perfect/superabundant First Cause; and (6) Lāhījī's own reflective analogy. None should simply be labelled “Lāhījī teaches absolute numerical identity of all beings.”

## Page 283 — report of Sufi unity and its non-discursive claim

**Working rendering.** Lāhījī opens by reporting the Sufi view: procession is a descent or self-disclosure of the cause into the level at which the effect is manifest, and this leads them to assert one reality of existence traversing all existents. Individual quiddities, on this account, are considerations, and actual things are manifestations of that one reality. They deny that this amounts to *incarnation* or a union of two already independent realities, since those notions already suppose two substantial beings.

He reports that practitioners maintain this cannot be comprehended by ordinary discursive reasoning alone; the appropriate experiential discipline involves detachment from self-conscious appropriation, all sensible and imagined phenomena, and exclusive attention to the divine through spiritual exercises and invocation. The resulting claimed disclosure is **not** presented as a standard syllogistic proof.

*Original-language anchor:* «صوفيّه بر آنند كه صدور معلول از علّت عبارت است از تنزّل علّت به مرتبه وجود معلول».

**Editorial function:** The discourse begins as a **report of another school**. It would be a mistake to isolate the phrase «لا موجود إلّا واحد» and attribute it to Lāhījī as an unqualified statement of his own philosophical ontology.

## Page 284 — epistemic status and the “light” of disclosure

**Working rendering.** Continuation of the reported experiential account: when such discipline is combined with correct beliefs and intellectual preparation, an innate intelligible illumination may become actual, allowing the practitioner to apprehend truths directly. Lāhījī counsels a combination of caution and sympathetic openness. He concedes the *possibility* that such a state may be veridical, while resisting the notion that the literary descriptions offered by its advocates give the concept in communicable, demonstrative form. The purpose of their words can be to assist qualified seekers and kindle longing rather than to provide exhaustive discursive definitions.

He warns against accepting every literal formulation uncritically or rejecting all of them indiscriminately. The supposed “light” is not an external beam newly entering the body but the activation and refinement of a native human capacity through disciplined purification of sensory, imaginative and estimative distractions.

*Original-language anchor:* «مراد ايشان از آن نور فايض ... نه نورى است از خارج».

**Reply to the earlier report:** Lāhījī thus neither embraces every literal metaphysical metaphor nor dismisses the possibility of an experiential referent. He differentiates the condition of **experience** from the warrant of **scholastic propositions** derived from descriptions of that experience.

## Page 285 — unity as a cipher for dependence and the analogy of a universal

**Working rendering.** Lāhījī proposes that the reported unity of existence may encode an especially intimate causal dependence: the effect depends so radically on the cause, which remains its sustaining principle, that figurative “descent” language expresses the relation. A natural warmth can be described as fire reduced to a certain mode, or wetness as water specified in a certain respect, without requiring that the resulting distinct modes have the numerical identity of every separate thing named.

He then describes a philosophical reconstruction advanced by some expositors of Sufism. A universal nature can be considered (a) under the condition that it has no individual determination, and then it is an intellectual matter that cannot subsist externally; (b) under the condition of a determination, and then it is this particular; or (c) without either condition, and then as natural universal it is present in the individual with its existence. Some propose to construe the *reality of existence* analogously with the determinations of finite essences.

*Original-language anchor:* «مراد ايشان از وحدت وجود ، رمزى است كه از كيفيّت صدور و شدّت ارتباط معلول به علّت».

**Critical question now raised:** Can existence, which is said to subsist of itself in the Necessary Existent, truly be handled as though it were a natural universal requiring individuation to exist?

## Page 286 — Lāhījī's objection to the universal analogy

**Working rendering.** The reconstructed model proposes three respects of the reality of existence: without any determination, as the Necessary Existent on the philosophers' account; without making determination a condition either way, as the Necessary Existent on the Sufi account; and under a definite finite quiddity, as the reality of each contingent thing. On this picture, finite quiddities would be limiting considerations annexed to one all-encompassing existent.

Lāhījī says the model is attractive in one respect but extremely distant from the truth in another. A natural universal considered in itself is indefinite, and it does not subsist outside the intellect without a concrete determination. By contrast, the alleged reality of existence is precisely that which exists through itself and has no need of acquired determinations to subsist. It is therefore mistaken to model it as a subject which **needs** the determination of finite quiddities in the way a universal nature requires individuation. This objection motivates a distinct, more exact reconstruction in the ensuing pages.

*Original-language anchor:* «اين توجيه اگر چه به وجهى قريب است ، اما به وجهى ديگر در نهايت بعد است».

**What the objection answers:** It rejects the *analogy between self-subsisting reality and an indeterminate natural universal*, not every legitimate comparison between unity and the dependence of actual things.

## Page 287 — the shared concept, the Necessary Reality and created truths

**Working rendering.** One should not identify the obvious, common *concept* of existence with the intrinsic reality by which something is actual. The same universal predicate “exists” does not itself stand as an independent extra-mental thing added to every individual quiddity. For contingent things, existing means having a determinate actuality capable of grounding that predicate; but that status is not supplied by their essence taken in separation. Only the Necessary Existent has that status intrinsically and without any relation to another. Other realities ground the predicate only through a definite dependence and relation to the Necessary Existent.

Lāhījī therefore offers a sense in which the true grounding reality of existence is one—the Necessary Reality—while the **realities of things that exist** remain multiple. He notes that some Sufi statements appear instead to say that the actual realities of all things themselves are numerically one; this should not be confused with saying there is only one *independent* existent. The so-called taste of the philosophically contemplative may agree that one reality subsists without need and other beings have derivative standing.

He then transitions into the standard philosophers' description of God as **perfect and beyond perfection**, whose overflowing agency does not add a perfection to the First.

*Original-language anchor:* «حقيقت وجود واحد باشد و حقايق موجودات متكثر».

**Key distinction:** A unity of **unconditioned existential ground** does not entail numerical identity of all dependent realities. This source argues for that differentiation and is directly relevant to post-Ṣadrian reception of metaphysics.

## Page 288 — perfect and superabundant, without acquired benefit

**Working rendering.** The *perfect* is an existent whose being and essential perfections are complete in itself, actually and by itself, rather than received from another; this applies strictly to the Necessary Existent. Other things either receive perfection from outside while having unrealised potentialities, as embodied human beings do, or possess actuality yet depend on a higher giver, as in separate intellects. The *superabundant* is so perfect that the existence and perfections of others also flow from it without diminution or need of acquired benefit.

This is then rendered through the analogy of an agent possessing a complete power of acting: once such an agent intelligibly conceives an order and its will fully determines it, the order is actualised. The entire good order of existents is already known to the Necessary Existent, with its particular temporal determinations. The perfection of divine willing is not improved through acts; the contingent order appears by reason of the first cause's sufficiency.

Lāhījī advances a more subtle comparison: external reality relative to God may be understood analogously to the mental field relative to a human knower—not because existing things are unreal illusions, but to express how dependent their actuality is on the superior cause's knowledge and power.

*Original-language anchor:* «پس وجودات و كمالات وجودات جميع ممكنات ... تراوشى بلا تشبيه».

**No overinterpretation:** The analogy of the world as to God's cognition **cannot** be taken to mean without argument that every created thing is merely a human-like mental image.

## Page 289 — a qualified unity, not identity of God with each thing

**Working rendering.** In a final reflective analogy, Lāhījī compares the dependence of things on Necessary Reality to the dependence of analytic components such as genus and differentia on the single nature in which they are understood. This suggests why the unity of the grounding existence might be discussed together with diverse created realities without the latter becoming unqualifiedly independent substances.

He cites a formulation from unnamed eminent figures: that the existence of the Necessary Existent is identical with the **existences of all existents considered collectively**, not identical with the existence of **each one singly**. He immediately breaks off with an expression of the difficulty of the subject: the pen has come this far and its head is broken.

**Original-language anchor:** «عين وجودات همه موجودات است ؛ نه عين وجود هر يك از موجودات».

**Limit:** The image or maxim is presented as a difficult speculative suggestion attributed to others. It does **not** establish an unqualified identity of God with a given created individual, and its formal ontological meaning remains to be argued.

### Textual and historiographical apparatus

1. **Source voice control:** Statements of Sufis, philosophical Sufi reconstructions and unattributed eminent figures are deliberately distinguished from Lāhījī's own rejoinders.
2. **Material translations here:** the whole seven-page Section III is rendered in the order its arguments occur. This is a page-based *working English philosophical translation*, not an independently established Persian manuscript-critical or line-aligned bilingual edition.
3. **Reported variants:** the printed edition includes A/B/C witness variants—for example p.286 `تعيين` / `تعيّن` and p.288 `ترشّحى` / `رشحى`. The original lettered witness identities and printed scans have **not** been independently collated.
4. **Independent work, not duplicate Tajrīd:** The separate *Tajrīd* project collates Lāhījī's *Shawāriq*. This *Gawhar* discussion follows a **new original textual chain** of made quiddity → unity and effect → intellect hierarchy → natural form-bestowal.
5. **Central post-Ṣadrian interpretive question:** How Lāhījī's account of a created actual quiddity (Section II) and one ultimate reality of existence (Section III) cohere with his distinct treatment of mental and extra-mental existence and with Narāqī's existence-first theory. This must be approached as a real argument, not repaired by labels like “Peripatetic,” “Akbarian,” or “Sadrian” without textual comparison.
