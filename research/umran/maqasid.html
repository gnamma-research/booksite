Maqāṣid · a guide to purposes and legal reasoning

What is the law seeking to secure?

The theory of maqāṣid al-sharīʿa provides principles for understanding legal benefits and harms. It is indispensable to the larger programme, but it is not a substitute for revelation, legal method, spiritual discipline or a complete philosophy of civilization.

Scholarly synthesis, edition v4. The five essentials are classical categories. Broader and contemporary extensions are labelled as such.

The classical essentials

Five indispensable goods

Al-Ghazālī's enumeration of religion, life, intellect, progeny and property became foundational to the juristic treatment of necessary interests. Their relevance to modern institutions is a matter for careful derivation—not a licence to declare any project obligatory.

Classical necessityحفظ الدين

Religion and revealed guidance

A core field of the five necessities

To establish and cultivate

Cultivate sound belief, worship, knowledgeable practice, sincerity and the capacity to transmit religious knowledge.

To safeguard

Address ignorance, misrepresentation, loss of qualified teachers, coercion and practices undermining religious responsibility.

Qualification: The full reality of knowing God and spiritual excellence is not exhausted by a juridical safeguarding category.

Related fields: religious teachingfuneralscontemporary curricula

Classical necessityحفظ النفس

Life and bodily security

A core field of the five necessities

To establish and cultivate

Develop competent medicine, appropriate care, reliable infrastructure and conditions of human safety.

To safeguard

Prevent avoidable death, serious illness, violence, unsafe service and acute deprivation.

Qualification: Distinguish health outcomes from professional headcounts and official targets.

Related fields: medical carewater sanitationdependent caremental healthemergency response

Classical necessityحفظ العقل

Intellect, knowledge and sound judgment

A core field of the five necessities

To establish and cultivate

Educate, cultivate reasoning and deliberation, transmit expertise, and support intellectual institutions.

To safeguard

Resist falsehood, avoidable ignorance, impairments and systems that degrade independent judgment.

Qualification: A philosophical programme of intellectual renewal is a proposed positive application, not a direct claim that every project is legally necessary.

Related fields: contemporary curriculareligious teachingai safetylivelihood training

Classical necessityحفظ النسل

Family, continuity and responsible care

A core field of the five necessities

To establish and cultivate

Sustain family responsibilities, upbringing, education, intergenerational support and secure households.

To safeguard

Prevent neglect, insecurity, exploitation and conditions obstructing the care of dependants.

Qualification: Do not identify flourishing with demographic or economic maximisation.

Related fields: dependent carehousehold sufficiencydisplaced shelter

Classical necessityحفظ المال

Property, trustworthy exchange and stewardship

A core field of the five necessities

To establish and cultivate

Make lawful livelihoods possible, protect the reliability of transactions, support honest crafts and durable institutions.

To safeguard

Prevent fraud, unjust taking, corrupt procurement and destruction of essential resources.

Qualification: Growth of wealth is not the final human end; lawful means, distribution and formation matter.

Related fields: food accesslivelihood traininghousehold sufficiencycommunity institutions

Sources: Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: al-Ghazālī and Aḥmad al-Raysūnī, study of al-Shāṭibī.

The degrees of interest

Essential, needed, and refining

The categories are not a ranking of people or occupations. Nor should the word “refinement” be mistaken for something morally trivial.

Ḍarūriyyāt

Indispensable interests

Goods whose serious failure undermines the basic religious and worldly order. Establishing these is a strong starting point in analysing public duties.

Necessity requires a defensible legal and factual determination.

Ḥājiyyāt

Relief of hardship

Conditions and arrangements that enable life to proceed with appropriate ease and remove significant difficulty without every absence causing total collapse.

Need is not synonymous with a verified local shortage.

Taḥsīniyyāt

Excellence and fitting conduct

Good customs, virtues, propriety and the quality of human activities. They matter greatly to the formation of persons, even when not classified as existential necessities.

The legal levels do not measure nearness to God.

Research caution:

These levels classify types of legally considered interests. They do not tell us, by themselves, who must change careers, which state should build a university, or which research institute deserves funding. Such conclusions require additional juristic and empirical reasoning.

The two directions of ḥifẓ

Build the good. Protect it from loss.

Al-Shāṭibī discusses preservation through the establishment of a good and through protection against its destruction. This is a much stronger basis for positive institutional development than a website focused only on emergencies.

AimPositive establishmentProtection against harm
ReligionCompetent worship, teaching, inherited sciences, spiritual practiceAgainst preventable loss of learning, manipulation of religious authority and corruption
LifeHealth systems, shelter, secure food and waterAgainst serious preventable danger, unsafe infrastructure and deprivation
IntellectEducation, reasoning, thoughtful public discourse and disciplined researchAgainst systematic ignorance, intellectual dependency and harmful uses of technology
FamilyFormation and care across generationsAgainst neglect, abuse and conditions undermining security of dependants
PropertyLivelihood, productive and honest crafts, reliable exchangeAgainst fraud, theft, exploitation and damage to essential resources

These contemporary examples are our applications of the distinction, not a claim that al-Shāṭibī named the corresponding modern institutions or technologies.

Beyond the classical enumeration

Broader ends and proposed extensions

Higher social and moral purposes are prominent in the wider tradition, particularly in Ibn ʿĀshūr's account of reform and sound social order. We keep them in view without falsely representing all ten of our categories as an agreed classical list of ten obligations.

Broader human endالتزكية والإحسان

Spiritual and moral formation

Islamic life and ethics beyond a five-item legal checklist

To establish and cultivate

Cultivate worship, remembrance, humility, sound habits, discernment and virtues practiced in family and public life.

To safeguard

Guard against using professional success or institutional power as substitutes for sincerity and responsibility.

Qualification: No metric, job ranking or outcome indicator can establish a person's nearness to God.

Related fields: religious teachingdependent care

Cross-cutting purposeالعدل والأمانة

Justice, trust and right governance

General public aims discussed in later maqāṣid and social-order literature

To establish and cultivate

Build reliable adjudication, trustworthy administration, equitable access and accountable institutions.

To safeguard

Resist domination, corruption, concealment of harms and arbitrary exclusion.

Qualification: A proposed governance model must be justified through evidence and juristic reasoning; not every reform is obligatory.

Related fields: community institutionshousehold sufficiencyai safety

Cross-cutting purposeصلاح العمران

Civilizational and institutional flourishing

General social-order and reform aims, particularly developed by Ibn ʿĀshūr

To establish and cultivate

Enable households, professions, schools, crafts, governments and associations to cooperate in legitimate goods.

To safeguard

Protect institutional continuity, just public order and access to essential provision.

Qualification: Growth, scale and control are not equivalent to the good of the people who inhabit institutions.

Related fields: community institutionsmedical carewater sanitationfood accessemergency response

Research programme / extensionالتجديد والإحياء

Intellectual and religious renewal

Proposed civilizational programme informed by tajdīd/iḥyāʾ literature

To establish and cultivate

Renew understanding through sound scholarship, critical reconstruction, curriculum, living teaching and serious engagement with contemporary questions.

To safeguard

Prevent loss of competent transmission, shallow imitation, fragmentation and ideology replacing scholarship.

Qualification: A general mandate to renewal does not establish that any particular translation, school or curriculum is binding.

Related fields: religious teachingcontemporary curriculacommunity institutions

Research programme / extensionعمارة الأرض

Ecological and material stewardship

A proposed application under the classical necessities, public benefit and responsible use of the earth

To establish and cultivate

Support water, land, food systems, healthy settlements and intergenerational care of common resources.

To safeguard

Prevent pollution, depletion, unsafe infrastructure and damage that undermines necessary human provision.

Qualification: Not presented as a historically unanimous sixth canonical necessity.

Related fields: water sanitationfood accessemergency response

For general public aims and reform, consult Ibn ʿĀshūr, Treatise on Maqāṣid. For academic discussion of wider objectives and their diversity, see Islamic Normative Legal Theory: Framework and Applications.

Connecting purposes to practice

From a maqṣad to a legitimate programme

  1. Identify the aim. Explain its textual and juristic basis, including any disagreement.
  2. Identify positive and protective dimensions. What must flourish, and what harms must be averted?
  3. Identify the distinct function. What must a family, city or larger community actually possess or access?
  4. Identify an institution or programme. What real arrangement might advance that function without violating other duties?
  5. Evaluate evidence. Is the programme effective? Is any relevant provision inadequate? What alternatives exist?
  6. Connect suitable people. Identify training, skills and circumstances without assigning spiritual rank or a universal personal obligation.
