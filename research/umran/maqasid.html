Purpose and maqāṣid

What are the projects for?

A life directed towards God is the ultimate end. The *maqāṣid* of Islamic law help us judge the goods that collective action must secure. Neither a project nor a profession is its own final purpose.

A short orientation. Read the scholarly notes and qualifications.

Classical foundations

Five indispensable goods

The classical necessities concern religion, life, intellect, family/lineage, and property. They guide reasoning about legal interests; they do not exhaust the ends of spiritual life.

Classical necessity

Religion and revealed guidance

Living guidance, worship and religious learning.

Classical necessity

Life and bodily security

The protection and provision of human life and health.

Classical necessity

Intellect, knowledge and sound judgment

Sound thought, education and intellectual competence.

Classical necessity

Family, continuity and responsible care

Family continuity, upbringing and care.

Classical necessity

Property, trustworthy exchange and stewardship

Lawful livelihood, property and honest exchange.

Three levels of need

Ḍarūriyyāt

Essential

Basic religious and worldly order cannot be sustained without them.

Ḥājiyyāt

Needed

Conditions that relieve hardship and support stable life.

Taḥsīniyyāt

Refining

Virtue, excellence, fitting conduct and the quality of practice.

Build the good; protect it from loss

A community needs more than emergency repair. Religious learning must be taught; intellectual traditions must be transmitted; dependable livelihoods, care and institutions must be cultivated.

Each purpose raises two related questions: what establishes and develops this good? And what prevents its corruption or destruction? That distinction underlies the four great projects.

Broader ends

What the five do not exhaust

We also study moral formation, justice, ʿumrān, renewal and stewardship. These are wider aims or proposed applications, not five additional universally agreed canonical necessities.

Broader aim / extension

Spiritual and moral formation

Spiritual and moral formation beyond measurable output.

Broader aim / extension

Justice, trust and right governance

Justice, trust and legitimate use of authority.

Broader aim / extension

Civilizational and institutional flourishing

Institutions that sustain a rightly ordered common life.

Broader aim / extension

Intellectual and religious renewal

Scholarly, ethical and intellectual renewal.

Broader aim / extension

Ecological and material stewardship

Responsible stewardship of shared material conditions.

Keep the levels distinct.

Maqāṣid helps clarify the good. Fiqh determines particular obligations. Empirical inquiry tests whether provision is sufficient. Vocational discernment asks what a particular person can appropriately do. None establishes spiritual rank from a job title.
