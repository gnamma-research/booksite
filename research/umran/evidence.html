Evidence desk · research edition 2026.11

From a reported problem to an assessed obligation.

Social indicators, administrative records, service targets and crowdfunding appeals are different kinds of evidence. We must examine their relation before claiming that a community is failing to meet a particular obligation.

Four field studies, four evidentiary positions

01 · Pulau Pinang (Penang), Malaysia · state

Penang: household security and welfare provision

Baseline + provider activityNo personal legal ruling

Partial: documented condition and distribution, no coverage conclusion

1.9%Households below the official absolute poverty line2024 · Penang stateSource ↗
RM7,386Median monthly gross household income2024 · Penang stateSource ↗
RM192.73mReported total zakat distribution2025 · Penang stateSource ↗
45,831Reported zakat recipients (persons)2025 · Penang stateSource ↗

What is established

A measured poverty incidence and an aggregate administrative account of zakat distribution are both available. The MASA@Sungai Nibong study describes a targeted household-centred intervention through waqf-linked accommodation, training and continued support.

What remains unknown

The two sources do not identify the same individual households, and their dates and concepts differ. There is no verified comparison of eligible household needs, benefit adequacy, exclusions, access, service quality or outcomes across the state. Recipient persons are not interchangeable with households. Zakat distribution is not the entire welfare system.

Research standard, missing evidence and possible contributors

Proposed standard of adequate provision

A defensible locally defined household sufficiency standard, distinguishing official poverty-line income from juristic and administrative ḥadd al-kifāyah criteria, plus evidence of accessible and reliable provision.

What must be measured next

  • The actual Penang zakat ḥadd al-kifāyah criteria and eligibility rules by household composition
  • Unduplicated numbers of eligible households, recipients, applicants and unsuccessful applicants
  • Value, duration, timing and material adequacy of assistance
  • Disaggregated local conditions, access barriers and non-zakat welfare provision
  • Independent household follow-up, financial durability and programme evaluations

Possible contributors

Zakat authority · Waqf managers · Social workers · Household-assessment specialists · Livelihood programme evaluators

Possible actions—not automatic duties

  • Evaluate household adequacy rather than just funding volume
  • Improve case management and eligibility access
  • Mentor and train livelihood programme staff
  • Support careful, consent-based follow-up

Important limits

  • Different population units and years must not be used as a coverage numerator and denominator
  • State statistics cannot certify the need of each locality or individual family
  • A qualitative case study does not demonstrate a causal state-wide impact

Normative question

What duties of provision, zakat administration and protection of those in need bear upon different actors? Farḍ kifāya cannot be inferred from a poverty percentage alone.

Related communal functions: Household sufficiency · livelihood training

02 · Malaysia · national

Malaysia: foundational learning and competent teaching

Outcomes observed · service gap unresolvedNo personal legal ruling

Outcome concern documented; provider sufficiency unknown

35%Reached at least PISA Level 2 in mathematics2025 · MalaysiaSource ↗
44%Reached at least PISA Level 2 in reading2025 · MalaysiaSource ↗
54%Reached at least PISA Level 2 in science2025 · MalaysiaSource ↗

What is established

The OECD's 2025 PISA country note reports comparatively low shares of tested 15-year-olds attaining minimum proficiency in mathematics, reading and science. It includes comparisons to OECD averages and trend measures.

What remains unknown

We do not know from this comparison which forms of provision are inadequate: teacher supply, instructional quality, curriculum alignment, household support, language factors, school access or other conditions. Nor can these results be transferred to a particular mosque school or specialised religious curriculum.

Research standard, missing evidence and possible contributors

Proposed standard of adequate provision

For the specified learner population, a defensible standard of learning, access and continuity, supplemented with relevant curricular and educational quality indicators; then assess what supply conditions would meet it.

What must be measured next

  • Subject-specific and locality-specific attainment with uncertainty estimates
  • Existing teaching capacity, qualifications, contact time, attendance and access
  • Curriculum quality, assessments, school support and effective interventions
  • Comparative evidence on teacher preparation and remediation
  • Longitudinal evaluation of proposed curricula and learner development

Possible contributors

Teachers · Teacher educators · Curriculum researchers · Assessment specialists · School and community administrators

Possible actions—not automatic duties

  • Pilot and evaluate targeted teaching materials
  • Train practitioners in diagnosis and remediation
  • Support qualified curriculum development and teacher mentorship
  • Avoid launching a new institution until its comparative advantage is demonstrated

Important limits

  • PISA proficiency is a specific assessment construct, not the whole of education
  • A low learning outcome is not necessarily a teacher headcount shortage
  • National sample data do not measure access or outcomes in a particular subcommunity

Normative question

Which forms of knowledge and educational provision are obligatory or otherwise needed, for which populations? PISA does not itself establish a particular Islamic legal classification.

Related communal functions: contemporary curricula · religious teaching
Sources and dates

03 · Malaysia · national

Malaysia: mental-health care and specialist capacity

Source-defined workforce target gapNo personal legal ruling

Documented ministry psychiatrist ratio below stated target; broader access unresolved

4.6%Depressive symptoms reported among adults2023 · MalaysiaSource ↗
1:69,860MOH psychiatrist-to-population ratio1 April 2026 · MalaysiaSource ↗
1:40,000MOH stated psychiatrist ratio target1 April 2026 · MalaysiaSource ↗
40MENTARI community mental-health centres1 April 2026 · MalaysiaSource ↗

What is established

NHMS 2023 provides a population-health indicator. The Ministry of Health reported 445 ministry psychiatrists and a psychiatrist-to-population ratio of 1:69,860, below its target of 1:40,000, alongside 40 MENTARI centres as of 1 April 2026.

What remains unknown

The figures do not quantify national unmet episodes of treatment, staffing of non-MOH providers, patient waiting times, distribution across regions, quality of multidisciplinary care or treatment outcomes.

Research standard, missing evidence and possible contributors

Proposed standard of adequate provision

Clearly defined access, clinical quality, safe referral and appropriate specialist capacity, including the ministry workforce benchmark as one partial indicator rather than a complete sufficiency criterion.

What must be measured next

  • Public and private specialist services by district and population
  • Service volumes, wait times, referrals and discontinuation rates
  • Case-mix, supervision, staffing distribution and multidisciplinary capacity
  • Patient outcomes and appropriate safeguarding
  • Professional training and retention data with local underserved subpopulations

Possible contributors

Psychiatrists · Clinical psychologists · Mental-health nurses · Clinical supervisors · Primary care and social care partners · Service administrators

Possible actions—not automatic duties

  • Increase appropriate specialist training and supervision
  • Improve equitable access, referrals and service continuity
  • Support independent quality and workforce evaluation
  • Establish services only after feasibility and workforce planning

Important limits

  • The government target does not, on its own, constitute a juridically binding threshold
  • Ministry staff numbers do not measure every provider or service
  • A prevalence survey and workforce target are complementary, not directly interchangeable denominators

Normative question

Which necessary forms of clinical provision and prevention of grave harm require collective or public arrangements, and through which qualified institutions?

Related communal functions: mental health · medical care

04 · Bangi, Selangor, Malaysia · local

Bangi: educational crowdfunding as a field-research lead

Organiser's claim · unverifiedNo personal legal ruling

Organiser claims only; no independent local need or effect established

53Qurʾān classes stated by campaign organiserCampaign archive, status: closed · Malaysia, claimed programme footprintSource ↗
12Science and mathematics classes stated by organiserCampaign archive, status: closed · Malaysia, claimed programme footprintSource ↗
587+Children reportedly provided with supportCampaign archive, status: closed · Malaysia, claimed programme footprintSource ↗

What is established

A closed LaunchGood appeal by Pertubuhan Kecemerlangan Pendidikan Ummah describes free education and support for children described as asnaf and poor, and advertises Qurʾān, science and mathematics provision.

What remains unknown

The campaign claims have not been independently audited here. We lack dated enrolment registers, verified learning outcomes, coverage, cost per learner, learner retention, programme capacity, and evidence of alternative services. This is not a current opportunity or donation recommendation.

Research standard, missing evidence and possible contributors

Proposed standard of adequate provision

Age-appropriate, competent, accessible learning with meaningful progress, protection and continuity for a clearly defined beneficiary population.

What must be measured next

  • Independent verification of participants, locations and teaching credentials
  • Educational baselines, comparable alternatives and accessibility barriers
  • Outcomes, retention, family needs and costs
  • Evidence of sustained institutional capacity and safeguarding
  • Applicable zakat/charity eligibility and legal compliance

Possible contributors

Teachers · Curriculum developers · Social workers · Programme accountants · Educational evaluators

Possible actions—not automatic duties

  • Observe or audit provision before recommending expansion
  • Improve learning evaluation and teacher formation
  • Assess family needs alongside instructional support

Important limits

  • Organiser-reported campaign quantities are not validated indicators of demand or impact
  • Campaign is marked closed; no current donation opportunity is claimed
  • Location of campaign impact does not prove every reported class is in the same district

Normative question

Does the necessary educational provision for identified learners require additional capacity, and are the proposed classes an effective and permissible response?

Related communal functions: religious teaching · contemporary curricula · Household sufficiency

The method

Six questions before recommending action

01

What good?

Specify the collective function and its juristic foundation. A job title or project is not an obligation.

02

For whom?

Identify the relevant population, place and period; do not generalise a national datum to a neighbourhood.

03

How much?

Define an evidence-based threshold of adequate competence, accessibility, quality and continuity.

04

What exists?

Map public, private, charitable and household provision; distinguish provider outputs from beneficiaries' outcomes.

05

What is missing?

Find the actual bottleneck, test alternatives and separate official performance targets from legal minima.

06

Who can act?

Identify qualified practitioners, trainers and institution-builders, without inferring a binding personal obligation.

What counts as a conclusion?

These records report observations and evidence readiness. A legally meaningful finding of inadequacy would require an appropriately established standard, sufficiently complete and comparable data about provision, expert review, and attention to alternative arrangements. A personal religious ruling would require separate qualified juristic judgment.
