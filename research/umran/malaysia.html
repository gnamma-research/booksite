Research laboratory · Malaysia

From communal duty to working institutions

Malaysian scholarship offers a particularly fruitful link between legal theory, educational formation, mosque and waqf institutions, zakat sufficiency, and concrete local programmes.

Source reading edition · 13 November 2026. Nine annotated records, with the boundaries of each claim kept explicit.

The central distinction

FARḌ KIFĀYA

A collective legal duty

What function must be adequately performed, and by whom? Legal obligation is established by recognised juristic reasoning, not by the appearance of a fundraising appeal.

ḤADD AL-KIFĀYAH

A threshold of household sufficiency

What level of resources and access is adequate when assessing poverty, welfare or zakat entitlements? It is informative for the social research, but it is a different legal and administrative concept.

Why the Putrajaya resolution matters. International Islamic Fiqh Academy Resolution 164 (2007), adopted at a meeting in Malaysia, treats human development as the cultivation of physical, mental, psychological and spiritual capacities. It links useful worldly sciences, educational curricula, institutional leadership and research to the needs of the community. It supplies a major normative framework, not a checklist of legally binding modern jobs.

Nine texts and research programmes

These include Malaysian studies, Malaysian-published research, and one international resolution adopted in Malaysia. They are not all Malaysian-authored studies.

Institutional resolution · 2007

Human Resource Development in the Muslim World

International Islamic Fiqh Academy · 18th session, Putrajaya, Malaysia

Contribution: Establishes a comprehensive model of human capacities—physical, intellectual, psychological and spiritual—and calls for competent leadership, integrated curricula, research, and useful sciences connected to communal needs.

Limit: A resolution of an international academy meeting in Malaysia, not an independently authored Malaysian empirical study; broad collective principles do not establish any particular local deficiency.

Method/source: Official institutional resolution

The Delights of Knowledge

Work and food sovereignty · 2023

The Importance of Agriculture and Its Role in Food Sovereignty

Rizky Febrian and Sarif Hidayatullah · TAFHIM (IKIM)

Contribution: Examines al-Ḥubayshī on agricultural work, self-reliance and food sovereignty, presenting an argument for agriculture’s communal importance.

Limit: This is a textual argument about agriculture, not evidence that every locality is obliged to produce all its own food; compare alternative supply and trade arguments.

Lifelong religious learning · 2024

Local economic institutions · 2024

Contribution: Uses documents and interviews to investigate how mosques facilitate economic support, including zakat, waqf and other resources, and proposes a locally administered assistance model.

Standards of material adequacy · 2025

Penilaian Ḥadd al-Kifāyah Berasaskan Maqāṣid al-Sharīʿah

Abdul Muzil Abd Rahim · Universiti Kebangsaan Malaysia

Contribution: Examines poverty and zakat eligibility thresholds across Malaysian jurisdictions and recommends attention to religious learning, preventive healthcare, participation and human development alongside material costs.

A case that can be tested

Household formation and institutional action in Penang

The MASA@Sungai Nibong research considers families, vocational capacity, supportive institutions and continuing mentorship together, rather than simply distributing one-off relief. It suggests a design hypothesis for this site: a household may need adequate immediate provision and a feasible longer-term pathway towards security and competent participation.

To turn this into a communal-sufficiency finding, we would still need to define the population, measure unmet needs, examine the programme’s reach and costs, compare other forms of provision, and identify its legal and institutional responsibilities.

A similar distinction applies to mosque-based welfare and the LaunchGood project observations: the fact that an institution fundraises is neither proof of communal deprivation nor evidence that it should be replicated elsewhere.

Further Malaysian work on adequacy standards. A 2020 USIM literature review by Azman Ab Rahman and colleagues compares the items used to establish ḥadd al-kifāyah in different Malaysian state zakat arrangements and argues for better-calibrated standards. This reinforces the need to record place, living costs, household situation and the administrative purpose of an adequacy measure. Read the USIM review ↗

Outside Malaysia: al-Shāṭibī’s theory of qualified contribution

The 2022 study by Mahā al-Muṭawwaʿ and Najm al-Dīn al-Zankī on collective duties in al-Shāṭibī was published in the University of Sharjah Journal, not a Malaysian journal. A separate Arabic research record at Al al-Bayt University develops a four-part account of endowment, capacity, inclination and personal characteristics. These are relevant to vocation matching, but neither is evidence of a Malaysian social shortage.

Research plus empirical indicators.

Historical, jurisprudential and qualitative Malaysian studies explain what provision might mean. The Evidence Desk now brings those arguments into contact with source-dated information from the Department of Statistics Malaysia, the OECD, state zakat distribution statistics, and the Ministry of Health. It does not infer a personal obligation from those data.

Bangi programme, separately from the scholarly corpus. A closed LaunchGood education appeal describes Qurʾānic, mathematics and science classes for children. Its reported numbers are organiser claims, not independently assessed Malaysian education data. See the worked evidence audit for what remains unknown.
