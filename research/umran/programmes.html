From principles to research and formation

Programmes for a living civilization.

These twelve linked research agendas explore how a tradition can form competent people, renew understanding, sustain its institutions and address the actual needs of households and communities. They are proposals for inquiry, not a ranked list of obligations or a list of open jobs.

Read the larger framework →Why intellectual renewal matters

A disciplined project sequence

Every programme must explain its ends and means.

A programme can be valuable without being obligatory, and an institution can exist without fulfilling its purpose. These five checks guide early development.

01 · PURPOSE

What good?

Specify the human or civilizational aim, with its normative basis and any disagreement.

02 · CAPACITY

What competence?

Define what people must know, be able to do, and learn through repeated practice.

03 · INSTITUTION

What arrangements?

Describe teachers, apprenticeships, resources, governance, funding and cooperation.

04 · LEARNING

What would show progress?

Collect evidence of real practice and outcomes, without equating metrics with spiritual merit.

Form the next generation of qualified scholars

Why this catalogue is different from the existing communal-needs index

The programmes are affirmative directions for the formation and renewal of intellectual, spiritual and social life. The needs index asks a narrower question: which functions are established duties, and are those functions presently adequately supplied? A proposed programme can link to a legal purpose without becoming a certified farḍ kifāya.

Two worked examples

How the programme architecture changes decisions

Example A: intellectual curricula

A community wishes to build a sequence in Arabic, logic, jurisprudence, philosophical theology and contemporary scientific reasoning. The initial objective is to cultivate competent understanding, not simply to fill a vacancy. Researchers first establish curricular ends, available teachers, methods and prerequisites; they then examine whether existing institutions supply the required competence. Only after that inquiry should they decide whether a new school, a revised course, a translation, supervised mentorship or public materials are the best means.

Example B: technology and agency

Advanced technology raises safety and institutional questions, but also alters the way people learn, judge and deliberate. A project concerned with intellect and justice could examine AI safety alongside the quality of teaching, the formation of professional judgment, and the authority delegated to automated systems. Its legal status depends on specific functions and harms, not on the general prestige or novelty of the technology.
