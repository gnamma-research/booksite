## 2. Previous philosophical context

Prior research distinguished *substantia* and accidents, actual substance versus organized unity, formal *respectus ad*, real predicamental and transcendental relation, *relatio rationis*, intrinsic and extrinsic denomination, the difference between the psychological act of considering and objective content, real modes in Poinsot, and Deely's technically "subjective" versus "suprasubjective" being. Aquinas's fundamental order involves distinct real principles, especially matter→form and composite essence→*esse*, but neither composition is independently established merely by naming its concepts. An army or university can be really organized without another natural substantial form.

## 3. Actual verified preliminary discoveries

### 3.1. Sharḥ al-Maqāṣid I, 99–108

The sequence matters: (a) how a quiddity is considered, including conditions of no-addition and indeterminate genus; (b) simple and composite natures; (c) real versus relative simplicity and composition; (d) what constitutes real composition and why mere juxtaposition does not; (e) genus/differentia versus matter/form; (f) perspectival composition and army; (g) mixtures of real and relational conceptual constituents; and (h) the separate argument about whether quiddities are caused and by what sense of *jaʿl*.

- I, 103: the opposition between real and *iḍāfī* simplicity/composition is **relative-to-part** and differs from later real versus *iʿtibārī* composition. Do not translate both by one contrast.
- I, 104: the real composite is characterized by an intrinsic unity with appropriate necessary properties/effects and need among relevant parts. Mere juxtaposition of stone and person is insufficient.
- I, 105: genus and differentia may be logically distinct without being distinct actual constituents of one extramental species. Some natures have real corresponding material/formal principles; others (e.g., black as colour plus specific differentia) do not. Logical composition is not proof of actual matter/form composition.
- I, 106: an army or ten taken as one can be *iʿtibārī*; the army's additional *collective form* is a consideration of intellect and not another extramental entity. However, the text explicitly says **fīhi taraddud** on whether other compounds (e.g. pharmacological mixtures) acquire new substantial forms or only a temperament of accidents; do not turn this into an unconditional doctrine of all composites. The same page distinguishes real/relational/mixed parts and gives *al-aqrab* and the bed.
- I, 107–108: debates over whether a possible quiddity is *made* by an agent: some arguments distinguish making the quiddity from producing its existence or combining its parts, and the text analyzes whether being-created is necessary to the quiddity or belongs to its actuality. Critical lexicon: **jaʿl, iḥtiyāj ilā al-fāʿil, iḥtiyāj ilā al-ghayr, taqarrur, wujūd**.
- I, 171–172: relation of material/formal concepts to a composite; *ṣūra* in a **second, looser sense** is an accidental *hayʾa* in matter for swords, beds, houses, not a substance inhering in their wooden or metallic matter. A cross-reference is REQUIRED to explain the mixed-composite bed of I 106.
- I, 173: distinctions of intended end, mentally present aim, produced external end, and differences between philosophers' secondary causal influence and thinkers treating events as immediately caused by God. This further affects use of "cause"/"effusion"/"form".

Primary accessible URLs: https://usul.ai/ar/t/sharh-maqasid/103 , /105 , /106 , /107 , /108 ; companion paginated text at https://almntqim.com/٤٥٠١_شرح-المقاصد-في-علم-الكلام-التفتازاني-ج-١/١٧٢_الصفحة . Printing and digital pagination must be cross-verified.

### 3.2. Mawāqif III, 35–40 and 54–60

A paginated HTML witness for the al-Ījī matn embedded in al-Jurjānī's *Sharḥ*, followed by later **Sharḥ**/**ḥāshiya** subsections, exists at https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/9296/38?lang=ar and adjacent verified pages. In this source, parenthesized authorial lemma, continuous explanation, and later marked commentary are DIFFERENT LAYERS. Which later glossator authored each margin needs verification from a print image.

- III 36–37: relevant categorial and logical divisions include defining an object by adding relation to a cause, patient, form, end, or effect. Some expressions include *iḍāfa* in their intelligible content while the related thing is not thereby a component of the named material object.
- III 38: **khārijan = ḥissan** in immediate phrase, while later gloss entertains *khārij* opposed to mind. Some parts can be distinguished by intellect without being separated from actual material constitution.
- III 39: *al-aqrab* understood through nearness and augmentation, and *sarīr* through real wooden pieces and a particular relational *tartīb* not independently intelligible. Also distinguishes real from iʿtibārī unity among quiddities.
- III 40: discussion continues into whether quiddities are made (*maʿjūliyya*); the text's notion of “causal dependence” must be separated from inherence or logical necessity.
- III 54: *qāʾim bi-nafsihi* means not requiring a *maḥall* as the locus upholding it; this does NOT mean uncreated/uncaused. A later gloss insists failure of **qiyām** (inherence in another part) does not entail failure of broader **iḥtiyāj** (need/dependence).
- III 55–57: genuine unity of a composite and how to infer logical composition from sharing an essential feature and differing essentially. Marginal discussion challenges a unidirectional proof from dependence to substantial unity: a cause and effect can depend on one another without becoming one substantial quiddity.
- III 58: army and medicinal compound serve as objections to “parts of real composite depend upon each other”. An attempted reply inserts a collective *hayʾa* as formal part. Jurjānī challenges this as over-generating a substantial whole even for arbitrarily juxtaposed stone+person.
- III 59: a proposed medicinal specific form following *mizāj* is distinguished from mere elemental mixture. Some claim a substance may include a real wood/ingredient part and an accidental arrangement (bed) without allowing a substance to be grounded in an accident inhering in that very substance. The latter is REPORTED and DISPUTED, not endorsed by all glosses.
- III 60: the army as mere **majmūʿ al-āḥād** **mawjūd bilā shubha**, but its unity as one independent substantial thing is *iʿtibārī*. If a non-actual relational part is added to the army as an ontological constituent, a problem arises: a composite partly constituted by nonexistent things cannot straightforwardly be claimed to exist as a further real quiddity. A gloss says it is wood/pieces actually subject to arrangement rather than an independent “arrangement-thing” that constitutes the bed.

These passages are not reducible to the statement “no armies exist extramentally”. The army exists as the plurality of real members; a further substantial form does not. A genuinely **real non-substantial configuration** remains a question requiring more textual and philosophical clarification.

### 3.3. Historical senses of jawhar/khārij

Tahānawī, Kashshāf I, pp.657–658: early kalām jawhar can designate the indivisible atom, whereas later usage can equate *jawhar* with a more general *ʿayn*. Some accounts allow immaterial jawhar. Philosophers define a jawhar as a possible existent **not in a subject**, opposed to accidents existing in a subject. There is also a dispute about whether the definition speaks of a quiddity's mode of existence if actual or requires present actuality. This is NOT one equivalent to Aquinas's *substantia* nor the atom across all uses.

Jurjānī, Taʿrīfāt p.16, defines *al-amr al-iʿtibārī* restrictively as existing only in the mind of a considerer while considered; Spiker cites Bannānī I p.102 for two meanings, including objectively grounded no-current-consideration actuality. Must examine distinct texts and contexts before harmonizing or alleging contradiction.

## 4. What is Spiker's thesis and how to verify it?

Spiker moves from “an army or bed is not itself another separately individuated substantial entity” to “relations and *iʿtibāriyyāt* have objective truth/actuality not reducible to individuals and psychological events”, then ultimately to a **prior intelligible/exemplary ground** in nafs al-amr. The first step may be well supported, but the intermediate steps require differentiated evidence and new premises. For each step classify:
- E = author explicitly says this.
- Q = the author qualifies it.
- R = the author reports an objection or a school without endorsing it.
- I = interpretive addition by Spiker.
- C = another source passage challenges the broader reading.
- U = page/edition not yet verified.

Never infer C just from a narrower reading; identify actual textual conflict. At issue for the bed is not whether arrangements are intelligible: it is precisely WHAT "new actual form" means and how it differs from an incidental *hayʾa*.

## 6. Historiographical and comparative guards

- A concept's being *ḥaqīqī* is not automatically substantial, and an accident's lacking separate subsistence is not non-existence.
- The word *ṣūra* can mean substantial form, logical specifying content, artifact configuration, or exemplary intelligible form.
- The word *khārij* sometimes means sensibly differentiated, sometimes outside the intellect, sometimes independently individuated subject.
- “What exists” (ontology), “what is true” (objective ground), “how it is known” (cognition), “what is named one thing” (unity) are separate questions.
- Do not read Suárez's mode theory or Poinsot's suprasubjectivity into an Arabic source before its own terms are settled.
- When later AT comparison begins, use Poinsot as main account of relational modes and of difference between actual relation and transcendental ordering; Suárez as alternative only where needed.
