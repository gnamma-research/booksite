## Part I. The conceptual classification: III pp35–40

### III 35–37 — physical and intellectual parts; how reference enters a definition

The discussion classifies parts of composite definitions and essences: overlapping and mutually exclusive, actual or negative, distinguished by reason or by sensible perception, and things constituted with reference to their agent, recipient, form or end. Because different kinds of 'part' are discussed in consecutive sentences, one cannot assume every part is a material piece.

The relational nature of one conceptual constituent (being a relative to cause, recipient, end, or effect) does not automatically mean that the whole is a separate real relational accident. The Arabic includes examples where *iḍāfa* belongs to what the term signifies while the object named need not physically contain the referent as a component.

**Research requirement:** For each example, state whether the relation is part of the *mafhūm*, a consideration of the referent, or an actual dependent feature of the object. Revisit the p36–37 distinctions when working through Ibn Sīnā's earlier technical meanings of genus and relational concept.

### III p38 — critically important: khārijan = ḥissan, then another sense

A main passage distinguishes parts which differ *ʿaqlan* (by intellection) from ones which differ *khārijan*, explicitly glossed **ḥissan** — sensibly. There is an example of substantial matter/form and an objection about human soul and body as sensibly differentiable; the gloss raises an alternate reading.

A later gloss then says if *khārij* were instead understood as **that opposed to *dhihn***, hylomorphic matter and form would count as extramental constituents, although they were placed among items known by intellect rather than two separately observable bodies.

**Exact first key words:** 'أو خارجا أي حسا' and 'وإن أريد بالخارج ما يقابل الذهن'.

**Meaning:** A single word *khārij* has at least two *operational* senses in this local discussion:
- X1: distinguished by sense, in a contrast to the intellect as an epistemic power.
- X2: outside mind, in a contrast of modes of being.
The following claims cannot be equated: “cannot be distinctly sensed”, “not external to mind”, “not an individually subsisting thing”.

Some commentary calls a human compounded from soul and body *iʿtibārī* within the local division (with different interpretations of soul and whether body without soul can be sensed). This is a warning that *iʿtibārī* cannot have one universal sense 'artificial collective'. The local reference may be about the analysis of constituent parts, not substantive denial of the living human's unity.

Source: https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/9296/38?lang=ar

### III p39 — nearness and the bed

The text discusses *al-aqrab*, constructed as **al-qurb wa-l-ziyāda fīhi** (nearness and its increase), making the referent a relational conceptual compound. It also discusses the bed composed of actual wood and a specific arrangement **tartīb makhṣūṣ**, which is relationally intelligible. Note the terms **qurb, ziyāda, tarkīb, ḥaqīqī, iḍāfī, iʿtibārī, tajarrud** should be glossed with previous p38 definitions.

Do not take the philosophical conclusion to be 'the bed is a further natural substantial unit'. What is established here is a category of *mixed conceptual components*. The deeper question 'do such components make up a substantial nature' is reopened in III pp54–60.

Source https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/9296/39?lang=ar

### III p40 — continuity into the question of making

The argument does not stop after nearness and bed. It examines *maʿjūl*, whether the quiddity itself is made or only its external actuality, and objections concerning things not characterized by actual positivity. This links the composite's 'form' to the explanatory status of a creator/agent, a different question from classification of its defining components. 

Source https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/9296/40?lang=ar

## Part II. Unity, dependence, substantiality: III pp54–60

### III p54 — jawhar, dhāt, ṣifa, qiyām, and iḥtiyāj

Opening claim divides a composite into a **dhāt** (if qāʾim bi-nafsihi) or **ṣifa** (if qāʾim bi-ghayrihi). The discussion must not be read as 'uncausable substance' vs dependent accident.

**Exact key context:** 'معنى القيام بنفسه أن لا يحتاج في وجوده إلى محل يقومه' — in this context self-standing means not needing a locus of inherence to underlie existence. It then says 'كالسرير على تقدير تركبه من الخشب والهيئة' — **as with a bed, on the supposition that it is composed of wood and configuration**.

The conditional *ʿalā taqdīr* is essential: it is not unconditional proof that a bed has a new substantial essence. The page also warns that the actual underlying constituent can need another without inhering in it: **'انتفاء القيام الذي هو أخص لا يستلزم انتفاء الاحتياج الذي هو أعم'**. Inherence is narrower than dependence; the absence of the former does not prove absence of the latter.

Consequently:
- **qiyām bi-nafsihi** = not requiring an inherence subject in the stipulated sense.
- **iḥtiyāj** = broader existential/constitutive dependency not exhausted by inherence.
- This terminological split is required before analogizing jawhar to substantial being or subject in AT.

Source https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/9296/54?lang=ar

### III pp55–56 — essential commonality and differential features

A real composite can sometimes be inferred if two natures share a truly essential feature yet have truly distinct essential determinations; but one must determine which predicates count as essences and which as merely common accidents. Sharing accidental predicates and differing in accidents does not establish a composed real quiddity. Some objections challenge whether 'essential' as the concept's component actually has an independent external corresponding thing.

The exchange should not be condensed to 'definition = physical reality'. The text explicitly distinguishes the logical and metaphysical tests.

Sources https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/9296/55?lang=ar and /56.

### III p57 — parts' need is NOT a sufficient criterion for unity

After discussing essential commonality, a *maqṣad* introduces the thesis that parts of a true composite must stand in some dependence on each other. The gloss explicitly formulates the logical objection: if mere dependence were **sufficient**, every cause and effect, necessary implication or other dependent pair would be one composite substantial quiddity; manifestly false. There is even the example that the world depends upon its Creator, yet the world-plus-Creator is not thereby one true composite nature.

**Short phrase:** 'إن أريد أن الاحتياج كاف ... فباطل' — if it is intended that dependence is sufficient, this is false.

This is a deep internal critique of a simple 'parts depend therefore one actual substance' inference; it must be brought to bear on Spiker's gloss of real composition.

Source https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/9296/57?lang=ar

### III p58 — army and medicinal compound as counterexamples

The argument next gives objections involving:
- the army (*al-ʿaskar*), composed of individual soldiers independent for their own existence;
- compounded medicine (*al-maʿjūn*), ingredients also independently actual as things.

A response attempts to add a **collective hayʾa** (configuration) as the further formal part dependent upon the physical constituents.

The main commentary says this is weak because the same kind of apparent collective configuration can be imagined in a human standing beside a stone: it would then absurdly create another genuine substantial nature merely because a considered configuration depends upon material parts.

**Short crucial Arabic:** 'وهو ضعيف لأن مثل هذه الهيئة الاعتبارية عارضة للإنسان والحجر الموضوع بجنبه'.

This objection should be read as an anti-overgeneration argument against a too-easy substantial-form inference. It does NOT deny that physically arranged soldiers possess actual locations and operations.

Later gloss differentiates the cases: the medicine may involve additional physical temperament and species form; the army does not, at least as a single intrinsic natural essence. The text explains the earlier response's dialectical character, not simply a settled doctrine.

Source https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/9296/58?lang=ar

### III p59 — medicinal specific form, and the bed as a disputed mixed composite

The medicine is considered first. One account says the physical *mizāj* leads to a new **ṣūra nawʿiyya** causing specific effects not reducible to the ingredients' independent acts. Another senses *mizāj* as an accident, thereby raising whether a substantial whole may be constituted from **jawhar + ʿaraḍ**.

**Quoted example for reported view:** 'وقد جوزه بعضهم متمسكا بتركب السرير من جوهر هو القطع الخشبية وعرض هو الترتيب المخصوص' — SOME allowed this, appealing to the bed as wood (jawhar) and particular arrangement (ʿaraḍ).

A reported reply says what is impossible is a substance having an accident inhering in itself as its own prior constitutive part; a substance might be made from a different substance and an accident belonging to that other substance. But the later gloss notes a serious issue: does the relevant *tartīb/hayʾa* actually exist extramentally as proposed? If it is just relational, how does it count as an actual positive part of a substantial quiddity? It also warns that the theory may imply that every substance with any accidents has a further new real essence, which is implausible.

**Short source expressions:** 'قد جوزه بعضهم' ('some have permitted'), 'فتأمل' ('reflect on this'), 'كل جوهر مع عوارضه ماهية حقيقية' ('every substance together with its accidents would count as a genuine essence').

The claim "bed's relational arrangement can cause the subsistence of a new substantial form" must therefore be tagged **R/Q**, not an unqualified original-author conclusion.

Source https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/9296/59?lang=ar

### III p60 — the army EXISTING without doubt, yet its unity is merely considered

The central strongest text:

'مجموع الآحاد فقط وهو موجود بلا شبهة إلا أنه ماهية وحدتها اعتبارية والكلام في الماهية الحقيقية'

Close translation: the army [as a sum] is only the aggregate of the individual members, **and that aggregate exists without doubt**, though it is a quiddity whose unity is *iʿtibārī*, whereas the inquiry concerns a quiddity with genuine intrinsic unity.

It adds that if one makes the non-extramentally actual collective configuration an **extra actual component** of the new supposed substance, one risks saying the whole does not exist in the external domain, since a composite with a nonexistent component cannot be straightforwardly called a new real existent. A later gloss proposes the correct reading of a bed as **wooden parts actually subject to an arrangement** rather than wood plus a separate actual arrangement-entity.

This does **not** show the army is a new substantive individual. It does show that “there are only soldiers, no army” must specify *in which sense* there is no army—there is an objectively existing plurality with one considered unity, not simply non-being.

Source https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/9296/60?lang=ar

## Internal philosophical diagram, expressed textually

First question: do these constituents exist? — The army's soldiers: YES.
Second question: does the plurality have an additional single intrinsic substantial form? — DENIED by army comparison.
Third question: do participants possess real physical spatial/causal relations? — Not conclusively resolved simply by denying a new substance.
Fourth question: what formally constitutes a unity of order and institutional entity? — Requires further doctrine of iʿtibār, inshaʾ, ʿurf, and taʿalluq; do NOT import AT's *unitas ordinis* prematurely.

For the bed:
material wood actual — YES;
particular arrangement intelligible — YES;
specific arrangement as separately existing positive accident — DISPUTED;
new natural substantial essence of bed — NOT ESTABLISHED;
functional powers of resulting artifact — require empirical/ontological account not provided by a simple quotation.

## Claim-level consequences for Spiker

- SP-05 (*army*): Original acknowledges actual army as aggregation; qualifies Spiker's "no army in al-khārij" if read categorically. Main denial concerns extra substantial unity.
- SP-07 (*bed*): The cited III39 relates mixed conceptual constituents, but III59 offers both a reported hypothesis and serious objections about accident/form. Treat "bed as genuine new substance" as R/C.
- SP-02 (real parts): Interdependence may be necessary, not sufficient, for genuine substantial unity. Any proof based simply on need of parts is insufficient.
- SP-09 (nonindividuated relations): Lack of independent subsistence of a relational element is NOT equivalent, as yet, to nonexistence of actual physical configuration. This is the central distinction to investigate.
- SP-13 (prior intelligible realm): No argument in this dossier directly demonstrates a third antecedent source of objectivity; that is further metaphysical thesis requiring evidence.
