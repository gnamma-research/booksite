# Source Dossier 03 — Quṭb al-Dīn al-Rāzī al-Taḥtānī, Risāla fī Taḥqīq al-Kulliyyāt

**Stage 2.2 · 31 October 2026 · upstream textual genealogy.**

**Why this source?** The user requires comparison of historically variable technical meanings before interpreting Spiker. Quṭb al-Dīn al-Rāzī (d. 766 AH) lies in the immediate historical environment of al-Taftāzānī, Mubārakshāh and al-Jurjānī. An Arabic edited edition of his *Risāla fī Taḥqīq al-Kulliyyāt* gives a rare sustained argument—not just dictionary entries—about what it means to say that universal species and their constituents exist *outside* the mind. It provides an explicit internal debate on the distinction between intellectual form as a psychic quality and the *object known* through that form.

**Source witness:** Arabic text edited by Yūsuf b. ʿUmar ("Abū Yaʿqūb") posted 14 December 2020, https://atharah.net/mission-in-achieving-totalities/ . The edition's introduction lists five manuscript witnesses, including King Saud University MS 2631, Umm al-Qura 15926, Majlis MSS 311100 and 3960, and Süleymaniye Ayasofya 2567. It also notes a 2013 Turkish edition with commentators. This is not our independent manuscript collation. Manuscript readings are recorded by editor. Quote/translation in this dossier are short and interpretive.

## Argument bounded unit: Five qawāʿid and subsequent objections

1. **First qāʿida:** What does a universal *sharing* across particulars mean? If one numerical nature were independently present in many places, this would introduce contradictions of unity/multiplicity. The author's response analyzes matching cognitive representation, not a common numerical individual existing literally in multiple bodies.
2. **A distinction within mental form:** A cognitive representation is indeed an individual psychic occurrence. The term *ṣūra* however can name either that actual occurrence or the **known object as made distinct through it**. The latter is the term of universal apprehension, not simply the former physical/mental accident.
3. **Second qāʿida:** *Natural*, *logical* and *intellectual* universals are distinguished. The precise disputed assignment of universal predicability must be recovered from the author's definitions, not treated as three separate actual things.
4. **Third qāʿida:** In what sense is a universal 'in al-khārij'? A nature becomes universally describable when encountered by intellect; numerically particular entities are actual outside. Yet a real nature may be truthfully said of actual individual things.
5. **Fourth qāʿida:** Are genus and differentia two actual separate constituents or only two intellectual aspects? Quṭb sets out three rival positions and explicitly defends the account that the species is simple *externally in relation to its logical genus/difference*, while intellectually compound.
6. **Fifth qāʿida:** The differentia completes or removes the conceptual indeterminacy of genus. This is the cognitive meaning of the term *cause* when one says difference makes the generic nature determinate, and must not automatically be mapped to efficient production of a material body.
7. **Conclusion and objection replies:** The author explicitly answers the objection that mental forms must correspond to distinct external reality and reclarifies particular *ṣūra*, generic image, species, and the distinction between intellectual *ḥadd* and actual constituent *mabādiʾ*.

## Key conceptual distinction 1: ṣūra as a psychic quality versus object known

The source explicitly says **'الصورة تطلق بحسب الاشتراك اللفظي على معنيين'** — the word *ṣūra* has two senses in this discussion. The first is a *kayfiyya* arising in the intellect, the instrument of intellectual cognition; the second is *al-maʿlūm*, the known object differentiated through that form.

In the first sense it is a particular actual quality located in an individual's cognition. In the second, the universal concept applies to the content as object, rather than to the idiosyncratic psychic occurrence. Hence 'a mental object' cannot be automatically identified with an *individual mental accident*.

## Key distinction 2: three ontologies of genus and differentia

The fourth qāʿida reports three historical positions:

A. Genus and difference are actually distinct extra-mental constituents in existence, though not separately discriminated by the senses.

B. They are somehow distinguishable in **dhāt** (essence or nature) but united in existence with the species, a view ascribed to many of the later authors.

C. The species is **simple extramentally** in relation to logical genus/difference; their compositional distinction is made by reason. Quṭb defends C in this text.

The argument challenges A through the theory of predication (*ḥaml bi-l-muwāṭāʾa*)—if genus and difference were two independent actual existences, why could each be predicated of a species as the same thing? He challenges B through questions about the inherence of one quality in several distinct subjects and the ontological precedence of externally existing parts to the composite. The principal conclusion concerns logical constituent parts; it is not by itself a proof that no living thing has distinct matter and substantial form.

**Critical English:** “the actual species is one rather than a composite of separately existing genus and difference” is more precise than “there is no composition anywhere outside the mind.” This preserves his target.

## Key distinction 3: external individuated does not equal sensible

The source distinguishes a genus or species *as universally predicable* from the actually individuated nature being understood. A particular cognitive quality is itself numerically individual, even though it occurs within a mind. Thus “particular” and “mind-independent” are NOT equivalent: mental acts may be actual individual accidents.

The source editor's manuscript note concerning the phrase 'not differentiated in sense' in one disputed classification lists variants, including 'in existence' and 'in exterior', an example of wording that can shift the very contrast. This must be handled as a textual variation, not just a semantic ambiguity. The website apparatus also cites Ibn Sīnā's Shifāʾ, al-Ṭūsī and other authors. Track separate witnesses.

## Key distinction 4: genus is indeterminate in intellect; difference specifies there

In the fifth qāʿida, the author clarifies that generic nature considered without difference is not fully determined as species. The difference's 'causal' role is to remove the genus's cognitive indeterminacy (*ibham*), NOT by that very statement to efficiently manufacture the corresponding external being. This is central to interpreting Taftāzānī's I105 and Spiker's appeal to genus/fasl mapped to matter/form.

Quṭb notes that one may derive intellectual genus and specific difference by different considered aspects of an actual nature without there being two separate externally existent genus and difference bodies. The material/formal principles of some individual substances are a further question.

## Source caveats, comparison and next steps

A. The 2020 text is an edited web transcription with five listed manuscript witnesses; editorial variants present. It must not be called our own critical Arabic edition.
B. The biography says the author was teacher of some of the central commentators, but historical teaching relationships must be cross-verified rather than automatically used to assert direct lineal borrowing. The same argument can circulate through many works.
C. We should now compare this tract closely with *Sharḥ al-Maqāṣid* I105, *Tasdīd al-Qawāʿid* I406–407, and the relevant gloss in *Sharḥ Ḥikmat al-ʿAyn*. It could help locate the missing passages and avoid a false genus↔matter identity.
D. Investigate Quṭb's argument for the individual mental *ṣūra* versus *maʿlūm* in later Taftāzānī and Gelenbevī texts.
E. Defer Latin comparison (act vs species expressa / intentional relation) until all internal Arabic arguments have been tested.
