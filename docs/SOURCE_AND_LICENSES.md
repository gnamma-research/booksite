# Sources, licensing and editorial method

**al-Mustaṣfā primary Arabic witness.** OpenITI corpus, URI `0505Ghazali.Mustasfa.Shamela0005459-ara1`, [source file](https://github.com/OpenITI/0525AH/blob/master/data/0505Ghazali/0505Ghazali.Mustasfa/0505Ghazali.Mustasfa.Shamela0005459-ara1). The corpus is made available under **CC BY-NC-SA 4.0**, and the JSON segmentation is adapted from its digital transcription. [OpenITI licensing documentation](https://openiti.org/documentation/). Cite the URI and the version of the text in addition to al-Ghazālī. The JSON's Arabic punctuation and page/paragraph segmentation do not constitute manuscript collation.

**English translations.** The selected English renderings in `texts/al-ghazali/al-mustasfa/data/reader.json` are the Gnamma working translation, not Hammad's translation reproduced. Citation to Hammad's scanned 1987 dissertation serves as historical/editorial comparison where actually checked; his scans are NOT published here. No claim of comprehensive page-by-page Hammad verification.

**Uploaded scholarship informing technical terms.** Wael B. Hallaq, *A History of Islamic Legal Theories*; Felicitas Opwis, “Syllogistic Logic in Islamic Legal Theory: al-Ghazālī’s Arguments for the Certainty of Legal Analogy (Qiyās),” in *Philosophy and Jurisprudence in the Islamic World*; and the bibliographic studies registered within this text. These are contextual scholarship, NOT alternate Arabic witnesses. Cross-work citations are graded by evidence.

**Status:** working, selective bilingual translation and complete digital Arabic source, **not** unabridged translation or definitive critical edition.

**Permissions:** Contributions to the Arabic OpenITI derivative must maintain appropriate attribution, noncommercial use and share-alike conditions. Other bundled translations and supporting content may have separate rights. Do not infer that all of this repository is relicensed by the corpus licence.
