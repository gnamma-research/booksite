# Darūrī Hadith critical-review register (23 October 2026)

**Scope:** 33 existing scholarly Hadith note records, 55 section-level citation placements and 47 distinct primary collection reference IDs. This register distinguishes **tested website functionality** from a **completed traditional-critical Hadith edition**. It does not assert that critical collation has been completed.

## Editorial rules

1. Display the Arabic **collection text** and its full isnād and the project's original English translation without promoting either to Ibn Rushd's authorial words. Editorial vowels applied to set honorifics must not be mistaken for an independently collated vocalized manuscript.
2. Order independently established parallels in Bukhārī and Muslim first, but never substitute a different event or chain because its collection is more prestigious. State the substantive differences where the wording or judgement matters.
3. Use **premodern named authorities only** for the visible judgements of authenticity, weakness, interruption and variant quality. An original remark by al-Tirmidhī or Abū Dāwūd is a primary source; a named judgement cited in Ibn Ḥajar's *Bulūgh al-Marām* is a secondary premodern witness. Modern grading fields from digital datasets must not be presented.
4. **Nūr al-Dīn ʿItr, *Iʿlām al-Anām: Sharḥ Bulūgh al-Marām min Aḥādīth al-Aḥkām*** (Dār al-Farfūr, 1419/1998, four volumes) is a *modern orientation and source-finding aid*. The final visible judgement must be independently traced to the earlier scholar and wording he quotes, not attributed to ʿItr as an authority in lieu of a classical source.
5. Cross-check legal examples first with ʿAbd al-Ghanī al-Maqdisī's *ʿUmdat al-Aḥkām* where both Ṣaḥīḥs transmit the report; with Majd ibn Taymiyya's *al-Muntaqā*, Ibn Daqīq al-ʿĪd's *al-Ilmām*, al-Nawawī's *Khulāṣat al-Aḥkām*, Ibn ʿAbd al-Hādī's *al-Muḥarrar*, Ibn Ḥajar's *Bulūgh al-Marām*, al-Ṭaḥāwī's *Sharḥ Maʿānī al-Āthār*, al-Zaylaʿī's *Naṣb al-Rāya*, and Ibn Ḥajar's *al-Talkhīṣ al-Ḥabīr*, selecting the relevant source by topic and tracing it to its cited first-hand collection. Do not imply that each of these volumes has already been consulted for every row.
6. Sections concerning epistemic authority and consensus require **uṣūl and ḥadīth methodology criticism** beyond the aḥkām anthologies. Related Qurʾān and Hadith evidence should be placed together only when the textual relation is identified, not simply because they occur in one section.

## One independently cross-checked aḥkām example

- **§112 — Abū Dāwūd 181 / Busra bt. Ṣafwān.** Ibn Ḥajar, *Bulūgh al-Marām* no. 73 (Book of Purification, the nullification of ablution), explicitly records **al-Tirmidhī and Ibn Ḥibbān** as declaring this report ṣaḥīḥ. See [the Arabic entry](https://sunnah.com/bulugh:73), which states *وَصَحَّحَهُ التِّرْمِذِيُّ وَابْنُ حِبَّانَ*. Other earlier critical comparisons exist, but their precise competing chains must be verified before a comparative judgement is added.
- This does not mean that any unspecified modern digital source's "Sahih" tag has been adopted; the visible attribution belongs to the named premodern scholars.

## Outstanding critical checks, separate from website release

- Compare selected chapter placement and full route variants at the collection level rather than equating report numbers found in different digital editions.
- For **al-Tirmidhī 691**, distinguish the connected report from the *mursal* routes; for **1328**, retain his express statement on chain discontinuity. Do not fill §189's missing attribution by conjecture.
- For legal reports with multiple relevant traditions, collate the report's wording and the corresponding legal inference as separate layers; use an aḥkām anthology to identify **where to look**, not as licence to insert a parallel that has not been checked against the primary source.
- Verify translated collector remarks and the project's diacritized presentation against a traditional printed edition before calling the apparatus critically certified.
