# Darūrī external scholarly review: §§17, 23, 26

**Review received:** 5 November 2026. **Provenance:** a scholar's comments relayed by the reader, with no identity assigned. **Base:** Jamāl al-Dīn al-ʿAlawī (1994). **2015 comparator:** Ziad Bou Akl, *Averroès: le philosophe et la Loi*; no claim is made here of direct collation of these three clauses with the Escorial manuscript.

## Decisions and grounds

### §17 — wording of the definition of al-wājib
- **1994 reading (retained):** «لأن الواجب على مذهب أهل السنة لا يتصور دون الضرر أو النفع».
- **English:** Keep the literal reading in the main translation, *cannot be conceived apart from harm or benefit*, and add a clearly labelled **textual crux** in the notes. Do not silently emend an Arabic text.
- **Reviewer’s hypotheses:** Either an intrusive «لا» reverses the assertion, or an intrusive «أو النفع» weakens the more direct connection to threatened harm. Neither is established as a manuscript variant.
- **Why the crux matters:** The added definitional clause «مع توعد بالعقاب على تركه» distinguishes obligation from mere recommendation. Benefit is insufficient by itself to distinguish an obligation. In *al-Mustaṣfā*'s discussion of the definition, al-Qāḍī suggests that an obligation may obtain without an explicit threat, while al-Ghazālī queries obligation absent any practical preference over omission. The printed clause could also be a broad *necessary-condition* observation rather than an attempt to supply a *sufficient* differentia. This possibility is not ruled out.
- **Editorial status:** **Unresolved** pending examination of Bou Akl's Arabic and apparatus at the relevant section and, where possible, the transmitted witness.

### §23 — scope of «والواجب من هذا»
- **Decision:** **Correction accepted.** The anaphor refers to the category of obligations not specified by a fixed measure, **including both examples**: «مسح الرأس» and «الطمأنينة في الركوع».
- **Translation repair:** Replace “In the latter case” with “For obligations of this unmeasured kind” to eliminate the false narrowing to the second example.
- **Qualification:** A minimum denoted by a name does not by itself adjudicate the school dispute about the extent of valid head-wiping. Furthermore, the author limits the described apportionment to an act whose parts are successive; al-Ghazālī's fuller treatment also considers simultaneous acts. Retain that difference rather than erasing it in the name of agreement.

### §26 — affirmative rhetorical comparison
- **Decision:** **Correction accepted; earlier meaning was reversed.**
- **Source:** «وأبين من هذا أن يرجع إلى ما كان قبل من إباحة، إذ ليس يتضمنها جنس هذه المتقابلات الذي هو الطلب».
- **Repair:** Withdraw the editorial interpolation “More evident still [is the error of supposing]”. In context «وأبين من هذا» compares a reversion to **prior permissibility** with the claim of automatic reversion to prohibition; the rationale is that permissibility lies outside the genus of demand «الطلب».
- **Qualification:** Returning to a *previously applicable* permission is not equivalent to claiming that every abrogated obligation must be permissible. Al-Ghazālī's *al-Mustaṣfā* specifies that one returns to what obtained before (whether prohibition or permission). The Arabic is not emended. Replace note N011 so the old interpretation cannot survive in the apparatus.
