# Al-Mustaṣfā · Translation and cross-work method (v0.3)

This memorandum controls the bilingual Gnamma working reader. All terms are to be translated according to their **argumentative function in each passage**, not by imposing a one-to-one dictionary equivalence across law, logic, theology, and ethics.

## 1. Source hierarchy and edition control

1. **Primary authorial source:** the Arabic *al-Mustaṣfā*. The edition used for the current electronic register is a linked online transcription with working printed-page anchors, pending collation with a selected printed edition. No editor silently normalizes divergent readings without registering the witnesses.
2. **English comparator:** Ahmad Zaki Mansur Hammad, University of Chicago, 1987 dissertation, user-provided as two image-only scans of 303 and 489 PDF pages. Hammad's analytical part must be separated from his translation (which begins at dissertation p. 300). The scanned translation covers Arabic Volume I but does not encompass the entirety of al-Mustaṣfā.
3. **Al-Ghazālī's other writings:** count as authorial textual evidence only where an actual passage is cited and compared. A named work is a bibliographical cross-reference, not automatic proof of an equivalent definition.
4. **Historical dictionaries:** distinguish authorial use, earlier/roughly contemporary lexicography, and later technical definitions. A short excerpt is not a full dictionary article.
5. **Modern scholarship:** useful in commentary only with explicit attribution, dates, and locators. Interpretative agreement is not a manuscript variant or authorial quotation.

## 2. Logical and juristic distinctions

**Taṣawwur / taṣdīq.** Conception identifies the content of terms; assent affirms or denies a proposition. This foundational distinction grounds the organization of al-Ghazālī's logical introduction and links explicitly to his earlier *Miḥakk al-Naẓar*. Neither term should be conflated with an individual's religious faith or a psychological feeling of certainty.

**Ḥadd / burhān.** Definition is an instrument of clarifying conception; demonstration orders propositions to establish what follows from premises. A formally valid syllogism can have premises which are merely likely or commonplace; therefore its inferential form alone does not confer apodictic certainty. Preserve the difference between the `matter` and `form` of demonstration.

**Yaqīn / qaṭʿī / ẓann.** Certainty, decisive evidentiary force and juristic probability are not synonyms. Ẓann in legal contexts often points to preponderant yet corrigible judgment. A lexicon may show a wider lexical spectrum; it cannot cancel the specific uṣūl contrast.

**Qiyās.** Identify whether the context is syllogistic reasoning or analogical extension in law. Do not presume a single epistemic force or translate every occurrence simply as 'deduction'. In the third axis al-Ghazālī compares the legal attributes by differing evidentiary strengths, including *muʾaththir*, *munāsib*, *shabah*, and *ṭard*.

**Ḥukm / khiṭāb.** In the author's Ashʿarī framework a normative legal ruling is understood relationally through the divine address. Distinguish this from good, bad, benefit, and harm as descriptions of objects and actions. Also distinguish `the address`, `the act`, `the person addressed` and `the manner of applicability`.

**Sabab / sharṭ / ʿilla / ḥikma / maqṣad.** An occasion, a condition, an operative legal ground, a rule's intelligible wisdom, and a Lawgiver's purpose are not interchangeable; any comparison with Aristotelian causation must be argued rather than asserted by a misleading English synonym 'cause'.

**Maṣlaḥa / mafsada and necessities/needs/refinements.** Technical legal interest concerns preservation of legally recognized purposes. An appeal to benefit is not thereby an autonomous fifth primary source of rulings. Grades of interests are not social classes or psychological types.

**Istithmār and the four axes.** Preserve al-Ghazālī's extended fruit analogy: *thamara* (ruling as fruit), *muthmir* (productive proof), *ṭarīq al-istithmār* (the way inference draws the fruit), and *mustathmir* (the jurist who acquires it). This explains the architecture of the treatise and invites controlled comparison with Ibn Rushd's later abridgement.

## 3. Translation comparison against Hammad

Dissertation p. 323 n.15 states why the logically introductory material was omitted from the English translation. This is a documented difference in **coverage**, not merely in English style. When assessing the overlapping chapters, label differences as (a) literal sense, (b) technical terminology, (c) sentence arrangement, (d) editorial expansion, (e) omission or (f) argument structure. A different stylistic choice is not automatically a mistranslation.

The website's eight cases are a **selective** preliminary register: preface and mixed sciences; definition and general sources; fruit metaphor and four axes; the omitted logical introduction; classification of good and bad; five legal rules; *sabab* and *ʿilla*; and *ʿazīma/rukhṣa*. The latter comparison is an editorial terminological proposal, and not evidence that the entire passage has been retranslated.

## 4. Cross-work confidence classes

- `direct-authorial-reference`: al-Ghazālī himself names the other work at the locus. This establishes an identification, **not** a claim that an identical treatment occurs there.
- `direct-bibliographic`: a securely identified book title is mentioned, but a specific parallel quotation awaits collation.
- `verified-primary-parallel`: a separately found verbal/conceptual passage in a primary Ghazālī text is cited and excerpted. Even this is not necessarily identical doctrine; the context may differ.
- `topical`: similarity of topic; no exact collated passage, so displayed only as a research lead.
- `contextual`: a conjectural relation requiring a further interpretative argument.

## 5. Necessary stages to reach definitive status

Fix a reference Arabic edition, collate the full original Arabic sequentially, assign persistent book/chapter/paragraph numbers, translate *all* chapters (including the entire logical introduction), independently proof every English passage, complete a transparent Hammad comparison register, complete historical dictionaries **with full Arabic and English texts**, verify other-work crossreferences using edition-specific locators, build indices of technical vocabulary/verses/hadith/persons/disputed rulings, and conduct a print proof against original Arabic. The current release has not passed these milestones.
