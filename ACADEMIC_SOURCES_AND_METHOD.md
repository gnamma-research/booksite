# Scholarly technical translation apparatus — al-Mustaṣfā, working edition 0.5

## What this edition is, and what it is not

The bilingual part of this release contains **103 independently prepared, non-consecutive translated excerpt cards**, of which **14 are newly translated complete consecutive Arabic paragraphs** from the preface and initial architecture of al-Ghazālī’s treatise (OpenITI digital witness V01P003–V01P007). Other cards are shorter previously prepared selections, with overlapping loci. **There is no complete English translation of al-Mustaṣfā or a complete critical Arabic edition in this release**. The new 14 paragraphs have been compared with the identified Arabic digital witness, but **not systematically collated against a print edition, manuscript, or Hammad's page-by-page English**. The complete Arabic is offered as a separately labelled digital reading witness, not as editorially established text.

## Basis and hierarchy

1. The Arabic source determines the sentence-level translation and legal argument. Working witness: OpenITI `0505Ghazali.Mustasfa.Shamela0005459-ara1`, a transcription with its *own* page markers. The hosted whole-Arabic reader is based upon this text, not upon a facsimile of the Arabic original. Opening pages are marked `V00P000` or `V01P003` onward. Metadata can be wrong or incomplete; validate each source against a fixed printed edition before definitive publication.
2. The user-supplied Hammad (1987) dissertation is a comparison witness. Scans total 792 pages (303 + 489) and are **not** redistributed. The second scan includes a partial English translation of Arabic Volume I beginning at dissertation p. 300; its logic prolegomenon is omitted by Hammad (p. 323 n.15). His explanatory prose is not the substitute for original Arabic.
3. The scholarly interpretative apparatus is informed by user-uploaded academic books and studies. These contextualize questions, not replace the wording of the source.
4. Technical dictionaries illuminate usage when genuinely verified. They do not override an author's use of a term, nor are later definitions automatically attributable to al-Ghazālī.

## Major academic comparators drawn from user-supplied files

- **Felicitas Opwis**, “Syllogistic Logic in Islamic Legal Theory: al-Ghazālī’s Arguments for the Certainty of Legal Analogy (Qiyās),” in *Philosophy and Jurisprudence in the Islamic World* (2019), pp. 93–112. **Translation impact:** the form of *qiyās* and the inferential status of its premises must be separated; not every syllogistic argument is demonstratively certain. Thus `qiyās` cannot always simply be translated “analogy,” or conversely “deduction.” See al-Ghazālī’s distinction between certain (`qaṭʿī`) and probable (`maẓnūn`) premises and its effect on juridical inference.
- **Ulrich Rudolph**, “Al-Ghazālī on Philosophy and Jurisprudence,” in the same volume, pp. 67–91. **Translation impact:** logic, *kalām*, and the particular sciences must be distinguished when describing disciplinary order in al-Mustaṣfā; the author's mention of another book is an explicit bibliographic connection but not proof of shared unchanged technical doctrine.
- **Wael B. Hallaq**, *A History of Islamic Legal Theories: An Introduction to Sunnī Uṣūl al-Fiqh* (1997), on epistemological development, legal proofs, and the incorporation of logic. **Translation impact:** `qaṭʿ` and `ẓann`, `dalāla`, the evidentiary status of Sunna and ijmāʿ, and the history of juristic inference are treated with their historical disciplinary context.
- **Ahmad Zaki Mansur Hammad**, *Abū Ḥāmid al-Ghazālī’s Juristic Doctrine in al-Mustaṣfā min ʿIlm al-Uṣūl* (University of Chicago, 1987). **Translation impact:** a witness to earlier English lexical and conceptual decisions, **not** a text to paraphrase wholesale. The comparison register offers selective cases, not a complete line-by-line collation.

## Required technical consistency (with contextual exceptions)

| Arabic | Default English rendering | Necessary distinction |
|---|---|---|
| `فقه` fiqh | jurisprudence; understanding (lexical) | Qualified legal knowledge, not a generic theoretical knowledge of acts |
| `أصول الفقه` uṣūl al-fiqh | principles of jurisprudence; legal theory | Evidence, general modes of indication, and method, not individual cases |
| `حكم` ḥukm | legal ruling or legal qualification | Distinguish divine address, action, addressee, and application |
| `خطاب الشرع` khiṭāb al-sharʿ | the revealed law's normative address | Do not reduce to a sound or descriptive statement |
| `دلالة` dalāla | indication / mode of signification | Distinguish form, implication, necessary presupposition, inferred rationale |
| `قياس` qiyās | juridical analogical inference; syllogistic reasoning | Follow argument: validity of inference ≠ certainty of premises |
| `علة` ʿilla | operative legal ground / effective attribute | Not automatically efficient causation or purposive wisdom |
| `ظن` ẓann | predominant, corrigible judgment | Not arbitrary guess; not certainty (`qaṭʿ`) |
| `برهان` burhān | demonstration / conclusive proof | Premises' epistemic matter must support demonstrative conclusion |
| `مصلحة` maṣlaḥa | legally cognizable interest | Not just personal utility; read in relation to maqāṣid |
| `مستثمر` mustathmir | harvester, the jurist who extracts ruling | Preserve the fourfold fruit/means/method/person metaphor |

## Evidence grades and remaining tasks

The website distinguishes author's direct self-references, independently located primary parallels, and *topical* or *contextual* research leads. Hammad comparison cases are selective and incomplete. Later lexicon entries are short verified extracts, not full dictionaries. Before labeling this a definitive scholarly translation: fix the Arabic reference edition, translate every paragraph of the entire logical introduction and four quṭbs, collate competing Arabic readings and Hammad wherever possible, proof every technical inference, add whole-article dictionary translations and specific Arabic citation anchors to the other Ghazālī works, and review indexes, Qurʾān, hadith, and doctrine.
