A name from the landscape

Gnamma

A gnamma is a hollow in rock where water gathers: a place of replenishment and reflection. It also symbolises a nexus toward the vertical—the meeting of the earthly, the subterranean, and the transcendent.

The name is not intended as a simple dictionary definition. It gestures instead toward the kind of layered, symbolic interpretation found in works such as ʿIqd al-Ḥimam and al-Kāshānī’s lexicon, where a word may be understood through its linguistic, spiritual, cosmological, and metaphysical resonances.

In Arabic, sharīʿa originally denotes a path to water. In Islamic usage, it refers to the revealed way: the divinely given norms and practices that orient human life toward God. See Encyclopaedia of Islam, “Sharīʿa”.

Qudra (قُدْرَة) means power, ability, or capacity. In theology, al-qudra denotes divine power: God’s capacity to create, sustain, and act. It is not simply “the order of what lies below,” nor is it synonymous with sharīʿa. See Lane’s Arabic-English Lexicon, s.v. قدر.

Together, gnamma and sharīʿa join place and path: one gathers water; the other leads toward it. Qudra names the power that sustains both the world and its orientation toward its source.

Gnamma Research follows this pattern: it situates texts within their own worlds while tracing the connections among traditions, disciplines, and times.
