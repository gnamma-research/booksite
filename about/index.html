A name from the landscape

Gnamma

Gnamma takes its name from Noongar, an Aboriginal language of southwestern Australia. The word refers to a hollow in rock where water gathers. In dry country, a waterhole sustains life. It is also a meeting place: people travelling through different landscapes and cultures may pause there, drawn together not by agreement but by a shared need for sustenance.

The Arabic sharīʿa likewise evokes a watering place and the way to it. In Islam, it is the revealed way orienting human life toward God. The imagery thus has a vertical dimension—our dependence upon the source of life—and a horizontal dimension: the encounters, exchanges, and conversations that become possible around what sustains us.

Gnamma Research seeks to be such a meeting place for inquiry. We first read texts in their own languages, histories, and intellectual worlds; then trace their connections across traditions, disciplines, and times. Through critical editions, translations, and digital tools, we seek to make these encounters possible without effacing the differences that give each tradition its character.
