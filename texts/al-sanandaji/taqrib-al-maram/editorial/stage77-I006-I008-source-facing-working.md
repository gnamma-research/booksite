## I:6 — Arrangement of Tahdhīb and the distinction between logic and kalām

### I006.upper.01 — The intended portion of the book
**Arabic:** «(هذا) أي ما سيأتي عليك (قسم الكلام من) الكتاب المسمى (بالتهذيب)» · «وقع التبويب» · «المقدمة أولاً».

**English:** “This”—that is, the material about to be presented—is the **division on kalām** in the book called *al-Tahdhīb*. The reason the author has arranged it in chapters is that there are matters which ought to be introduced before the substantive questions of theology. They form the first chapter, a preliminary section; only once those conceptual preliminaries have been set out can the later questions be investigated. The work's division and naming are therefore not arbitrary ornaments but mark the progression of the inquiry.

**Status:** Upper text first-pass source-guided reading.

### I006.ruled.01 — On the expression ‘section of kalām’
**Arabic:** «(قوله أي ما سيأتي عليك)» · «هذا إشارة من الألفاظ من حيث دلالتها على المعاني» · «(قوله المسمى بالتهذيب)».

**English:** On the words “what will come before you,” the commentator points out how the demonstrative is attached to what follows in the written exposition. The title ‘Tahdhīb’ and the expression ‘the division of kalām’ are accordingly explained as designating a **part of a composed text**, not an independently existing scientific object. The gloss also discusses the phrasing and attribution of the title; a short name in the final line is not confidently legible.

**Status:** Short ruled annotation with an uncertain proper-name reading.

### I006.lower.01 — Can the chapter plan itself count as the beginning of kalām?
**Arabic:** «(قوله هذا قسم الكلام من التهذيب)» · «غاية تهذيب الكلام» · «في تحرير المنطق وتحرير الكلام» · «ألفاظ ... قسماً من العلم».

**English:** The marginal writer examines what is implied by calling this the kalām division of a book which also concerns logic. One might take the wording to mean that the book is ‘refined’ in its entirety; another reading limits the title's effect to the matters explicitly treated under it. The gloss distinguishes a **textual division**, a **field of knowledge**, and the act of **refining a discourse** about that field. The point is relevant because the two sections need not have precisely the same objects, terminology, or demonstrative aim.

**Status:** Source-facing marginal reading; not every subordinate example individually translated.

### I006.lower.02 — Preliminary matters and subject-matter
**Arabic:** «المقدمة» · «الحدّ والموضوع» · «مباحث العلم والنظر» · «التعريف».

**English:** The marginal argument also asks which preliminary matters are genuinely needed for the science. A definition of a discipline may address its name, subject, end and benefit; a preliminary discussion may also address the meaning of knowledge and the role of inquiry. Those questions are introduced as **instruments** for the ensuing theological investigation. The writer warns against treating every preliminary logical discussion as though it were already a doctrinal conclusion.

**Status:** Connected interpretative lower text, requires literal second pass.

## I:7 — Six chapters of theological inquiry

### I007.upper.01 — Sequence of the later chapters
**Arabic:** «الثلاثة في الوجود وهو الباب الثاني في الأمور العامة» · «الثالث في الأعراض» · «الرابع في الجواهر» · «الخامس ... بالواجب تعالى» · «السادس في السمعيات ... الباب الخامس في الإلهيات».

**English:** After the preliminary chapter, the subject is arranged in a progression: the **general matters of being**; matters concerning **accidents**; matters concerning **substances**; the **Necessary Existent**, or what pertains to God; and finally the **things known by transmission**, where the revealed reports and the mission of the Messenger are treated. The chapter headings have their own technical ordering, and the commentator explains how the labels for the wider theological divisions correspond to the actual sequence within the *Tahdhīb*.

**Status:** Upper source reading. Chapter numeration across the very abbreviated line should be rechecked before formal table of contents reconstruction.

### I007.ruled.01 — The beginning of the introduction
**Arabic:** «(الباب الأول في المقدمة)» · «تقدّمه معرفة حدّه وموضوعه وغايته».

**English:** “Chapter One: the Introduction.” Before undertaking the theological proofs, the reader is given an account of the science's **definition**, **subject**, and **aim**. These preparatory notions make the manner in which the subsequent problems are investigated intelligible.

**Status:** Title and middle short printed line; not a named authorial note.

### I007.lower.01 — What does ‘chapter’ denote?
**Arabic:** «في الباب الأول في المقدمة» · «الظاهر أن الأبواب هي الألفاظ أو النقوش» · «النظر أولاً».

**English:** The lower writer observes that a ‘chapter’ can designate the **written headings or sections** of the treatise rather than a metaphysical part of the subject-matter. He is concerned to avoid a purely verbal inference from a heading to a division of reality. The introductory chapter is not the whole of kalām merely because it contains prerequisites of kalām; its function is to explain what the science is and how its arguments proceed.

**Status:** Marginal source-facing explanation; some editorial connective phrasing used.

### I007.lower.02 — What should come before the scientific questions?
**Arabic:** «مباحث العلم والنظر» · «التصور والتصديق» · «المقدمة» · «ما يتوقف عليه الشروع في مسائل العلم».

**English:** The gloss asks whether the actual investigation of first principles should precede the definition of the science, or whether the introductory description is sufficient for a reader who has not yet learned the relevant principles. Some knowledge of **conception**, **assent**, and **valid reasoning** is indispensable before proof is possible. But it does not follow that every such preliminary must itself be independently proven at the beginning of the treatise. The author is explaining a pedagogical order, not claiming that the order of printed headings dictates the order of things in the world.

**Status:** Provisional connected gloss; word-exhaustive proof pending.

## I:8 — Why kalām is distinguished from logic and fiqh

### I008.upper.01 — The specific scope of theological science
**Arabic:** «الكلام خاصة» · «كمباحث العلم والنظر والدليل» · «لا تحصل العقائد بطريق النظر والاستدلال» · «الإلهيات خاصة».

**English:** The designation ‘kalām’ does not simply mean every investigation of **knowledge, inquiry, and evidence**. Those subjects also belong to logic and the theory of demonstration. Nor is its defining mark merely that doctrinal propositions are asserted. What distinguishes this science is the way its investigations are ordered toward establishing **religious convictions** through arguments. The statement therefore moves from the preparatory discussion to a definition of kalām.

**Status:** Upper text; one negative clause is printed compactly, so it is retained as a cautious interpretative rendering.

### I008.upper.02 — Definition through doctrinal knowledge
**Arabic:** «(الكلام هو العلم بالعقائد)» · «وهي الأحكام التي يتعلق الغرض بمجرد اعتقادها بالقلب».

**English:** “Kalām is knowledge of the doctrines.” These are judgments for which the relevant end is **assent in the heart** as such, rather than the bodily execution of a practice. This distinction does not imply that religious practice is unimportant; rather, it determines the formal respect under which the science studies its judgments, in contrast to a discipline concerned with the practical derivation of rulings.

**Status:** Upper matn/sharḥ boundary read from printed punctuation.

### I008.ruled.01 — Remarks on the naming of the discipline
**Arabic:** «(قال المصنف رحمه الله الكلام هو العلم الخ)» · «أي علم الكلام».

**English:** On the author's words “kalām is knowledge, etc.”: the phrase is intended as **‘the science of kalām’**, not a statement that every instance of speech counts as knowledge. The gloss alludes to other historical explanations of why the discipline came to be called ‘kalām’; these alternatives do not all supply its formal definition.

**Status:** Short ruled gloss, part source quotation and part source-facing clarification.

### I008.lower.01 — Why distinguishing kalām by ‘belief’ is difficult
**Arabic:** «(قوله خاصة)» · «الاختصاص بعلم الكلام» · «الاختصاص ... على ما صرح به المصنف في شرح المقاصد» · «اعتقاد القلب».

**English:** The marginal commentator insists that the difference between kalām and logic or law cannot simply be a difference in **subject terms**. Many theological proofs concern bodies, accidents, possibility, causality and language, all of which can also be examined in other disciplines. The relevant differentiation is through the **end for which the inquiry is undertaken**: establishing a religious belief rather than, for instance, deriving a legal action or analysing the formal structure of an inference.

**Status:** Lower source-guided analysis; not a complete literal transcription.

### I008.lower.02 — Necessary judgments and practical judgments
**Arabic:** «الأحكام التي يتعلق الغرض بمجرد اعتقادها» · «وجوب القلب» · «الأحكام الشرعية» · «كيفية العمل».

**English:** Some religious judgments call for the **assent** of the believer; others are concerned with the manner of acting. The marginal writer examines whether this separation is absolute or is better expressed by a difference in the **proximate aim** of the relevant science. An obligatory practical act may also be something one must believe is obligatory; that does not make its detailed derivation a question of kalām. Likewise, a proposition about theological doctrine may have practical consequences without ceasing to be a doctrinal proposition.

**Status:** Connected lower reading, several compact examples unverified.
