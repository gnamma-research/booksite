## I:61 — affirmative predication and the subject–predicate relation

### I061.upper.01 — final requirements for a true affirmative predication

**Arabic anchors:** «وافتقر الحمل الإيجابي إلى اتحاد الطرفين» · «ذاتا» · «وهوية» · «ما يصدق عليه في الخارج أو الذهن عنوان الموضوع» · «هو بعينه الذي يصدق عليه مفهوم المحمول».

**English.** —[The previous discussion concludes with what is required for predication.] An affirmative predication depends upon the two terms' being united **in respect of the thing itself and of its identity**. The meaning is that the item of which the subject-expression is true—whether externally or in the mind—is precisely the item of which the predicate-concept is true. A judgement of the form “this is that” is otherwise not warranted where the purported subject and predicate designate two distinct entities.

### I061.upper.02 — why difference of concepts is nevertheless required

**Arabic anchors:** «وإلى تغايرهما مفهوما» · «مفهوم عنوان الموضوع مغايرا لمفهوم المحمول» · «ليفيد» · «فائدة يعتد بها» · «وقد يكون فاسدا ... و باطلا».

**English.** Yet the concepts signified by the two expressions must also be different: the concept conveyed by the subject-expression must differ from the predicate-concept, **so that the predication conveys a genuine benefit or information**. The author's reasoning considers cases in which this requirement appears to fail, including judgements that are formally true but explanatorily pointless and judgements whose apparent agreement depends upon a merely mental consideration. Thus one must distinguish identity of the *referent* from identity of the *concepts*; to confuse those two identities is to obscure the purpose of affirmative predication. [The exact status of two short transitions in the closing upper lines requires a second reading.]

### I061.lower.01 — why merely invoking the 'unity of the terms' does not answer the objection

**Arabic anchors:** «(قوله يعني أن يكون ما يصدق عليه في الخارج الخ)» · «المراد اتحاد الطرفين» · «في الوجود الخارجي» · «للتغاير في المفهوم».

**English.** The commentator explains that the phrase “what the expression is true of externally” points back to the explanation of the unity of the two terms. If we take their unity to mean an external identity, that cannot simply be read as the identity of their meanings: the fact that two expressions attach to one existent does not show that their significations are interchangeable. Conversely, an identity secured only through a consideration of the intellect may fall short of the external union a predication is being used to affirm. This is an objection to an overly compressed account of what “unity” is doing in the definition.

### I061.lower.02 — the mind's judgement and the external case

**Arabic anchors:** «فإن اتحاد الطرفين ... في الخارج» · «الذهن» · «مع الوجود» · «مع اعتبار العدم» · «اعتزال ماهية المعلومة موجودة».

**English.** If the relation is taken in the external order, there must be an item there under both considerations; if it is taken in the mind, the question becomes whether the very same item can be regarded under incompatible conditions. The objection presses the asymmetry: a subject may be considered under existence, under nonexistence, or simply as a quiddity, but not all these considerations can be treated as independently existing properties of one extra-mental thing. A mental judgement may truly affirm a predicate of a quiddity without the relevant combination being realised in external existence.

### I061.lower.03 — why the subject may not be 'outside' yet remain an intelligible subject

**Arabic anchors:** «فإن الحكم على الذات» · «لا يلزم أن يعتبر فيه أحدهما» · «لأن الحكم على الذات من غير أن يعتبر معه شيء».

**English.** The commentator goes on to reject an inference from an attribution made to the essence to the positive presence of every qualification required to formulate it. The essence is considered without adding an existential condition: ascribing the predicate to it does not force us to posit two real entities, the quiddity and a separate “being-there,” nor does denying existence turn the very subject of the judgement into absolute nothingness. These alternatives would only arise if one mistook a distinction in consideration for an external division.

**Open reading:** The dense final lower line joins the opening marginal discussion of I:62. The exact sequence of references to existent/nonexistent essence and the words surrounding «الأوصاف اللازمة» should be compared at higher resolution; this passage is not promoted to full literal-proved status.

## I:62 — 'in itself', external existence, and mental consideration

### I062.upper.01 — what is intended by 'the thing in itself'

**Arabic anchors:** «ما في نفس الأمر» · «والمراد بالأمر الشيء وبالنفس الذات» · «في حد ذاته وبالنظر إليه» · «مع قطع النظر عن حكمنا» · «وإدراك المدرك».

**English.** [Truth is discussed by appeal to] what obtains **in the thing itself** (*mā fī nafs al-amr*). By *amr* is meant “thing,” and by *nafs* “essence” or “self.” The intended sense is what we understand when we say, “This thing is such-and-such *in itself*”: that is, with regard to what it is, considered in its own identity, **disregarding our judgement about it and the apprehension of the person who apprehends it**. It does not follow that “in itself” designates a second external receptacle beyond the thing.

### I062.lower.01 — whether this account is applicable where there is no existing thing

**Arabic anchors:** «(قوله والمراد بالأمر الشيء وبالنفس الذات الخ)» · «قال في شرح المقاصد ما حاصله» · «كيف يتصور هذا فيما لا ذات له ولا شيئية له في الأعيان» · «اجتماع النقيضين محال».

**English.** The commentator introduces a passage from the *Sharḥ al-Maqāṣid*: How can the expression be applied to what possesses neither an essence nor thinghood among external things? For we say that the conjunction of contradictories is impossible *in itself*, and that a statement about what is possible is or is not in accordance with what obtains in the thing itself. These claims are not restricted to an independently present external object. The suggested answer is that, upon considering two opposed terms, the intellect discerns an affirmative or negative relation between them—sometimes a necessary relation, sometimes one excluded by necessity, and sometimes one that may obtain or fail. The question is what makes that discernment correct, and whether it is independent of the particular act of judgement.

### I062.lower.02 — the two relata and the truth of their connection

**Arabic anchors:** «العقل عند ملاحظة الطرفين» · «نسبة إيجابية أو سلبية» · «مقتضاها الضرورة أو البرهان» · «خصوصية المدرك والمخبر».

**English.** The relation is not exhausted by the fact that somebody conceived it or reported it. Once the parties to the judgement are considered, the mind may discover that one relation follows necessarily, or can be demonstrated, whereas an incompatible relation cannot follow. In this discussion “what is in itself” is therefore invoked to distinguish the **ground of a correct judgement** from the mere occurrence of its being judged. One must not confuse the relation's being intellectually apprehended with its being produced by an arbitrary act of apprehension.

### I062.lower.03 — whether all mental forms must be jointly present

**Arabic anchors:** «صور جميع الكائنات والمعدومات» · «في العقل الفعال» · «لا يكون في نفس الأمر» · «الإيمان» · «الإذعان».

**English.** A further possibility is reported: perhaps all the forms of existent and nonexistent things are present in the Active Intellect, and correspondence is correspondence with those forms. But this does not yet resolve what a true attribution consists in. An affirmation can be held with assent (*iḏʿān*), or presented merely as a conceived proposition; its truth cannot consist in the speaker's act of assent. The author distinguishes the mind's determination of the conceptual terms from the truth of the relation asserted between them. References to assent, existence in the intellect and external realization are not to be collapsed into one claim.

### I062.lower.04 — possible existence and the case of something thought but not realised

**Arabic anchors:** «أن يكون في نفس الأمر» · «في مرتبة الشيء وحد ذاته» · «في الأعيان أو يكون في الأذهان» · «إن لم يكن في الأعيان».

**English.** The final argument returns to the thing's rank or essence, even where what is considered does not exist in external particulars. A quiddity can be conceived, and an essential relation to it can be correctly affirmed without thereby furnishing a corresponding external object. Conversely, a merely imagined relation is not established by the fact that it has been imagined. The discussion therefore separates **external being, mental presence, and truth or necessity belonging to a predicative relation**.

**Open reading:** The compressed references to the Active Intellect and to *assent* in the lower middle-to-bottom lines require line-by-line retranscription. The four units above are a connected provisional interpretation of visible argument spans, not a diplomatic whole-page translation.

## I:63 — opening of the section on quiddity

### I063.upper.01 — the subject of the chapter

**Arabic anchors:** «فصل في الماهية» · «آخر مباحث ما بحث الوجود والعدم» · «لأن البحث عنهما من حيث إنهما صفتان للماهية معروضتان لها».

**English.** **Section: On Quiddity.** It is placed after the discussions of existence and nonexistence, because inquiry into the latter two is an inquiry into them as descriptions attributed to quiddity, the thing of which those descriptions are predicated. To discuss existence and nonexistence in that manner presupposes an explanation of what is intended by the quiddity receiving the two descriptions.

### I063.upper.02 — what 'what is it?' asks for

**Arabic anchors:** «لفظة ما مأخوذة عن ما هو» · «ماهية الشيء ما يجاب به عن السؤال بما هو».

**English.** The word *māhiyya* (“quiddity”) is derived from the question *mā huwa* (“What is it?”). **The quiddity of a thing is what is given in reply to the question, “What is it?”** The requested answer identifies what the thing is rather than supplying a merely accidental circumstance associated with it. [The last phrase proceeds into I:64.]

### I063.lower.01 — the many senses of the term

**Arabic anchors:** «(قوله فصل في الماهية الخ)» · «إذا اعتبرت مع التحقق سميت ذاتا وحقيقة» · «وإذا اعتبرت مع التشخص سميت هوية» · «الوجود الخارجي».

**English.** On “Section: On quiddity.” The commentator cites the *Sharḥ al-Maqāṣid* to explain that, when quiddity is considered with actuality, it may be called an essence (*ḏāt*) or reality (*ḥaqīqa*); when considered with individuation it is called a concrete identity (*huwiyya*). The term *identity* may also be applied to individuation itself or to external existence, and *essence* can designate the essence insofar as it is predicable of individuals. These are different **respects under which one quiddity is contemplated**, not automatically different objects in external existence.

### I063.lower.02 — can quiddity be made up of all its accidents?

**Arabic anchors:** «الماهية تعتبر جميع عوارضها لازمة أو مفارقة» · «الأمور العامة باطل» · «الأحوال والعوارض».

**English.** The gloss contests a proposed way of gathering all concomitants into the definition of quiddity. If quiddity were understood as the totality of its necessary and separable accidents, every predicate would become constitutive of what the thing is, and the distinction between essence and what is predicated of the essence would collapse. That cannot be the meaning of the standard definition. The author examines what is necessary to the thing, what belongs to it by accident, and what can be set aside without destroying what is answered to the question “What is it?”

### I063.lower.03 — what is signified by the word 'what'

**Arabic anchors:** «الماهية ما صدقاتها» · «المفهوم الصادق» · «ما هو ذي ماهية» · «(قوله عن السؤال بما هو الخ)».

**English.** The expression *mā* in “What is it?” directs attention to an answer indicating an essence rather than to a bare occurrence of the word *thing*. But one must beware of construing the answer as nothing more than a name for the existing individual; an individual may bear many descriptions, none of which individually supplies the definition demanded. The question seeks the content by which the thing is what it is. The gloss therefore distinguishes what a general expression signifies, the instances of which it is true, and the essence that is intended by the definitional answer.

### I063.lower.04 — definition, the intelligible, and signification

**Arabic anchors:** «هو حد الشيء» · «ما يؤخذ منه الموصوفة» · «المقصود بالإشارة» · «هذا المحدد».

**English.** The commentator further considers whether “quiddity” is being used for a complete definition, for the intelligible meaning delivered by such a definition, or for the thing to which it points. These may coincide in the act of successful definition, but must not be confused when disputing a definition's wording. In particular, the *meaning* intended by the question may be the same whether the corresponding thing happens to be externally present or not. The concluding transition leads to the different ways in which a quiddity can be taken or considered.

**Open reading:** The long gloss is highly compressed and its citations to another commentary need clearer punctuation and word-by-word source proof. The English above preserves the overall progression but is not promoted to literal-complete.

## I:64 — taking a quiddity without, with, or without specifying accidents

### I064.upper.01 — the three considerations

**Arabic anchors:** «قد تؤخذ بشرط شيء» · «أي المقارنة للعوارض» · «تسمى المخلوطة» · «تخلطها مع العوارض» · «ولاحقا في وجودها».

**English.** Quiddity may be considered **on the condition of something** (*bi-šarṭ šayʾ*), namely with the accompanying accidents. In that respect it is called a **mixed quiddity** (*al-māhiyya al-maḫlūṭa*), because it has been taken together with accidents that accompany it in existence. The example begins with human existence among external individuals, in which a quiddity is not encountered bereft of all determinations.

### I064.upper.02 — external individuals and the relation of their accidents

**Arabic anchors:** «فإن وجود الأشخاص في الخارج» · «بين كرم وعرض من أفراد الإنسان».

**English.** The existence of individuals externally always occurs with particular qualifications and accidents—one individual may possess generosity, another some other attribute. Such differences are genuinely associated with existing persons, but they do not all enter the definition by which each is human. The discussion is not asserting that every accident is essential merely because no particular exists without accidents.

### I064.ruled.01 — a separate objection to describing the division as a real partition

**Arabic anchors:** «(قوله قد تؤخذ الخ)» · «إنما لم يقل تنقسم الماهية إلى كذا» · «المقسم يجب أن يكون واحدا» · «هذه الاعتبارات للماهية الواحدة».

**English.** On “It may be taken ...”: The expression is **not** simply “quiddity is divided into these species,” because the thing divided must be one in the relevant sense, whereas these are modes of taking or considering the same quiddity. A partition into different sorts of real things would be misleading: one does not first have three externally distinct quiddities which are subsequently labelled by their conditions. One rather makes different conceptual qualifications of what is one as a subject of consideration.

### I064.lower.01 — whether the same quiddity is also 'the agent'

**Arabic anchors:** «ليس بمحل الفاعل كما صرح به في شرح المقاصد» · «يجعل الفاعل ما به يكون الفاعل» · «بها يكون الشيء هو هو».

**English.** The commentator remarks that what is at issue is the quiddity itself, not an arbitrary attribution of agency or action to it. A definition of *agent* appeals to what makes an agent an agent; the definition of *quiddity* appeals to what makes the thing the thing it is. It does not follow that a quiddity, simply because it is a quiddity, is its own producer. Confusion can arise if the particle “by which” is given one and the same causal force wherever it occurs.

### I064.lower.02 — the 'whatness' of the essence cannot be identified with all its descriptions

**Arabic anchors:** «وأقول ذلك ممنوع على هذا التقدير أيضا» · «الماهية ما به الشيء هو هو» · «المعروض للعوارض» · «تغاير المفهوم».

**English.** “I say: that is denied even on this interpretation.” The proposed account still threatens to identify the subject described with its accidents or with the meaning of a phrase predicated of it. The question *what is it?* is not answered by supplying any description that happens to be true. Rather, the definitional content must be distinguished from subsequent concomitants, even when the same actual subject supports them all. This is why a verbal change in the proposed definition does not automatically resolve the difficulty.

### I064.lower.03 — generic, specific, and differential descriptions

**Arabic anchors:** «الجزء» · «الضرر واللزوم» · «الضربين من الضمير» · «خبر الذي» · «الماء ماء».

**English.** The gloss then tests the account against the familiar parts of definition: what is predicated by a genus, what is added as a differentia, and what belongs only as a property or accident. The expressions may refer to the same subject and differ in their meanings, yet still not be interchangeable in an answer to *what is it?* Repetition of an identical noun (“water is water”) supplies no new conception, whereas a suitable definition does; an accidental description, even if informative, may not answer the question in the requisite way. The last lines introduce an objection concerning the **quiddity considered without an accompanying condition**, continued on I:65.

**Open reading:** Several lower-line exempla and grammatical references are uncertain, and the three lower units above compress their syntax; preserve them as provisional until the full transcription and second proof. The separately ruled note is distinct from the longer lower gloss.
