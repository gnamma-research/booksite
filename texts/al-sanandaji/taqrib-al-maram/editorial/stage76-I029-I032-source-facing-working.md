## I:29 — The prerequisite of an unconditional obligation; text and consensus

### I029.upper.01 — Closing the proof from the obligatory means
**Arabic locating text:** «أي بالنص والإجماع والمقدور الذي كان مقدمة وهو قوله فالواجب المطلق».

**English:** —that is, **by scriptural text and consensus**, and with respect to the prerequisite **within one's power**, which was the earlier premise of the argument, namely the statement: “Therefore the unconditional obligation …” The sentence is continued from I:28 and the following upper argument begins on I:30. One must not make this isolated upper line into a separate complete proof.

### I029.ruled.01 — What an unconditional obligation means
**Arabic locating text:** «(قوله الواجب المطلق) المراد بالواجب المطلق أن لا يكون وجوبه مقيداً بما يتوقف عليه، كتوقف الزكاة على حصول النصاب منه».

**English:** On his words “the unconditional obligation”: what is intended is an obligation **whose binding force is not conditioned upon a prerequisite required for its performance**, unlike the obligation of the alms-tax, which is conditioned upon the attainment of the legally specified minimum holding (*niṣāb*). The note concerns the **condition for a duty's becoming binding**, not the claim that duties can be performed without their means.

### I029.lower.01 — Objections to an obligation established by text and consensus
**Arabic locating text:** «(قوله أي بالنص والإجماع الخ)»؛ «فاعلم أنه لا إله إلا الله»؛ «ليس بقطعي الدلالة»؛ «بل يدعي الخصم الاكتفاء بالتقليد»؛ «وأجيب بأن الظن كاف في الوجوب الشرعي»؛ «دليل إجمالي»؛ «فرض عين ... فرض كفاية».

**English:** On “that is, by scripture and consensus”: it has been denied that proof has been established that **knowledge must be obtained by investigation**. As to the scriptural text, the Almighty's saying, “Know that there is no deity but God,” is not decisive as a proof of obligation: the imperative is not invariably employed for obligation. As to consensus, the objection is that its transmission in this particular matter is not decisive. The opponent claims that **uncritical reliance on authority** suffices, pointing out that the Companions accepted professions of faith from common people without demanding the procedures of learned disputation, verification, and demonstration. The answer is that a **probable indication** may suffice to establish an obligation of religious law, and the Companions' acceptance of ordinary believers does not show that those believers lacked every proof. They could possess recognition resting on **general indications**. The margin therefore distinguishes two demands: possessing an elementary indication that raises one above mere imitation is an **individual duty**; possessing sufficiently detailed argumentation to remove sophistical doubts is a **collective duty**. The source signals that a related question returns towards the close of the book. This is a *reported dialectic*, not an editorial declaration that all Muslims actually carried out formal demonstrations.

### I029.lower.02 — The disputed extension of “unconditional”
**Arabic locating text:** «ليس معنى الواجب المطلق ما يكون وجوبه على جميع التقادير والأحوال»؛ «ما من واجب إلا وهو مقيد ببعض التقادير»؛ «تحصيل الحاصل»؛ «لو نظر وجبت المعرفة وإلا فلا»؛ «مقيد بوجود مقدمتها ... لا بوجوب مقدمتها».

**English:** On the expression “the unconditionally obligatory”: it does **not** mean an act obligatory under every conceivable supposition and circumstance. That would leave nothing whatsoever unconditionally obligatory. Every duty is subject to some qualifications; at the least, a duty is not imposed anew under the supposition that precisely what is demanded has already been performed, since this would amount to **acquiring what has already been acquired**. Rather, the relevant point is that a duty is not conditioned, *as regards its becoming binding*, upon independently requiring the agent first to acquire the prerequisite. The disputed analysis can be expressed by saying that the knowledge whose obligatory character is being discussed may be obligatory whether the person has investigated or has not yet investigated, while **the emergence of the knowledge** still depends on the investigation in its proper order. The margin disputes a formula that makes the main duty obligatory only if its prerequisite has already occurred; in that formula, the prerequisite's **existence** is treated as a condition for the obligation, which is the reverse of the unrestricted duty being analysed. **Reading caution:** The dense final lines distinguish several scopes of “conditioned on a prerequisite,” and their pronoun references require a final Arabic transcription before this can be classed as literal complete.

## I:30 — Whether the duty to investigate precedes religious law

### I030.upper.01 — Positions concerning the source of obligation
**Arabic locating text:** «(وعند المعتزلة) النظر في معرفة الله تعالى واجب لكَوْنِه مقدمة للمعرفة الواجبة»؛ «(لكونها دافعة لضرر) مظنون وهو (خوف العقاب)»؛ «فالوجوب عندنا شرعي وعندهم عقلي».

**English:** “According to the Muʿtazilites,” examination directed toward knowledge of God, exalted is He, is obligatory, since it is a prerequisite for the knowledge that is obligatory. That knowledge wards off a harm which is thought possible, namely **fear of punishment**. Thus for us the obligation is **legal/revelatory**, whereas for them it is **rational**. The parenthesized phrases belong to the quoted matn, while the explanatory connectives belong to the sharḥ.

### I030.upper.02 — Their circularity objection against a revealed command to inquire
**Arabic locating text:** «(قالوا لو لم يجب) النظر (بالشرع لما صح)»؛ «(النظر في المعجزة)»؛ «وفي جميع ما يتوقف ثبوته على ثبوته»؛ «(قبل ثبوت الشرع)».

**English:** They argue: if inquiry were not already obligatory before appeal to revelation, what would justify the demand that one investigate the Prophet's miracle and the other indications on which the establishment of revelation's authority depends? A command of religious law to perform the very inquiry by which the truth of that law is to be established cannot, without circularity, be the **first ground** of the inquiry's obligatoriness. More particularly, the Prophet, blessings and peace be upon him, directs a hearer to the miracle; the hearer might say that he is not obliged to investigate until the obligation has been established by the law, while the authority of that law depends on the examination of the miracle. The opponent thus attempts to derive a **rationally prior** obligation.

### I030.ruled.01 — Why fear has an obligatory force on the opponent's account
**Arabic locating text:** «(قوله خوف العقاب) يعني أن الخوف من العقاب على ترك معرفة الله ضرر ... فكانت واجبة».

**English:** On “fear of punishment”: the feared punishment for abandoning recognition of God constitutes the apprehension of harm. Because obtaining knowledge removes that fear, the Muʿtazilite argument treats knowledge, and what is needed to obtain it, as obligatory. The note explains the **rational avoidance-of-harm inference**; it does not independently establish that this particular danger is actual.

### I030.lower.01 — What is presupposed in demanding inquiry into a miracle
**Arabic locating text:** «(قوله وفي جميع ما يتوقف ثبوته على ثبوته الخ)»؛ «إن الشرع موقوف على ثبوت الصانع وإرساله الرسول والتصديق له بالمعجزات الباهرة»؛ «فلو قال النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم انظر في هذه الأمور ...»؛ «النظر يمكن الحصول وإن لم يجب»؛ «إلزام النبي ... على غير الواجب».

**English:** On his words “and in everything whose establishment depends upon its establishment”: the establishment of the law is dependent on establishing the Creator, His sending a messenger, and the messenger's truthfulness through manifest miracles. The proofs of those matters involve inquiry. If the Prophet tells someone to examine the matters—including the miracle—upon which the authority of religious prescriptions depends, that person might object: “I shall not investigate until this religious obligation is proved to me.” But the authority claimed for that obligation is itself among the things that require inquiry. The reply acknowledges that **inquiry can take place without its having first been commanded**: what is possible to perform need not already be obligatory. Yet the point at issue is the Prophet's **binding demand** for inquiry; an obligation to comply does not follow from mere feasibility. The marginal argument therefore separates a person's ability to investigate from the authority to **compel** him to investigate. It concludes by returning to the danger of making the sending of prophets ineffective under the proposed circular dependency. **Reading caution:** The closing phrase about the mission's futility is compressed in the printed line and should be rechecked before publication as a verbatim quotation.

## I:31 — The first duty and the status of the law before investigation

### I031.upper.01 — Can someone refuse inquiry until inquiry has been proved obligatory?
**Arabic locating text:** «ولا يثبت الشرع عندي ما لم أنظر لأن ثبوته نظري»؛ «(ورد) أولا ...»؛ «لأن النظر وإن كان واجباً عقلاً لكن وجوبه ليس ضرورياً بل كان نظرياً»؛ «لا أنظر ما لم يجب ولا يجب ما لم أنظر».

**English:** The interlocutor says: “Religious law is not established for me until I investigate, for establishing its authority is itself a matter of inference.” The answer begins by challenging the alleged priority. Even if inquiry is deemed obligatory by reason, the **judgment that it is obligatory** need not be a primitive self-evident judgment; it can itself be established inferentially. The sceptical hearer could then say, even to the Muʿtazilite: “I shall not investigate until doing so is proved obligatory, and it cannot be proved obligatory until I investigate.” Merely moving the duty from revelation to reason does not automatically remove the threatened circle. The lower notes take up the force of this counter-objection.

### I031.upper.02 — Does inquiry or knowledge come first among the obligations?
**Arabic locating text:** «(الشرع) عند المكلف»؛ «(هو العلم بالوجوب) أي وجوب النظر»؛ «(لا نفس الوجوب)»؛ «(ثم إنها) أي المعرفة (أول) الواجبات المقصودة»؛ «(والنظر فيها) ... (وسيلة إليها)»؛ «فإن أراد أول المعرفة ...».

**English:** The author distinguishes the **obligation itself** from **knowledge of the obligation**. A requirement can be binding without the agent's already having arrived at an explicit demonstration that it is binding; otherwise each needed act would presuppose a prior complete knowledge of its own duty. The inquiry then turns to the expression “the **first duty**”: some mean the first **object intended**, namely recognition of God; others mean the earliest **means to the object**, namely rational examination. Yet a third formulation says that the **first intention to investigate** precedes the actual investigation. The conclusion depends on whether “first” refers to priority **as an aim** or priority **in the process of bringing about the aim**. The last upper clauses continue into I:32.

### I031.ruled.01 — What exactly depends on proving the law?
**Arabic locating text:** «(قوله بأن المتوقف على ثبوته الخ)»؛ «وفي نسخة بأن المتوقف على النظر ...»؛ «فلا يحتاج إلى تقدير الثبوت عند المكلف».

**English:** On “what depends on its establishment”: a separate note refers to a **different printed wording** in another copy: “what depends on investigation.” On that reading, the sentence requires no supplementary insertion of “the law's establishment *for the legally responsible person*.” This is a reported reading, **not a manuscript variant verified in Stage 76**. The note should remain distinct from the principal commentary.

### I031.lower.01 — The investigation/obligation regress and competing first duties
**Arabic locating text:** «(قوله ولا يثبت الشرع عندي ما لم أنظر الخ)»؛ «(قوله وثانياً الخ)»؛ «الجواب بأن عدم الإلزام ...»؛ «وجوب النظر في نفس الأمر»؛ «وجوب العلم»؛ «فأول الواجبات هو النظر والمعرفة لفظاً ...».

**English:** The gloss reconstructs the opponent's refusal to investigate: if the religious law cannot be established without an inquiry into its proofs, then telling him that he is legally obligated to inquire seems to presuppose what the inquiry is meant to establish. The marginal author replies that the threatened regress depends on conflating the **objective bindingness** of a duty with the person's **awareness of that bindingness**. If what is at issue is knowledge of the legal command, further examination may be needed before one knows it, but it does not follow that there is no duty in the order of things. The margin separately distinguishes rationally knowing that one must investigate, actually investigating, and acquiring the knowledge sought. Since these are not one and the same act, asking which is “first” without indicating the ordering relation produces a verbal dispute. Some schools make **knowledge** first as the end; others **investigation** first as the means; still others the **intention** to investigate, because a deliberate act involves a prior determination. **Reading caution:** The bottom line of the marginal text continues the debate; this paragraph is a connected first-pass reading, not a line-exhaustive verified transcription.

## I:32 — Inquiry, intention and the sources of theological proof

### I032.upper.01 — Differing accounts of the first obligation
**Arabic locating text:** «الواجبات مطلقاً وإلا فلا نزاع في أن أول الواجبات النظر لتوقف المعرفة عليه»؛ «(والدليل) هو ما يمكن أن يتوصل بالنظر فيه إلى حكم»؛ «(جازم أو غير جازم)»؛ «(وقد خص) بالحكم (الجازم في مقابلة الأمارة)».

**English:** —[if one speaks of the first of] the obligations **without further qualification**; otherwise there is no disagreement that investigation precedes knowledge in the order of **the means required for obtaining it**. “Proof” is then defined as that by the consideration of which one may reach a judgment—whether a **decisive** or a **nondecisive** judgment, according to the broader usage. The expression “proof” is also employed more strictly for what brings about a **decisive judgment**, in contrast with an *amāra*—an indication that yields probability. The notes explain why the broader and narrower uses should not be silently collapsed.

### I032.upper.02 — Rational proof and reliable report
**Arabic locating text:** «(على عقل) وسماع من الخبر الصادق»؛ «(والا) يتوقف شيء منه عليه»؛ «(عمومة القرآن القطع)»؛ «(ولازم بالحكم)»؛ «(لا يثبت إلا بالعقل)».

**English:** A proof is divided into **rational evidence** and evidence transmitted from **truthful report**. The text invokes the Qurʾān and transmitted reports and distinguishes the grounds on which their evidentiary force is established. An argument from report cannot simply bypass reason altogether: the conditions that make an utterance *reliable evidence* must themselves be known, while some of those conditions are established through rational investigation. Conversely, the fact that reason supplies indispensable premises does not erase the distinct evidentiary role of transmission. **Reading caution:** Several short connectors in the crowded upper printing need a second exact transcription; these English clauses preserve the argumentative order, not an independently certified Arabic critical text.

### I032.ruled.01 — The sense of a “prior intention”
**Arabic locating text:** «(قوله بل القصد الخ)»؛ «وربما يظهر للنفس ما يصير مبدأ باعثاً على القصد»؛ «القصد أعم ...».

**English:** On “rather, the intention”: a preliminary apprehension can arise in the soul and become the motive that prompts intention. This should not be made into an infinite series of **deliberate intentions before every intention**; the earlier occurrence is not necessarily an act of intending in the same sense as the later one. The note therefore distinguishes a first prompting apprehension from the deliberate intention to proceed with rational inquiry.

### I032.lower.01 — What rests on the prior establishment of duty?
**Arabic locating text:** «(قوله بل القصد الخ)»؛ «على ما لم يثبت الشرع ...»؛ «(قوله بل يلزم الدور الخ)»؛ «المعرفة بمعنى الجهل بالمطلوب»؛ «القدرة والإرادة»؛ «النظر في نفس الأمر».

**English:** The gloss returns to the concern that, if the authority of law has not been established, the attempt to derive an **obligation to investigate** from a legal command threatens circularity. But it is not necessary that **the agent know the duty's proof** before the duty be binding in itself. Some writers instead take the lack of prior knowledge of the **sought object** to be the motive for investigation: inquiry ordinarily begins with not yet knowing the conclusion, not with a demonstration of the very duty to inquire. The marginal writer distinguishes that epistemic condition from the **power and will** needed to undertake the inquiry. If one calls the earlier conditions “the first obligations,” a further specification of what “first” means is needed; otherwise a disputed logical ordering is merely restated as a practical command.

### I032.lower.02 — Is transmitted certainty dependent on rational preliminaries?
**Arabic locating text:** «(قوله لأن يلزم الدور الخ)»؛ «ثبوت النقل من العلم بصدق الناقل المبني على ثبوت الصانع وبعثة النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم ودلالة المعجزة»؛ «بطريق إثباته في العقل دون ...».

**English:** The argument concerning revelation is sharpened: certainty that a report is authoritative rests on knowledge that the reporter is truthful; in the prophetic case this includes the existence of the Creator, the sending of the Messenger, and the evidentiary significance of miracles. These are not established merely by repeating the report whose authority is under examination. Thus the **reliability of transmission**, as used in theological proof, presupposes some prior rational considerations. The note ends with a transition to the general philosophical topics opened on I:33. **Reading caution:** The final lower words run into a heavily crowded line; the summary here does not claim a complete diplomatic extraction or conclusive wording.
