# Sanandajī + Wasīm — Stage 26 source-critical edition
## Reward, punishment, entitlement and moral motivation · original Taqrīb II:261–263

**Witness:** user-supplied 632-image combined *Taqrīb al-Marām*, original printed II:261–263 = physical PDF 556–558. **Separate base-text witness:** photographed original Cairo 1912 *Tahdhīb al-Kalām*, its own printed pp.108–109; the latter is an independently physically checked printed witness for the parenthesized base lemmata, **not Sanandajī's running explanation**. All pages were visually inspected; the upper original has been source-aligned in 23 selected bilingual clauses, while the lower commentary is mostly outstanding. **This is an extensive selected first pass, not a complete all-register translation of the three pages.**

### Exact textual structure

**Printed II:261** opens with the end of the preceding afterlife section, including scriptural descriptions of the bridge and balance. Then a genuine **فصل** begins *reward as God's favour and punishment as His justice*. Its al-Taftāzānī lemmata and Sanandajī's inserted explanations are not one voice. The main argument continues through **II:262** and the **upper portion of II:263**; II:263 below the horizontal rule is a separate long small-type argument, not a continuation of the upper commentary by default.

### The base-text witness, independently photographed: Cairo 1912 *Tahdhīb* pp.108–109

The Cairo original contains the following **connected base-text proposition** across printed pp.108–109, selected and punctuated for ease of reading (this is **not** a photographically certified complete diplomatic collation of Sanandajī's entire page, whose wording is interleaved with commentary):

> الثواب فضل من الله والعقاب عدل منه، ومعنى وجوبهما أنه وعد أو أوعد فلا يخلف الله وعده على خلاف في الوعيد، ومعنى استحقاقهما ملاءمة إضافتهما إلى الطاعات والمعاصي في مجاري العقول والعادات، لأنه لا واجب على الله، ولأن الطاعات وإن كثرت لا تفي بشكر بعض النعم، ولأنهما لو استحقا لما سقطا عمن عاش على الكفر ثم آمن أو على الإيمان ثم كفر. وقول المعتزلة إن عدم وجوبهما يفضي إلى التواني في الطاعات والاجتراء على المعاصي وأن إيجاب المشاق بلا نفع يقابلها ظلم ولا مضرة في تركها موجب لوجوب كل ما في فعله منفعة مردود بأن مجرد جواز الترك غير قادح ومجرد الوقوع كاف في المقصود وأن الغرض لا ينحصر فيما ذكر.

**Close connected English:** “Reward is a favour from God and punishment an act of His justice. Their being obligatory means that He has promised or threatened, and He does not fail to keep His promise—though the status of threats is disputed. Deserving them means the appropriateness of assigning them to obedience and disobedience in the ordinary course of reason and practice. Nothing is incumbent upon God; acts of obedience, however plentiful, cannot repay even some of His gifts. Were reward and punishment genuine claims owed to the servant, they would not fall away for someone who had lived in unbelief and then believed, or in belief and then disbelieved. The Muʿtazilites object that without an obligation [of divine reward and punishment], people would slacken in obedience and become emboldened in sin; and that imposing hardships without a corresponding benefit would be unjust, while an action's beneficialness, where omitting it causes no harm, would make every beneficial act obligatory. The reply is that the mere possibility of nonperformance does not defeat the purpose; actual performance can suffice, and the relevant end is not confined to what has been specified.”

The connected matn reconstruction here is reported as **first-pass editorial collation** supported by visible original images, not as a separate final critically established base edition. Any differences between the Cairo print and Sanandajī quotations will be enumerated only upon exact second proof; the separate user Tahdhīb project is untouched.

### Argument structure (critical commentary, distinct from original translated text)

**1. Two meanings of the 'necessity' of reward.** The context rejects a necessity imposed upon God from outside while affirming a necessity in the sense of fidelity to His own **promise**. The phrase **على خلاف في الوعيد** is indispensable: it says the standing of punishment-threats is debated and allows a different analysis of mercy or remission. It does not license treating promise and threat as logically equivalent.

**2. Two meanings of ‘deserving’ (*istiḥqāq*).** The opening grants that it is fitting to connect obedience with reward and disobedience with punishment. It does not grant that obedience establishes a debt or metaphysically binding right against God. The argument that obediences cannot repay God's previous gifts targets this further inference to independent entitlement. The Sanandajī explanations must therefore remain separate from the briefer base proposition.

**3. A concrete thought experiment from conversion.** The source asks whether a claim-right acquired through earlier conduct would survive the person's transition from disbelief to belief or vice versa. Its point is not an empirical statement about every individual's psychology, but a counterexample to the inference from past deeds to an unchangeable divine debt.

**4. Two distinct Muʿtazilite objections, not one.** The first is motivational: without a guaranteed reward/punishment, people might neglect obedience or be bold in sin. The second concerns distributive justice: burdens without compensating benefit and duties towards all beneficial actions. The source supplies differentiated replies: *the mere possibility of not doing something* does not invalidate an effective exhortation, while an actual benefit can be sufficient without a separate rational duty that it must exist. The lower marginal note on II:263 expressly says the source editor's division of these replies matters.

**5. The end need not be restricted to compensation.** II:263 proposes thanksgiving for previous blessings and the joy of fulfilling a difficult duty as alternatives to an entitlement model. Those are distinct explanations of why worship can be worthwhile, even before requiring that reward be legally or rationally due.
