### 1. Speech, II:141 — corrected direct translations

The following snippets are source-matched in the predecessor, but not continuous with one another. They must not be assembled as a fabricated paragraph. The speaker/lemma allocation needs a second visual audit.

| Clause | Source Arabic, previously recorded | Revised close English |
|---|---|---|
| C2.141.01 | ليس من جنس الأصوات والحروف | It does not belong to the genus of sounds and letters. |
| C2.141.02 | صفة أزلية قائمة بذاته تعالى | A beginningless attribute subsisting in His essence, exalted is He. |
| C2.141.03 | يدل عليها بالعبارة والكتابة والإشارة | It is signified by verbal expression, writing and gesture/sign. |
| C2.141.04 | إن المعقول من لفظ الكلام هو الحسي المنظوم من الحروف المسموعة الدالة على المعاني المقصودة | What is understood by the word “speech” is the perceptible, ordered complex of audible letters signifying intended meanings. |

**Translation repair:** the preceding UI rendering of `الإشارة` as “indication” in C2.141.03 repeated `يدل عليها` unhelpfully. “Gesture/sign” renders the third **means of signification**, distinguished from the verbal and the written. Do not convert the negative claim about divine speech's genus into denial that audible/written signs can refer to it. C2.141.04 records an **opposing account** of ordinary sensible speech, not a straightforward assertion by the commentator.

### 2. Takwīn, II:150 — syntactical and ontological distinctions

The predecessor provides the relatively continuous selected upper-register wording:

> والحق أنه ليس صفة حقيقية كالعلم والإرادة بل هو معنى إضافي يعقل من تعلق المؤثر بالأثر

| Clause | Arabic | Revised close English |
|---|---|---|
| C2.150.01 | والحق أنه | The position presented here as correct is that it |
| C2.150.02 | ليس صفة حقيقية كالعلم والإرادة | is not a real attribute such as knowledge and will; |
| C2.150.03 | بل هو معنى إضافي | rather, it is a relational meaning |
| C2.150.04 | يعقل من تعلق المؤثر بالأثر | conceived through the producer's relation to its effect. |

**Translation (selected words in sequence):** “The view presented here as correct is that it is not a real attribute like knowledge and will; rather, it is a relational meaning conceived from the producer's relation to the effect.”

**Critical commentary:** `صفة حقيقية` here contrasts with `معنى إضافي`: a real intrinsic perfection versus an expression under a relational consideration. This must **not** be mistranslated as a claim that the production itself is imaginary, that the effect is unreal, or that `التعلق` denotes a physical connector. Conversely, `يعقل` by itself does **not** settle whether the relation has an extramental foundation, a real categorial mode, or a merely mental status. That requires the missing surrounding objection and reply. In the broader kalām tradition, treating `takwīn` as a distinct eternal attribute is disputed; no positive author or school attribution should be inferred from this excerpt alone. The opening `والحق` signals a position defended *in the present printed argument*, but attribution to the original matn versus Sanandajī needs inspection of the source typography.

### 3. Divine vision, II:151 — nonspatial perception

Two **noncontiguous** preserved spans:

- `من أهمها هل يمكن أن يرى وهل يمكن العلم بحقيقته` — “Among its principal questions are: can He be seen, and can His reality be known?” These are **two separate questions**; no identification of knowledge of divine reality with visual perception is established.
- `من غير جهة ولا مقابلة` — “Without direction or spatial facing/opposition.” `مقابلة` has the technical sense of face-to-face position, not disagreement or logical contradiction.

The available extended source selection has deliberate ellipses:
`أنه تعالى يصح أن يرى بمعنى حصول الحالة الإدراكية الحاصلة لنا ... التي نسميها الرؤية لكن ... لتنزهه تعالى عن ذلك`.
A cautious **fragmentary rendering** is: “He, exalted is He, may be seen in the sense of the occurrence of a perceptual state of the kind we experience … which we call vision; but … since He is exalted above that.” The antecedent of `ذلك` and the exact scope of the concession must **not** be reconstructed from ellipses. The original section starts at **II:151**; the predecessor's earlier II:154 placement was already corrected.

### 4. Lower-register kasb gloss, II:177 — continuous selected passage

**Authority:** lower small type, previously inspected original PDF **472**, historical **author unassigned**. This is **not** certified as Wasīm; the full surrounding marginal note is absent. The following is a **new close clause-by-clause translation** of the prior recorded Arabic words, rather than an independently newly transcribed source.

> والسر في ذلك هو أن الكسب هو أن يستفيد أحد شيئا لنفسه وهو لا يقتضي إيجاده بل يصح مع وجوده بغيره أيضا بخلاف الخلق فإنه إعطاء الوجود

| Clause | Arabic | Revised close English |
|---|---|---|
| M2.177.01 | والسر في ذلك هو أن | The reason underlying this is that |
| M2.177.02 | الكسب هو أن يستفيد أحد شيئا لنفسه | acquisition consists in someone's gaining something for himself; |
| M2.177.03 | وهو لا يقتضي إيجاده | it does not entail bringing that thing into existence; |
| M2.177.04 | بل يصح مع وجوده بغيره أيضا | indeed, it can obtain even when the thing exists through another as well; |
| M2.177.05 | بخلاف الخلق فإنه إعطاء الوجود | unlike creation, which is the bestowal of existence. |

**Revised continuous English:** “The rationale is that acquisition consists in someone's obtaining something for himself. This does not entail bringing it into existence; rather, it can obtain even when the thing is brought into existence through another. Creation, by contrast, is the bestowal of existence.”

**Logical reconstruction, editorial not quoted:**
1. The concept of `kasb` does not **entail** the acquirer's own efficient origination (`lā yaqtaḍī ījādahu`).
2. It is compatible with existence bestowed `bi-ghayrihi` — through another.
3. `khalq` is predicated under a different formal notion: giving existence.
4. Therefore one cannot infer *from acquisition alone* that the acquirer creates the acquired thing. This alone does **not** establish voluntariness, responsibility, or exactly how secondary power acts; those require the larger source.

**Lexical controls:** `يستفيد` describes acquiring/gaining and does not guarantee that the thing is morally beneficial. `إيجاد` and `إعطاء الوجود` mean causing/bestowing existence; the excerpt does not expressly contain `من العدم`, so “out of absolute nothing” must not be silently inserted. `بغيره` indicates dependence upon another, not necessarily **temporal priority** of the externally originated object. The referent of `ذلك` in the initial clause remains underdetermined without the preceding marginal lines.

### 5. The choice test, II:178 — avoid a false inference

Preserved **separate short** source fragments:
- `وأما المعتزلة القائلون بكون العبد موجد الأفعال` — “As for the Muʿtazilites who maintain that the servant brings acts into existence …” (reported position, with the following argument omitted).
- `حركة سقوطه من السطح` — “His motion in falling from the roof.”
- `حركة صعوده` — “His motion in ascending.”

The contrast of involuntary falling and voluntary ascending **can test** a theory of human agency, but by itself does not prove a particular account of created power; its role as objection, concession or rebuttal remains open until the full printed sequence is read. Note the restriction `القائلون`: do not present the quotation as a clause-complete history of every Muʿtazilī position.
