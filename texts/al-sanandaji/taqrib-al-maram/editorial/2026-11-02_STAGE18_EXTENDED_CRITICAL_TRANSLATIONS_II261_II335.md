# Stage 18 — extended selected source translations and technical apparatus
## Taqrīb al-Marām, printed volume II:261–335 (source-only excerpts, not complete pages)

**Primary witness:** user-reuploaded complete 632-image two-volume facsimile `taqribmaram(2).pdf`, printed volume II positions `p` = original physical PDF `p + 295`. This note translates **selected connected source spans** at the section openings, identifies their arguments, and distinguishes authored main text from secondary layers. Missing intermediate words are marked by ellipses, rather than invisibly supplied. **Individual Wasīm ḥāshiya units are not transcribed or assigned anew.**

### 1. II:261 — reward as generosity, punishment as justice

**Original upper-source selected connected statement:**

> فصل. الثواب فضل من الله والعقاب عدل منه، لأن الكل ملكه فلا ظلم منه ولا وجوب عليه ولا استحقاق من العبد خلافا للمعتزلة.

**Close English:** “Chapter. Reward is a favour bestowed by God, while punishment is an exercise of justice on His part, for everything is His possession. Consequently, there is no injustice on His part, no obligation incumbent upon Him, and no entitlement belonging to the servant—in contrast to the Muʿtazilites.”

**Technical reading:** This is not a merely psychological declaration of divine generosity: it answers the specific normative claim that a servant obtains a claim-right by an act of obedience. The repeated negative `lā` governs distinct objects: injustice (`ẓulm`), incumbent obligation (`wujūb ʿalayhi`), and the servant's entitlement (`istiḥqāq min al-ʿabd`). The theological meaning of `ʿadl` is itself disputed and must not be flattened into modern distributive justice. The full printed objection/reply sequence continues beyond the selected quotation.

### 2. II:264 — permanence of the states of the Hereafter

**Original selected opening:**

> فصل. لا خلاف بين المسلمين في خلود من دخل الجنة، وفي خلود الكافر في النار.

**Close English:** “Chapter. There is no disagreement among Muslims concerning the perpetual abiding of one who has entered Paradise, or concerning the unbeliever's abiding in the Fire.”

**Scope:** The passage immediately proceeds to exceptional or disputed cases; this opening must not be taken as a complete account of the destiny of children, grave sinners or anyone to whom no prophetic proof has reached. In its subsequent development the work distinguishes the destiny of those permanently excluded from salvation from the fate of sinning Muslims, and the lower gloss tests the conceptual meaning of `khulūd`.

### 3. II:270 — forgiveness, the major sinner and intercession

**Original selected connected opening:**

> فصل. اتفقت الأمة على أنه تعالى يعفو ويغفر عن الصغائر مطلقا، وعن الكبائر بعد التوبة، ولا يعفو عن الكفر قطعا ...

**Close English:** “Chapter. The community has agreed that He, exalted is He, pardons and forgives minor sins without qualification, and major sins after repentance, whereas He does not forgive unbelief—categorically ...”

**Critical note:** The subsequent argument considers whether major sins *without* repentance may nevertheless fall under divine pardon or intercession. The adverb `muṭlaqan` applies in the opening specifically to minor sins; it should not be carried without qualification over the subsequent and differently framed account of major sins. The last clause is stated as part of the author's reported consensus, while the nature of the relevant unbelief and the temporal situation are debated in adjoining text. The rest of the printed page is not silently included in this translation.

### 4. II:275 — repentance

**Original selected opening:**

> فصل. في التوبة، هي في اللغة الرجوع، يقال تاب إذا رجع ... وفي الشرع هي الندم عن المعصية لكونه معصية ...

**Close English:** “Chapter. Concerning repentance: in linguistic usage it means returning; one says that a person repented when he turned back ... In the Law, it is regret for an act of disobedience precisely insofar as it is disobedience ...”

**Formal distinction:** `li-kawnihi maʿṣiya` does crucial work. Mere regret over an unpleasant consequence, social loss, or failure of advantage is not necessarily `tawba` under the definition being discussed. Nor do the abbreviated words resolve all further conditions: cessation, decision not to return, restitution and the possible renewal of repentance must be read from the remainder of the argument.

### 5. II:282 — faith as assent, not merely verbal profession

**Original source heading and lemma:**

> فصل في حقيقة الإيمان، هو في عرف أهل اللغة التصديق ...

**Close English:** “A chapter on the reality of faith. In the conventional usage of language, it is affirmation or assent ...”

**Analysis:** The subsequent question is what counts as `taṣdīq` under theological and legal usages: an inner act or state, a spoken profession, the role of works, and the visibility of a Muslim's social/juridical standing. To translate `taṣdīq` simply as “belief” already risks conflating the distinction the author is about to examine. The selected heading alone cannot certify the whole surrounding dispute about actions and disbelief.

### 6. II:297 and II:301–302 — faith, Islam, certainty and following authority

**Original page II:297 selected text:**

> فصل في الإسلام. الإجماع على أن كل مؤمن مسلم وبالعكس ...

**Close English:** “A chapter on Islam. There is agreement that every person of faith is a Muslim and conversely ...”

This is the opening claim whose scope requires distinguishing the extension of religious communities from the intensional definitions of `īmān` and `islām`. A terminological identity cannot simply be inferred from reciprocal legal application.

**Original heading at II:301:**

> مطلب الكلام على صحة الاستثناء في الإيمان

**Close English:** “A discussion concerning the validity of adding a qualification or exception to the assertion of one's faith.”

The question touches how present assent is known, the status of ultimate salvation and whether “if God wills” expresses principled uncertainty or humility about final outcome. The book's complete objection-and-answer cannot be settled solely from the title.

**Original opening at II:302:**

> فصل. الجمهور على صحة إيمان المقلد وترتب الأحكام عليه في الدنيا والآخرة ...

**Close English:** “Chapter. The majority maintains the validity of the faith of the one who follows authority without independent proof, and the applicability of the relevant rulings to him in this world and the next ...”

**Analytical point:** The author distinguishes the correctness of the lay believer's assent from the further question whether there is a duty of independent rational inquiry. Not possessing a technical syllogistic demonstration does not, by itself, imply the absence of assent. Conversely, accepting the validity of this faith is not equivalent to declaring reflective inquiry pointless. This distinction should be retained in the eventual complete translation.

### 7. II:315–335 — a source arrangement needing explicit critical collation

**Printed II:315 / original physical PDF 610** is an end-piece or colophon and mentions a date of **1319 AH**; it is not a normal continuation of the chapter on the faith of the `muqallid`. **II:316–317** are separate commendations and poetry with named contributors. **II:318–320** are the original printed contents. The contents list ends the prior work at the faith of the `muqallid`.

Nevertheless, **II:321** resumes with the actual source heading:

> فصل في الإمامة

**Translation:** “A chapter concerning the imamate.”

And the beginning includes the distinction:

> لا خفاء في أنها من فروع الدين ...

**Close English:** “There is no doubt that it belongs to the practical/derivative branches of religion ...”

**At II:327**, a second printed heading begins:

> فصل. الإمام الحق بعد رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم ...

**Translation:** “Chapter. The rightful imam after God's Messenger, may God bless him and grant him peace ...”

The subsequent text discusses Abū Bakr, ʿUmar, ʿUthmān and ʿAlī and reports arguments of distinct communities concerning their respective claims; these authorial and opposing viewpoints must be identified separately. **II:335** supplies a closing prayer and a further editorial note.

**Source-critical question:** why is this substantive imamate material printed after the colophon, endorsements and table of contents? The available original scan proves the ordering, but **not** whether it reflects an authorial supplement, printing/binding arrangement, a marginal appendix moved to the end, or a later editorial insertion. No specific hypothesis is established without another print/manuscript witness. A definitive edition should mark this section as *post-index*, preserve its printed numbering and compare another independent witness.

## Completion levels

The present work provides verified **selected** Arabic wording and close English translations, alongside the original book's navigational architecture. It does **not** transcribe the entire upper running prose or the lengthy marginal commentary of either volume. Positively verified separate Wasīm note units remain extremely sparse (the earlier short II:117 fragment is preserved), and it is impossible to claim a complete `Ḥāshiyat al-Muḥākamāt` percentage without an enumerated note corpus.

The independent al-Taftāzānī `Tahdhīb al-Kalām` translation project has not been altered.
