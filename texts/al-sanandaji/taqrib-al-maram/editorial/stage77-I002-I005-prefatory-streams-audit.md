# Sanandajī and Muḥammad Wasīm — parallel authorial introductions, I:2–5

**Stage 77 source-layer audit, 12 November 2026.** Būlāq 1318 AH, first numbered volume, pp. I:2–5 (combined-source PDF pp.15–18). **Do not misrepresent two parallel authorial prefaces as one commentary.** The upper printed stream is ʿAbd al-Qādir al-Sanandajī's introduction to the sharḥ. The lower stream is Muḥammad Wasīm's *positively self-identified* introduction to his notes, unlike many subsequent anonymous lower annotations. Source SHA256: `4addd660de68794f9d64e062e3b25a2782497b3342eb4580218c3e07e8d12134`.

The long Wasīm introduction is **already separately translated and structured** in the existing booksite module [`stage51-wasim-preface.js`](https://github.com/gnamma-research/booksite/blob/master/texts/al-sanandaji/taqrib-al-maram/stage51-wasim-preface.js). It must be **reused rather than silently rewritten**: its four sequential records carry original I:2–5 and explicit positive authorial evidence on I:3. It reports Wasīm's relationship to Sanandajī, the use of *Sharḥ al-Maqāṣid* in the commentary's development, his intention to explain difficulties, disruptions to the writing project, and its eventual completion. No claim that every later marginal paragraph is by Wasīm follows from this preface.

## I:2 — Separate openings on a single printed leaf

### I002.upper.01 — Sanandajī's invocation
**Arabic:** «بسم الله الرحمن الرحيم» · «الحمد من هدانا إلى طريق الإسلام» · «تهذيب الكلام وتقريب المرام».

**English:** In the name of God, the All-Merciful, the Compassionate. Praise to the One who guided us to the way of Islam; thanks to the One who granted us success in refining theological discourse and bringing its aims nearer. Blessings and peace upon Muḥammad, the master and messenger sent to mankind, and upon his family. The praise is cast in deliberately rhythmic prose; its invocation continues on the next leaf.

**Status:** Retained upper first-pass rendering, newly source-position checked; exact rhymed epithets not all separately translated.

### I002.wasim.01 — Distinct secondary invocation, linked to Wasīm I:3
**Arabic:** «بسم الله الرحمن الرحيم» · «الحمد لله الذي هدانا لدين الإسلام» · «وفقنا على تحرير المقاصد الدينية».

**English:** A **separate** invocation begins in the lower print: praise to God for guidance to Islam and assistance in elucidating religious purposes, with blessings upon Muḥammad and his family. The writer names himself in the following leaf as **Muḥammad al-Wasīm**. This is not the tail of Sanandajī's upper invocation.

**Status:** Established authorial-preface stream, *retained* from the prior English translation. Exact full preface in the linked Stage51 source, not reproduced as an independent new translation here.

## I:3 — Two named speakers

### I003.upper.01 — Sanandajī explains his undertaking
**Arabic:** «فيقول العبد الفقير إلى ألطاف ربه» · «عبد القادر» · «قسم التهذيب من الكلام» · «العلامة الثاني السعد التفتازاني».

**English:** Sanandajī identifies himself as ʿAbd al-Qādir, a servant dependent upon God's favour, and describes the kalām section of Taftāzānī's *Tahdhīb* as unusually concise, carefully ordered and rich in subtle points. Although pressed by circumstances, he was urged by a person whose request he could not disregard to explain this difficult text. He therefore undertook a commentary intended to clarify its expressions and recover its intended senses. The sentence continues on I:4.

**Status:** Inherited source-reading of the sharḥ author's preface; ornamental rhetoric not yet independently proofed word by word.

### I003.wasim.01 — Wasīm names himself and describes the commentary's origin
**Arabic:** «محمد الوسيم ابن المرحوم الشيخ محمد سعيد» · «ومنهم الأخ الأكبر الماهر الشيخ عبد القادر» · «يراجع شرح المقاصد».

**English:** In the lower stream, Muḥammad al-Wasīm explicitly names himself. He explains that students studying Taftāzānī with their father included his elder brother ʿAbd al-Qādir. During that study, ʿAbd al-Qādir consulted *Sharḥ al-Maqāṣid*, recording subtle explanations in brief marginal notes; he later made those into a continuous commentary. The shared family and teaching setting explains why the page must preserve **two different narrating voices**.

**Status:** Retained and positively attributed historical preface; exact previous Arabic–English draft available in the Stage51 module. A revised definitive English must be checked against the printed leaf, not merely that module.

## I:4 — Programme and historical circumstances

### I004.upper.01 — Sanandajī's purpose and self-assessment
**Arabic:** «معانيه وتوضيح عباراته» · «فجاء بحمد الله ذلك شرحاً» · «خالياً عن شوائب الخلل والنقصان» · «فانظر ثم ارجع البصر هل ترى من فطور».

**English:** The commentary aims to elucidate obscure expressions, clarify intellectual difficulties, and make subtle points accessible. The writer expresses the hope that his labour will be free of significant defects, yet rhetorically invites inspection: ‘Return your gaze: do you see any fissures?’ His praise also acknowledges the teaching of his father and the circumstances that allowed the work to come into being. This is an **authorial rhetorical appeal**, not a modern editorial claim of textual perfection.

**Status:** Existing main-preface translation retained, with the scriptural-sounding allusion preserved as such.

### I004.wasim.01 — Revisions, hardship and relocation
**Arabic:** «الشرح مرة ثانية» · «جرح وتعديل» · «أكتب على الشرح حواشي» · «تموجت أمواج المحن والفتن».

**English:** Wasīm recounts that his elder brother revised the commentary, changing or clarifying some passages. He had long hoped to compose notes on the commentary, to resolve difficult locations and questions. Repeated events delayed him, and disturbances grew until his elder brother left his homeland. Wasīm's account includes the brother's reception in Sulaymāniyya and Wasīm's own grief at separation. It is autobiographical source material; **these historical particulars are not part of Sanandajī's original kalām argument**.

**Status:** Positively identified Wasīm text, prior translation preserved separately. Exact literary lineation and poetry require final source proof.

## I:5 — The named sharḥ and the conclusion of the other preface

### I005.upper.01 — The title *Taqrīb al-Marām*
**Arabic:** «سميته تقريب المرام في شرح تهذيب الكلام» · «محمد الهاشمي القرشي».

**English:** Having composed the commentary, Sanandajī gives it the title *Taqrīb al-Marām fī Sharḥ Tahdhīb al-Kalām*—roughly ‘Bringing the Intended Aim Near: a Commentary on the Refinement of Theological Discourse.’ He closes with a prayer that it benefit its readers and a benediction upon Muḥammad the Hāshimī and Qurashī. The prayer, like the opening, is in rhymed prose.

**Status:** Inherited upper translation, not an entirely newly certified diplomatic rendering.

### I005.wasim.01 — Wasīm closes his introduction
**Arabic:** «سنة ألف ومائتين وثلاثة وسبعين» · «النبوة».

**English:** Wasīm's prefatory account concludes with its own reflection on the work's publication, the benefits and difficulties of the commentary, and a stated completion date of **1273 AH**. That date belongs to **his account of completing the prefatory/annotation work**, not automatically to the Būlāq edition or to every portion of the sharḥ. Its exact referent should be established from the entire closing sentence during independent checking.

**Status:** Retained source-attributed Wasīm text. Do not add 4 new 'complete translated pages' to this stage by repackaging an earlier translation.

## Editorial consequences

1. **Two streams on each of I:2–5:** principal Sanandajī preface above; Wasīm's named authorial preface below. Do not merge them for continuous reading.
2. Later anonymous lower ḥāshiya and separately ruled notes require positive personal attribution; neither typography nor position authenticates Wasīm.
3. Preserve the full Stage51 Wasīm Arabic–English translation and its source-page identities as prior work. Do not double count it as freshly translated or independently proven.
4. This Stage77 document provides a **source-layer and reading audit**, not a fresh exhaustive translation of all rhythmic words in these prefaces. The complete base-print proof gate for the four leaves remains open.
