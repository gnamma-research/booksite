# Taqrīb al-Marām / Ḥāshiyat al-Muḥākamāt — Stage 17
## Printed II:221–260, original physical scan images 516–555

**Source:** complete original scanned Arabic `taqribmaram(2).pdf`, 632 physical PDF images. This dossier follows the original book's sections on transmission, prophecy, miracles, prophetic impeccability and resurrection. **FORTY preliminary page-context locators, TWELVE verified short bilingual excerpts; NOT forty fully translated pages, nor a completed transcription of every marginal gloss.**

### Original book architecture, checked against printed text

- **II:221** ends the prior divine-names discussion then opens the authentic original heading `الباب السادس في السمعيات` — **Book Six: matters known by transmitted revelation**; it also opens `الفصل الأول في النبوة`.
- **II:228** opens the source heading `فصل في نبوة نبينا محمد صلى الله عليه وسلم` — the chapter on Muḥammad's prophethood, the evidential role of miracles and the Qurʾān.
- **II:234** opens `فصل من شرائط النبوة` — conditions of prophethood; the source includes the prophet's capacity and protection from what contradicts the miracle.
- **II:240** opens `فصل في المعاد` — resurrection, the return of embodied life and the problem of reconstituting identity.

### Critical translation / edition notes

**Sixth Book classification.** `Samʿiyyāt` is not a general term for epistemology, but covers matters whose details are received through transmitted revelation, including the prophet's claim, miracles, reward and resurrection. This does **not** imply that evidence for a prophet's truthfulness rests only on unexamined reports: the pages explicitly reason from claimant, signs and witnesses.

**The miracle and linguistic proof.** The discussion moves from a claimed prophethood to verification and the Qurʾānic challenge. A prophet's claim and the manifestation of a miracle are distinct premises, and the original text treats objections concerning the scope of the challenge. The many lower-register rejoinders still need continuous translation.

**Prophetic `ʿiṣma`.** The discussion at II:234 ff. must distinguish the level of protection necessary to establish truthful conveying from more expansive claims about freedom from all possible sins or errors. No view about every disputed case should be imposed without the full Arabic.

**Resurrection and identity.** At II:240 the definition of `maʿād` explicitly includes return after passing away and restoration of bodily parts after their separation. Later pages test bodily individuation, original constituent parts, reconstitution and how restoration differs from replacement. Generic resurrection paraphrases cannot substitute for exact transmitted objections.
