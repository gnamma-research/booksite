### E. Source-critical problems and theological distinctions

**201–203 — does *taʿlīl* impose need?** The printed sequence grants that certain legal acts are connected with ends, especially benefits accruing to servants and the purposes underlying legal analogy. It immediately asks whether *all* divine acts **must** have purposes and whether their purposes must benefit God. The first claim does not entail the latter. In particular, the selected wording “closer to identify the dispute with necessity or universality” is more precise than a blanket assertion that divine acts are entirely purposeless.

**204–208 — three different senses of guidance.** The source distinguishes directing to the way, creating or enabling the act by which someone follows it, and an agent's wrongful inducing of error. A statement that guidance is ascribed to God does not remove every possibility of calling a messenger a guide in the sense of indication. Conversely, `iḍlāl` is examined both as creating a state of being astray and as a different mode of attribution. The various objection/reply clauses still need complete transcription.

**209–210 — appointed time versus culpability.** The authorial doctrine that the person killed dies at his appointed `ajal` must not be conflated with a denial of the killer's wrongdoing. These are distinct predications: the death's occurrence at a divinely known time and the murderer as the culpable agent of an unjust act.

**211–215 — provision and the obligations of divine beneficence.** The original **second section within II:211** must not be lost: after the issue of whether illicit food constitutes `rizq`, the work pivots to the debate whether God is rationally bound to do what is most beneficial for servants, and subsequently toward reward, punishment and their duration. One source page (II:214) is almost entirely smaller-type argument; creating an upper Sanandajī paragraph there from a generic summary would be wrong.

**216–220 — name, named, and naming.** `ism` can designate a word; `musammā` what that word is intended to denote; `tasmiyah` the act of bestowing the name. The dispute over `al-ism ʿayn al-musammā` requires controlling which level is compared in each premise. Identical reference does not by itself make two utterances or two divine predicates synonymous. The longer marginal dispute remains untranslated and individually unattributed.
