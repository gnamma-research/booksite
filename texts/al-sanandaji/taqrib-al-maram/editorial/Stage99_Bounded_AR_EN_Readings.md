# Taqrīb al-Marām · Volume I · Stage 99 — bounded Arabic–English source readings

**17 November 2026.** Copytext: Būlāq 1318 AH, printed I:27–28, combined PDF physical pages 40–41. **Only the clauses quoted here have been source-checked in the scan.** Ellipses explicitly signal omitted or textually uncertain matter. This is not a complete translation of either page, nor a diplomatic collation or independently second-proofed critical edition.

## 1. `I027.upper.01` · printed I:27 · upper register

**Arabic (bounded image reading):**

> لوجوده (واجب)

**English, following the quoted Arabic:**

> “… concerning His existence, [inquiry] **is obligatory**.” This is only the printed closing predicate of the sentence whose beginning is already recorded on I:26; it is not a new independent argument. The parenthesized “obligatory” is a main-text lemma. […]

**Source scope:** short complete visible upper-line clause only; the opening of the sentence is in I026.upper.01 and is deliberately not copied

**Outstanding issue:** The prepositional phrase depends syntactically on the previous printed page; its grammatical linkage must be independently proofed. Do not count the two records as two unrelated claims.

## 2. `I027.lower.04` · printed I:27 · lower register

**Arabic (bounded image reading):**

> لكن التعليلين المذكورين لقول الأكثر بل أكثر التعليلات المذكورة لا تنطبق على هذا المراد ولا تقصده، بل إنما تنطبق على إرادة استلزام إيجاب الشيء [...] مقدمته، كما هو واضح. وإن أرادوا منها أن إيجاب ذلك الشيء يستلزم ويقتضي وجوب مقدمته كما هو الظاهر من أكثر [...].

**English, following the quoted Arabic:**

> But the two justifications offered for the majority position—indeed, most of those mentioned—do not apply to that intended meaning and do not aim at it. Instead, they apply to understanding the imposing of an obligation on an act as entailing […] its prerequisite, as is evident. If the famous premise is intended to mean that obligating the act entails and requires its prerequisite to be obligatory, as appears from most […].

**Source scope:** two directly imaged portions of the closing lower paragraph of I:27; intervening and continuation words omitted

**Outstanding issue:** The small grammatical link immediately before “its prerequisite” in the first sentence and the continued proof on I:28 are not established. The surrounding treatment of a legally instituted condition has already been entered in I027.lower.03 and is not reproduced.

## 3. `I028.lower.02` · printed I:28 · lower register

**Arabic (bounded image reading):**

> فالذي يظهر هو أن إيجاب الشيء ليس ناطقاً بوجوب مقدمته. وما يوهم من أنه لو لم يكن ناطقاً به يلزم التكليف بالمحال ضرورة استحالة الشيء بدون ما يتوقف عليه، مدفوع بأن المحال هو التكليف بوجوب الشيء بدون وجود المقدمة، وليس بلازم. إنما اللازم هو التكليف بوجوبه بدون التصريح بوجوبها، وليس بمحال، لأن السكوت عن وجوب مقدمة الشيء عند إيجابه لا ينافي وجوبها كما هو واضح. […]

**English, following the quoted Arabic:**

> What emerges is that making something obligatory does not itself expressly state the obligation of its prerequisite. The suggestion that, unless it did explicitly state this, it would amount to imposing the impossible—since an act cannot occur without what it depends on—is answered as follows: what would be impossible is requiring the act to be performed **without the prerequisite existing**, but that does not follow. What does follow is requiring the act **without expressly declaring its prerequisite obligatory**; this is not impossible, for silence about the obligation of a prerequisite when commanding an act does not negate the prerequisite’s obligation, as is evident. […]

**Source scope:** continuous source-facing excerpt from the middle of the I:28 lower gloss, following the earlier I028.lower.01 objection

**Outstanding issue:** This does not translate the beginning of I:28 (already represented in I028.lower.01) or all subsequent discussion of necessary means. The phrase “without the prerequisite existing” must not be collapsed into “without a separately explicit order for it.”

## 4. `I028.lower.03` · printed I:28 · lower register

**Arabic (bounded image reading):**

> (قوله أمر مقدوراً إلخ) بناء على أن المقدور أعم من أن يكون فعلاً تتعلق به القدرة أو غير فعل لكن تتعلق القدرة بإيجاده وأسبابه، كما هنا على بعض تقاديره. وإليه أشار بالتعبير بالأمر دون الفعل. […] (قوله المعرفة الواجبة إلخ) استشكل [...] وأجيب بأن إمكانه ضروري، والغافل من لم يبلغه الخطاب أو بلغه ولم يفهمه، لا من بلغه وفهمه لكنه لم يكن عارفاً بما كُلِّف بمعرفته، وهو متعلق الخطاب.

**English, following the quoted Arabic:**

> On “a thing within one’s power”: the scope of what is within power is wider than an act to which power directly relates; it can also be something other than an act, where power nevertheless relates to bringing it about and to its causes, as in certain possibilities considered here. This is indicated by saying “thing” rather than “act.” […] On “obligatory knowledge”: an objection is raised […]. The reply is that its possibility is evident. The heedless person here is one to whom the address has not reached, or to whom it reached but who did not understand it—not someone who received and understood the address but had not yet known what he was charged to know: that is the object of the address.

**Source scope:** two distinct bounded glosses at the bottom of I:28; the objection between them has been left out pending independent reading

**Outstanding issue:** The lower note gives an argument about a recipient of a command and its object; do not replace it with the previous interpretive summary of the general duty of inquiry. The precise wording of the intervening objection is not entered.
