## I:41 · The cause of the Necessary's existence and the possible's quiddity

### I041.upper.01 — Main text, beginning in I:40
**Arabic anchors:** «الواجب في وجوده محتاجاً إلى غيره فلا يكون الواجب واجباً»؛ «أن تكون نفس الماهية والعلة متقدمة بالوجود على المعلول»؛ «فيلزم تقدم الماهية بالوجود على الوجود وذلك محال»؛ «وأجيب بأن العلة متقدمة ...».

**English reading:** [Were the Necessary to depend for its existence on something other than itself,] the Necessary would be dependent on another and would not be necessary [in itself]. Another alternative is that the very quiddity acts as the cause. But a cause precedes its effect with respect to existence, so this would entail that the quiddity exists prior to its own existence, an impossibility. The reply distinguishes the sort of priority required here. It is the *efficient* cause that must precede the effect in existence, not every condition or receptacle in which existence may be said to obtain. [The text turns to how the possible's quiddity is conceived as a subject for existence; the last clause runs onto I:42.]

### I041.ruled.01 — Separate short note
**Arabic anchor:** «(قوله هو العلة الفاعلة) دون القابلة بناء على أن قيامه بالماهية في الممكن إنما هو ذهناً لا خارجاً كما يأتي اه منه».

**English:** On “It is the efficient cause”: [this is] in contrast to a receptive cause, based on the position that existence subsists in a possible quiddity only **mentally**, not as an additional, separately inhering item outside the mind, as will be explained. “From him” is a printed attributional formula but does not by itself give a personal name.

### I041.lower.01 — The distinction between a possible thing and its existence
**Arabic anchors:** «متغايرين بالمفهوم والاعتبار لكنهما في الخارج موجود له هوية واحدة»؛ «الإمكان الذاتي للوجوب»؛ «غير محتاج إلى غير ذاته»؛ «اتصاف الذات بالوجود».

**English reading:** A quiddity and its existence can differ in concept and mode of consideration while having a **single identity externally**. There is then no further item outside the mind by whose addition the thing is first made an existent. The gloss warns that an argument from possible existence to necessary existence must not assume the very external duality that is under dispute. If possibility attaches to a thing considered through itself, this does not mean a real quiddity is already present outside the mind, waiting for an extramental accident called existence. Likewise, necessary existence through itself must not be described as though a separately actualized existence were attached to a preceding subject. The discussion concerns how our concepts are applied to what is necessary and what is possible, and whether the proposed distinction is merely intellectual or requires a second actual thing.

### I041.lower.02 — A question about the priority required of causes
**Arabic anchors:** «الممكن الذاتي»؛ «الواجب ما يحتاج في ثبوت الوجود له إلى غير ذاته» [read against surrounding lines: **the exact negation/wording must be checked**]؛ «(قوله تقدم ماهية الممكن الخ)»؛ «القابلة للوجود».

**English reading:** The gloss further contrasts the possible thing, whose existence is dependent on something else, with that which exists by itself. It distinguishes the priority of an efficient cause from the status of a thing capable of receiving existence: the receptacle cannot simply be treated as an independently existent cause of that same existence. The margin's compressed formulation of the relation between what is necessary through itself and what is dependent on an efficient cause still needs a word-for-word second proof; the distinction is recorded without supplying a fictitious variant.

## I:42 · Existence as an intellectual consideration and the Necessary's special reality

### I042.upper.01 — The argument and its answer
**Arabic anchors:** «تقدير إمكانه يحتاج إلى فاعل فيلزم المحال»؛ «لكن الوجود عند القائلين بزيادته أمر اعتباري لا يحتاج إلى موجود فلا إشكال»؛ «قائم بنفسه متميز من غيره مخالف لوجود الممكن في حقيقته»؛ «مع تفرده ... بالقيام بالماهية».

**English reading:** If the added existence itself had to be a possible existing entity, it would require an agent and the difficulty would recur. But those who say existence is additional [to quiddity] take that addition, in the relevant sense, to be **an intellectual consideration**, not an independently existing entity requiring its own further existence. So the objection does not arise in that form. The Necessary's existence is described as subsisting through itself, distinct from what is other than it and different in reality from the existence of the possible. This is so despite the existence of the possible being conceived as standing in a quiddity. The relevant distinction is between the unique self-subsistence of the Necessary and the possible's mode of dependence, not simply between two things represented by the same universal concept.

### I042.upper.02 — What abstraction cannot mean
**Arabic anchors:** «مع تفرده»؛ «بالقيام بالماهية»؛ «لأن مقتضى الذات الذي لا يتخلف عنه»؛ «فيكون وجود الممكن مجرداً أيضاً وهو باطل»؛ «فلا يرد أن تجرد الوجود عن الماهية ...».

**English reading:** One must not say that existence, considered through its own nature, is necessarily abstracted from every quiddity; whatever follows essentially from a nature would then accompany it in every case. The possible's existence too would have to be independent of quiddity, contrary to the very contrast being drawn. The possible quiddity, as possible in itself, admits nonexistence; its possibility is not abolished merely by abstractly conceiving its existence. [The upper discourse continues into I:43.]

### I042.lower.01 — Why an efficient cause must be conceived as existent
**Arabic anchors:** «ما لم يلاحظ كون الشيء موجوداً امتنع أن يلاحظ مبدأ الوجود ومفيداً له»؛ «بخلاف القابل للوجود»؛ «وإلا لزم حصول الحاصل»؛ «ولا العدم وإلا لزم اجتماع النقيضين».

**English reading:** Before the intellect can consider a thing as the **source or giver of existence**, it must consider that thing as existent. A thing understood merely as receptive of existence is different: it must not be presumed already to have the very existence that it is supposed to receive, for that would amount to bringing about what is already realized. Nor can one simply put it under nonexistence while ascribing to it the standing of an actually existent subject; that risks combining contradictories. The gloss is concerned with the exact stage and sense in which something is called a “subject,” “cause,” or “receptacle,” not the crude claim that a nonexistent thing performs a causal act.

### I042.lower.02 — The distinction between an *iʿtibārī* predicate and an external constituent
**Arabic anchors:** «الماهية على التقدير المذكور تصير علة اعتبارية لا خارجية»؛ «القائلين بزيادته»؛ «أقول فتقرر في المباحث السابقة»؛ «الاتحاد في المفهوم»؛ «مغايرة له من حيث المفهوم».

**English reading:** On this account, the priority assigned to quiddity for the purpose of predicating existence is a priority **within consideration**, not the priority of an independently present extramental efficient cause. Thus the common assertion that existence is “additional to quiddity” must be situated within a precise theory of what the addition means. It need not assert two separate things outside the mind. The marginal critic presses the question of how this can be reconciled with the philosophers' account of the unique Necessary, and with the many possible things whose existence is spoken of in a shared manner. When the Necessary's existence is called identical with its quiddity, this is not simply saying that the *concept* of existence and the *concept* of quiddity are indistinguishable; the argument concerns the reality indicated by each.

## I:43 · Common existence versus the unique Necessary

### I043.upper.01 — The special existence is not the universal concept
**Arabic anchors:** «فلا يكون واجباً ولا راداً بأن الوجود معلوم بالضرورة وحقيقة الواجب غير معلومة اتفاقاً»؛ «البحث ليس في الوجود المشترك بل في الوجود الخاص المخالف لسائر الوجودات في الحقيقة»؛ «والمجرد هو الثاني والمعلوم بالضرورة هو الأول».

**English reading:** [If the possible's existence were separated from quiddity in the same manner,] it would not thereby become necessary, and the objection that “existence is necessarily known, whereas the reality of the Necessary is agreed to be unknown” does not reach the intended point. The discussion is **not about existence as a concept shared by many things**. It concerns the *special existence* differing in reality from all other existences. The existence described as independent or separated [from quiddity] is the latter; the existence grasped necessarily is the former, common notion. They must not be interchanged in the objection.

### I043.upper.02 — What “inhering in quiddity” signifies
**Arabic anchors:** «ذهناً لا عيناً»؛ «إذ لا يتقرر كل متحقق على حدة في الخارج»؛ «كبياض الجسم»؛ «فإنه قائم في الخارج»؛ «فلا يلزم أن تقوم الماهية ...».

**English reading:** The possible's existence is said to subsist in quiddity **in the mind, not as two separately actualized things outside it**. One must not picture the quiddity as first possessing an independent extramental existence, and then receiving the accident of existence, like the colour white borne by a body. The illustrative analogy with whiteness is thus qualified. It is the mind that distinguishes what it conceives as the thing and as its being; outside the mind there is no need to posit a second, already existent quiddity that serves as a host for an equally independent existence. “The existence of everything is additional to it ...” passes into the next leaf.

### I043.ruled.01 — Separate short objection
**Arabic anchor:** «(قوله إذ لا يتقرر الخ) بناء على أنه ليس الهوية المتحدة مع الماهية في الخارج لأنه أمر اعتباري لا تحقق له في الخارج كما هو عند المتكلمين فافهم اه منه».

**English:** On “Since it is not separately established, etc.”: this proceeds on the assumption that the alleged additional existence is not itself the one external identity united with the quiddity, for it is an intellectual consideration with no separate external realization, as on the theologians' account. Understand. [The note ends with a formula of derivation, not a verified personal signature.]

### I043.lower.01 — Does denying extramental addition deny the quiddity itself?
**Arabic anchors:** «ضرورة توقف وجوده على تجرده»؛ «التجرد ... عن المقارنات»؛ «قائم به في الخارج»؛ «أن ما هو في الخارج زيادة عليه خارجاً معنى تنافيهما»؛ «المعروض له ليس في الخارج شيء واحد».

**English reading:** The marginal commentator tests the assertion that a quiddity “subsists” and that existence stands in it. A requirement that the thing be separated or individuated does not by itself prove a **second independent being** alongside the individuated thing. If the reality is one outside the mind, the two analyses—“quiddity” and “existence”—may designate it under different considerations without being two substances or an accident and a substance. The critic asks whether the proposed explanation surreptitiously makes one of those conceptual factors actual before the other. Such an account would fail to explain how a single existent has the identity it does.

### I043.lower.02 — The whiteness-and-body analogy
**Arabic anchors:** «فإن كان المعروض له ليس في الخارج شيء واحد يطلق عليه البياض والجسم»؛ «بل شيئان كل منهما متحقق على حدة»؛ «لكن لا تسلم إن الماهية مع الوجود بعد عروضها لها ...»؛ «فلا يلزم ما ذكر».

**English reading:** When a body is white, whiteness and body are not merely two interchangeable names for one individual in the way under dispute: they are distinct grounds of predication, each considered as having its own mode of realization. But one cannot simply transfer this relation to quiddity and existence. The gloss insists on distinguishing the concept of what the thing is from the concept of its being, without inferring that its existence must be another extramental item attached to an already actual quiddity. The analogy therefore needs a careful qualification. [The long final clause continues on I:44.]

## I:44 · Common existence, individuation and predication by gradation

### I044.upper.01 — The end of the rebuttal
**Arabic anchors:** «وأيضاً الماهية المعروضة إما معدومة فمتناقض أو موجودة فدور أو تسلسل»؛ «ليس كقيام البياض بالجسم»؛ «وجود الواجب له ...»؛ «والوجود المشترك ...»؛ «بالتشكيك».

**English reading:** If the quiddity supposedly receiving existence were **nonexistent**, the position would be contradictory; if **already existent**, the argument would move in a circle or require an endless regress. Hence the addition of existence is not to be understood exactly as whiteness inhering in a body. The Necessary has its special existence, while the common notion of existence can be predicated across things that are not identical in their modes of realization. It is in this setting that the text speaks of the common predicate and of gradational predication (*tashkīk*). The marginal discussion asks what kind of unity this common predicate has, rather than presupposing a single external genus of existents.

### I044.upper.02 — The distinction between the shared predicate and its realization
**Arabic anchors:** «هو الوجود المشترك المقول على الوجودات الخاصة (بالتشكيك)»؛ «المحلول في الجوهر أو ...»؛ «كالبياض ... كالحركة»؛ «فالوجود عارض الماهية ... بالتشكيك».

**English reading:** The existence predicated of particular existences is a **common meaning**, predicated with gradation. If it is spoken of as something “inhering,” the intended inherence varies with the category under consideration; comparison with whiteness and with motion should not be confused with a proof that every particular existence is itself a separately existing recipient of an accident called *being*. The analogy is a way of explaining shared predication despite different instances, and the precise force of “gradation” remains part of the ensuing controversy.

### I044.lower.01 — The priority of quiddity and the Necessary's existence
**Arabic anchors:** «الفرق الظاهر بين الصورتين هو أن قيام البياض بالجسم مسبوق بوجود الجسم بخلاف قيام الوجود بالماهية»؛ «الوجود الواجبي عين الماهية»؛ «معنى كون وجود الواجب نفسه»؛ «غير محتاج إلى وجود آخر».

**English reading:** The obvious difference between the two cases is that whiteness comes to stand in a body whose existence is already presupposed. The supposed standing of existence in a quiddity cannot be treated in the same manner. For the Necessary, saying that existence is its very reality is intended to deny that its being depends on another existence. If every actual existence had to be supplied by some further existence, no existence would ever be explained. It is therefore no objection merely to say that we distinguish the notion of the Necessary's essence from our notion of existence; the point is whether the being of that essence requires some second actual thing.

### I044.lower.02 — A critical test of the explanation
**Arabic anchors:** «إنه غير محتاج إلى وجود آخر يقوم به»؛ «لا معنى للواجب سوى ما يكون تحققه بنفسه»؛ «كان الممكن ... لم يفتقر إلى وجود آخر»؛ «المصنف في شرح المقاصد»؛ «هذا المذهب إلى الضلال البعيد».

**English reading:** A thing whose existence is through itself does not require a further being to make it actual; that is how the claim about the Necessary is explained. But the marginal writer then presses a difficulty: if “being by itself” merely means not needing a separately added existence in the same way as a possible thing does not require a new extramental item called its existence, the proposed account risks losing the difference between what is necessary and what is possible. The possible still depends on a cause; the Necessary does not. The critic invokes the author's discussion in *Sharḥ al-Maqāṣid* and contests the attribution of a particular metaphysical thesis to it. The concluding polemical expression about a doctrinal view being “far astray” is **reported as the marginal writer's claim**, not endorsed here. [Some compressed words before the final formula require checking against a clearer image or second print.]

## I:45 · Particular existence, *kawn* and whether being is an accident

### I045.upper.01 — The nature of gradational predication
**Arabic anchors:** «فهو له المقول على الوجودات ... بمنزلة العلة»؛ «ليكون الكون عارضاً»؛ «ولا يلزم من ذلك تكثر الوجودات الخاصة»؛ «لأن زيادة العارض لا تستلزم زيادة المعروض».

**English reading:** The predicate common to the particular existences is spoken of according to a gradational or derivative predication. On the account in question, *kawn*—the fact of being—is treated as an accident. It does not follow from its being predicated of many existents that the individual existences themselves multiply in exactly the same way, for multiplicity of the predicate or accident does not entail multiplicity of what bears it. The particular existences are each distinguished in their realities; this is the problem that the common concept has to account for.

### I045.upper.02 — The accident–substrate difficulty
**Arabic anchors:** «فإن كون واحد من الوجودات الخاصة قائماً بنفسه»؛ «لكن لا يخفى أن الوجود الخاص لكل شيء»؛ «فلا يرد أنه لو كان ... فلا يصح جعل الكون بمعنى الثبوت عليهم»؛ «كالنور على الأنوار».

**English reading:** If the particular existence of a thing means nothing over and above its **actual establishment**, then saying that being inheres in it risks making the predicate and its subject the same thing under two descriptions. That difference of consideration is insufficient for a real accident standing in a real substrate. If particular existence is a reality besides actual establishment, however, the proposal that *kawn* means establishment when predicated of it becomes obscure. The analogy, “as light is predicated of lights,” cannot simply settle the question; the comparison is continued on I:46.

### I045.ruled.01 — What the upper text means by “establishment”
**Arabic anchors:** «(قوله بمعنى الثبوت) مع أن الظاهر أن جعله عليه لا يكون إلا بهذا»؛ «لفظ الوجود مشتركاً لفظياً»؛ «جعل الكون على الوجودات».

**English reading:** On “in the sense of establishment”: the marginal writer notes that the interpretation appears unavoidable if the term is to retain a meaning across particular instances. Otherwise “existence” might become a merely equivocal word, with no conceptual unity. But interpreting it this way makes the relation of predicate and subject problematic. A verbal or conceptual distinction alone does not amount to one real item supervening on another.

### I045.lower.01 — The nominal character of the common predicate
**Arabic anchors:** «(قوله جاز كون واحد من الوجودات الخاصة الخ)»؛ «ما ثبت من اشتراك الكل في مفهوم الوجود لا يقتضي تصادق أفراده»؛ «كأفراد الماني من أنواع الحيوانات»؛ «الوجود المطلق صادق على الوجودات الخاصة المختلفة الحقائق».

**English reading:** The gloss objects that sharing in the **concept of existence** does not require distinct actual existences to coincide or be identical. The common term “animal” can be said of many living kinds without those species becoming one external thing; so “existence,” too, may be said of existences different in their particular realities. But one must distinguish a concept shared by things from a single actual entity shared among them. In such cases, what is common is the mode of predication, not an additional piece of reality lying between the individuals.

### I045.lower.02 — Existence, the subject, and the universal
**Arabic anchors:** «الحقيقة الواحدة صادقة على الوجودات الخاصة المختلفة الحقائق»؛ «التواطؤ»؛ «التشكيك»؛ «الموجود»؛ «الوجود لا الوجودات»؛ «كأفراد المفهوم».

**English reading:** There remains a difficulty if the particular existences are treated as radically different realities and the shared predicate “existence” as a real genus. What is supposed to be common is not literally the same as what individuates each thing. The note asks whether predication here is univocal (*tawāṭuʾ*), gradational (*tashkīk*), or only common in name. The distinction between the concept of “what exists” and the existence of the individual is important: the former is a mental universal, while the latter is the actuality of that individual. The writer then considers the opponent's suggestion that establishing the particular existences requires an additional “being” and rejects inferring a separate accident simply from this language. The argument continues on I:46.

## I:46 · Lights and existence as quiddity

### I046.upper.01 — The light analogy
**Arabic anchors:** «أنوار الشمس ونور القمر ونور السراج مختلفة بالحقيقة مشتركة في عارض النور المطلق»؛ «فما مثاله»؛ «في الكل»؛ «عن الواجب والممكن».

**English reading:** The light of the sun, the light of the moon and the light of a lamp differ in reality, yet [it is asserted that] they share the accident or common predicate of “light” without qualification. This supplies the proposed analogy for predicating existence of diverse instances. “In all” means: in the Necessary as well as in the possible. The force of the comparison depends on whether that shared light is an independent accident or a common intelligible description; the printed discussion does not permit those alternatives to be silently identified.

### I046.upper.02 — The Ashʿarī attribution
**Arabic anchors:** «(نفس الماهية)»؛ «كما نسب إلى الشيخ الأشعري رحمه الله»؛ «وكأنه لهذا قال المعدوم ليس شيئاً».

**English reading:** Another view makes existence **the very quiddity itself**, a position here attributed to Shaykh al-Ashʿarī—may God have mercy on him. The text suggests that this is why he said that “the nonexistent is not a thing.” The claim is reported as an attribution in the source, not assumed to settle every dispute about what the Ashʿarī school itself teaches.

### I046.ruled.01 — Distinguishing external reality and an extracted concept
**Arabic anchors:** «تلك الوجودات الخاصة موجودة بحقيقة لأن لازم ...»؛ «الوجود حقيقة فيها هو أثر الفاعل في الممكنات وعين الذات في الواجب تعالى»؛ «الكون العارض إنما هو بطريق التجوز»؛ «أمر اعتباري انتزعه العقل».

**English reading:** Those particular existences are actual in virtue of their individual reality: in possible things, the reality of existence is the effect of an efficient cause; in the Necessary, it is identical with the essence. To call an intellectually extracted predicate—*kawn* or “being”—an additional existing accident is, on this account, to speak figuratively. The intellect has drawn out a feature from what is genuinely existent. The writer emphasizes that reality as the **principle of effects** must be distinguished from an abstracted consideration to which a similar name is applied. [The small final connective phrase in the ruled note requires a second literal reading.]

### I046.lower.01 — How “existence” is used
**Arabic anchors:** «وجود الوجود لفظاً»؛ «حمل الخلاف في الوجود»؛ «المعنى النسبي»؛ «الماهية»؛ «مبدأ الآثار»؛ «المشتق ... مشتق معنوي أو لفظي».

**English reading:** The marginal writer examines the possibility that the disagreement about the addition of existence is in part **verbal**. Those who assert an addition may be speaking of the relational or mental meaning of existence, whereas those who say existence and quiddity are identical mean the concrete reality which grounds effects. The note then asks whether the common predicate is a genuine conceptual derivative, or only a linguistic extension; how can it be predicated of existences which are supposed to differ in reality? The answer cannot be obtained simply by saying that the word “existence” occurs in both accounts. The sense of the predicate must be specified.

### I046.lower.02 — What follows from the attribution to al-Ashʿarī
**Arabic anchors:** «(قوله فيعارض النور المطلق)»؛ «(قوله كأنه لهذا قال المعدوم الخ)»؛ «المصنف في شرح المقاصد»؛ «العدم ممتنع على القول بزيادة الوجود».

**English reading:** On the analogy with unrestricted light, the critic wonders whether the general predicate is superadded to individual lights in external reality, or belongs to them as something the intellect understands. On the suggestion that al-Ashʿarī denied the thinghood of the nonexistent: this may follow if existence is the very reality of the thing; but it does not follow simply from a verbal definition. The note appeals to the author's discussion elsewhere in *Sharḥ al-Maqāṣid*, and points out that some interlocutors affirm the **possibility of nonexistent quiddities being things** precisely because they regard existence as additional. The detailed inference continues on I:47.

## I:47 · Is the nonexistent a thing?

### I047.upper.01 — The contrast of the positions
**Arabic anchors:** «فإن الوجود إذا كان نفس الماهية فإذا ارتفع ارتفعت»؛ «فكان المعدوم نفياً صرفاً»؛ «بخلاف ما إذا قيل بأنه زائد عليها كالمعتزلة»؛ «فإن ارتفاعه لا يلزم ارتفاعها»؛ «فإن ارتفاع العارض لا يستلزم ارتفاع المعروض».

**English reading:** For, if existence is the very quiddity, then when existence is removed, the quiddity itself is removed. The nonexistent is then sheer negation. That is unlike the view of those who say existence is additional to quiddity, such as the Muʿtazila: the removal of existence does not necessarily remove quiddity, just as the removal of an accident does not necessarily remove that in which it inheres. This is the reason that some of them affirm that the nonexistent is a thing, or possesses establishment [of a qualified sort]. The text is reporting the connection between the two theses, not assuming that everyone who calls existence additional must accept extramentally existent nonexistent things.

### I047.upper.02 — Not numerical identity of the concepts
**Arabic anchors:** «(في المعنى) أي المفهوم من الوجود هو المفهوم من الشيء»؛ «(يعني أنه لا ينفرد كل) من الوجود والماهية»؛ «(بتحقق على حدة في الخارج)»؛ «(وأما هو) أي ليس الشيء هوية ولعارضه المسمى بالوجود هوية أخرى».

**English reading:** “In meaning” could be taken to imply that what is understood by existence is the same as what is understood by the thing; but that is not what is intended. Rather, existence and quiddity do not **each possess a distinct realization outside the mind**. There is not a quiddity with one extramental identity and an additional accident called existence with another extramental identity. Nor does the rejection of two identities make the notions synonymous; it is precisely the difference between conceptual distinction and external separation that matters here.

### I047.lower.01 — The critic on existence as an intellectual meaning
**Arabic anchors:** «(قوله كأنهم قالوا ... الخ)»؛ «الوجود الصدري الاعتباري»؛ «الماهية»؛ «ليس في الخارج تحقق واحد»؛ «الوجود الاستحقاق» [last printed phrase uncertain].

**English reading:** The gloss says that some of the reported positions about the nonexistent may have arisen from a failure to distinguish **considered existence** from independently actual existence. One may grant a quiddity mental establishment in order to think about it without thereby positing its actuality in the external world. But the objection remains: if existence is regarded as an added feature, exactly where does the alleged addition take place? If there is no separately actualized existence, one cannot explain the doctrine by making the non-existent quiddity into a real extramental subject already prepared to receive an accident. [An expression near the first lines of the gloss is unclear; the argument is retained provisionally without supplying a conjectural reading.]

### I047.lower.02 — Restriction of “one realization”
**Arabic anchors:** «(قوله فإن نفاه نفسه)»؛ «تحقق هو الموجود لا هو والوجود بتحققين»؛ «أم حاصلها أن المقصود كون وجود الشيء هو أنه إذا عرض الوجود على الماهية»؛ «لم يحصل هناك موجود واحد بوجود واحد»؛ «الاتصاف بالوجود».

**English reading:** Saying that the thing and its existence do not have two distinct external realizations does not mean that the **concept of existence** is the **concept of quiddity**. Nor should it be construed as the claim that an independently existing accident and its independent substrate have fused into one. The objector illustrates the problem: if one says that existence comes to a quiddity, and a single existent then results, one has already smuggled in the idea of the pre-existing quiddity and of something additional. If existence itself is only the intellectual relation used in judgement, it cannot do the causal work that a real principle of effects would have to do. The margin follows this question across to I:48.

## I:48 · External, mental, verbal and written existence

### I048.upper.01 — The sense of existence relevant to effects
**Arabic anchors:** «أقول أنا لم أرد بالوجود المعنى النسبي الذي لا تحقق له في الخارج وإنما أريد به ما هو مبدأ الآثار وهو الهوية»؛ «ولا شك أنه بهذا المعنى نفس الشيء بلا تحقق» [final phrase imperfectly legible].

**English reading:** I say: I did not intend by “existence” the **relative meaning** which has no independent external realization. I intended that which is the *principle of effects*: the actual reality or identity of the thing. There is no doubt that existence in this sense is the thing itself. This is different from the abstract relational predicate which can be asserted of it; the author is identifying actuality in the relevant sense with the very existent, not with a second accident alongside it.

### I048.upper.02 — External and mental existence
**Arabic anchors:** «(ثم الوجود منقسم إلى العيني)»؛ «وهو الوجود المتأصل المقصود عليه الذي به تتحقق ذات الشيء ... في الخارج»؛ «(وإلى الذهني)»؛ «وجود غير متأصل بمنزلة الظل للجسم»؛ «والصورة المطابقة للشيء عيني إنها لو تحققت في الخارج لكانت ذلك الشيء».

**English reading:** Existence is then divided into **external** and **mental**. External existence is primary or original existence, by which a thing's essence obtains in the external world—indeed, it is the external realization of that essence. Mental existence is non-primary, resembling the relation of a body's **shadow** to the body. What is present in the mind is a form corresponding to the external thing: were the form itself actualized outside the mind, it would be that thing. Likewise, if a body's shadow were to become a body [in the proposed analogy], it would be that body's counterpart. This analogy does not equate the mental form with a separately existent person or object.

### I048.upper.03 — Word, writing and the kind of reality intended
**Arabic anchors:** «(حقيقة)»؛ «(و) منقسم (إلى اللفظي والخطي مجازاً)»؛ «(إذ ليس الموجود في اللفظ والخط من الإنسان)»؛ «(الشخص)»؛ «(ولا الماهية)»؛ «(في الخارج)»؛ «(في الذهن)»؛ «(بل)».

**English reading:** Mental existence can be called genuine with respect to the thing's quiddity and its reality as apprehended. “Verbal” and “written” existence, by contrast, are **figurative** modes of speaking about the existence of the thing: what exists in the spoken or inscribed word “human” is neither an external human individual nor the quiddity present as an intelligible form in the mind. What exists in speech is an utterance; what exists in writing is an inscription. These signify the thing but are not themselves that thing. The last printed “rather ...” continues at I:49, where the relation of the sign to the signified is clarified.

### I048.lower.01 — The intention behind “I say ...”
**Arabic anchors:** «(قوله أقول الخ) حاصله أن ما ذكر مبني على أن المراد من الوجود هو الأمر الاعتباري»؛ «أما لو أريد به ما به التحقق فلا حاجة في القول بعينية الوجود للماهية»؛ «دور الماهية»؛ «القول بأنه اعتباري».

**English reading:** The marginal writer says the previous difficulty arises if “existence” is taken as a **considered or relational item**. But if the intended meaning is what makes something genuinely actual, then the statement that existence is identical with the thing's quiddity requires a different treatment. It is not enough to repeat that “existence is additional,” since the sense of the addition is precisely at issue. Nor does the rejection of an additional independently existing feature establish that the two ideas—existence and whatness—mean exactly the same thing.

### I048.lower.02 — Two senses of predicating existence of a quiddity
**Arabic anchors:** «(قوله ولأنت الخ)»؛ «(قوله بالإضافة إلى ذات الشيء وحقيقته)»؛ «إذا قيل للإنسان مثلا وجود في الخارج ووجود في الذهن»؛ «الوجودين»؛ «البياض والجسم»؛ «الإنسان وحقيقته».

**English reading:** The gloss asks how to interpret the statement that the same quiddity has both **external existence** and **mental existence**. One must not imagine that there are necessarily two separately real entities called “the human being” merely because the human being can be conceived in the mind and realized outside it. Nor does the analogy with whiteness and body establish that mental existence is a further accident borne by a pre-existing thing in precisely the same way as an extramental colour is borne by a body. Rather, both the identity and the difference of the two modes must be explained: external actuality belongs to the thing in the world, while a mental form realizes the content for the knower. This resolves part of the equivocation without turning the individual quiddity itself into a universal that lives outside the mind.

### I048.lower.03 — The literal gloss continues into I:49
**Arabic anchors:** «(قوله بل الموجود في اللفظ الخ)»؛ «الوجود في اللفظ»؛ «الوجود في الخط»؛ «حقيقة الإنسان».

**English reading:** When the text moves to “what exists in the word,” the commentator warns that the expression is not univocal. Spoken and written signs exist externally as sounds and traces, but the human quiddity designated by them does not thereby exist **as that human** within the sound or inscription.
