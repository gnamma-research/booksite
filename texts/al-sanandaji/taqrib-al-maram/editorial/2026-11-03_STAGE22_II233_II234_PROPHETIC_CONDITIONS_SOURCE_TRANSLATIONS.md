### II. Exact base witness: the conditions of prophecy (Cairo 1912, printed p.100)

**Selected continuous passage in the 1912 printed Tahdhīb:**

> من شرائط النبوة الذكورة وكمال العقل وقوة الرأي والسلامة عما تنفر عنه الطباع السليمة أو يخل بالمروءة أو حكمة البعثة. ثم المختار أن الأنبياء معصومون عما ينافي ...

**Translation of the quoted source segment:** “Among the conditions of prophethood are being male, possessing complete intellect, strength of judgement, and being free of characteristics that sound human dispositions recoil from or that compromise personal dignity or the wisdom of sending prophets. The position subsequently adopted is that the prophets are protected from whatever contradicts ... [the source continues].”

The beginning of this 1912 print is a distinct, independently inspected witness, not simply a copy of the existing bilingual working *Tahdhīb* project. Its main lemmata correspond to Sanandajī's **parenthesized** original words, while Sanandajī inserts explanatory terms such as **الفطنة** (intelligence), **ولو في الصبي كعيسى عليه السلام** (even with regard to a child, as with Jesus), and examples of repellent ailments. These explanatory additions must not be silently included as words of al-Taftāzānī himself.

### III. Philosophical and theological issues requiring full subsequent source review

**Capacity versus biological maturity.** The text's qualification about a prophet's intellect *even in a child* matters: this is not a straightforward deduction from ordinary chronological maturity to prophecy. It introduces the difference between empirical development and the perfection of relevant faculties in an extraordinary case.

**Repellent qualities versus moral and epistemic probity.** The clause on traits that repel sound dispositions is tied to the **wisdom or purpose of a prophetic mission**, not necessarily the proposition that aesthetic attractiveness is a sufficient condition for prophetic truth. The commentary gives examples of illness or blemishes as part of the historical theological discussion; these do not amount to medical facts or norms endorsed by the modern translator.

**Scope of prophetic protection.** The key adopted principle is protection against what undermines the evidentiary force of a miracle and truthful communication. The following continuation debates protection from unbelief and particular classes of sin before and after the mission; no claim of unanimous doctrine on every degree of sin should be inferred from a truncated lemma. A later full pass must follow every qualification, objection, opponent and reply across the page boundary.

**Night Journey and Ascension.** The selected proposition on the visit to the Farthest Mosque is presented as scriptural evidence. The subsequent assertion about a later-generation consensus that the event occurred bodily while awake is a historical theological claim in the source; it is not independently proven by this English translation.
