### II. What the argument is actually testing

**Linguistic inimitability versus ṣarfa (II:229).** Sanandajī's printed prose reports the account attributed to al-Naẓẓām, and also to many Muʿtazilite and Shiʿite disputants, that the Qurʾān's challengers were prevented through divine diversion or withdrawal of what they would otherwise have possessed. The contrast is not simply that the Qurʾān was beautiful versus that it was unanswerable. It concerns *what grounds* its inimitability: an intrinsically exceptional expressive achievement, or an extrinsic restraint on would-be rivals. The source's own critique argues that emphasis on fine Arabic eloquence would then be an odd explanatory focus if the entire impossibility arose merely from restraint.

**Do not collapse the evidence (II:229–230).** The printed sequence shifts from the Qurʾān's extraordinary linguistic character to reports of matters unseen or unknown to the prophet through ordinary human transmission, including earlier prophetic narratives, and to predictions about later events. The examples of the Romans, the defeat of the opposing host and promised spoils belong to an argument from predictive knowledge, not the same demonstration as Arabic eloquence.

**Additional miracle reports (II:230).** The source also reports extraordinary signs, among them the palm trunk and the splitting of the moon. These are reports offered within a historical theological proof; this transcription does not turn them into independently adjudicated modern historical claims. A critical edition should preserve the actual Arabic textual witnesses, the kind of transmission invoked, and any objections in the longer marginal discussion.

### III. Critical apparatus and limits

1. The phrase `لا لعدم تأتّي المعارضة مع سهولتها` is a **negative restriction**. Omitting `lā` reverses the source's argument, while replacing `ṣarfa` with a generic 'miracle' removes the specific historical target.
2. The attribution to al-Naẓẓām belongs to the **printed Sanandajī scholastic argument** and needs separate doxographical verification before being treated as a verbatim quotation from al-Naẓẓām.
3. II:229–230 embed parenthetical Tahdhīb lemmata and interspersed explanation. All quoted Arabic is source-selected, not a continuous full diplomatic transcription of the main text.
4. The complete marginal note units below the horizontal dividing rule remain untranscribed and historically unidentified. No new separately authenticated Muḥammad Wasīm passage is claimed.
5. The two printed pages are NOT critically certified in their entirety; their `P2.229` and `P2.230` source-location guides remain separate from the new clause records.
