## Actual textual improvement

- **II:55 — multiple comparators of balance.** The previous Stage45 English gave a broad summary. The new connected English follows the four distinct applications of `iʿtidāl`: species, subordinate variety (`ṣinf`), individual (`shakhṣ`), and bodily organ (`ʿuḍw`). For each, the source distinguishes an external range enabling the thing to remain what it is from an internal optimum within its range. The final argument connects the claim that humankind is nearest true equilibrium with its receptivity to the rational soul. Some closing wording remains unverified.
- **II:56 — competing geographies of temperament.** This page explicitly reports Ibn Sīnā’s claim about the equatorial region, then the opposing claim of many later scholars concerning inhabitants of the fourth clime (`al-iqlīm al-rābiʿ`). It ends by opening the threefold division of mixed bodies. The long lower-page objection to equatorial balance is separately printed and remains untranslated in full.
- **II:57 — mineral, vegetal and animal.** The branching definition uses nutrition/growth, sensation, and voluntary motion. It then considers mineral subdivisions, including malleable and fusible substances and the seven traditional metallic bodies. The exact mineral-name enumeration and last alternatives need a second source reading; they have deliberately not been completed by guesswork.

The replacement English comprises **978 whitespace-delimited words** over three original source positions. This replaces earlier provisional summaries in the reader. It does **not** add three newly complete, independently certified pages. Both the Taftāzānī embedded lemmata and Sanandajī’s upper explanations remain **mixed-upper** until separately bounded by a collated base text.

## Marginal and authorship cautions

The lower-printed notes on these three pages are lengthy, particularly the objections concerning the fourth clime and whether plants display sensation. No individual marginal writer is established from location or typographic style. These passages are **not attributed to Wasīm** merely by being in the lower register. Wasīm’s self-identified authorial preface is independently available at I:2–5.
