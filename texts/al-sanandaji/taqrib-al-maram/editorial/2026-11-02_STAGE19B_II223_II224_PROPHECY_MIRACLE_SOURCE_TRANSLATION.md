# Stage 19B — Prophecy and miracle: direct source-clause translation
## Original Taqrīb al-Marām II:223–224 (physical PDF 518–519)

**Primary witness:** user-reuploaded complete raster two-volume `taqribmaram(2).pdf`. The following is *new source-language reading and first-pass English for specifically selected original clauses*, not a verbatim complete-page translation. Original Taftāzānī lemmata in parentheses and Sanandajī explanatory prose are identified separately. Every lower-margin note remains to be fully transcribed and historically attributed.

### A. Selected source clauses, with physical page and authorial register

#### B6.MATN.II223.01 — Recognizing the divine sender by proofs
**Arabic (II:223, source PDF 518):** يعرف المبعوث بنصب الأدلة
**English:** The person sent is enabled to recognize the divine sender through the establishment of evidences.
**Editorial reading:** Parenthesized embedded Taftāzānī lemma. The source has a larger surrounding argument about how a prophet can know the identity of the one who commissioned him.

#### B6.SHARH.II223.02 — Signs identifying the source of the commission
**Arabic (II:223, source PDF 518):** له الدالة على أن القائل له أرسلتك هو الله تعالى دون الجن بأن يظهر الله له آيات ومعجزات ينقطع عنها جميع الخلق وتكون مفيدة لذلك العلم
**English:** Evidence indicating that the one who says to him, 'I have sent you', is God exalted, and not a jinn: God manifests to him signs and extraordinary events beyond the power of the rest of creation, sufficiently indicating that knowledge.
**Editorial reading:** Sanandajī's running explanatory prose. Running explanation of the preceding base lemma. Read against the original image; the complete argument before this selection is not copied.

#### B6.MATN.II223.03 — Immediate knowledge as another avenue
**Arabic (II:223, source PDF 518):** أو بخلق العلم الضروري
**English:** Or through the creation of immediate, necessitating knowledge.
**Editorial reading:** Parenthesized embedded Taftāzānī lemma. A distinct alternative to the route through outward evidence; it is not a second physical miracle.

#### B6.SHARH.II223.04 — The knowledge of who sends
**Arabic (II:223, source PDF 518):** فيه بأنه المرسل
**English:** within him that He is the sender.
**Editorial reading:** Sanandajī's running explanatory prose. The pronoun refers to the person commissioned.

#### B6.MATN.II223.05 — The term miracle
**Arabic (II:223, source PDF 518):** المعجزة
**English:** The miracle (muʿjiza).
**Editorial reading:** Parenthesized embedded Taftāzānī lemma. The discussion begins to delimit a miracle as a proof of prophethood.

#### B6.SHARH.II223.06 — Etymology of miracle
**Arabic (II:223, source PDF 518):** مأخوذة من العجز المقابل للقدرة وحقيقة الإعجاز إثبات العجز
**English:** It is derived from inability (ʿajz), the opposite of power; causing someone to be unable is, strictly, the establishment of that inability.
**Editorial reading:** Sanandajī's running explanatory prose. The intervening original continues the extension of this term to a causal sign.

#### B6.MATN.II223.07 — Formal definition of a prophetic miracle
**Arabic (II:223, source PDF 518):** وهي في العرف أمر خارق للعادة مقرون بالتحدي مع عدم المعارضة
**English:** In technical usage, it is an event contrary to the ordinary course of things, accompanied by a challenge, with no effective counter-performance.
**Editorial reading:** Parenthesized embedded Taftāzānī lemma. This is not a mere 'unusual phenomenon'; each of the three qualifications matters.

#### B6.SHARH.II223.08 — Why the genus is 'event' rather than a positive action
**Arabic (II:223, source PDF 518):** وإنما قال أمر ليتناول الفعل كإخراج الماء من بين الأصابع وعدمه كعدم إحراق النار
**English:** He says 'an event' so that the definition includes a positive act, such as causing water to issue from between the fingers, and also a non-occurrence, such as fire not burning.
**Editorial reading:** Sanandajī's running explanatory prose. A first-pass reading of Sanandajī's explanatory source text.

#### B6.SHARH.II223.09 — Beginning of the exclusions
**Arabic (II:223, source PDF 518):** واحترز بقوله مقرون بالتحدي
**English:** By specifying 'accompanied by a challenge', he excludes ...
**Editorial reading:** Sanandajī's running explanatory prose. The completion is printed on the next original page, II:224. This sentence must not be treated as finished on II:223.

#### B6.SHARH.II224.10 — Excluding saintly wonders and magic
**Arabic (II:224, source PDF 519):** عن الكرامات وبقيد عدم المعارضة عن السحر
**English:** saintly marvels; and by the stipulation 'without counter-performance', he excludes sorcery.
**Editorial reading:** Sanandajī's running explanatory prose. Actual cross-page continuation of the clause beginning at the foot of II:223.

#### B6.MATN.II224.11 — The miracle's evidentiary force
**Arabic (II:224, source PDF 519):** ووجه دلالتها على صدق دعوى الرسالة أنها عند التحقق بمنزلة صريح التصديق
**English:** The manner in which it signifies the truth of a claim to prophethood is that, when it occurs, it has the force of explicit attestation.
**Editorial reading:** Parenthesized embedded Taftāzānī lemma. Source parenthesized phrasing; the argument continues in the next explanatory sentence.

#### B6.SHARH.II224.12 — God's creation of knowledge on witnessing the sign
**Arabic (II:224, source PDF 519):** لما جرت به العادة من أن الله تعالى يخلق عندها العلم الضروري بالصدق
**English:** For it has been the established customary order that God creates, on its occurrence, immediate knowledge of the claim's truth.
**Editorial reading:** Sanandajī's running explanatory prose. The efficacy is stated in terms of divine creation of cognition, not bare mechanical inference.

### B. Source-argument reconstruction (editorial, not a claim of complete quotation)

The argument is explicitly concerned with **how the prophet himself knows that God sent him**, not merely how an audience subsequently recognizes a claimant. The source presents two routes: evidence that the commissioning voice is truly divine and not an impostor, or immediate knowledge created by God in the person commissioned. Both routes belong to the source's epistemology of prophetic commissioning; neither is equivalent to the audience's ordinary reliance upon a bare assertion.

The miracle is then defined by conditions that matter **jointly**: (1) a departure from customary occurrences, (2) accompaniment by a challenge, and (3) the absence of a rival performance. ‘Event’ is intentionally broad enough to include both a striking occurrence (water issuing from the fingers) and an extraordinary non-occurrence (fire failing to burn). The qualification about a prophetic challenge distinguishes the event from a saintly marvel; the lack of effective counter-performance distinguishes it from sorcery. These are the **commentary's own formal exclusions**, not a modern analogy invented by the editor.

The miracle's epistemic force is described as **tantamount to explicit attestation**: in the customary order God creates immediate knowledge of truth when the sign occurs. This is more precise than saying simply that a miracle is unusual or that the extraordinary occurrence is an independent natural producer of knowledge. Whether the argument admits further qualifications appears in the untranscribed response beneath the divider.

### C. Page-boundary and attribution apparatus

The end of printed II:223 contains `واحترز بقوله مقرون بالتحدي`, completed by `عن الكرامات وبقيد عدم المعارضة عن السحر` at the **top of II:224**. Their joined English is presented above, but the original physical pagination is preserved. The main printed line on II:224 then proceeds to a further demonstration using a hypothetical public claim to prophecy; that example and the extensive lower commentary are NOT yet fully translated.
