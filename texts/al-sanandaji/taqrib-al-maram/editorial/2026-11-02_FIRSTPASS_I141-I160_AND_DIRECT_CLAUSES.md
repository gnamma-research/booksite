# Taqrīb al-Marām and its printed marginal apparatus — new I:141–160 source-image reading

**Edition checkpoint:** 2 November 2026. **Witness:** the user's combined 632-image al-Maktaba al-Azhariyya (2012) reproduction of the older Cairo edition, originally printed in two parts. Printed vol. I:141–160 corresponds exactly to physical PDF pp.154–173. The Arabic was visually inspected from the original page images, not represented as clean OCR.

## Reader status and attribution

**This is not the complete translation of twenty printed pages.** Each page below contains an image-based explanatory English first reading and **only a selected Arabic anchor**. A large proportion of the lower register remains untranslated; it cannot automatically be credited to Muḥammad Wasīm because the title page also names other marginal contributors. Six further *directly selected* Arabic–English clauses are supplied after the page accounts. Their quotations are substantially closer to the Arabic than the page summaries but are still not full pages. Editorial punctuation is added and independent second-witness collation remains outstanding.

## P1.141 — The complete cause and the partial cause

**Primary page:** vol. I:141; scan PDF p.154. **Source layer:** Sanandajī upper-register argument, excerpted (lower marginal register untranscribed in full).

**Short Arabic anchor:**

> والناقصة هي بعض ما توقف عليه الشيء

**First English explanatory reading (not verbatim or complete):**

Sanandajī distinguishes the totality of what an effect depends upon from a merely partial cause. In some cases the complete cause consists of an efficient agent alone, as with a simple effect attributed to a necessitating producer; in others it includes the end toward which a voluntary agent acts; in composite things matter and form also belong to the relevant causal account.

## P1.142 — Does a cause precede its effect?

**Primary page:** vol. I:142; scan PDF p.155. **Source layer:** Sanandajī upper-register argument, excerpted (lower marginal register untranscribed in full).

**Short Arabic anchor:**

> وعند تمام الفاعل بجميع جهات التأثير من الشرائط والآلات والقابل يجب وجود المعلول

**First English explanatory reading (not verbatim or complete):**

The whole complete cause cannot invariably precede its effect in time, because constituents such as the material cause coexist with the thing constituted. When the efficient cause is complete in all required respects, including conditions, instruments and a suitable recipient, the effect must occur. Otherwise the occurrence rather than nonoccurrence of the effect would call for a further determining factor.

Sanandajī reports a proposed inference from numerical unity of effect to unity of producing cause. ## P1.156 — Why a totality of dependent members remains dependent

**Short Arabic anchor:**

> الجملة ليست نفسها

**First English explanatory reading (not verbatim or complete):**

If the causes in a series of dependent possibles never terminate in a cause that is not itself dependent, the totality of such possibles also requires an explanation. The whole cannot serve as its own independent cause simply because it is described collectively. The commentary argues that a cause adequate to the dependent totality cannot merely be a dependent member taken in precisely the same respect.

## P1.157 — Circular causation, the independent source and the necessary being

**Primary page:** vol. I:157; scan PDF p.170. **Source layer:** Sanandajī upper-register argument, excerpted (lower marginal register untranscribed in full).

**Short Arabic anchor:**

> فيلزم أن الموجود الخارج عن سلسلة الممكنات لا يكون إلا واجبا

**First English explanatory reading (not verbatim or complete):**

A circular chain would make a member ultimately account for itself, directly or indirectly. The commentary excludes that outcome and concludes that the existent outside the series of contingent things must be necessary, since something existing outside that series cannot itself be one of the same contingent dependent members. Most of the printed page consists of marginal objections to the proof.

## P1.160 — The regress argument and a necessary existent outside the series

**Primary page:** vol. I:160; scan PDF p.173. **Source layer:** Sanandajī upper-register argument, excerpted (lower marginal register untranscribed in full).

**Short Arabic anchor:**

> إذا لم يكن في المجموع شيء سوى الأجزاء

**First English explanatory reading (not verbatim or complete):**

If a whole contains nothing over and above its parts, then describing the series collectively cannot introduce an independent explanatory cause absent from those parts. The proof excludes the series being adequate to itself and rejects a circle of causal dependence. Its claim is about explanatory dependence, not merely a statement that finite numerical lists must have first elements.

# Source-facing selected direct translations (new)

### T.I.141.01 — Complete and incomplete causes · I:141 / PDF 154

**Arabic, selected clause:**

> والناقصة هي بعض ما توقف عليه الشيء، والتامة قد تكون هي الفاعل وحده، كالسبب الموجد للبسيط إيجابًا، وقد تكون هو مع الغاية، كالسبب الموجد للبسيط اختيارًا، فإن فعل المختار قد يكون لغرض يدعو إليه، وقد تكون هو مع المادة والصورة أيضًا كالموجد للمركب

**English:**

The incomplete cause is some of what the thing depends upon. The complete cause may be the agent alone, as with a cause that produces a simple thing by necessitation. It may be the agent together with the end, as with a cause that produces a simple thing through choice, for the act of a freely choosing agent may be directed to a purpose that moves him to it. The complete cause may also include matter and form, as in the production of a composite.

*Critical status:* first-pass source-image reading; not a complete printed page. Continuous selected upper-register lines. Punctuation is editorial; the final part of the page continues the same causal taxonomy.

### T.I.142.02 — The completion of the efficient cause · I:142 / PDF 155

### T.I.150.03 — Agent and recipient: necessity and possibility · I:150 / PDF 163

**Arabic, selected clause:**

> بأن نسبة الفاعل إلى المفعول بالوجوب، ونسبة القابل إلى المفعول بالإمكان

**English:**

Because the agent’s relation to the effect is characterized by necessity, whereas the recipient’s relation to the effect is characterized by possibility.

*Critical status:* first-pass source-image reading; not a complete printed page. Argument attributed to a reported proof in the source; not an unconditional endorsement by Sanandajī. The translation distinguishes 'agent' from 'recipient' rather than conflating their modes.

### T.I.156.04 — The totality of contingent beings · I:156 / PDF 169

**Arabic, selected clause:**

> لو تسلسل من غير أن تنتهي إلى علة محضة لكان هنالك جملة هي نفس مجموع الممكنات الموجودة بالفعل

**English:**

If the series proceeded without terminating in an unqualified [independent] cause, there would be a totality consisting of the entire collection of contingent beings that actually exist.

*Critical status:* first-pass source-image reading; not a complete printed page. Selected clause in the argument from a series of dependent possibles. The word 'independent' in brackets interprets 'pure cause' and is not independently present in the Arabic.
