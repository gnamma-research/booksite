## I043.upper.02 · The particular existence is at issue; alleged inherence of existence in possible quiddity

**Arabic (selected, ellipses are omissions)**

> إنما ذلك لأن البحث ليس في الوجود المشترك بل في الوجود الخاص المخالف لسائر الوجودات في الحقيقة، والمجرد هو الثاني والمعلوم بالضرورة هو الأول. […] والمراد بقيام الوجود بالماهية في الممكن قيامه بها (كبياض الجسم) […] إذ لا ينفرد كل بتحقق على حدة في الخارج […] فينقل الكلام إلى وجوده ويتسلسل […]

**English — only the corresponding selected passages**

> That is because the inquiry concerns not existence shared in meaning but the particular existence that differs in its reality from other existences. What is [described as] separate belongs to the second [account], while what is known immediately is the first. […] By existence’s subsisting in the quiddity of a possible thing, the discussion means its subsisting there, “as whiteness [does] in a body”; […] it is not the case that each has a separately realised actuality outside the mind. […] Otherwise the dispute would be carried over to existence’s own existence and an infinite regress would follow. […]

## I044.lower.01 · Whether existence inheres in quiddity as whiteness inheres in an already existent body

**Arabic (selected, ellipses are omissions)**

> في الخارج بين الوجود والماهية، قلت: كذلك بين البياض والجسم، فليتأمل. ثم الفرق الظاهر بين الصورتين هو أن قيام البياض بالجسم مسبوق بوجود الجسم، بخلاف قيام الوجود بالماهية؛ فإن اكتفى في الفرق بين القيامين بمجرد هذا كان سهلا، لكن لا يندفع به إلا الإيراد الثاني المذكور بقوله: وأيضا الماهية المعروضة، الخ. […] إذا تقرر هذا وجب التسليم في دفع الأول بما حققه المحققون من أن زيادة الوجود إنما هي بالنظر إلى الماهيات التي هي غير الوجود، وأما بالنسبة إلى نفسه فلا […] فلا تسلسل قطعا. […]

**English — only the corresponding selected passages**

> [Concerning the alleged difference] in external reality between existence and quiddity: I reply that the same can be said of whiteness and a body; consider this. The evident distinction between the two situations is that whiteness inheres in a body whose existence is already presupposed, whereas existence’s relation to quiddity is not like that. If this alone is taken to distinguish the two types of inherence, the response is easy enough, but it answers only the second objection, beginning “Furthermore the quiddity that receives the attribute ...” […] Once this is established, the first objection must be addressed with the investigators’ finding that existence is additional only in relation to quiddities other than existence itself, and not additional to itself. […] There is therefore no infinite regress. […]

**Boundary:** I:44 long lower register opening, continuing unfinished I043.lower.02 discussion. Editorial lower.01 division is inherited; the source prints a continuous lower passage, not a separately ruled new note.

**Open proof obligation:** Source begins mid-argument from I:43. Some omitted supporting clauses after “إذا تقرر هذا” and before “فلا تسلسل قطعا” need full diplomatic transcription and independent second proof. Do not assign Wasīm by proximity.

## I044.lower.02 · Why existence realised in itself does not make every existence necessary

**Arabic (selected, ellipses are omissions)**

> فإن قيل: فيكون كل وجود واجبا، إذ لا معنى للواجب سوى ما يكون تحققه بنفسه. قلنا: ممنوع، فإن معنى كون وجود الواجب بنفسه هو أنه مقتضى ذاته من غير احتياج إلى فاعل، ومعنى تحقق الوجود بنفسه أنه إذا حصل، سواء من ذاته كما في الواجب أو من غيره كما في الممكن، لم يفتقر إلى وجود آخر يقوم به. […] وقد سبق بيان ذلك إن شاء الله تعالى. […]

**English — only the corresponding selected passages**

> If it is objected, “Every existence would then be necessary, since being necessary means only being realised through itself,” we reply: This is denied. The Necessary’s existence being through itself means that it follows from its essence without needing an efficient cause. But an existence being realised through itself means that, once it has occurred—whether from itself, as in the Necessary, or from another, as in the possible—it does not require yet another existence in which to subsist. […] The explanation of this has been given earlier, God willing. […]

**Boundary:** Second inherited editorial lower unit on I:44, beginning with “فإن قيل” within the continuous lower register; separates the theological modal objection from the earlier whiteness analogy; concluding qualifications are not fully translated.
