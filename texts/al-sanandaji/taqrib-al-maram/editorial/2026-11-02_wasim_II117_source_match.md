# Wasīm al-Kurdistānī · Ḥāshiyat al-Muḥākamāt · II:117 · first-pass witnessed fragment

**Arabic witness:** combined Taqrīb/Ḥāshiya, printed vol. II p.117, facsimile PDF physical p.412, **lower register**. Cross-attribution: Dr Waleed Ibn al-Salah, 20 April 2022, quoting this text and explicitly crediting Muḥammad Wasīm, https://drwaleedbinalsalah.com/2-الأدلة-العقلية-على-امتناع-حلول-الحوا/ . ## Arabic text · provisional punctuation

> ثم أقول لا يبعد أن يقال المراد من التغير المحال عليه الحاصل بذلك الاتصاف تأثره بحدوث ذلك الحادث فيه ولو بإحداث من ذاته، فإنه كما يمتنع تأثره بحدوث ذاته ولو من ذاته كذلك يمتنع تأثره بحدوث الوصف فيه ولو من ذاته، فليتأمل تأملا يليق بالمقام.

## English · new first-pass translation

Then I say: One may reasonably hold that the change impossible for Him, arising from His being qualified by this [new] attribute, means His being affected by the occurrence of that originated thing within Him, even if its production is ascribed to His own essence. For just as it is impossible that He should be affected by the origination of His own essence, even if ascribed to Himself, so too it is impossible that He should be affected by the origination of an attribute within Him, even if ascribed to Himself. This calls for reflection befitting the question.

## Argument

Wasīm's proposed criterion for impossible divine change is **taʾaththur** (being affected), rather than merely the logical attribution of a new predicate to God. The contrast needs examination alongside theories of real intrinsic modification, efficient production, and extrinsic relational predication. Such comparisons are editorial interpretations, not claims of intellectual dependence.

### Associated Sanandajī upper-register witness, printed II:117 · PDF 412

> ورد بأن الممتنع في الأزل هو الاتصاف في الأزل لا نفس الاتصاف ... وضد الحادث حادث لأنه منقطع إلى الحادث ... لأن ما ثبت قدمه امتنع عدمه

**First-pass English, explicit omissions:** A reply is offered: what is impossible in eternity is being qualified eternally, not qualification as such. … The opposite of an originated attribute is itself originated, since it ceases upon the arrival of that originated attribute. … Whatever has been established as beginningless cannot cease to be.

**Embedded lemma:** «ويجب زوال ضده» — “Its opposite must cease.” This is quoted within the upper commentary, not supplied as a fresh complete Tahdhīb matn translation.
