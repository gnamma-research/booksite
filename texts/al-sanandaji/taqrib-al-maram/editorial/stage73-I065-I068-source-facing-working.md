### I065.upper.01 — The quiddity taken on condition of abstraction

**Arabic anchors:** «تؤخذ بشرط لا شيء»؛ «أي الخلو عن العوارض واللواحق»؛ «وسميت المجردة»؛ «ولا توجد في الأذهان فضلا عن الأعيان»؛ «لأن الوجود في الذهن ... من العوارض».

**English:** A quiddity may be taken “on condition of no thing,” that is, conditioned by being free of concomitants and accidents. It is therefore called the **abstracted** quiddity, because of the abstraction of all such things from it. So understood, “it does not exist in minds, still less in external things.” For existence in the mind is itself an accident of the apprehended thing. This holds whether the accidents being excluded are all accidents without qualification or only those taken to be external, since mental existence also supplies a determination to what is conceived. The explanation turns on distinguishing the nature in itself from the features attaching to the nature *as apprehended*. The phrase continues onto I:66.

### I065.ruled.01 — The kinds of accident, separate short note

**Arabic anchors:** «قوله لأن الوجود الخ»؛ «قسموا العارض للشيء قسمين»؛ «عارض الوجود»؛ «عارض الماهية»؛ «الوجود الخارجي»؛ «الوجود الذهني»؛ «كالكلية والجزئية».

**English:** On “Because existence ...”: scholars have divided what supervenes on a thing into accidents of existence and accidents of quiddity. They further distinguish accidents associated with external existence, such as sensible characteristics, from accidents associated with mental existence, such as universality and particularity. What is being excluded by abstraction must therefore be specified: exclusion of independently external accidents is not identical to exclusion of anything attaching to a nature in the act of thinking. The accompanying note cautions against using the term “unconditioned” as though it meant a condition of exclusion. [The printed note's final short reference to the three conditions requires a more literal second reading.]

### I065.lower.01 — The question of actuality and mental occurrence, unassigned lower commentary

**Arabic anchors:** «المركب منها الذي اشتهر أن الفرد»؛ «ليس من التشخص كذلك»؛ «الوجود والاختصاص»؛ «قولـه لتجردها عنها»؛ «تسمى الماهية بشرط لا أيضا».

**English:** The individual is often described as a composite of the quiddity and its individuation, but this composite must not simply be confused with an entity in which individuation subsists apart from what is individuated. What is claimed to be excluded under the abstracting consideration needs care: an act of abstraction neither abolishes the thing's quiddity nor proves the existence of a bare quiddity outside. The lower writer calls attention to the language of particular existence, qualification, and individuation. In discussing “because it is abstracted from them,” he insists that abstraction *from concomitants* is an intellectual operation, not the appearance of a new concrete component corresponding to the abstracted aspect. The alleged absence of accidents is precisely the consideration under which the quiddity receives the technical label “taken on condition of no thing.”

### I066.upper.01 — Does mental conception itself violate the abstraction?

**Arabic anchors:** «الذهن»؛ «فإن الشيء الذي تفرضه موجودا في الذهن»؛ «يعرض له بنفس الوجود في الذهن»؛ «من غير أن يعتبر الذهن عارضا له»؛ «وأما الحكم عليها باستحالة وجودها فلا الحكم على الشيء إلا بعد تصوره».

**English:** The mental attribute under discussion does not have to be separately *attached* by an act of consideration: something posited as existing in thought acquires that determination through its very existence in thought, even without the intellect subsequently predicating it as an accident. But if it is said that the abstracted quiddity cannot exist, how can one make a judgement concerning it at all? A judgement about a thing presupposes some conception of the thing. It is not sufficient to declare the quiddity absolutely inconceivable; one must distinguish conceiving the nature and conceiving the nature *with the stipulated absence of every concomitant*. The argument continues in the ruled and lower comments.

### I066.ruled.01 — What the expression “an accident” excludes

**Arabic anchors:** «قوله بأن تعتبر له عارضا»؛ «ليس المراد من هذا أن هذا العارض ليس عارضا له أصلا»؛ «وإنما هو محض اعتبار الذهن له»؛ «من يحصل في الذهن أن ذلك العارض عارض له».

**English:** On “that you consider it as having an accident”: the author's intention is not that the supposed accident is in no sense an accident of the object. Rather, the question is whether the intellect merely *considers* it as added, or whether it belongs to the way the object occurs in the mind and so cannot be omitted by an arbitrary stipulation. Conceiving a nature and then noticing its mentally supervening attributes are distinguishable acts, but the mental existence underlying the latter is not thereby eliminated. ### I066.lower.01 — Mental abstraction, external accidents, and the apparent inconsistency

**Arabic anchors:** «قيد العوارض بالخارجية جوز وجود المجردة في الذهن»؛ «المعروض الخارجية بهذا المعنى»؛ «سواء ...»؛ «فالماهية المجردة لا توجد من حيث هي مجردة»؛ «قولـه وأما الحكم عليها باستحالة وجودها».

**English:** Restricting the excluded accidents to *external* accidents appears to allow an abstracted nature to exist mentally, since the mentally present nature need not bear the external accompaniments of its external instances. But the classification is not so straightforward: the mental determination itself is an accident for one purpose, and no such external accident for another. The note compares the manner in which intellects count or consider objects with the kinds of qualification inherited from external things. If all mental determinations are excluded as well, then the supposedly abstracted quiddity cannot have mental existence *under that restrictive consideration*. Yet the intellect may still grasp the meaning to which that condition is appended; otherwise it could not form the judgement that such an abstraction is impossible. This last point is the bridge to the next leaf.

### I067.upper.01 — The judgement “impossible” is made under a different consideration

**Arabic anchors:** «كانت من حيث ذاتها مجردة مقارنة للمخلوطة»؛ «ومن حيث وجودها في الذهن قسما من المخلوطة»؛ «فيصح الحكم عليها باستحالة الوجود من حيث ذاتها»؛ «وقد تؤخذ لا بشرط شيء من مقارنة العوارض والتجرد عنها»؛ «وهي أعم من المخلوطة».

**English:** A quiddity may be abstracted when considered in itself and yet, insofar as it is present in the mind, belong to what is mixed with determining features. We can therefore judge its existence impossible **under the consideration of sheer abstraction**, without denying that it can be apprehended under another consideration. It may further be taken “without the stipulation of anything”—neither stipulating the accompanying accidents nor stipulating abstraction from them. The nature so considered is more general than what is taken with accompanying accidents, because the latter is only one of the possible instantiations admitted by the former. The comparison continues on I:68.

### I067.lower.01 — Why there need not be a contradiction in conceiving what is judged impossible

**Arabic anchors:** «قوله من حيث ذاتها»؛ «فلا تناقض»؛ «الماهية المجردة»؛ «الوجود في الذهن»؛ «الاستحالة».

**English:** The gloss objects that the apparent resolution still risks a contradiction. If the intellect judges a quiddity to be impossible *as abstracted*, it must apprehend what the expression designates. If its mental apprehension makes it mixed, what exactly is the subject of the impossibility judgement? The reply depends on whether a judgement of possibility is made about the nature considered absolutely, or about a nature under a condition of excluding all concomitants. Merely knowing a meaning does not establish that the precise *condition* added to it is instantiated in the same act of knowing. The writer explains that the two considerations can be distinguished, but warns that a switch from one to the other must not occur without notice.

### I067.lower.02 — The act of predication and the object of predication

**Arabic anchors:** «المحكوم عليه»؛ «جواز الوجود»؛ «التجرد في الذهن»؛ «الوجود في الخارج»؛ «إذا كانا متحدين من وجه ...».

**English:** The subject of a proposition about possibility can be an intellectually signified nature, not an abstracted entity already realised in the mind in every respect specified in the proposition. It follows that saying “the abstracted quiddity is impossible” need not assert that the quiddity possesses no intelligible meaning; rather, the predicative subject is distinguished from the restriction about which the judgement is made. The marginal discussion considers different senses of “abstracted” and “mixed,” and argues that the answer must fix which sense is intended before claims about conceptual identity, external being or impossibility can be tested. ### I068.upper.01 — Unrestricted nature is not an externally separate part

**Arabic anchors:** «وهي أعم من المجردة لصدقها على ما ...»؛ «وصدق المطلق على المقيد»؛ «فتوجد الماهية لا بشرط في الخارج لكونها نفسها ... أي نفس المخلوطة في الخارج»؛ «لا جزءا منها»؛ «إذ ليس الموجود من الإنسان إلا زيد وعمرو»؛ «فلا يصدق عليه المطلق لامتناع صدق الجزء الخارجي ...».

**English:** The nature taken without a condition is more general than the abstracted nature, since it can apply both when there are accompanying features and when such features are abstracted. This is the sense in which an unrestricted concept can be predicated of a restricted instance. The quiddity taken without stipulating anything “exists externally,” because it is the very quiddity found with its individuating concomitants—**not merely a part of that external composite**. Only particular humans, such as Zayd and ʿAmr, occur outside the intellect, but it would be wrong to infer that unconditioned humanity is a separately existing part in them. Rather, the nature under this conceptual consideration is predicated of individuals. If it were an externally detached constituent, predication of it as the whole could not straightforwardly be made. The argument continues on I:69.

### I068.lower.01 — What is being denied by “not a part of it”?

**Arabic anchors:** «قوله لصدقها عليهما»؛ «مفهومه غير ذلك المفهوم»؛ «قوله لا جزءا منها»؛ «الماهية المخلوطة»؛ «الفرد»؛ «المطلقة».

**English:** The gloss first examines how the unrestricted nature applies both to what is accompanied and to what is abstracted, noting that a sameness of external subject does not imply the same mental meaning. It then objects to the statement that the nature is “not a part” of the composite. If the individual is made up of a quiddity and individuating features, one may describe quiddity as a constituent of the individual; but whether it is an independent part with its own external existence is another question. The use of “mixed” sometimes refers to the whole individual and sometimes to the quiddity qualified by concomitants. These cannot be interchanged without explaining how predication relates the part and whole.

### I068.lower.02 — The difference between predication and conceptual inclusion

**Arabic anchors:** «الوجود في الخارج»؛ «مطلق إنسان»؛ «زيد»؛ «ماهية من العوارض»؛ «القول».

**English:** It is not necessary that an unrestricted human nature exist alongside Zayd as a second external entity before “human” can truly be predicated of Zayd. Conversely, the mere possibility of forming an unrestricted human concept does not prove that this concept, considered with its mental universal qualification, itself exists externally. The question is instead whether a nature predicated of an individual is the very nature in that individual, or merely one of its constituents. The marginal writer explores the alternative senses of “individual,” “absolute,” and “mixed,” which opens the more elaborate set of objections on I:69–70.
