# Taqrīb al-Marām — Stage 09B · volume I, printed pages 241–260

**2 November 2026.** Original combined Sanandajī and Wasīm printed facsimile, source PDF **images 254–273**. All twenty original images were visually examined; do not present OCR or a page outline as a complete translation of the Arabic.

**Editorial status:** 20 NEW page-located, argument-based **first-reading guides**, not continuous direct English of the source, and **15 short selected direct Arabic–English phrases**. Substantial upper commentary and almost all the lower-register annotations remain untranscribed and untranslated. A short phrase may be a lemma originally written by al-Taftāzānī, and must be collated before assigning the entire phrase to Sanandajī. **No new passage is independently identified as Wasīm in this segment.**

The philosophical sequence moves from the capacity/act dispute, through affective and sensible qualities, health and illness, figure and predispositional qualities, to **فصل في الأين** (printed p.250), the category of 'where'. The subsequent discussion of *akwān*, motion and rest distinguishes repeated occupancy of a region from movement to another region and from philosophical accounts of motion as gradual actualization.

## Part A — twenty page-level argument guides (not full translations)

### P1.241: Created power and the act's occurrence

**Printed I:241, physical scan PDF 254**. Source layout: upper-and-marginal-distinct. 

**Selected Arabic anchor:** «القدرة».

**English orientation:** The argument continues the dispute over the relation between a creature's capacity and the act that occurs. It distinguishes the agent's possession of power from the act's being prevented by a contrary condition and asks whether the same capacity can be referred to mutually exclusive alternatives. The lower commentary supplies a more extensive reply concerning the occurrence of power when action is withheld.

**Incomplete:** continuous full Arabic/English, author-specific glosses, quoted lemmata and second visual verification.

### P1.242: Before, during, and after the act: competing doctrines of capacity

**Printed I:242, physical scan PDF 255**. Source layout: upper-and-marginal-distinct. 

**Selected Arabic anchor:** «عند المعتزلة ... وعند الأشاعرة».

**English orientation:** The positions of the Muʿtazila and Ashʿarīs on whether created power precedes an act or accompanies it are contrasted. Further questions arise about the persistence of capacity and whether an agent's inability is an absence of power or a positive opposing determination. The marginal debate must be read separately before attributing a proposed proof to either school.

### P1.243: The power of acting and the presence of a contrary

**Printed I:243, physical scan PDF 256**. Source layout: upper-and-marginal-distinct. 

**Selected Arabic anchor:** «وعدم القدرة».

**English orientation:** The dispute asks how an agent can be described as able to do something while an actual condition prevents its occurrence. One response treats ability as an available disposition independent of the subsequent act, while another distinguishes the completed exercise of ability from mere preparedness. The page also revisits the semantics of power and the possibility of opposites.

### P1.244: Opposition among psychological qualities

**Printed I:244, physical scan PDF 257**. Source layout: upper-and-marginal-distinct. 

**Selected Arabic anchor:** «اللذة والألم».

**English orientation:** A transition brings the pleasurable and painful states into the classification of qualitative accidents. The text considers whether pleasure and pain are two contraries, merely the presence or absence of a single feature, or apprehensions of what accords with and opposes a nature. Related states are discussed by distinguishing the thing apprehended from the subject's apprehending it.

### P1.245: Pleasure, pain, and intellect versus sensation

**Printed I:245, physical scan PDF 258**. Source layout: upper-and-marginal-distinct. 

**Selected Arabic anchor:** «العقل والحس».

**English orientation:** The text weighs the definition of pleasure and pain by reference to the perception of what is agreeable or disagreeable to a living thing. Intellectual and sensory modes of apprehension are compared: an intellect may understand things unavailable to the senses, while the senses register particular bodily qualities. The main and marginal arguments differ in how they explain the immediate felt character of the states.

**Incomplete:** continuous full Arabic/English, author-specific glosses, quoted lemmata and second visual verification.

### P1.246: Health, illness, living organisms and perception

**Printed I:246, physical scan PDF 259**. Source layout: upper-and-marginal-distinct. 

**Selected Arabic anchor:** «الصحة والمرض».

**English orientation:** Health and illness are considered as qualities or dispositions of a living organism rather than merely as names for the absence of a given symptom. The account relates an organism's capacity for appropriate activity to conditions of its body and organs, while questioning whether health is a stable possession or a relation among bodily states. The subordinate glosses examine the difficulty of treating it as a simple contrary of disease.

**Incomplete:** continuous full Arabic/English, author-specific glosses, quoted lemmata and second visual verification.

### P1.247: Disposition, the nonexistence of a quality, and privation

**Printed I:247, physical scan PDF 260**. Source layout: upper-and-marginal-distinct. 

**Selected Arabic anchor:** «الملكة ... عدمها».

**English orientation:** The text asks whether disease is the negation of health or another quality, and whether a privation must be understood through the capacity of its appropriate subject. It distinguishes a state of the organism from the absence of an expected operation; a subject's inability to perform may be relative to a particular faculty. It then moves toward further kinds of quality.

### P1.248: Colour as a surface quality and the figure of a body

**Printed I:248, physical scan PDF 261**. Source layout: upper-and-marginal-distinct. 

**Selected Arabic anchor:** «اللون من خواص السطح».

**English orientation:** Colour is treated in relation to the visible surface of a body. The classification distinguishes a surface quality from the body with its three dimensions and from geometrical figure. Questions about the outline of a curved surface and about shape arise because the same body can be considered according to several accidental respects without multiplying independent substances.

**Incomplete:** continuous full Arabic/English, author-specific glosses, quoted lemmata and second visual verification.

### P1.249: Angle, shape, and the predispositional qualities

**Printed I:249, physical scan PDF 262**. Source layout: upper-and-marginal-distinct. 

**Selected Arabic anchor:** «الكيفيات الاستعدادية».

**English orientation:** The page continues problems concerning geometric shape and angle, including boundaries and the unity of a surface, then introduces qualities of preparedness or disposition. Such qualities do not describe a presently accomplished action but an aptitude for acting or receiving an influence. The explanatory sense of strength and weakness must be distinguished from quantitative magnitude.

**Incomplete:** continuous full Arabic/English, author-specific glosses, quoted lemmata and second visual verification.

### P1.250: A new chapter: place-occupancy or 'where' (al-ayn)

**Printed I:250, physical scan PDF 263**. Source layout: upper-and-marginal-distinct. 

**Selected Arabic anchor:** «فصل في الأين».

**English orientation:** A new chapter opens on *al-ayn* (the category of being where). The main theological classification identifies bodily 'comings-to-be' (*akwān*) in a place, and proceeds to motion, rest, conjunction and separation. It distinguishes the status of a body occupying a location from the account given of change between locations. This chapter is not simply a continuation of the taxonomy of sensory quality.

**Incomplete:** continuous full Arabic/English, author-specific glosses, quoted lemmata and second visual verification.

### P1.251: Being in a region, movement out, and the six spatial directions

**Printed I:251, physical scan PDF 264**. Source layout: upper-and-marginal-distinct. 

**Selected Arabic anchor:** «في حيز آخر».

**English orientation:** The argument examines the difference between a body's first occurrence in a spatial region and its subsequent occupancy of another region. It also treats bodily position, directions and whether leaving a region entails an independent positive accident over and above the occupancy of the next region. Different kalām descriptions of motion and rest are reported.

**Incomplete:** continuous full Arabic/English, author-specific glosses, quoted lemmata and second visual verification.

### P1.252: Whether motion is more than successive occupancy

**Printed I:252, physical scan PDF 265**. Source layout: short-upper-extensive-margin. 

**Selected Arabic anchor:** «الحركة».

**English orientation:** The running commentary is relatively brief on this page, and the marginal discussion comparatively extensive. The central problem is whether a motion is a distinct property or whether it can be adequately represented by successive instances of a body's being in different regions. The argument differentiates a change in place from an imagined additional entity called motion.

**Incomplete:** continuous full Arabic/English, author-specific glosses, quoted lemmata and second visual verification.

### P1.253: Continuity and the interval between different locations

**Printed I:253, physical scan PDF 266**. Source layout: short-upper-extensive-margin. 

**Selected Arabic anchor:** «في الحيز».

**English orientation:** A short upper passage is accompanied by an extended lower disputation. The objection concerns the possibility that a body remains in a region until its next occupancy, and what it means to say that the intervening instant has no separate positive occupancy. The text's exact distinction between instant and temporal interval remains a full-translation obligation.

**Incomplete:** continuous full Arabic/English, author-specific glosses, quoted lemmata and second visual verification.

### P1.254: The four akwān and the definitions of motion and rest

**Printed I:254, physical scan PDF 267**. Source layout: upper-and-marginal-distinct. 

**Selected Arabic anchor:** «الحركة والسكون والاجتماع والافتراق».

**English orientation:** The text describes several modes of bodily occurrence in spatial regions: motion, rest, conjunction and separation. The classification depends upon comparison with another place or body; not every relational description is an independently distinct substance. It also reports disagreements over whether the four are irreducible divisions or different descriptions of bodily spatial occupancy.

**Incomplete:** continuous full Arabic/English, author-specific glosses, quoted lemmata and second visual verification.

### P1.255: Motion and rest: first and second occurrence

**Printed I:255, physical scan PDF 268**. Source layout: upper-and-marginal-distinct. 

**Selected Arabic anchor:** «الحصول الثاني».

**English orientation:** Motion is contrasted with rest by reference to the first and second occupancy of a region or to a succession of regions. Whether two successive occurrences count as motion or rest depends upon which spatial region is held fixed. A lower objection asks whether combining occurrences rather than taking a single one is required, and whether this entails the persistence of one identical accident.

**Incomplete:** continuous full Arabic/English, author-specific glosses, quoted lemmata and second visual verification.

### P1.256: The limits of defining motion by successive regions

**Printed I:256, physical scan PDF 269**. Source layout: upper-and-marginal-distinct. 

**Selected Arabic anchor:** «خروج من الحيز».

**English orientation:** The text tests whether motion may be defined as departure from a region, passage across a boundary, or the acquisition of a second region. The relations among the moving body, the boundary and the passage of time are distinguished; one cannot simply assume that the interval between departure and arrival constitutes another separate body or substance.

**Incomplete:** continuous full Arabic/English, author-specific glosses, quoted lemmata and second visual verification.

### P1.257: Motion through time and an instant as temporal boundary

**Printed I:257, physical scan PDF 270**. Source layout: short-upper-extensive-margin. 

**Selected Arabic anchor:** «طرف الزمان».

**English orientation:** The upper text explains a difficulty with taking motion to occur all at once, because an instant is a boundary of time, not an independently extended temporal duration. The question is how motion can be present through successive positions when its supposed parts do not exist simultaneously. Much of the page consists of marginal responses to an objection about the body's being between origin and destination.

**Incomplete:** continuous full Arabic/English, author-specific glosses, quoted lemmata and second visual verification.

### P1.258: Motion as the body's intermediate state

**Printed I:258, physical scan PDF 271**. Source layout: upper-and-marginal-distinct. 

**Selected Arabic anchor:** «التوسط بين المبدأ والمنتهى».

**English orientation:** The discussion distinguishes motion as the body's being intermediate between the starting point and the endpoint from motion understood as a gradual unfolding across time. It examines how a moving object may be said to have reached parts of its course while other parts remain to be traversed. The analysis depends on different senses of the term 'motion' rather than on a single unqualified definition.

**Incomplete:** continuous full Arabic/English, author-specific glosses, quoted lemmata and second visual verification.

### P1.259: Beginning, end and the intermediate course of motion

**Printed I:259, physical scan PDF 272**. Source layout: upper-and-marginal-distinct. 

**Selected Arabic anchor:** «المبدأ والمنتهى».

**English orientation:** The motion of a body is analyzed through its starting position, end position and passage between them. A more general division classifies motion according to quantity, quality, place and posture, asking which kind of change is appropriate to each category. The marginal debate challenges whether an apparent motion is an extended whole or a succession of distinct stages.

**Incomplete:** continuous full Arabic/English, author-specific glosses, quoted lemmata and second visual verification.

### P1.260: The four kinds of change: where, posture, quantity and quality

**Printed I:260, physical scan PDF 273**. Source layout: short-upper-extensive-margin. 

**Selected Arabic anchor:** «الحركة في الأين ظاهرة».

**English orientation:** The passage concludes this portion by distinguishing locomotion from change of posture and from change in quantity and quality. Movement from one location to another is not identical with rotation in posture, increase or decrease of magnitude, or alteration of a sensible quality. The upper text is relatively short compared with the extended lower treatment, which remains incompletely translated.

**Incomplete:** continuous full Arabic/English, author-specific glosses, quoted lemmata and second visual verification.
