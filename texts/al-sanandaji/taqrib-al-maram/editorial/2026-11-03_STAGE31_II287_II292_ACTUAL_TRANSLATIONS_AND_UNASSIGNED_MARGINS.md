### III. Connected argument in English — editorial explanation, NOT substitute for original untranslated lines

**1. Knowledge, assent, and obstinate rejection (II:287).** The text uses the case of people who recognize the truth of a prophet while failing to accept it. Recognition, certain knowledge, and an assent involving *idhʿān* (submission or acquiescence) are not automatically coextensive. The source quotes scriptural examples of knowing yet denying through injustice or arrogance. The argument turns on **which formal character of assent constitutes faith**, not the crude claim that religious faith has nothing to do with knowledge.

**2. What earlier scholars meant by 'recognition' (II:287).** Some statements of earlier scholars use terms meaning knowledge or recognition instead of *taṣdīq*. The author seeks to interpret these in a qualified sense, as *ʿilm taṣdīqī* or knowledge accompanied by assent. This is an attempt to reconcile apparently different definitions rather than a proof that a contemporary reader can indiscriminately translate all these words as 'belief'.

**3. Knowledge as a psychic quality and the issue of voluntariness (II:288–290).** The source asks whether assent can be an act within the scope of choice if cognition is an acquired state or a quality of the soul rather than a freely produced bodily motion. A distinction is needed between **directly choosing to have a belief** and **voluntarily undertaking its prerequisites**, such as examining evidence, arranging premises, or directing attention. The lower printed discussion at II:289 explicitly contrasts these two possible replies.

**4. Logical assent versus religious assent (II:289–291).** The text explores whether religious assent is the same species as what logic places over against mere conception (*taṣawwur*). The existence of certainty without acceptance, and of knowledge that is not attained through an elective inquiry, make the identification difficult. It would be inaccurate to assume the disputants agree in advance on the sense of 'logical assent' before reading their qualifications.

**5. Angels, prophets and the conditions of faith (II:290–291).** One line tests definitions requiring acquisition and choice against the assent of angels to information conveyed to them and prophets to revelation given them. The question is whether all assent that theologians call faith must be preceded by the same elective evidentiary process; this is a conceptual objection inside the source, not a historical statement about what angels were empirically observed to do.

**6. External behavior and internally affirmed content (II:292).** Denying verbally and rejecting commands are distinguished from having cognition or even an inward recognition of the truth. The argumentative question is which combination of knowledge, assent, voluntary acceptance and confession satisfies *īmān*, and how this relates to the earlier legal-status debate at II:283–286.

### IV. Source-critical limitations and translational next steps

- **Not complete:** 6 original printed pages were surveyed, but large portions of the continuous upper Sanandajī text and almost all lower-register note clauses remain untranslated.
- **Not exclusively Wasīm:** the original title page advertises Wasīm's ḥāshiya **and** miscellaneous notes by other hands. The four quoted smaller-text passages on II:289–291 are marked **author unassigned** until an independent named or signed textual witness is located.
- **A language issue on II:287:** the lower printed register contains a Persian-language explanatory passage intertwined with the Arabic printed note. This requires a **separate Persian transcription and translation** with uncertain historical author; it must not be silently replaced with a generic Arabic summary.
- **Source variants:** no claim is made to having resolved all variant readings of the smaller, sometimes blurred Arabic printing or of the mixed Persian text.
- **Next deep original transcription:** continue at printed II:293 with the source's discussion of works, salvific faith and the relation between *dīn*, *īmān* and *islām*, while retrospectively completing lower notes from II:287–292 and identifying any actual Wasīm author markers.
