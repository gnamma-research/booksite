# Taqrīb al-Marām — Stage 28
## Source-first translation of divine pardon, repentance and intercession · printed II:269–274

**Primary source:** Complete user-supplied image-only two-volume *Taqrīb* and its separate lower registers; original numbered II:269–274 = physical PDF pages **564–569**. All six original page images were individually inspected. **Independent base-lemma witness:** original Cairo 1912 Taftāzānī *Tahdhīb* printed **pp.110–111**, directly inspected from the photographed original, not the previous bilingual working edition.

**Status:** 21 carefully delimited source-language and close-English selected clauses, plus two separately source-checked fragments of much longer lower-register commentary. This is **not** the exhaustive translation of six original printed pages. The lower-register historical writers remain **UNASSIGNED**; no excerpt is labelled Wasīm without an independent printed or other witness. Neither original printed page nor any complete source note has been critically certified.

### Original sequence and page boundaries

The **continuing argument at II:269** asks whether benefits from acts of obedience can affect the consequences of major sins, and how divine pardon relates to promise and threat. **A new original `فصل` opens partway down II:270** on God's pardon and forgiveness. It is not the same section as the earlier eternity-of-punishment argument. At II:270–271 the source considers universal-sounding verses on forgiveness alongside the exception for `shirk`; II:272–273 introduce reported Muʿtazilī restrictions and problems of specifying a general textual declaration. **II:274** connects the possibility of pardon with prophetic intercession. The source's marginal logic remains significantly longer than the upper selected passages.

### Critical analysis of argument structure — editorial, not additional original quotations

**1. Permission to pardon versus evidence that it actually happens.** The upper source asks whether God *may* remit punishment for a major sin without repentance, and separately adduces texts for *the occurrence* of pardon. The lower explanation on II:270 distinguishes a verse implying pardon after repentance from a verse concerning God's pardoning misdeeds without repeating the same restriction. `Jawāz` and `wuqūʿ` must not be translated interchangeably; nor should the ability to remit punishment be converted into an assertion that everyone is pardoned.

**2. General forgiveness and the `shirk` exception.** The texts `إن الله يغفر الذنوب جميعا` and `إن الله لا يغفر أن يشرك به` occur within an actual controversy over relative scope. The scholastic task is to ask which subjects and temporal conditions the apparently general expressions concern, including repentance, death in unbelief, and textual specification. An excerpted universal statement is not a completed doctrinal position until its exceptions and arguments have been read.

**3. Reported Muʿtazilī position and source of obligation.** The printed `وقالت المعتزلة يمتنع` heads a reported opposition to pardon of major sins without repentance, drawing on revealed threat texts and the claimed implications of God's truthfulness. That doctrine is a **report in Sanandajī**, not a verbatim independently sourced passage by a particular Muʿtazilī author. The response examines whether an apparent unconditional threat can be specified or remitted without thereby amounting to a divine lie.

**4. Threat, promise and contextual restriction.** The issue continues Stage 26's distinction between promise (`waʿd`) and punitive threat (`waʿīd`). It further concerns linguistic scope, universal quantification, qualification by repentant states and inferential justification for particular exceptions. The long lower registers on II:271–273 must be fully transcribed before claiming to have established the precise argument of every commentator.

**5. Intercession is not identical with a blanket erasure of punishment.** At II:274, the source invokes the Prophet's intercession for grave sinners, scriptural commands to seek forgiveness, and a theological debate over the kinds of benefits that intercession can bring: remission of punishment, preservation from entering it, or increase of rank in Paradise. The lower source explores the negative wording `فما تنفعهم شفاعة الشافعين` and whether this implies that the intercession helps another class of people. A full account must distinguish explicit scriptural quotation, report of a hadith and the commentator's inference; this project has not adjudicated the authenticity of individual traditions.

**6. No invented Wasīm attribution.** The source has multiple small-print registers and many anonymous `qawl` anchors. The II:270 and II:274 extracts are attached as **margins with UNASSIGNED writer** in the NAMA reader. The absence of positive individual attribution is a genuine critical-edition problem, not permission to count them as completed `Ḥāshiyat al-Muḥākamāt` units.
