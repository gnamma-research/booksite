### 1. Important independently resolved readings at II:222 (earlier Stage 19)

The parenthesized lemma recorded in Stage 19 as ⟦ومعارضته؟ / ومعاضدته؟⟧ has now been decisively read **ومعاونته** in *both* the enlarged Sanandajī image II:222 (original physical scan 517) and the independent Cairo 1912 *Tahdhīb*, printed Arabic p.97. Its correct translation is **“and assisting it [reason]”**. The previous two speculative alternatives are withdrawn. These are two physically distinct printed witnesses, not merely the same OCR repeated.

The **next** printed lemma is **ورفع الاحتمال** — “and removing uncertainty / conceivable doubt” — visible in the source originals. A separate prior *working* Tahdhīb parallel rendering had instead supplied **ورفع الإشكال** (“removing perplexity”). The printed image governs here; the working copy's reading should be treated as a transcription discrepancy, **not** as an independently established manuscript variant. The separate ongoing Tahdhīb matn project has not been edited as part of this Sanandajī project.

**Short matched lemma across both prints (Taftāzānī Cairo p.97 / Sanandajī II:222):**

> كإفاضة العقل ومعاونته ورفع الاحتمال وبيان المبهم

**Translation:** “Such as enriching the intellect, assisting it, removing uncertainty, and clarifying what is obscure.”

**Interpretation:** The order matters: reason can be developed; it can be helped where its unaided capacities are insufficient; uncertainty may be removed; and matters inaccessible or opaque are made clear. The source is not asserting that revelation inherently opposes reason.

### 3. Source argument: why a miracle can establish certainty despite imagined alternative causes

**The analogy of the king (II:224–225).** The base text compares a claimed prophetic mission to a person who publicly says that the proof he is the king's messenger is the king's rising from the throne three times; when the king does so, ordinary compelling knowledge results. The point is not that the occurrence is metaphysically immune to every verbally imaginable alternative. The Sanandajī printed explanation enumerates possible objections concerning a hidden power of the claimant, an extraordinary celestial configuration, a spirit or another extraordinary circumstance. These are *hypotheses*, not author-endorsed causal explanations.

**Certainty versus a mere possibility (II:225–226).** The source's crucial lemma is **العقلية لا تنافي العلوم القطعية العادية** — *rationally imaginable possibilities do not conflict with certain knowledge in the customary order*. The gloss adds the distinction between a real causal rival and a merely abstract hypothesis. It also stipulates that the challenge has resisted counter-performance even though opponents have a strong motivation to answer it. This is why **مع فرط الاهتمام** is not an incidental rhetorical embellishment; it qualifies the epistemic setting.

**God alone as effective agent in the specified kind of event (II:226).** The argument then narrows the considered miracle to a type for which no independently operating created agent is admitted. This should not be hastily generalized into the thesis that created powers have no efficacy in every ordinary situation; the present claim concerns the miracle's probative character. The commentary illustrates this with resurrection of the dead, the moon's splitting, uprooting of trees and the greeting of stones.

**Avoiding a circular proof (II:227).** The text denies that the evidentiary force of the miracle must presuppose an already established theological proposition that the sender's reported attestation is truthful. Otherwise the prophet's truth would have to be proved by a divine report whose truth was itself established only by the prophet, producing a vicious circle. The lower gloss elaborates **three** inferential moments and distinguishes their logical order.

**Actual original chapter transition (II:228).** The upper source now begins a distinct `فصل في نبوة نبينا`. It applies the general sign/reliability analysis to Muḥammad: a public prophetic claim, the Qurʾān as the offered extraordinary sign, eloquence in Arabic, and the intensity of opposition and competition among his contemporary Arab audience. The text reports the charge of sorcery; that is an opponent's allegation, not the narrator's adoption of it.

### 5. Cross-witness source wording of the embedded matn, Cairo 1912 p.98 (selected spans, NOT Sanandajī's prose)

> وطريق ثبوتها المعجزة وهي أمر خارق للعادة مقرون بالتحدي مع عدم المعارضة ... فإن الاحتمالات العقلية لا تنافي العلوم القطعية العادية على أن الكلام فيما ثبت العجز عن معارضته مع فرط الاهتمام وأنه شيء لا مؤثر فيه إلا الله ... وأن حصول التصديق لا يتوقف على كونه غرضا ولا كون الباعث صادقا في إخباره ليدور بناء على أنه سمعي

**Close English of the actually quoted spans:** “The way [prophethood] is established is the miracle: an occurrence contrary to ordinary custom, joined to a challenge and without successful counter-performance. … Rationally imaginable alternatives do not conflict with certain knowledge acquired in the customary order. The discussion concerns a sign which opponents have proved unable to match, despite their utmost efforts, and which can be effected by none but God. … Nor does the acquisition of certitude depend on making this confirmation a purpose [of the divine act], or on already assuming that the sender speaks truthfully in His report—which would make the proof circular because it would then rest on revealed knowledge.”

**Comparison note:** This paragraph reproduces **selected contiguous segments of the independent Cairo matn**, with ellipses marking intervening original words. Sanandajī's commentary occupies additional running text between the separately parenthesized base-text lemmata on printed II:224–228. The independent Cairo print is used *only as a collation witness* for those quotations; it is not edited into the separate canonical Tahdhīb translation project.
