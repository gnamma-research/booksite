# Sanandajī–Wasīm: Stage 27 original-source critical working edition
## The duration of Paradise and the Fire, original II:264–268

**Primary source:** user-uploaded complete scanned two-volume *Taqrīb al-Marām* with lower printed *Ḥāshiyat al-Muḥākamāt* and further marginalia. Original printed II:264–268 corresponds to physical PDF **559–563**; five source scans inspected directly. A separate photographed original **Cairo 1912 *Tahdhīb* printed pp.109–110** is an independent **base-lemma control**, NOT a source for inventing Sanandajī's explanations or any Wasīm note.

**Source boundaries:** an authentic original `فصل` opens on II:264. The page has a short **mixed matn/sharḥ upper register** and a far longer **lower-register dialectic**. Only the upper is continuously transcribed below as a provisional first pass; its words also appear in shorter individually layer-separated source records. The lower register runs across several pages. **This stage does not critically certify any complete printed page**.

### I. Continuous short upper printed register, II:264 (first-pass, physical PDF 559)

**Arabic (upper printing, including the embedded parentheses / explanation, with editorial punctuation):**

فصل لا خلاف بين المسلمين في خلود من دخل الجنة ولا في خلود الكافر في النار سوى الكافر حكما كأطفال المشركين فهم عند بعضهم لا يعذبون بالنار بل خدم أهل الجنة لأن تعذيب من لا جرم له ظلم ولقوله تعالى ولا تزر وازرة وزر أخرى ولا تجزون إلا ما كنتم تعملون

**Close English:**
Section. Muslims do not dispute the continuing residence of whoever enters Paradise, nor the abiding of an unbeliever in the Fire—except for those treated as unbelievers in legal classification, such as the children of polytheists. According to some, these children are not punished in the Fire but become attendants of the people of Paradise, since punishing one who has committed no wrong would be unjust. He cites the scriptural declarations that no bearer bears another's burden and that people are recompensed only for what they have done.

**Source-critical caution:** the source has **عند بعضهم** (‘according to some’) before the claim that the children are not punished in the Fire. No definitive endorsement or claim of universal consensus about the children of polytheists may be inferred from this mixed-voice passage. The shorter units below separate the source's quoted matn from Sanandajī's explanation. The image has not undergone independent second diplomatic proof or another-witness margin comparison.

### III. Separate lower-register source excerpts — historical writer UNASSIGNED

#### SAN27.G.II264.01 — Khulūd after entry, not a claim about immediate admission
**Printed II:264, original PDF 559.** Extended source-read opening of one long marginal note; writer UNASSIGNED, entire remainder untranslated and second independent proof outstanding.
**Arabic:** وهذا السر وهو النفع في العاجل (قوله لا خلاف في خلود من دخل الجنة) أي خلود من دخل فيها وإنما قال ذلك ولم يقل في خلود المؤمن مع أنه مقتضى المقابل بما بعده لما يأتي أن المؤمن العاصي قد يدخل النار فخلوده في الجنة إنما يكون بعد دخولها لا مطلقا
**Translation:** The point is benefit in this present life. ‘Regarding his words: there is no disagreement over the abiding of whoever enters Paradise’: that is, the abiding of anyone who actually enters it. He used this formulation rather than saying ‘the believer's abiding’—although that might be expected as the counterpart to what follows—because, as will emerge, a sinful believer may first enter the Fire. His abiding in Paradise therefore obtains after entry, not absolutely and without qualification.

#### SAN27.G.II268.01 — Whether eternal khulūd and time-limited abiding can share a meaning
**Printed II:268, original PDF 563.** Selected part of a longer marginal linguistic argument about khulūd and taʾbīd. Writer UNASSIGNED, no complete-note/second-proof certification.
**Arabic:** حقيقيا أو مجازيا لا يجوز شيئا من الأمرين إذ هو معنى واحد مطلق يصدق في حق الكفار مع التأبيد وفي حق الفساق مع الانقطاع ولا نسلم أن التأبيد في نحو قوله خالدين فيها أبدا تأكيد بل يجوز أن يكون تقييدا
**Translation:** Whether literally or figuratively, neither of the proposed alternatives follows, since there is a single unrestricted meaning that can apply to unbelievers with endless duration and to sinful persons with eventual cessation. We do not concede that the expression of permanence in a statement such as ‘abiding therein for ever’ is merely an emphasis; it could instead be a restriction.

### IV. Philosophical, theological and textual apparatus

**1. Scope of the original consensus statement.** The lemma expressly says `من دخل الجنة` (“whoever **has entered** Paradise”), not `من كان مؤمناً` (“whoever was a believer”). The lower gloss on II:264 argues that some believers can have an earlier period of punishment; the condition of *prior entry* therefore matters. A claim about ultimate destination is not identical to a claim about the chronological order of experiences in the hereafter.

**2. Children of polytheists — reported positions, not imposed orthodoxy.** The text excludes one class of persons legally treated as unbelievers from a simple claim about unbelievers' eternal punishment. The embedded base-text phrase and Sanandajī's explanatory `عند بعضهم` must remain distinct. The ethical and scriptural argument appeals to the rule that no one bears another's burden. Neither the source nor this translation proves independently which transmitted reports about children's status are authentic.

**3. The great sinner dying as a believer.** The II:265 upper text distinguishes a reported position that one who dies without repenting of a grave sin remains in the Fire from the alternate possibilities of pardon without punishment or release after a period of punishment. The decisive terms are `فعندهم` versus `وعندنا`, `مخلد` versus `بل يعفى عنه` and `أو يخرج من النار بعد حين`. Treating these as the same doctrine because all mention the Fire would efface the scholastic disagreement.

**4. Promise, rational desert and punishment are independent dimensions.** At II:266 the source describes a reward established **by divine promise in 'our' account** and **by reason in the opponents' account**. This continues II:261–263 and has direct bearing on whether a prior major sin permanently cancels all earned recompense. The argument from one drink of wine after a long life of obedience is a thought experiment concerning proportionality and duration, not a literal quantitative rule for every punishment.

**5. A central semantic dispute: *khulūd* versus *taʾbīd*.** The lower source at II:268 asks whether `khulūd` under one unrestricted meaning might describe continued dwelling even when a qualifying phrase specifies endlessness. It treats `خالدين فيها أبداً` (‘abiding therein for ever’) as potentially **restrictive**, rather than merely **emphatic**. Such a lexical strategy must be evaluated in the complete adjacent rebuttal and cannot be simply applied to every occurrence of Qurʾānic `khulūd` without examining textual context.

**6. Texts, analogy and polemical attribution.** The original source invokes the intentional killing of a believer and multiple threats of punishment. The position labelled `المشهور من مذهب المعتزلة` is a *reported* doxographic attribution; it is not certified by quoting a primary Muʿtazilī writer. An objection about analogical inference in revealed texts is likewise reported as part of the scholastic exchange, not independently established as a universal methodological norm.
