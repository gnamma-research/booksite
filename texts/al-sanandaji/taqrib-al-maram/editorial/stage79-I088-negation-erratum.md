# Sanandajī — Stage 79 checked erratum: I:88 lower ḥāshiya

**Source:** Original 1318 AH Būlāq printing, Volume I printed p.88, combined PDF physical p.101. Same original checksum as above.

## Reversal of a negation/causal clause

Earlier working Arabic typed: **«لا أن عدمه ضروري»**.

Printed Arabic on the image: **«لأن عدمه ضروري»**.

Earlier English: “not that its nonexistence is necessary.”

**Corrected English:** “because its nonexistence is necessary.”

The full relevant phrase is:

> فإذا قيل إن الممتنع ممكن فمعناه هو أن وجوده ليس بضروري لأن عدمه ضروري غاية الأمر أن سلب ضرورة الوجود قد يصدق بما عدمه ضروري فتفطن

> Thus, when someone says that the impossible is possible [in this one-sided, general sense], the point is that its existence is not necessary, *because* its nonexistence is necessary. At most, the denial of the necessity of existence can apply to something whose nonexistence is necessary. Attend carefully.

The distinction is essential to the discussion of *imkān ʿāmm* (general possibility) versus *imkān khāṣṣ* (specific possibility): the earlier negative paraphrase inverted the example. The corrected reading is sourced to the user's own image and is **not** presented as a variant from an independently collated manuscript. The rest of I:88 retains provisional source status; correcting one locus does not certify the entire page.
