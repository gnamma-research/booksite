## I086.upper.01 · upper

الواجب) أي الموصوف بالوجوب بالذات (واجب الوجود لذاته) كما إذا أخذ الوجود محمولا (وهو الله تعالى أو) واجب الوجود (لشيء آخر) هو الموضوع كما إذا أخذ رابطة بين المحمول والموضوع (كزوجية الأربعة) فإنها لازمة ماهية الأربعة وواجبة الوجود لها بالنظر إلى نفسها من غير احتياج إلى أمر آخر

—“the necessary,” that is, what is qualified by necessity intrinsically, “is necessarily existent in itself,” as when existence is taken as a predicate—“and this is God, exalted is He—or” necessarily existent “for another thing,” namely the subject, as when existence is taken as a copula between predicate and subject. An example is “the evenness of four”: it is inseparable from the quiddity of four and necessarily belongs to it when four is considered in itself, without need of anything else.

## I086.upper.02 · upper

(و) الموصوف بالذاتي (من الممتنع) أي الموصوف بالامتناع الذاتي هو إما (ممتنع الوجود لذاته) كما إذا أخذ الوجود محمولا (كشريك الباري أو) ممتنع الوجود (لشيء آخر) هو الموضوع كما إذا أخذ رابطة بين الموضوع والمحمول (كفردية الأربعة والموصوف بالغيري) من الواجب (كممكن هو واجب الوجود

“And” that which is intrinsically qualified “among the impossible”—that is, what is intrinsically impossible—is either “impossible in its own existence,” as when existence is taken as the predicate, “like a partner to the Creator,” or impossible in its existence “for another thing,” namely the subject, as when existence is taken as a copula between subject and predicate, “like the oddness of four. What is qualified as extrinsic” among the necessary is “like a possible thing that is necessarily existent—”

## I086.ruled.01 · ruled

(قوله لذاته) ليست اللام هنا وفي قوله لشيء آخر للتعليل فتكون متعلقة بالامتناع بل لام الإضافة تفيد إضافة الوجود إلى مدخولها اه منه «مد ظله»

On “In itself” (li-dhātihi). The lām here, and in “for another thing” (li-shayʾ ākhar), is not causal, so as to qualify impossibility. Rather, it is the lām of attribution: it attributes existence to the expression following it. End. [Signed:] “From him”—“may his protection endure.”

## I086.lower.01 · lower

الموضوع كوجود زيد مثلا حين وجوده وعدم بكر حين عدمه ووجود القيام لزيد كذلك وعدم القعود لبكر كذلك (قال واجب الوجود لذاته) يحتمل أن تكون اللام صلة الوجود للاختصاص لا للتعليل وكذا في المعطوف بقوله أو لشيء آخر فيكون المقصود إفادة تعميم الوجوب الذاتي على البسيط والمركب ويحتمل أن تكون اللام فيهما صلة الوجوب للتعليل فيكون المقصود إفادة التعميم مع التصريح بكون الوجوب فيهما ذاتيا فتأمل فإنه دقيق

—the subject: for example, Zayd’s existence while he exists, Bakr’s nonexistence while he does not exist, the presence of standing in Zayd in the same way, and the absence of sitting from Bakr in the same way. He says, “Necessarily existent in itself.” The lām may qualify “existence,” expressing specific attribution rather than causation; the same applies to the coordinated expression “or for another thing.” The purpose would then be to extend intrinsic necessity to both simple and compound existential propositions. Alternatively, in both places the lām may qualify “necessity” causally; the purpose would then be that generalization together with an explicit statement that the necessity in both is intrinsic. Reflect, for this is subtle.

Incoming connection: I085.lower.03.

Editorial note: The ruled note rejects a causal reading of the lām; the long lower gloss entertains it as an alternative. The disagreement is retained.

## I087.upper.01 · upper

—“while it exists; and” among the impossible, like a possible thing that is “impossible in existence while it does not exist.” Possibility in the sense just stated is specific possibility, opposed to intrinsic necessity and intrinsic impossibility. “It may be taken to mean the negation of the necessity of existence,” without considering the side of nonexistence. It then opposes necessity and includes specific possibility and impossibility. “Or” it may mean the negation of the necessity “of nonexistence,” without considering the side of existence. It then opposes impossibility and includes specific possibility and necessity. “It is therefore called general possibility, since it includes specific possibility and the necessity of the other side. ## I087.lower.01 · lower

لذاته أو لشيء آخر وكذا قوله ممتنع الوجود (قوله حين وجوده وحين عدمه) أشار به إلى أن يكون الأول واجبا بالغير والثاني ممتنعا به إنما يكون إذا لم يكن الوجوب والامتناع فيهما لذات الموضوع فإنهما لما اعتبر كونهما حين الوجود والعدم للموضوع فهما لعلة أخرى غير ذات الموضوع فتأمل

—in itself or for another thing; the same applies to his expression “impossible in existence.” On “While it exists” and “while it does not exist.” By this he indicates that the first is necessary through another and the second impossible through another only when the necessity and impossibility do not arise from the subject’s essence. Since the two are considered as obtaining at the time of the subject’s existence and nonexistence, they arise from a cause other than the subject’s essence. Reflect.

## I087.lower.02 · lower

(قوله المقابل للوجوب والامتناع) وذلك لأن بينهما انفصالا حقيقيا لأن كل مفهوم إما أن يكون ضروري الوجود أو لا والثاني إما أن يكون ضروري العدم أو لا فالثلاثة لا تجتمع ولا ترتفع والاعتراض بضروري الوجود والعدم غير وارد لأنه مفهوم إذا لاحظه العقل لم يكن إلا ضروري العدم فافهم والتقييد لذاته أنه لا ينفك من الوجوب والامتناع بالغير كما سبق قال في شرح المقاصد الإمكان ينفك من الوجوب والامتناع بالغير لكن بحسب التعقل بأن لا يلاحظ الماهية ولا علتها وجودا أو عدما لا بحسب التحقق اه

On “Opposed to necessity and impossibility.” This is because the division between them is a genuine exclusive and exhaustive disjunction. Every concept either has necessary existence or does not; in the latter case it either has necessary nonexistence or does not. The three therefore neither coexist nor all fail to obtain. The objection from something whose existence and nonexistence are both necessary does not apply, because, when the intellect considers that concept, it can only be necessarily nonexistent. Understand. The qualification “intrinsically” is used because possibility is not separated from necessity or impossibility through another, as explained previously. He says in Sharḥ al-Maqāṣid: “Possibility can be separated from necessity and impossibility through another, but in conception: neither the quiddity nor its cause is considered as existent or nonexistent. It cannot be separated from them in actual realization.” End.
