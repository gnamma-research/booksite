## I021.lower.02 · printed I:21 · lower register

**Arabic — selected printed phrases**

> … (قوله اسم طائفة من الحكماء إلخ) نقل عن بعض الفضلاء ما حاصله أن قوماً من الناس يظنون أن السوفسطائية … […] والعنادية القائلون بأنه ما من قضية إلا ولها معارضة … […] والعندية القائلون بأن مذهب كل قوم حق بالقياس إليه باطل بالقياس إلى نقيضه، وليس في نفس الأمر حق. […]

**English — rendering only these retained clauses**

> … On his words “the name of a group of philosophers,” and so forth: a report is cited from certain learned persons, to the effect that some people suppose that the Sophists … [intervening account omitted]. […] The “obstinate” [ʿinādiyyah] hold that there is no proposition without an opposing proposition. […] The “relativists” [ʿindiyyah] hold that the doctrine of each group is true relative to that group and false relative to its opposite, while there is no truth in the thing itself. […]

**Source scope:** Three selected expressions in the second lower-register topic on printed I:21, following the separate teacher-guidance gloss already entered as I021.lower.01. The report is a historical attribution, not a translator endorsement of a school taxonomy.

**Untranslated:** The report’s introductory narrative, its terminology and several connectors remain untranslated; the final line continues into printed I:22. The marginal voice remains unassigned. The quoted upper-register characterization of sophists on I021.upper.02 is not re-entered.

**Open reading:** The classification words are legible in the scan; a compact portion of the reported description between the opening and the named types is excluded. The precise extent of “ʿindiyyah” attribution requires second proof.

## I022.lower.02 · printed I:22 · lower register

**Arabic — selected printed phrases**

> … (قوله قدح في الحسيات إلخ) أي فقط وحصر المبادئ الأولى في البديهيات ... […] (قوله بأن حكم الحس إلخ) يعني أن الحس لا حكم له في الكليات، وهو ظاهر، ولا وثوق عليه في الجزئيات لتحقق غلطه فيها كثيراً. […] (قوله من قدح في الأوليات إلخ) أي دون الحسيات، وإن قالوا إن البديهيات فرع الحسيات، لأن الإنسان إنما يتنبه لها بعد الإحساس بالجزئيات ... […] وذلك لأنه لا يلزم من القدح في الفرع القدح في الأصل. […]

**English — rendering only these retained clauses**

> … On his words “he impugned sensible judgments,” and so forth: that is, these judgments alone, while restricting the first principles to immediate intellectual certainties … [further qualification omitted]. […] On “because the senses judge,” and so forth: sense has no judgment concerning universals, as is evident; nor can it be relied upon unconditionally with regard to particulars, since its mistakes in them occur frequently. […] On “those who impugned the primary propositions,” and so forth: that is, while not impugning sensible judgments—even if they say that primary intellectual judgments branch from sensible judgments, because a human becomes attentive to them only after sensing particulars … [intervening qualifications omitted]. […] For impugning what derives from a principle does not entail impugning the principle itself. […]

**Source scope:** Located lower-register commentary beginning with the gloss on “impugning sensibles” and continuing to the gloss on impugning primary judgments; printed I:22. The main upper-register argument about noncontradiction is a distinct record and not silently inserted here.

**Untranslated:** Further phrases about the priority of sensible cases and about the kind of dependence between intellectual first principles and sensation remain to be collated. Initial lower lines on sceptics and the quoted passage in the ruled register are not repeated.

**Open reading:** The abbreviated phrase after the first gloss is omitted, not silently expanded; one line about attention to similarities and differences is omitted. A second, independent word-by-word comparison remains necessary.

## I025.lower.02 · printed I:25 · lower register

**Arabic — selected printed phrases**

> … (قوله الفعل الآخر هنا إفادة العلم إلخ) أقول الفعل الآخر في التوليد … من فاعل واحد كما هو ظاهر. […] وإفادة العلم وإن سلّم كونه فعلاً فهو فعل النظر لا فعل فاعله وهو العبد، فليتأمل. […] (قوله من المبدأ الفياض إلخ) أرادوا به العقل الفعال المنتقش بصور الكائنات ... […] وزعموا أن الروح والكتاب المبين على لسان الشرع عبارتان عنه ... […] وهذا مبني على سلب الاختيار منه تعالى ... […]

**English — rendering only these retained clauses**

> … On his words “the other act here is imparting knowledge,” and so forth: I say that the other act in generation … [an intervening specification of the causal relation is omitted] … [proceeds] from one agent, as is evident. […] As for imparting knowledge, even if it is conceded to be an act, it is an act of inquiry, not an act of the one who performs inquiry, namely the servant; so reflect. […] On his words “from the effusive principle,” and so forth: what they mean is the Active Intellect, in which the forms of things that come into being are impressed … [qualifications omitted]. […] They have also claimed that “the Spirit” and “the Clear Book,” as expressed in the language of revelation, are two names for it … [the account is reported, not endorsed]. […] This is grounded upon denying the Exalted One freedom of choice … [the closing dialectical qualification is omitted]. […]

**Source scope:** Three source-located discussions in the second lower commentary record on I:25. This differs from, but presupposes, the I025.lower.01 hand–key example, which is already transcribed separately. It is not a retranslation of the ruled note or upper register.

**Untranslated:** The exact proposed constraint between generated acts has not been completed; the active-intellect identification and late qualifications about divine choice contain compressed phrases; English only translates the retained source excerpts.

**Open reading:** The expression between “the other act in generation” and “from one agent” is unread securely in this pass and is deliberately removed rather than reconstructed. The Arabic final clause is locatable but not fully transcribed.
