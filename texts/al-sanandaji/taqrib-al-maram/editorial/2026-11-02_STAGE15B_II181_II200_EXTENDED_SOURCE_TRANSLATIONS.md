## A. II:186 — approval of divine decree and the moral character of the decreed (original PDF p.481)

**Arabic, connected upper-source selection, punctuation editorial:**

فإن قيل لو كان الكفر بقضاء الله تعالى لوجب الرضا به لأن الرضا بالقضاء واجب واللازم باطل لأن الرضا بالكفر كفر قلنا الكفر مقضي لا قضاء، والرضا إنما يجب بالقضاء لا بالمقضي. فإن قيل الرضا بالقضاء يستلزم الرضا بالمقضي قلنا إنما يستلزمه من حيث أنه مقضي لا من حيث ذاته، والرضا بالكفر من حيث ذاته كفر لا من حيث أنه مقضي.

**Close English:**

“If it is said: Were unbelief to occur by God's decree, it would be obligatory to approve it, since approval of God's decree is obligatory. Yet the consequent is false, since approval of unbelief is itself unbelief. We reply: unbelief is something decreed, not the act of decreeing; and approval is obligatory with regard to the decree, not with regard to what has been decreed. If it is said that approving the decree entails approving what has been decreed, we reply: this follows only insofar as the latter is decreed, not insofar as it is considered in itself. Approval of unbelief considered in itself is unbelief, but not approval of it in the respect of its being decreed.”

**Logical notes:** The key contrast is `قضاء` (decree) versus `مقضي` (what is decreed), then two respects of the object: `من حيث أنه مقضي` versus `من حيث ذاته`. The passage denies an illicit transfer of moral approval between these respects. It does not claim that the factual event of unbelief is good in itself. The words belong to a dialectical objection and answer, not an unqualified admission of every claim preceding the objection. Parenthesized lemma boundaries await an independent source-layer pass.

## B. II:192 — the original opening of the goodness/badness section (physical PDF p.487)

**Source chapter heading:** «فصل في الحسن والقبح» — *A chapter on goodness and badness*.

**First connected upper-source selection:**

قد اشتهر أنهما عند الأشاعرة غير عقليان وعند المعتزلة عقليان، وليس النزاع في الحسن والقبح بمعنى صفة الكمال والنقص كالعلم والجهل، ولا بمعنى الملاءمة للغرض وعدمها كالعدل والظلم.

**English:**

“It is well known that, according to the Ashʿarīs, they are not rationally determined, whereas according to the Muʿtazilites they are rationally determined. The disagreement about goodness and badness is not over their meaning as a quality of perfection or deficiency, as in knowledge and ignorance, nor over suitability or unsuitability for a purpose, as in justice and injustice.”

**Second selected upper-source sequence, with skipped original connective words indicated by ellipsis:**

... وإنما النزاع فيهما بمعنى استحقاق الفاعل عند الله المدح والذم عاجلا والثواب والعقاب.

**English:**

“... Rather, the disagreement concerns the agent's deserving, before God, praise or blame in the immediate order and reward or punishment.”

**Interpretive control:** The text differentiates (1) perfective/defective predicates, (2) relation to an aim or interest, and (3) divine praise/blame and reward/punishment. The first two can be intelligible rationally without conceding the Muʿtazilī position on the third. Treating the whole controversy simply as whether reason knows *anything* about good and bad badly distorts the source.

## C. II:197 — the following printed chapter (physical PDF p.492)

**Source:** «فصل في التكليف بما لا يطاق» — *A chapter on obligating what cannot be performed*.

**Original continuation (direct source selection):**

والخلاف في جوازه وعدمه من فروع الخلاف في الحسن والقبح.

**English:** “The disagreement over its permissibility or impermissibility is one of the branches of the disagreement concerning goodness and badness.”

**Argumentative consequence:** A theory of `taklīf` here depends on whether impossibility itself yields a rational moral prohibition, not solely on physical capacity. The source's later pages test distinct senses of impossibility and the difference between conceivability of a prescribed act and power to perform it. The lower notes must be collated to determine precisely what each author grants or denies.
