### I073.lower.01 — Three different senses of quiddity “taken with a condition”

**Arabic anchors:** «أبو علي في الشفاء وخلصه المحقق في شرح الإشارات انتهى» · «ثم ذكر المصنف رحمه الله أن فيه مواضع بحث» · «ما سبق أوائل الفصل هي الاعتبارات المشهورة للماهية بين المتأخرين» · «وأما الشيخ الرئيس فقد يستفاد من كلامه المنقول اعتبارات غير ما اشتهر» · «الماهية المأخوذة بشرط لا شيء هي ما يتصور معناها وحده».

**English:** —[This account is attributed to] Abū ʿAlī [Ibn Sīnā] in the *Shifāʾ*, and [the investigator] summed it up in his commentary on the *Ishārāt*. End of citation. The author, may God have mercy on him, then said that there are points in it admitting discussion. We have already indicated some of them; consult what precedes. With that established, know that the considerations stated at the beginning of the chapter are the **familiar classifications of quiddity among later scholars**. But one may infer from the Chief Master's quoted wording considerations other than those conventionally reported. In one sense, *quiddity taken on condition of nothing* (*bi-sharṭ lā shayʾ*) is the meaning conceived **by itself alone**. If something accompanies it, the accompanying thing is regarded as external to it, not included within it. The meaning under this consideration cannot be predicated of the composite whole once another thing has been conjoined to it; rather, it will be a **part** or **material element** of that whole.

**Join:** incoming I072.lower.final. The whole of printed I:73 is marginal continuation; there is **no newly printed principal upper text** on this page.

### I073.lower.02 — “Taken with something” and “taken without a condition”

**Arabic anchors:** «والماهية المأخوذة بشرط شيء بقرينة تقابلها بما سبق ما يتصور معناها لا وحده بحيث لو قارنها شيء اعتبر داخلا فيها» · «فيكون ذلك المعنى عين المجموع المركب حال المقارنة» · «والمأخوذة لا بشرط شيء ما يتصور معناها لا وحده» · «ولا عدم دخوله فيكون مبهما محتملا للقسمة على المجموع حال المقارنة».

**English:** Quiddity *taken on condition of something* (*bi-sharṭ shayʾ*), understood in contrast to the preceding case, is conceived so that the accompanying qualification is **counted within it**; the original meaning is then treated as the composite whole in the circumstance of conjunction. By contrast, quiddity *taken without condition* (*lā bi-sharṭ*) is not conceived with the stipulation that the accompanying thing be included, nor with the stipulation that it be excluded. Its meaning remains **unspecified as regards inclusion**, capable of being predicated of the whole in which another qualification is present, and not defined as an intrinsically separate part of that whole. The distinction is a distinction in how the meaning is considered, not proof of additional externally existing constituents.

**Reading caution:** The long marginal argument includes subtle changes between *predicated of the whole* and *a component of the whole*. English has been kept tied to those conceptual alternatives. Certain compressed words between the three definitions need a second line-level comparison.

### I073.lower.03 — Animal, human, and the genus/species illustration

**Arabic anchors:** «إذا اعتبر المعنى الأول يكون جزءا للمركب الذي هو الإنسان مثلا» · «وإذا اعتبر المعنى الثاني يكون نوعا هو الإنسان مثلا» · «وإذا اعتبر بالمعنى الثالث يكون جنسا مقولا على الإنسان وغيره» · «الحقائق المختلفة المشتركة في الحيوانية».

**English:** For example, if “animal” is considered in the **first** sense, it is a part of the composite that is a human being. Considered in the **second** sense, it may be the species human [under the composite consideration]. Considered in the **third** sense, it is a **genus predicable of the human being and of others**, the different realities that share animality. On this usage, the quiddity taken under a restrictive condition may be a component of the composite, while what is taken without that condition can be a genus shared among individuals. Yet the conclusion must not be generalized to every logical combination without checking which meaning of “part,” “species” and “genus” is actually under discussion.

### I073.lower.04 — Matter, form, and why the alleged genus is not automatically a real part

**Arabic anchors:** «تكون الماهية المأخوذة بشرط لا شيء المسماة بالمخلوطة عبارة عن المركب من الماهية وما يقارنها» · «فتكون عين الأفراد من غير لزوم ما سبق من الاعتراض» · «ولا احتياج إلى ما سبق من المصنف من أن جعل النوع عبارة عن المأخوذ بشرط شيء مسامحة» · «لأن الماهية بالنسبة إلى الأنواع دونها بالنسبة إلى الأصناف والأشخاص».

**English:** If quiddity is taken with or apart from conditions in the different senses just described, the result can be described as the composite of a meaning with its accompanying qualifications, or as the individual things of which that meaning is said. The familiar objection—that the compound would then need a second constituent in addition to its asserted quiddity—does not follow automatically. Nor is there always a need to say, as the author previously did, that calling the species “the quiddity taken on condition of something” is merely a concession of usage. The logical status of the same meaning changes with the **level of comparison**: it may function as genus relative to what falls below it and as species relative to what falls above it, and when it is considered in relation to kinds, varieties and individual persons, the relations change accordingly. The commentator is distinguishing the conceptual function of these classifications rather than supposing that each name establishes an independent part outside the mind.

**Uncertain:** The compressed phrase naming the “mixed” or “conditioned” quiddity requires visual proof before its Arabic wording is fixed; the above is a working conceptual rendering, not a silently reconstructed quotation.

### I073.lower.05 — Universal predication, qualification, and the wording of the *Maqāṣid*

**Arabic anchors:** «من حيث المفهوم متنافية ومن حيث الصدق أعم منها» · «أن جعل غير المبهم من أقسام هذه الماهية» · «فمعنى قول المصنف في شرح المقاصد أن معنى ذلك على ما من كون الأول أعم من الثاني» · «لا بشرط أن يكون معه شيء إنما يكون جنسا محمولا».

**English:** Considered **as meanings**, some of these classifications stand in opposition; considered **in respect of predication**, one can be more general than another. The lower commentator accordingly questions the division that places an unrestricted meaning and its qualified instances within a single univocal taxonomy. A phrase in *Sharḥ al-Maqāṣid* that calls the first sense broader than the second should not be interpreted without specifying whether “broader” concerns the **concept** or the **things of which it is true**. Something counted as a genus in a predication may not, for that reason alone, be a real constituent of the external object. In particular, *not stipulating that something must accompany it* differs from stipulating that nothing accompanies it. The commentary continues into I:74 with a qualification about what may count as a genuine constituent.

### I074.upper.01 — Real composition implies simplicity, without a regress of actual parts

**Arabic anchors:** «لا جزء لها كالنقطة» · «وأما كيفية تقابلها كالجسم» · «ولا يخفى وجود الماهية المركبة ويلزم منه وجود البسيطة» · «(ولا بد من الانتهاء إلى البسيطة)» · «لأن الواحد مبدأ المتعدد».

**English:** —[A simple quiddity] has no parts, as in the case of a point; other examples of what stands opposed to it are treated according to the kind of composition meant, such as body. The existence of **composite quiddities** is not in dispute, and the existence of composites entails that there must also be **simple quiddities**. “There must be an end at something simple.” For unity is the principle of multiplicity: if every one were itself a multitude of parts, whether finite or infinite, and every supposed constituent were in turn divisible without terminus, the account of an actually existing composite would fail to provide the basic unities on which the composition depends. The point is not merely that the intellect can divide a concept, but that the analysis of an actual composite cannot proceed without end in the way alleged.

**Incoming join:** I072.upper.01, not I073: the entire intervening I:73 is printed marginal commentary.

### I074.upper.02 — Mutual dependence of the constituents of a real composite

**Arabic anchors:** «(واحتياج بعض الأجزاء إلى بعض في المركب الحقيقي ضروري)» · «إذ لو استغنى كل من الأجزاء عن الآخر لم يحصل منها».

**English:** “In a real composite it is necessary that some of its parts depend on others.” If each component were independent of every other one, no **single genuine compound** would arise from their aggregation. The argument proceeds on I:75 by distinguishing unity produced by a real form from the merely conceived unity of an aggregate.

**Outgoing join:** I075.upper.01.

### I074.lower.01 — The end of the I:73 classification, and the danger of moving between senses

**Arabic anchors:** «واتحاده معا فلو اعتبر فيه التغاير فقط أو الاتحاد فقط كان الأول جزءا والثاني نوعا لا جنسا» · «وظاهر عبارة الشارح» · «لم يخف أنه استثناء من جميع ما سبق أن الماهية سواء على القول المتصور أو على اصطلاح الشيخ».

**English:** —[The relevant meaning admits] both difference and identity together. If only **difference** were considered it would be a **part**; if only **identity** were considered it would be a **species**, not a genus. The outward wording of the commentator must therefore be read with the different senses distinguished. His expression does not by itself provide an unambiguous textual decision between the classifications previously discussed. Further, the earlier division of quiddity—whether according to the familiar classification or the Chief Master's terminology—divides it **as considered**, not as though the divisions were independently existing external quiddities. In the actual case one must still ask how the considered meaning is related to a thing's unity and its individuating features.

### I074.lower.02 — What “no parts” means and why the simple cannot be a hidden composite

**Arabic anchors:** «(قوله لا جزء لها) أي في حد ذاتها» · «(قوله تقابلها) أشار به إلى أن البساطة والتركيب بهذا التفسير متنافيان» · «أن البساطة والتركيب بهذا التفسير متنافيان لا يصدقان على شيء» · «وقد يؤخذان متضايفين».

**English:** On “It has no part”: that is, **in its own definition or essence**. On the discussion of opposition: the expressions **simplicity** and **composition**, under one sense of the definitions given, are mutually exclusive and cannot both be true of one and the same thing in the same respect. Yet the terms can also be used **relationally**. A thing may be simple relative to one analysis and composite relative to another. One must therefore prevent an inference that assumes they are absolutely contradictory while employing them in a comparative sense.

### I074.lower.03 — Conceptual parts versus real constituents

**Arabic anchors:** «قد يوخذ البسيط بمعنى ما لا جزء له بالقياس إلى ما هو مركب منه» · «وقد يؤخذ المركب بمعنى جزء المركب والمركب بمعنى الكل» · «وبهذا التقدير لا يمنع من صدقهما» · «(قوله لأن الواحد مبدأ المتعدد الخ) أراد من الواحد والمتعدد».

**English:** The simple may be taken in a restricted sense as **what is not a part of the relevant kind**, or as what does not have a part relative to a proposed composite. The composite in turn can be described in terms of its own component and the whole into which that component enters. Under a comparative interpretation some predicates of simplicity and composition can apply without inconsistency, so one cannot argue from the bare words to a contradiction. On “For unity is the principle of multiplicity”: what matters is the distinction between **the one and the many in the order being considered**. It should not be inferred without proof that an unrestricted unity is a real, separate constituent in every enumerated collection. The marginal argument on I:75 continues this issue by examining the attempted regress from multiplicity to unity.

**Outgoing join:** I075.lower.01.

### I075.upper.01 — Genuine unity and asymmetric dependence between parts

**Arabic anchors:** «ماهية واحدة ووحدة حقيقية» · «ثم الاحتياج فيما بين الجزأين قد يكون من جانب واحد كالتركيب من البسائط العنصرية» · «وعاما قد يكون من الصور المعدنية والنباتية والحيوانية إذ الصورة تحتاج إلى تلك المواد من غير عكس» · «وقد يكون من الجانبين كاحتياج الهيولى إلى الصورة وهن احتياج الصورة إلى الهيولى».

**English:** —[Otherwise the components would not give] one quiddity and a genuine unity. Dependence between two constituents can sometimes run **one way only**, as in a compound of elemental simples. The forms involved in minerals, plants, and animals require their material substrata, without the opposite dependence necessarily following in the same sense. Or dependence may run **both ways**, as with prime matter and form: matter requires form, while form too requires matter in the relevant kind of corporeal composition. These examples distinguish the relation of ontological dependence from mere juxtaposition.

### I075.upper.02 — Why the collective form of an army is not another produced thing

**Arabic anchors:** «(بخلاف المركب الاعتباري)» · «فإن قيل احتياج الهيئة الاجتماعية إلى الأجزاء المادية لازم قطعًا» · «قلنا الصورة الاجتماعية في المركبات الاعتبارية محض اعتبار العقل».

**English:** “Unlike a considerational composite.” If it is objected that the **collective configuration** surely depends upon the material components that constitute it, we reply that the collective form of a merely considerational composite is **nothing but a consideration of the intellect**. It is not established as an additional extramental entity needing a cause in the same manner as a substantial form. This is why dependence of a genuine compound cannot be inferred directly from the need of an army for its soldiers or the need of an aggregation for its members. The next page develops the point explicitly through the army example.

**Outgoing join:** I076.upper.01.

### I075.lower.01 — Substrate, multiplicity, and a contested regress

**Arabic anchors:** «أن يوجد فيه وحدات ويلزم منه انتهاء المعروض المتعدد إلى المعروض الواحد» · «لكن لا يلزم الانتهاء إلى البسيط بمعنى ما لا جزء له» · «بل جواز أن يكون ذلك المعروض الواحد مركبا» · «فتلزم كون العوارض بسائط بالمعنى المذكور ولكنه ليس هو المدعى».

**English:** If there is a multiplicity of unities in a subject, it is argued that an underlying plurality must ultimately terminate at a **single bearer** of those predications. The marginal commentator concedes that this may establish some kind of unity, but denies that it necessarily establishes an **absolutely simple thing having no parts**. The single bearer might itself be composite under a different analysis. That would mean only that the accidents considered in relation to it are simple under the restricted sense just given, not that the bearer is simple in the absolute sense intended by the proof. The inference therefore moves from *a unified subject* to *an indivisible quiddity* without sufficient justification.

### I075.lower.02 — The mistaken derivation of simplicity from intellectual composition

**Arabic anchors:** «وقد يستدل على انتهاء المركب العقلي إلى البسيط بأنه لو لم ينته إليه لامتنع تعقل الماهية» · «لامتناع إحاطة العقل بما لا يتناهى» · «فإن معنى المركب العقلي هو ما لا يكون تمايز أجزائه إلا بحسب العقل».

**English:** Another argument says that intellectual compounds must terminate in something simple; otherwise the intellect could not grasp a quiddity, since it cannot encompass an infinite number of constituents. The objection is that this misconstrues **intellectual composition**. Its parts are distinguished *by the intellect's analysis*, and it does not follow that the intellect must successively traverse an actually infinite multitude of separately real things in order to apprehend the whole. The conclusion would require an additional demonstration that the distinction of conceptual constituents implies a corresponding multiplicity which must be completed in actual cognition.

### I075.lower.03 — Unity of real composition, and a warning about “material” parts

**Arabic anchors:** «(قوله وحدة حقيقية الخ) للقطع بأنه لا يحصل من الحجر الموضوع بجنب الإنسان مثلا واحد حقيقي» · «(قوله من الجانبين) أي لكن لا باعتبار واحد وإلا لزم الدور» · «(قوله كذا قالوا) إشارة إلى أن مبناه على قواعدهم» · «(قوله قلنا الصورة الاجتماعية) حاصل الجواب».

**English:** On “A genuine unity”: there can plainly be no genuine single being made merely by placing a stone beside a human being. On “On both sides”: the dependence must not be asserted **under a single and identical respect**, since that would yield circularity. On “Thus they say”: the expression reports the philosophers' principles rather than presenting them as a demonstration without remaining questions. On “We reply: the collective form”: the answer is that one must distinguish the **constitution of a real composite** from an intellectual consideration of togetherness. If “dependence” is said to concern the material constituents of genuine compounds, the claim is not automatically true of every aggregation of physical things. What needs proving is dependence among the **really existing constituents and the form** that makes them one. The next lower gloss takes up this point and qualifies the relation to the producer.
