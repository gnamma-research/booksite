### I076.upper.01 — On what can be externally real, and the status of collective unity

**Arabic locating anchors:** «لا تحقق لها في الخارج إذ ليس من العسكر في الخارج إلا تلك الأفراد» · «ولو جعلت الهيئة جزءا من العسكر لم يكن العسكر أمرا موجودا في الخارج لأن ما جزؤه عدمي فهو عدمي» · «ثم اعلم أنهم اختلفوا في الماهيات الممكنة أهي مجعولة الجاعل أم لا».

**English:** —There is no realization of it in the external world, for what exists externally of an army is nothing beyond its individual soldiers. If the collective configuration is made a constituent of the army, the army would no longer be an externally existing thing, since a thing one of whose constituents is non-existent is itself non-existent. Know, then, that they have differed about possible quiddities: are they things produced by an agent, or not? This is another disputed question, and the author has chosen to express it in terms of whether the possible thing needs an agent, for that is one of the concomitants of [being] a quiddity or of [the way it is treated as] produced. The difference in formulation must not be flattened into the claim that the author has already determined the issue.

**Join:** incoming I075.upper.final. The boundary between the last sentence about the collective form and the heading on possible quiddities is a transition in the printed main argument.

### I076.upper.02 — What is meant by the claim that quiddity is not made

**Arabic locating anchors:** «(ومن ثالث في مجعولية الماهية)» · «فقال بمجعولية الماهية أراد أنها تعرض للماهية في الجملة» · «ومن ثالث في مجعولية الماهية» · «(أراد أنها) أي المجعولية (من لوازم الوجود) وعوارض الهوية (كتناهي الأجسام) فإنه ليس».

**English:** [The text] introduces a third disagreement about the *made-ness* (*majʿūliyya*) of a quiddity. The position denying that quiddity is produced concerns whether made-ness is attributed to quiddity as such, or whether it belongs to the quiddity only under a certain consideration. In explaining what was intended by the expression, the discussion identifies made-ness with the concomitants of existence and the accidents of concrete actuality, as in the finitude of bodies. The comparison extends into I:77; it should not be read as an argument that bodies themselves, or their quiddities considered in isolation, are independently created as distinct additional entities.

**Uncertain:** The compressed typography in the clause before «أراد أنها» requires a separate line-level proof. The English here preserves the reported issue but is **interpretive at that exact point**, not a certified literal rendering.

**Join:** outgoing I077.upper.01.

### I076.ruled.01 — A separately ruled correction to the reading of “concomitant of existence”

**Arabic locating anchor:** «(قوله من لوازم الوجود الخ) يعني ليس مراد المخالف أن الصادر عن الفاعل هو الوجود دون الماهية».

**English:** On “Among the concomitants of existence, etc.” What the opponent means is **not** that what issues from the agent is existence without quiddity, as though existence were an external, self-subsisting entity and quiddity wholly excluded from the act of production. Existence is a matter of consideration, needing no agent considered as an independent entity. Rather, the point is that what is produced is quiddity **on condition that it is mixed with existence**. Under that description it requires an agent; it does not follow that the unqualified quiddity, considered apart from existence, likewise requires one. The dispute may thus turn on the *wording* or on which consideration of quiddity is intended. [The printed attribution/signature at the close is a reference to a prior discussion, not a securely identified individual author.]

**Separately printed:** This note is bounded by two horizontal rules. Do not conflate it with either the upper sharḥ or the long lower ḥāshiya.

### I076.lower.01 — The lower commentator objects to how the debate is framed

**Arabic locating anchors:** «الاجتماعية في المركب الاعتباري ليست موجودة في الخارج» · «مثل الترياق والسكنجبين مركبا حقيقيا» · «فإنه يحدث بتركيبه صورة في نفس الأمر» · «المركب الحقيقي من الجوهر والعرض» · «كالنفس والبدن للإنسان» · «(قوله إن المراد من المجعولية الخ)».

**English:** The collective character of a **considerational composite** is not present externally. But if someone asks how a **real composite** is possible, consider a compound medicine such as *tiryāq* or *sakanjabīn*: through the composition, a form really arises and the components require that form as a principle or source. Even if the emergent form is granted to be a substantial form, the result does not by itself settle the special case of a composite constructed of accidents. One can only say that a real composite of substance and accident is possible if its constitutive relation is established—not that every aggregation must have the status of a genuine unity. The note also directs us to examples of genuinely existing constituent parts: soul and body in a human being; or such oppositions as necessity of existence and impossibility of nonexistence, which must not be confused with merely verbal enumeration. On “What is meant by made-ness, etc.” a previous author/commentator's account is introduced, and its full continuation is on I:77.

**Uncertain:** A short expression following the medicinal examples is compressed in the scan. The translation conveys the explicit comparison but does not introduce an unprinted substantial form as an established conclusion. The marginal stream continues across I076→I077.

### I077.upper.01 — Possible quiddities and the four-element example

**Arabic locating anchors:** «من لوازم ماهية الجسم حتى لو تصورنا جسما غير متناه كان جسما» · «(لا من لوازم الماهية كزوجية الأربعة)» · «فإنها من لوازم ماهية الأربعة حتى إذا تصورنا الأربعة ليست بزوج لم تكن أربعة» · «(وإلا) يكن مراد المخالف ما ذكر لكن (كان احتياج الممكن إلى العلة ضروريا)».

**English:** —[Bodily finitude belongs among the concomitants of the existence of body,] not the concomitants of the very quiddity of body: even if we imagined an infinite body, it would still be a body. “It is not like the evenness of four,” which belongs to the quiddity of four: if four were conceived as not even, it would not be four. “Otherwise”—if the opponent did not mean the distinction just mentioned, and instead denied that possible quiddities require an agent even under the relevant existential consideration—“the possible thing’s need of a cause would be necessary.” On the author's clarification, that would render the dispute only verbal; further investigation of the reality of existence and quiddity is therefore postponed to its proper place.

### I077.lower.01 — Whether the need is for an agent or for something other than the composite

**Arabic locating anchors:** «إن المجعولية قد يراد بها الاحتياج إلى الفاعل وقد يراد بها الاحتياج إلى الغير على ما يعم الجزء أيضا» · «وكلاهما من عوارض الممكن» · «الأول من لوازم هويته مطلقا والثاني من لوازم ماهية المركب دون البسيط».

**English:** “Made-ness” may mean dependence on a producing agent; it may also mean dependence on something **other than itself**, in a sense broad enough to include dependence on a constituent part. Both are attributes of the possible thing. The first, however, is a concomitant of its concrete actuality, while the second pertains to the quiddity of a composite rather than that of a simple thing. Thus the word “made” is liable to equivocation unless the kind of dependence is determined.

### I077.lower.02 — Qualification of the alleged merely verbal dispute

**Arabic locating anchors:** «(قوله فعلى ما ذكر يكون الخلاف لفظيا)» · «أقول هذه المسألة ... كما أشار إليه الشارح» · «لا تكاد تنضبط مذاهب المتكلمين» · «الماهيات بسيطة كانت أو مركبة تجعل الجاعل» · «جمهور الفلاسفة إلى أنها ليست بجعل الجاعل مطلقا».

**English:** On “On what has been stated, the disagreement would be merely verbal”: I say that the question is not so easily brought under a single formula. The positions and terms used by the theologians are not readily reduced to one pattern. Some hold that quiddities, whether simple or composite, are produced by an agent, even where this requires acknowledging the production of a composite without separately producing its parts. The majority of philosophers are reported to hold that quiddities as such are not products of an agent in any unqualified sense; others distinguish simple from composite quiddities or disagree over whether a composite needs a producer by virtue of its parts. The commentator is warning that the seemingly verbal disagreement overlays several distinct propositions.

**Reading caution:** The marginal enumeration and attribution of positions must be checked against the full preceding and following notes before assigning a view to a named school.

### I077.lower.03 — The objection about an independently produced quiddity

**Arabic locating anchors:** «فإن معنى المركب العقلي هو ما لا يكون تمايز أجزائه إلا بحسب العقل» · «ولا يستلزم كونه معقولا بأجزائه» · «لو كان جعل الجاعل لزم في تقدير عدم الجاعل أن لا يكون الإنسان إنسانا» · «فإنه عند ارتفاع الجاعل يرتفع الموجود وتبقى الماهية معدومة».

**English:** The point is not answered by identifying an intellectual composite with a mere aggregate of separately existing things. A composite in the intellect is one whose components are differentiated by the intellect, and this need not entail separate actual existence for every intelligible constituent. The objector then says: if a producer makes the very quiddity of man, the removal of that producer would imply that a human being is no longer human. Two forms of the objection are considered. If this is a negative proposition, we do not concede that the quiddity of a human remains a subject about which that negative assertion could be made; the existent is removed with the producer, and the quiddity is left without external existence. If the claim is understood as one about the intelligible essence independently of the act of existing, the answer differs. The argument continues at the opening of I:78.

### I078.upper.01 — Chapter on individuation (*taʿayyun*)

**Arabic locating anchors:** «فصل في التعين» · «تعين الشيء كما في شرح المقاصد غير ماهيته ووجوده ووحدته» · «لتكون كل من هذه الأمور مشتركا بينه وبين غيره بخلاف التعين» · «ولذا صدق قولنا الكلي ماهية وهو موجود وواحد» · «ولا يصدق أنه متعين وإن كان التعين مفهوما كليا».

**English:** **Chapter on individuation.** The individuation of a thing, as stated in *Sharḥ al-Maqāṣid*, is other than its quiddity, its existence, and its unity. Each of those—quiddity, existence, and unity—can be shared in some way by a thing and another thing; **individuation**, by contrast, is what prevents that shared predication. Thus we can truthfully say that a universal has a quiddity, is existent [under the relevant sense of existence], and is one, but we cannot predicate individuation of the universal as such—even though *individuation* is itself a universal concept, as explained by what follows.

**Join:** outgoing I079.upper.01 (“as applicable to many...”). The top register on I:78 ends mid-sentence.

### I078.lower.01 — Completion of the I:77 objection

**Arabic locating anchors:** «عند ارتفاع الجاعل لا يبقى الإنسان حتى يصح موضوعا للإيجاب» · «كذا في شرح المقاصد» · «وثانيا أن الوجود أو الاتصاف ماهية من الماهيات في القول بمجعوليتهما اعتراف بمجعوليته».

**English:** —When the producer is removed, there is no human being left to serve as the subject of an affirmative proposition. Such is the discussion in *Sharḥ al-Maqāṣid*, and it deserves examination. Secondly, existence itself, or the qualification of a thing by existence, is a quiddity among quiddities if treated as an intelligible object; to affirm that its existence or its qualification is produced is thus to concede the production of a quiddity [under that consideration]. One must therefore attend to what exactly is asserted when the debate is called verbal.

**Join:** incoming I077.lower.03.

### I078.lower.02 — Existence and determination: the disagreement is not just an argument over words

**Arabic locating anchors:** «فلا نزاع حقيقة وفيه أيضا نظر فإن النزاع في الماهيات التي هي حقائق الأشياء» · «لا فيما صدقت الماهية عليه من الأفراد» · «أكثر أدلة المتكلمين على تقدير تمامها أيضا لا تفيد إلا كون الوجود» · «بل اتصاف الماهية به من الفاعل في الحقيقة».

**English:** But there is a further difficulty. The actual controversy concerns **quiddities insofar as they are the realities of things**, not simply the individual things of which a quiddity is predicated. Most of the proofs offered by the theologians, even supposing them to be complete, would establish only that existence—or the attribution of existence to a quiddity—comes from an efficient agent. They do not, simply as they stand, show that the quiddity in its own right is also produced. The individual expressions can be verbally reinterpreted while the metaphysical issue remains unresolved.

### I078.lower.03 — What “shared” can mean in the claim about universals

**Arabic locating anchors:** «(قوله مشتركا بينه وبين غيره)» · «فإن المراد من اشتراك تلك الأمور أما اشتراك كليها بين الأشياء» · «ولكن منع عدم اشتراك التعين كذلك» · «وأما اشتراك أفرادها» · «فممنوع ضرورة أن الفرد الموجود من الوحدة مثلا في هذا الشيء غير الفرد الموجود في ذلك الشيء».

**English:** On “Shared between it and another.” The intended sharing may refer to the **universal notions** of these things being shared among particulars. But that would not establish that individuation as a universal notion is incapable of being shared—indeed, the text itself admits that it is a universal concept. Alternatively, the intended sharing may refer to **individual instances** of existence, unity, and quiddity. That reading is denied: the individual unity existing in this thing is not numerically the same individual unity existing in another. On either interpretation, the asserted contrast needs further clarification.

### I078.lower.04 — Individual unity and a generic universal: the force of the objection

**Arabic locating anchors:** «فإن كل شيء نوع منحصر في فرده» · «(قوله ولذا يصدق قولنا الكلي)» · «فإن عدم صدق قولنا الكلي متعين لا يقتضي عدم اشتراك التعين الذي بينه وبين غيره» · «إلا أن يشار بكلمة الإشارة إلى تعاير تلك الأمور».

**English:** More strictly, each thing can be considered as belonging to its own determinate kind under the relation in question; it is not established that all the individual marks of one thing coincide with those of another. On the sentence “Therefore we can say of the universal...,” the failure of “the universal is individuated” to be true does **not** entail that there is no shared *concept* of individuation predicable in discussing that universal and others. Perhaps the contrast becomes defensible only if the expression is being used demonstratively, to point to **this particular** individuation rather than the common notion of individuation. The commentator therefore challenges the proposed inference, rather than simply repeating it.
