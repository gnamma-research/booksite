## I:69 — The external existence of the *natural universal*
*Physical combined PDF page 82. Upper register begins in a sentence continued from I:68; lower ends in the middle of an objection continued on I:70.*

### I069.upper.01 — Upper, Taftāzānī lemma embedded in Sanandajī's commentary

**Arabic source anchors:** «الوجود الكلي عليه»؛ «وإنما ذلك التغاير بين المطلق والمقيد في العقل دون الخارج»؛ «ثم إذا اعتبرت المأخوذة لا بشرط شيء معروضة للكلي المنطقي»؛ «فهو ... الكلي الطبيعي»؛ «وإنما يوجد منه المعروض دون الكلي العارض»؛ «مجردا عن العوارض ... من الكلية والجنسية والنوعية».

**English working translation:** —the universal's existence [or its predication] of it. “That,” namely the distinction between what is unrestricted and what is restricted, obtains **in the intellect**, not externally. “Then if” the quiddity “taken without stipulating anything” is considered as something to which the logical universal is predicated, it is—the quiddity under that consideration—the **natural universal**. What exists externally of it is “that which receives the predicate,” that is, the quiddity of which universality is predicated, “not the universal that is predicated of it.” This last item is the abstract attribute of universality, not an additional external thing. One might object that the quiddity too should have no external existence, since the predicated universality excludes it. But the quiddity is not present externally *stripped of all accidents*: universality, genus-character and species-character, and similar attributes are mental accidents which, considered as such, preclude external existence. This clarifies the celebrated saying that the natural universal has existence outside: its externally present subject is not the external instantiation of the *mental predicate* of universality. The sentence continues on I:70.

### I069.lower.01 — Long marginal discussion, unassigned author: unrestricted and restricted

**Arabic anchors:** «وحينئذ يتم القول بأن المطلقة نفس المخلوطة في الخارج لا جزؤها»؛ «وإنما التغاير في العقل»؛ «قوله دون الخارج ... أي بحسب المصدوق»؛ «وإنما التغاير بين المطلق والمقيد».

**English working translation:** On this basis the assertion is completed that the unrestricted nature is the very mixed nature externally, rather than a part of it: that is because the unrestricted nature does not need a separate external determination to distinguish it from the mixed nature. The distinction lies in thought. The words “not externally” may be read with reference to what the two concepts apply to, rather than as a denial that their *meanings* differ. The commentator had explained “mixed” in one sense, while an objector took it otherwise; the objection therefore risks shifting between the mixed quiddity as an entire compound and the unrestricted nature as considered without its qualification. As for the unrestricted human being and one restricted by some particular specification, one cannot simply infer that the unrestricted human subsists as an external item distinct from the restricted human. [The precise syntax of the initial reference to the author and commentator requires a clearer reading.]

### I069.lower.02 — The mixed nature as whole, or the nature as a part

**Arabic anchors:** «لو كانت المخلوطة جزءا للفرد في الخارج»؛ «المطلقة ... لم يصح ما يأتي»؛ «المخلوطة هي الأفراد في الخارج»؛ «لا يصح حمل نحو الإنسان على زيد مثلا»؛ «بأن يقال الفرد قد يطلق ويراد به المجموع».

**English working translation:** If one were to say that the mixed quiddity is only a *part* of an individual existing externally, the unrestricted quiddity, insofar as it coincides with that mixed quiddity, would likewise become a mere part; the subsequent predication would then fail. Moreover, the claim that the mixed quiddity just *is* the external individuals must be carefully distinguished from saying that all the parts of a complex individual are each identical to the entire individual. Otherwise it would be impossible to predicate “human” of Zayd, when such predication is plainly accepted. One answer is that the word “individual” may denote the entire composite, while in another use it may denote the quiddity together with the specifying accidents. The author warns that distinctions in conceptual consideration must not be turned into unsupported distinctions between independently existing external objects.

### I069.lower.03 — Reply to the remark about the nature “taken without a condition”

**Arabic anchors:** «قوله ثم إذا اعتبرت المأخوذة لا بشرط»؛ «إن المأخوذ لا بشرط يمتنع أن يوجد في الخارج لأنه كلي طبيعي»؛ «لأن الوجود فيه يستلزم التشخص المنافي للكلية»؛ «وتنافي اللوازم دليل ...».

**English working translation:** On “Then if the thing taken without a condition is considered ...”: it can be objected that what is taken without a condition cannot exist externally if its being a natural universal is understood as requiring universality itself, since every external existent must be individuated, and individuation conflicts with the mental universal taken as universal. The implication of the conflicting consequences is itself evidence that the respective premises cannot all be maintained together. This objection is continued, with its answer, at the start of I:70.

## I:70 — The quiddity exists externally, but the mental accident of universality does not
*Physical combined PDF page 83.*

### I070.upper.01 — Upper commentary: existence of the externally individuated nature

**Arabic anchors:** «أما صدقه هو عليه وهو المأخوذ لا بشرط شيء فموجود فيه»؛ «ووجوده فيه إنما هو عند عروض التشخص»؛ «فالوجود حقيقة هي المخلوطة»؛ «وهي الأفراد»؛ «وأما المأخوذ مع عارض الكلية فلا توجد»؛ «الكلي العقلي».

**English working translation:** As for its being truly predicated of that subject, the thing taken without stipulating anything does exist in it. Its existence there, however, only obtains when individuation supervenes. Accordingly, the actually existent quiddity is the **mixed** quiddity, namely the individuals. The feminine pronoun may be explained through the grammatical predicate, or because its referent is the quiddity. But a quiddity considered along with the accident of universality does not exist externally; nor does the compound of quiddity and that mental accident, namely the **intellectual universal** (*al-kullī al-ʿaqlī*).

### I070.upper.02 — Upper commentary: Ibn Sīnā's alternative terminology

**Arabic anchors:** «ثم ما ذكر من معنى الماهية بشرط لا شيء هو المشهور»؛ «وقد يقال»؛ «والقائل ابن سينا»؛ «الماهية بشرط ...».

**English working translation:** What has just been given as the meaning of a quiddity “taken on condition of no thing” is the familiar account. “But it has also been said”—the proponent here is Ibn Sīnā—that the quiddity is taken “on condition that ...”. The expression is completed, and its new meaning worked out, on I:71. The alternative must not be silently equated with the common later terminology.

### I070.lower.01 — The objection and answer to I:69, unassigned margin

**Arabic anchors:** «تنافي الملزومات»؛ «مجرد المأخوذ لا بشرط ليس كليا طبيعيا بل مع اعتبار كونه معروضا للكلي المنطقي»؛ «والوجود هو مجرد ذلك من غير اعتبار الكلية»؛ «وأما المأخوذ مع عارض الكلية فلا وجود له في الخارج».

**English working translation:** —the incompatibility of the necessary consequences establishes incompatibility in the things that entail them. The answer, in summary, is that the thing *merely* taken without a condition is not thereby a natural universal; rather, it becomes one when considered as the subject of the logical universal. External existence belongs to the subject as such, without including the mental universality among its constitutive features, and that existence is realised when individuating accidents arise. What is taken together with universality as an accident has no external existence. This is the intended sense of the familiar statement that the natural universal exists externally. Consider it carefully: the explanation would not permit an unrestricted quiddity to float free of its external instantiations.

### I070.lower.02 — Mixed quiddity is not unambiguously an external composite

**Arabic anchors:** «قوله فالوجود حقيقة هي المخلوطة وهي الأفراد»؛ «لا يجوز أن تكون عبارة عن المقيدة المقترنة بالعوارض لا المجموع»؛ «كان الأول معتبرا مطلقا والآخر مقيدا»؛ «أجزاء الكل موجودة بوجود واحد»؛ «فلا محيص إلا بأن يقال أن المخلوطة هي المجموع المذكور كالفرد».

**English working translation:** On “What truly exists is the mixed quiddity—that is, the individuals”: this is correct if the mixed quiddity is taken to mean the composite whole. It must not simply be made identical with the nature qualified by accidents while excluding the whole, since such usage allows the nature, in thought, to be a constituent of a compound without therefore being a separate constituent existing in reality. Were one part unrestricted and another restricted by something else, one could speak of them as different conceptual parts; yet externally the parts of a whole need not possess a separately multiplied existence. One may instead say that the quiddity and its accompanying accidents subsist together with one external existence; or that several existences are attributed to constituents. The claim “mixed” thus requires an explicit account of what sort of composition is meant, and whether “individual” stands for the whole or for the qualified nature. The author's language is examined on both possible readings, rather than assuming a conclusion.

### I070.lower.03 — Transition to Ibn Sīnā, unassigned margin

**Arabic anchors:** «قوله والقائل ابن سينا»؛ «الماهية قد تؤخذ بشرط لا شيء بأن يتصور معناها بشرط أن يكون ذلك المعنى وحده».

**English working translation:** On “The one who says it is Ibn Sīnā”: the quiddity may be taken on condition that no thing be with it—meaning that its signification is conceived on its own, with the stipulation that that meaning be considered alone. The note's detailed account proceeds across the page boundary.

## I:71 — Matter, subject, genus and the meaning of conditional quiddity
*Physical combined PDF page 84.*

### I071.upper.01 — Upper commentary: a nature taken with a stipulation of separateness

**Arabic anchors:** «لا شيء بمعنى أن يفرض عليها كل ما يقارنها»؛ «فتكون مادة للشخص»؛ «متقدمة عليه في الوجودين»؛ «فالمراد من التقدم هو التقدم بالطبع»؛ «ولا يصح حملها على الشخص لانتفاء شرط الحمل وهو الاتحاد في الوجود».

**English working translation:** —“no thing,” in the sense that whatever accompanies the quiddity is assumed to be additional to it, and the quiddity is considered apart from those accompanying features. “It then becomes matter” and a constituent “of the individual,” understood as the composite of the nature and what accompanies it, “prior to it in both kinds of existence,” external and mental, since a whole cannot be realised without its constituent. The priority intended is **priority by nature**, not temporal priority. Nor may it be predicated of the individual under that consideration, because the condition of predication—unity in existence—does not obtain.

### I071.upper.02 — Upper commentary: the example of animal and rational

**Arabic anchors:** «الحيوان إذا أخذناه بشرط أن لا يكون معه الناطق»؛ «كان جزءا ومادة للمجموع المركب من الحيوان والناطق ولا يحمل عليه»؛ «وإذا أخذناه بشرط أن يكون معه الناطق كان نوعا»؛ «وإذا أخذناه لا بشرط أن يكون معه شيء كان له جهتان».

**English working translation:** Thus, when we take *animal* on condition that the rational does not accompany it, it is a part and a material constituent of the composite of animal and rational, and is not predicated of that composite. When we take animal on condition that rationality accompanies it, it becomes a **species**. When we take it without stipulating whether anything accompanies it, it admits two aspects: we can consider the difference between it and what accompanies it, or consider their unity. The first animal is thus a part of the human being, preceding it in both modes of existence, and cannot be predicated of it. The contrast between the second and third cases closes in I:72.

### I071.ruled.01 — Separately printed short note (not Sanandajī's sharḥ)

**Arabic anchors:** «قوله في الوجودين الخ»؛ «واعلم أنهم اختلفوا أن الكل موجود في الخارج أولا بل الموجود فيه هي الأفراد فقط»؛ «وكلام المصنف هنا ظاهر في الثاني».

**English working translation:** On “in the two modes of existence, etc.”: know that they have disagreed as to whether the universal exists in the external world in the first place, or whether only individual instances exist there. The author's wording here appears to favour the latter. This note should be kept distinct from both the surrounding main text and the lengthy lower annotation.

### I071.lower.01 — Does the material part precede the composite in both existences?

**Arabic anchors:** «ولا يكون المعنى الأول مقولا على المجموع مع حال المقارنة بل جزء منه مادة له»؛ «قوله مثلا الحيوان إذا أخذناه بشرط أن يكون معه الخ»؛ «هذا هو المذكور بقوله وقد يقال الماهية الخ».

**English working translation:** The first meaning is not predicated of the composite while the accompanying feature is present. It is instead its part and serves as matter for it. The passage concerning animal taken with a further condition is an exposition of the alternative account introduced by “It has also been said concerning the quiddity ...”. Yet this depends on taking the example exactly as specified, not on treating every use of “animal” as denoting the same concept in the same respect.

### I071.lower.02 — Objection: a composite is not necessarily an exterior part plus an interior part

**Arabic anchors:** «ومعناه على ما سبق آنفا»؛ «حيث لو اقترن به النطق كان النطق زائدا عليه وصار المجموع مركبا من الحيوان والناطق»؛ «فلا تكون الحيوان مقولا على ذلك المجموع بل كان جزءا ومادة له»؛ «ما هو زائد عليه متناقضا».

**English working translation:** If rationality is associated with animality, then on the restricted reading rationality is an addition to it and the whole is a composite of animal and rational. Animality so construed cannot be predicated of the whole; it is a constituent and its matter. But if “animal” is instead treated simply as that which can have something accompanying it, whether the concomitant is an accident or something else, the conclusion cannot be transferred without argument. At issue is whether the addition is part of the thing's definition, or only a condition under which the thing is apprehended; confusing the two makes the assertion “animal is the whole” contradict “animal is its part.”

### I071.lower.03 — Objection concerning genus, species and differentia

**Arabic anchors:** «قوله كان نوعا»؛ «واعترض عليه بأن النوع مجموع الجنس والفصل»؛ «فكيف جعل عبارة عن المأخوذ بشرط شيء»؛ «وأجيب بأنه تسامح مبني على أن الجنس والنوع والفصل واحد بالذات لكن ...».

**English working translation:** On “it is a species”: an objection is raised that the species is the combination of genus and differentia. How, then, can species be identified with the genus taken under the stipulation that the differentia accompany it? One reply describes this as a permissible looseness of expression, founded on the claim that genus, species and differentia are one in their external subject, while intellectually distinguished. The marginal writer stresses, however, that their unity in subject does not make their *conceptual definitions* interchangeable. The discussion passes into the next page.

## I:72 — The closure of the threefold example and an extended marginal critique
*Physical combined PDF page 85. Only a short upper line is printed; almost the entire remaining page belongs to the long marginal text.*

### I072.upper.01 — The whole upper text on the leaf

**Arabic:** «عليه والثاني نفس الإنسان والثالث جنس محمول عليه (ثم) الماهية البسيطة».

**English translation:** —of it. The second [consideration] is the very human being, and the third is a genus predicated of it. “Then” [the discussion passes to] the simple quiddity. The running principal text then jumps to I:74, because I:73 contains only marginal commentary.

### I072.lower.01 — The proposed resolution and the commentator's disagreement

**Arabic anchors:** «فيه تأمل والتحقيق ما ذكر»؛ «قوله والثالث جنس له محمول الخ»؛ «حقيقة الكلام أن المأخوذ لا بشرط شيء إذا اعتبر بحسب أنحاء ...»؛ «هذا يخالف ما في الشرح نوع مخالفة فتأمله».

**English working translation:** There is room for reflection here, and the correct resolution requires further precision. On “The third is a genus predicated of it”: it has been said that the real account is as follows. If the nature taken without stipulating anything is considered with respect both to its difference from an accompaniment and to its unity with it, it can be an essential constituent capable of predication. Considered under sheer unity, it may be taken as a species. I say, however, that this differs in a certain respect from the account given in the commentary. Consider that difference carefully. The comparison must therefore not be presented as an unqualified agreement between Ibn Sīnā and the later terminology.

### I072.lower.02 — What precisely is meant by 'without stipulating'?

**Arabic anchors:** «لا يشترط أن يكون ذلك المعنى وحده ولا وحده مع تجويز أن يقارنه غيره وأن لا يقارنه»؛ «ويكون المعنى الأول مقولا على المجموع حال المقارنة»؛ «قد يكون غير متحصل بنفسه»؛ «بل يكون مبهما محتملا للمقولية على أشياء مختلفة الحقائق».

**English working translation:** A meaning taken without a condition does **not** stipulate that it be alone, nor that it be alone with the additional requirement that some other thing may or may not accompany it. Rather, both accompaniment and non-accompaniment are left open. Under this consideration the first meaning may be predicated of a composite when an accompaniment is present; it need not be independently complete by itself. It can remain indeterminate and open to predication of things with different essential realities. Its further completion arises from what is conjoined to it, enabling it to be specified and become, in some instances, one of several genuinely distinct natures.

### I072.lower.03 — Animal as genus, and the source of the problem

**Arabic anchors:** «كما في الأنواع الداخلة تحت الجنس وهو نوع مثلا إذا أخذ الحيوان بشرط أن لا يكون معه شيء»؛ «وإن اقترن به ناطق صار المجموع مركبا من الحيوان والناطق»؛ «وإذا أخذ بشرط أن لا يكون معه الناطق مختصا ومتحصلا به كان نوعا»؛ «وإذا أخذ لا بشرط أن يكون معه شيء».

**English working translation:** This may be illustrated by the species falling under a genus. If we consider “animal” under the condition of not including any further thing, then, when rationality accompanies it, a composite of animal and rational results; animal in that respect is its material component rather than something predicated of the completed whole. If it is taken together with rationality as specifying and completing it, it is the species. If it is taken without stipulating what may accompany it, the relation to completion remains open. The controversy is about whether “animal” in each of these examples stands for the same conceived nature or for a nature taken under an additional condition. The latter would make apparently simple claims of identity or priority equivocal.

### I072.lower.04 — Matter, priority, and conceptual versus external order

**Arabic anchors:** «الأول جزء الإنسان مقدم عليه في الوجودين والثاني نفس الإنسان والثالث جنس محمول عليه»؛ «لا يكون جزءا لأن الجزء لا يحمل على الكل بالمواطأة»؛ «الفصل أنه جزء من النوع»؛ «أنه لا بد للعقل من ملاحظتهما في تحصيل صورة مطابقة للنوع»؛ «فيكون متقدما على النوع في العقل»؛ «وأما بحسب الخارج فيكون متأخرا لأنه ما لم يوجد الإنسان في الخارج لم يعقل له شيء يعمه وغيره».

**English working translation:** Thus the first [animal] is a constituent of the human being and prior to it in the two modes of existence, the second is the human being itself, and the third is a genus predicated of it. Nevertheless, a constituent, precisely insofar as it is a constituent, cannot be predicated of the whole *univocally* as though it were identical with the whole. The genus and differentia must be distinguished in their role in an intellectual definition: each must be considered in order to form a concept adequate to the species, and in that respect the constituent is prior to the species in understanding. In the external order, however, the nature as an abstract genus is not found prior to concrete humans; the argument therefore cannot simply infer an independent external genus from its conceptual priority. The last lines connect to I:73, which is a full leaf of continuing marginal discussion, not a new principal-text page.
