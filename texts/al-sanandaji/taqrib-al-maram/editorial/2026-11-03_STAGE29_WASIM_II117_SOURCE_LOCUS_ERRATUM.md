# Critical erratum: reported Wasīm quotation at II:117 is NOT source-matched in the uploaded facsimile
## Original-source re-audit, 3 November 2026

**The previously published GNAMMA statement that `W.II.117.01` had been visually matched to `taqribmaram(2).pdf` physical PDF page 412 / printed II:117 is WITHDRAWN.** The Arabic and English text itself has been preserved only as an **externally attributed quotation**, pending exact source-location verification. No further Wasīm marginal note has been independently verified in this scan.

### Distinguish the two actual sources

**Secondary published witness:** Waleed Ibn al-Salah, “[2] الأدلة العقلية على امتناع حلول الحوادث بذات الله تعالى عند الأشاعرة,” updated **20 April 2022**, https://drwaleedbinalsalah.com/2-%d8%a7%d9%84%d8%a3%d8%af%d9%84%d8%a9-%d8%a7%d9%84%d8%b9%d9%82%d9%84%d9%8a%d8%a9-%d8%b9%d9%84%d9%89-%d8%a7%d9%85%d8%aa%d9%86%d8%a7%d8%b9-%d8%ad%d9%84%d9%88%d9%84-%d8%a7%d9%84%d8%ad%d9%88%d8%a7/. At the article's discussion of divine change, he states **`ولكن العلامة محمد وسيم السنندجي محشي يقول`** and quotes the argument that God's being passively affected by an originated attribute would amount to a prohibited kind of change. He footnotes **`تقريب المرام في تهذيب الكلام ج2ص117`**. This is independent evidence that the secondary author **attributes the quoted words to Muḥammad Wasīm**, but is *not* an image of the underlying primary printed note.

**Original printed facsimile actually inspected:** the user's 632-image `taqribmaram(2).pdf`. Its **physical image 412 displays printed II:117**. The upper and small-print lower text on that leaf were read directly, and the adjacent original physical images **411 (II:116)** and **413 (II:118)** were also inspected at enlarged resolution; a further contact overview of physical **406–420** was checked. The quotation beginning “`لا يبعد أن يقال المراد من التغير المحال عليه`” is **not found on the actual printed II:117 / physical page 412**. This does NOT prove the quotation does not appear elsewhere in the two-volume original or another printing.

**Possible explanations, still UNPROVEN:** citation to a different edition with different pagination; a misprinted secondary page locator; a source quote from a related treatise or marginal note that has been moved; or a misquotation. The fact that the 1901/2012 facsimile's p.117 has a nearby theological topic does not turn that page into a matching source. The exact stemmatic issue is OPEN.
