# Taqrīb al-Marām with Ḥāshiyat al-Muḥākamāt — Stage 13

## Volume II, original printed pp.101–140 (physical PDF pp.396–435)

**Checkpoint:** 2 November 2026. **Witness:** the user's original two-volume combined facsimile `taqribmaram.pdf`, 632 scanned image pages. The forty pages II:101–140 were directly inspected at their printed loci, including main and marginal registers. This document is synchronized with the JavaScript records used in the independent NAMA reader.

**Do not mistake this for a complete translation.** The English paragraphs in Part A are image-informed *argument guides*, not the complete authorial Arabic rendered clause by clause. Only the short Arabic phrases actually displayed in Parts B and C are directly translated. Most upper prose and nearly all marginal disputes on these forty pages remain untranslated. The lower register contains Wasīm's *Ḥāshiyat al-Muḥākamāt* and contributions from other writers; **no new excerpt is attributed to Wasīm unless independently corroborated**. The existing source-matched Wasīm II:117 passage is preserved by reference, not translated again.

### Structural discoveries and critical divisions

- **II:101–103:** concluding philosophical accounts of separate intellects, celestial souls, postmortem human souls, and angels as immaterial or subtle-bodied beings.
- **II:103:** original heading **«الباب الخامس في الإلهيات»**; immediately proceeds to the first chapter on proofs of divine existence. This ends the separate-intellect section.
- **II:104–117:** possible and necessary existence, the denial of circular regress, divine oneness and transcendence, and the problem of temporal qualities or alteration in an immutable necessary being.
- **II:117:** the previous Wasīm gloss on *taʾaththur* (being affected by a newly originated attribute) was already source-identified and translated. It appears as `W.II.117.01` with the corresponding selected upper Sanandajī context, so **this stage does not duplicate it**.
- **II:118:** original heading **«فصل في الصفات الوجودية»**, beginning the discussion of knowledge, power, will and other divine perfections.
- **II:119–140:** status of divine attributes, essence and attribute, the power/will distinction, temporal objects of eternal knowledge, voluntary specification, and the predicates life, hearing, seeing and speech. These debates are often reported objections rather than simple authorial assent.
