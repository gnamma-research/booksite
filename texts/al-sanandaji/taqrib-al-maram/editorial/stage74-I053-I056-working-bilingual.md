## I:53 — mental existence and the 'fixed' nonexistent

### I053.upper.01 — what is being disputed about the mental form

**Arabic anchors:** «الخلاف في الوجود الذهني بالقطع» · «الكل متفق في الصورة» · «القائلين بالوجود الذهني» · «باتحاد الصورة وذي الصورة».

**English.** The disagreement concerns **mental existence**. Everyone, it is said, accepts the presence of a *form* in cognition; the proponents of mental existence maintain that the form and what it is a form of are united under the relevant consideration. The opponents do not accept that identity. The issue is not whether anyone ever thinks of things, but whether the thing known is itself present mentally or whether a form distinct from it supplies the act of cognition.

### I053.upper.02 — being, fixity and the denial of intermediates

**Arabic anchors:** «ثم المعقول من الوجود والشيئية» · «الثبوت» · «ضرورة فكل ما هو ثابت فهو شيء وموجود وبالعكس» · «(و) المعقول (من العدم)».

**English.** It follows that, on the account being defended, **existence, thinghood and establishment** are not three independently distinct ontological ranks. Whatever is established is a thing and is existent, and conversely. The notion signified by *nonexistence* is the contrary of existence; consequently one cannot assume an unargued middle state in which the object is at once absolutely nonexistent and yet positively established as a thing.

### I053.lower.01 — the narrower character of the mental-existence agreement

**Arabic anchors:** «(قوله فإن الكل متفق في الصورة الخ)» · «الفلاسفة» · «الصورة الحاصلة في الذهن» · «حقيقة».

**English.** On “All agree concerning the form”: the glossator observes that this common ground does not remove the disagreement. The philosophers' claim of union between the intelligible form and what is known may be meant in the level of quiddity or truth, whereas those denying mental existence understand the known form as a description or representation. If the union is stated without fixing the relevant respect, a merely verbal accord conceals a substantive ontological divergence. One must ask whether it is the genuine quiddity, or only something resembling it, which becomes present in understanding.

### I053.lower.02 — philosophers, theologians and the relation between the form and its object

**Arabic anchors:** «هل هي عين ذي الصورة» · «وحدتها كماله عليه أهل التحقيق» · «الوجود الذهني» · «الوجود الحقيقي».

**English.** The marginal author compares the accounts attributed to philosophers, verifying scholars and those who deny the identity. The questions are whether a mental form possesses the same essence as its external object, whether mental and external existence are two manners of realizing one quiddity, and whether the form is only an analogical representation. If the form is merely another thing, then the object known may remain absent; if the object itself is asserted to be present, it becomes necessary to distinguish its mental mode of presence from the properties of its external occurrence.

### I053.lower.03 — 'everything established is existent' and its dialectical limits

**Arabic anchors:** «(قوله فكل ما هو موجود معدوم الخ)» [opening uncertain] · «الوجود والثبوت والعدم» · «عندهم» · «الواسطة».

**English.** The lower gloss reports that theologians differ over whether nonexistents can have a fixed status that is not existence. One camp holds the division between existence and nonexistence to be exhaustive. Another proposes the so-called fixed nonexistent, arguing from the meaningfulness of distinctions and judgements about absent things. The author's challenge is to say what this supposed middle rank adds to conceptual differentiation without simply renaming a mode of existence. [The final objection continues on I:54.]

## I:54 — is an absolutely nonexistent thing 'established'?

### I054.upper.01 — the refusal of positive fixity for absolute nonexistence

**Arabic anchors:** «شيء ولا ثابت» · «(فكذا المعدوم ليس بشيء ولا ثابت)» · «المعدوم في الخارج» · «بالوجود الذهني».

**English.** —[The argument denies that the nonexistent can be a thing in the relevant sense or positively fixed.] Thus “the nonexistent is neither a thing nor established.” Admittedly, something nonexistent in **external** reality can be the subject of a judgement in the **mind**, on the theory of mental existence. But this does not make the absolutely nonexistent an existing thing outside the intellect. The dispute concerns the type of establishment claimed for it.

### I054.upper.02 — the argument for an intermediate rank

**Arabic anchors:** «(لا واسطة بينهما)» · «بين الوجود والعدم» · «(ومنهم من أثبتها)» · «المعدوم» · «السواد».

**English.** The author first denies any intermediate state between existence and nonexistence, then reports those who **affirm such a middle condition**. They say that an object may be *established* although not existent, so that the affirmation of a predicate may be made concerning something that has not yet been externally realised. The argument is tested with predicates like blackness and with the supposed fixed nature of a nonexistent possible thing.

### I054.lower.01 — logical intermediates and real intermediates are not interchangeable

**Arabic anchors:** «يلزم الترادف» · «(قوله والنزاع ... الخ)» · «الثبوت» · «النفي» · «الوجود».

**English.** The glossator asks whether denying an intermediate level forces existence and positive fixity to be completely synonymous. Even if they are equivalent in extension under the disputed account, it need not follow that every use of their words has precisely the same meaning. The proposed intermediate rank may also conceal an ambiguity: an object can be known, named or considered mentally while being nonexistent externally, but none of these facts alone supplies a third ontological condition between existence and nothingness.

### I054.lower.02 — blackness, whiteness and the basis of an affirmative predication

**Arabic anchors:** «(قوله إذ هو بحيث يتلوه الخ)» · «اللفظ» · «حقيقة في الوجود» · «المعدوم».

**English.** The objection presses the interpretation of the opponent's examples. If the absolutely nonexistent can have the attribute of blackness or whiteness as an externally established property, the subject must be explained; if only the possibility of the predicate is intended, the argument does not establish the third rank. A difference among words, among conceived predicates, or among possible future instances must be distinguished from an actual difference among external entities.

## I:55 — does an added 'existence' generate an infinite regress?

### I055.upper.01 — existence as an attribute of an already established subject

**Arabic anchors:** «(قال)» · «يجعل الوجود صفة إذا وجد تسلسل» · «الوجودات» · «أو عدم اتصاف بالنقيض».

**English.** “They say”: if existence is treated as an additional positive attribute which must itself become existent, then one seems required to posit another existence for it, and so on without end. Alternatively, if nonexistence is said to be simply the subject's being qualified by a contradictory predicate, the account must explain how the subject is present in order to receive that contradictory qualification.

### I055.upper.02 — replies to the regress and to the paradox of the subject's being absent

**Arabic anchors:** «(ورد)» · «أنه موجود» · «(لا أن وجوده عينه)» · «(عدم)».

**English.** The reply notes that an account of existence as an attribute need not grant that every attribution has the same form as an accident inhering in a substance. Without first resolving what it means for the subject to be there, one cannot pass immediately to an infinite series. Likewise, speaking of an object's nonexistence does not necessarily establish an independently existent contradictory accident in that object. These are competing explanations of one and the same predication.

### I055.lower.01 — the regress objection depends on a particular interpretation of qualification

**Arabic anchors:** «(قوله أو ما يصدق عليه الخ)» · «التفسير» · «اتصاف» · «عدم».

**English.** The lower commentator argues that the regress arises under a specific interpretation of a subject's receiving existence. It may be true that an externally subsisting accident requires a subject, but the controversial question is whether existence itself is just another accident of this sort. If the subject has to exist prior to its being qualified as existent, the account becomes circular; if not, the meaning of the attribution needs to be distinguished from ordinary inherence. The explanatory terms cannot be substituted for one another without argument.

### I055.lower.02 — why the other side also fails if it simply makes nonexistence a positive attribute

**Arabic anchors:** «(قوله وأنا نقضه اللاوجود)» [reading uncertain] · «لا يصح» · «نقيض» · «الثبوت والعدم».

**English.** The glossator tests the opposing account: if nonexistence is made a positive attribute, a nonexistent subject will have to obtain the attribute in some way, and the same puzzles recur. But if nonexistence means simply the absence of existence, there is no need to imagine a further quality inhering in a fixed object. The difference between negating a predicate and affirming an opposite positive property remains indispensable.

### I055.lower.03 — blackness and whiteness as misleading analogies

**Arabic anchors:** «السواد والبياض» · «الصفات الثبوتية» · «الصفات السلبية» · «لهم».

**English.** Blackness and whiteness are positive qualities that can belong to a body. Nonexistence, by contrast, cannot be placed in the same class merely because the grammar of a proposition allows it to be predicated. A subject can be described as not black without acquiring a new positive accident called *not-blackness*. Similarly, to say that something does not exist may deny its being rather than give it another positive quality. This helps expose the difference between contradictories, contraries and the denial of an attribute.

## I:56 — 'nonexistence' versus 'the nonexistent'

### I056.upper.01 — the alleged logical contradiction

**Arabic anchors:** «(و)» · «اتصافه بالنقيض بل بالمعدوم» · «(نقيضه العدم لا المعدوم)» · «بطرق الاشتقاق».

**English.** The dispute is sharpened by distinguishing **nonexistence** (*ʿadam*) from **the nonexistent thing** (*maʿdūm*). If the contradiction of existence is nonexistence, it does not follow that “the nonexistent” is the same sort of predicate. The latter is a derived description applied to a subject; the former expresses negation of being. Arguments that treat them as interchangeable may therefore depend on an unnoticed shift from an abstract contradictory to a derived adjectival predicate.

### I056.upper.02 — why existence and nonexistence cannot be treated as two substances

**Arabic anchors:** «فإن كل صفة فائقة شيء» [source reading uncertain] · «الوجود» · «العدم» · «نقيض».

**English.** An apparent paradox arises if both existence and nonexistence are handled as independently positive qualifications. The author asks whether an attribution of existence makes the subject independently existent beforehand, and whether attributing nonexistence does likewise. The regress and contradiction can be avoided only by fixing the relevant sense of qualification. The upper text ends by opposing the *actual negation of existence* to the supposedly positive *object described as nonexistent*.

### I056.lower.01 — the two formulae 'existence is not there' and 'the nonexistent is there'

**Arabic anchors:** «اللاوجود عند القائلين بالواسطة» · «المعدوم عند المثبتين» · «عند النافين» · «العدم».

**English.** The marginal commentator distinguishes the views of those who posit an intermediary rank from the views of those who deny one. The former may call the nonexistent *established* but not *existent*, allowing possibility and conceptual predication without external being. The latter refuse to grant anything outside the exhaustive pair existence/nonexistence. The gloss warns that both sides use the term “established” and the term “nonexistent” under varying specifications; to argue against one formulation while quietly using another is not a sound refutation.

### I056.lower.02 — being a subject of denial does not establish a subject in actuality

**Arabic anchors:** «(وأقول)» · «كان الوجود معدوما» · «لو كان حالا» · «كلا».

**English.** “I say”: even if a possible thing receives the judgement that existence is absent from it, this does not force the existence of a substrate in which that absence subsists. The difference between the *subject as conceived* and the *subject as externally fixed* matters at every step. The gloss considers the objection that nonexistence must occur as a positive state and replies that doing so would contradict the very point of denying an extra-mental existent.

### I056.lower.03 — what does it mean to be an intermediate condition?

**Arabic anchors:** «الواسطة» · «ليست موجودة ولا معدومة» · «لا يمكن» · «الوجود لا صفة».

**English.** The alleged intermediary between the existent and the nonexistent may be said to be neither positively existent nor absolutely nonexistent. But if the intermediary itself is a subject of predication, the argument still owes an account of its ontological status. Is it an objective condition, a mental consideration, or a change in terminology? The commentator disputes whether the original formulation truly supplies a third alternative or only shifts the meaning of its terms.

### I056.lower.04 — the marginal author's cautious conclusion

**Arabic anchors:** «وهو بين البطلان» · «من شرح المقاصد» · «بطريق التفسير واللطف» · «تحقيقه من رسالتنا».

**English.** The gloss finally suggests that much of the difficulty may arise from the way the rival schools explain their terms. The same surface expressions are employed differently by those who admit an intermediary and those who reject it. A reconciliation is possible only if one shows that their actual ontological claims coincide; otherwise the differences cannot be dissolved by linguistic adjustment alone. The writer refers to another, more detailed treatment of the point and closes the discussion before the illustration at I:57.
