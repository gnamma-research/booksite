## P1.131 · Essential attributes, relational predicates, and the conditions of association

**Printed I:131 · PDF image 144**

**Selected Arabic anchor**

> هي الصفات الثبوتية الدالة على نفس الذات

**English argument-oriented first reading**

The text distinguishes affirmative attributes that point to the subject's own essence from other kinds of predication. Such attributes are called essential or 'of the self' (*nafsiyya*) in the technical scheme, and are contrasted with attributes expressing an added meaning or relation. The passage then asks when two features can coexist in one subject and what 'difference' between them amounts to. It is not enough merely to say that their names are different: their logical and ontological incompatibility must be specified.

## P1.132 · Contrariety and the impossibility of coexistence

**Printed I:132 · PDF image 145**

**Selected Arabic anchor**

> ومنها التضاد ... كون الشيئين بحيث يستحيل اجتماعهما

**English argument-oriented first reading**

The discussion introduces contrariety as a form of opposition: two determinations may be such that they cannot obtain together in a single subject under the relevant aspect. The precise identity of that aspect matters. It would be too crude to infer from the existence of two different concepts that they are mutually contrary; difference of meaning is not sufficient. The marginal examination tests whether merely asserting non-identity establishes a genuine impossibility of simultaneous predication.

## P1.133 · Motion and rest as an example of contrary predicates

**Printed I:133 · PDF image 146**

**Selected Arabic anchor**

> كالحركة والسكون المعلومين

**English argument-oriented first reading**

Motion and rest illustrate the claim that contrary states cannot coexist in the same place, at the same time, and in the same respect. Their incompatibility cannot be proved merely by observing that they are verbally opposed; one must specify what is changing and what is at rest. The commentary distinguishes a necessary condition for a particular contrary pair from a universal definition of all contraries. It also tests examples drawn from knowing and ignorance, where the relevant subject and capacity require careful qualification.

## P1.134 · Contradiction, contrariety, and unity of the subject

**Printed I:134 · PDF image 147**

**Selected Arabic anchor**

> متقابلان أن امتنع اجتماعهما في محل واحد من جهة واحدة

**English argument-oriented first reading**

The treatment specifies that contradictory and contrary attributions cannot both be established for one subject in the same respect. In contrast, opposition between two predicates of two different subjects need not prevent their coexistence in the world. The notes distinguish contrariety from contradictory affirmation and negation, and explain why the presence of a contrary in one subject is a different question from the absence of any corresponding state. The phrase 'one subject in one respect' therefore performs essential logical work.

## P1.135 · Why distinct subjects may exemplify opposed qualities

**Printed I:135 · PDF image 148**

**Selected Arabic anchor**

> كبياض الرومي وسواد الحبشي

**English argument-oriented first reading**

The text allows that what are called opposite qualities can be present at the same time in different subjects, as illustrated by whiteness and blackness attributed to different people. The contradiction arises only when the relevant unity of subject and aspect has been fixed. This makes it necessary to distinguish the definition of an opposite pair from the conditions under which predication is inconsistent. The lower discussion considers whether being simultaneous in existence suffices to disprove contrariety; the answer is no, when the opposed attributes have different bearers.

## P1.136 · The four kinds of opposition

**Printed I:136 · PDF image 149**

**Selected Arabic anchor**

> كالسوَاد والبياض ... كعدم البصر ... وكالأب والبنوة

**English argument-oriented first reading**

The classical divisions of opposition are brought together: affirmation and negation (contradiction), contrary qualities such as black and white, privation and possession as with blindness and sight, and relatives such as parent and child. Each has different conditions. Privation requires a subject fitted to possess the missing power, while relatives require correlated descriptions. Contradiction concerns affirmation versus its negation; contraries can be opposed even when neither is present in a given subject. These categories must not be conflated.

## P1.137 · Opposition presupposes the relevant unity of respect

**Printed I:137 · PDF image 150**

**Selected Arabic anchor**

> يشترط في التقابل وحدة الموضوع

**English argument-oriented first reading**

The exposition makes explicit that opposition requires a properly unified locus of attribution. The possibility that something is one in one respect and many in another must not be confused with contradictory predication. Negation does not demand a positive contrary quality, and privation differs from mere absence because of the subject's appropriate capacity. The lower register discusses whether the unity required is numerical identity of subject or a more precise identity of aspect, location and time.

## P1.138 · The transition from opposition to the nature of causation

**Printed I:138 · PDF image 151**

**Selected Arabic anchor**

> فصل في العلة والمعلول

**English argument-oriented first reading**

After completing the divisions of opposition, the text opens a chapter on cause and effect. Causation is discussed as a general consideration of being, alongside the types of priority and dependence already established. The first classification distinguishes causes internal to the constituted thing from causes external to it. The chapter asks whether a cause is simply that on which the effect depends, or whether more exact accounts of form, matter, agent and end are required. This sets up the next substantial philosophical comparison.

## P1.139 · Internal causes: matter and form

**Printed I:139 · PDF image 152**

**Selected Arabic anchor**

> فهي علة مادية ... وصورية

**English argument-oriented first reading**

The first kinds of cause are those that belong within a compound: matter, which is receptive to form, and form, which actualizes or specifies the relevant constituent. The question is not merely whether one must exist before another, but what each explains. If one says that matter makes a thing possible or that form makes it actual, it is necessary to distinguish the formal explanatory role from an independent efficient act. The lower register examines whether the existence of each is inseparable from the other and how their definitions can be maintained.

## P1.140 · External causes: the agent and the end

**Printed I:140 · PDF image 153**

**Selected Arabic anchor**

> والآخران أعني الفاعلية والغائية ... يرجع الشروط

**English argument-oriented first reading**

The companion division concerns the efficient cause and the final cause, rather than the two constitutive causes. Matter and form are classified as causes of quiddity, for a compound's account requires their consideration; the efficient and final causes explain its existence or the goal for which it is produced. The text also asks which enabling conditions should be counted with causes and which should be classified as conditions. The lower register tests whether a material/formal dependence automatically implies a dependence of extramental existence of the same sort.
