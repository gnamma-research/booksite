# Wasīm al-Kurdistānī — source collection and attribution investigation
## Parallel to *Taqrīb al-Marām*, checkpoint 3 November 2026

### 1. What is positively established, without conjectural attribution

**Primary combined printed title:** the user-supplied scan explicitly identifies the *Ḥāshiyat al-Muḥākamāt* of Shaykh Muḥammad Wasīm al-Kurdistānī alongside `حواشٍ متفرقة لبعض الأفاضل` (additional glosses by other scholars). Thus the lower-register corpus has **more than one possible writer**. The fact that a paragraph is printed below the rule does not alone prove that it is by Wasīm.

**Independent institutional cataloguing:** The Turkish Manuscripts Institution / Süleymaniye library record **338330**, collection **Tırnovalı 01048-002**, separately names `Hâşiyetü’l-Muhâkemât / حاشية المحاكمات` and identifies **Kürdistânî, Muhammed Vesîm** as its author. This is a *printed* item dated 1318 AH, described as 280+335 pages. In the same bound catalogue entry family, the parallel Sanandajī `Takrîbu’l-Merâm` is listed as **Tırnovalı 01048-001**, bibliographic record **338329**. Source: https://portal.yek.gov.tr/works/search/full/647600?search_form_type=ALL_FIELDS&search_key_type=2 .

**Independent Arabic library catalogue:** the Dr. ʿAbd al-Wahhāb library record for the 1901 Cairo second volume describes it as *Sanandajī’s Taqrīb* accompanied by Wasīm’s `حاشية المحاكمات` and further miscellaneous glosses. https://drabusulaimanlibrary.com/bib/15085 .

**Another relevant modern print:** The Tunisian National Library catalogues a 2009 al-Maktaba al-Azhariyya printing additionally mentioning attached glosses of al-Siyālkūtī and al-Ḥalabī. This is a bibliographic lead only; it was NOT collated in the present turn. https://www.bibliotheque.nat.tn/BNT/detailstatic.aspx?RSC_BASE=SYRACUSE&RSC_DOCID=5675475&TITLE=&_lg=en-US .

### 2. One quotation explicitly attributed to Wasīm in an independent published discussion

Dr Waleed Ibn al-Salah's 2022 discussion of whether new occurrences can inhere in God explicitly introduces **Muḥammad Wasīm** as a commentator, quoting a passage beginning:

> ثم أقول لا يبعد أن يقال المراد من التغير المحال عليه الحاصل بذلك الاتصاف ...

**Indicative close English:** “Then I say: one may reasonably hold that the change impossible for God, arising from His being qualified by that attribute ... [is His] being affected by the occurrence of something originated within Him ...” The rest of the quotation and working translation are preserved in the separate historical record `W.II.117.01` and `wasim-apparatus.html`.

**Important primary-source mismatch:** Dr Waleed cites this text to `تقريب المرام ج2ص117`. High-resolution inspection of the supplied scan's printed II:117 (physical PDF 412) and adjacent leaves did **not** find that wording. Accordingly this text is currently **externally attributed**, NOT source-matched to the supplied scan. Do not place it as a positively identified marginal passage at p.117 until the pagination/source problem is reconciled. Secondary discussion: https://drwaleedbinalsalah.com/2-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A3%D8%AF%D9%84%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B9%D9%82%D9%84%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D8%B9%D9%84%D9%89-%D8%A7%D9%85%D8%AA%D9%86%D8%A7%D8%B9-%D8%AD%D9%84%D9%88%D9%84-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AD%D9%88%D8%A7/ .

### 3. Four new Arabic lower-register passages collected for translation and author collation

These are **genuine excerpts from the supplied printed primary source**, but without an individually identified writer. They must be presented as *Wasīm candidates / other marginalia*, not as authenticated Wasīm quotations.

#### SAN31.LOWER.II289.01 — Two possible replies to the objection that faith is commanded and must be voluntary
**Printed II:289, source physical PDF 584. Status: INDIVIDUAL WRITER UNASSIGNED.**
Original Arabic: وحاصل الجواب السابق أنا سلمنا أن الإيمان نفسه مأمور به حقيقة لكن لا يلزم أن يكون نفسه فعلا اختياريا بل هو أعم من أن يكون نفسه اختياريا أو مقدماته اختيارية
Close English: The upshot of the preceding reply is this: We grant that faith itself is genuinely commanded, but it does not follow that faith itself must be a voluntary act. It is more general than that: either faith itself is voluntary, or its preliminaries are voluntary.
Scope: Direct selected stretch of a much longer lower-register discussion; the individual writer is UNASSIGNED. Original note continues across other arguments; not a full note.

#### SAN31.LOWER.II289.02 — An alternative reply: command attaches to the acquisition of faith
**Printed II:289, source physical PDF 584. Status: INDIVIDUAL WRITER UNASSIGNED.**
Original Arabic: وحاصل هذا الجواب هو أنا لا نسلم أن الإيمان نفسه مأمور به بل المأمور به هو مقدماته أعني إيقاعه واكتسابه
Close English: The upshot of this reply is that we do not grant that faith itself is what is commanded. Rather, the object of the command is its preliminaries—that is, bringing it about and acquiring it.
Scope: Selected contiguous argumentative sentence from a long lower-register objection; source speaker unassigned. The difference from the first reply is essential.

#### SAN31.LOWER.II290.01 — Dispute over whether assent is an intellectual state or an elective act
**Printed II:290, source physical PDF 585. Status: INDIVIDUAL WRITER UNASSIGNED.**
Original Arabic: فإن التصديق المعتبر في الإيمان هو قسم من العلم كما هو ظاهر
Close English: For the assent that is relevant to faith is a kind of knowledge, as appears evident.
Scope: Short original phrase from the lower register, not a translated note unit; individually author UNASSIGNED.

#### SAN31.LOWER.II291.01 — Distinguishing assent required for faith from another sense of logical assent
**Printed II:291, source physical PDF 586. Status: INDIVIDUAL WRITER UNASSIGNED.**
Original Arabic: التصديق المعتبر في الإيمان هو التصديق المنطقي
Close English: The assent relevant to faith is logical assent.
Scope: Source-selected phrase within an extended dialectical lower paragraph; NOT proof of its author's final conclusion or complete marginal-note text.

### 4. Additional collation requirement discovered at II:287

The lower register on printed **II:287** includes Persian explanatory wording interwoven with Arabic scholastic text. The language shift is visible in the scan. That text needs separate Persian-first transcription and translation; it cannot safely be treated as a Wasīm Arabic gloss solely because of its position. No definite individual author has been identified in that segment.
