## P1.091 · I:91 · PDF 104

**Arabic anchor:** أن كل حادث بهذا المعنى ... الإمكان الاستعدادي ... ما يحتاج الممكن ... إلى المؤثر

**Provisional English page account:** The discussion distinguishes the preparedness of something to occur from the thing's intrinsic possibility. A contingent essence does not, simply as such, determine itself to exist rather than not exist. It therefore requires a determinant for the preponderance of one of the two alternatives. The lower register asks whether this necessity of a determinant follows from the proper meaning of possibility or from a further premise.

## P1.092 · I:92 · PDF 105

**Arabic anchor:** ومعنى الممكن ما لا تقتضي ذاته وجوده ولا عدمه ... ومعنى الاحتياج أن كلا من وجوده وعدمه لا يكون لذاته بل لأمر خارج

**Provisional English page account:** The meaning of 'possible' is explicated as that whose essence requires neither existence nor nonexistence. Neediness or dependence is then explained as the fact that neither its existence nor its nonexistence arises through the essence alone but through an extrinsic factor. One must not treat the transition from this definition to an actual cause as demonstratively complete without examining the claimed relation of preference between the two alternatives.

## P1.093 · I:93 · PDF 106

**Arabic anchor:** الترجيح الذي قلنا بامتناعه ... بعض الإرادة ... الفاعل المطلوب الذي هو المختار

**Provisional English page account:** The dispute turns on what sort of 'preference' is supposed to be impossible without a further determinant. A freely choosing agent is not equivalent to an effect being necessitated by an impersonal complete cause; yet choice is also not a license to dispense with explanation. The passage asks what exactly is meant by denying a preference without grounds, and how a choosing agent's act is related to the selection of one among otherwise comparable alternatives.

## P1.094 · I:94 · PDF 107

**Arabic anchor:** فامتنع التأثير مطلقا ... التأثير في وجود الشيء وعدمه

**Provisional English page account:** An objection examines whether the agent's influence could occur with respect to existence, nonexistence, or the transition between them. It distinguishes a purported influence upon a state already obtained from the obtaining of that state itself. The underlying issue is whether an effect's occurrence can be explained without positing an incoherent mediation between being and nonbeing. Claims that an effect 'exists because it exists' or that an absence 'is produced' require different analyses.

**Margin, authorship unverified (Source-image transcribed lower-register excerpt · not independently assigned to Wasīm):**

> قوله فامتنع التأثير مطلقا أي إذا ثبت امتناع التأثير في كل من حالي الوجود والعدم ثبت امتناعه مطلقا

His statement, 'influence is impossible absolutely': that is, if influence is shown to be impossible in either state, existence and nonexistence, then influence is impossible without qualification.

## P1.095 · I:95 · PDF 108

**Arabic anchor:** الاحتياج للممكن ... وجود ما حصوله ... المرجح

**Provisional English page account:** The text considers a renewed argument that a contingent thing requires a producer because otherwise the occurrence of one of its alternatives would lack a sufficient explanation. A possible entity's essence is neither self-subsistent nor self-negating; the real question is what establishes its existent state. The lower-register discussion revisits whether the proof is circular if it presupposes the actual existence whose explanation is sought.

## P1.096 · I:96 · PDF 109

**Arabic anchor:** والخروج هو الإمكان أو الحدوث ... ولكل وجهة

**Provisional English page account:** The author reports disagreement as to whether a thing's need for an efficient cause is grounded in its being originated in time (ḥudūth) or in its intrinsic possibility (imkān). The former emphasizes the transition from nonexistence into existence; the latter emphasizes that a quiddity is equally related to existence and absence until an extrinsic ground specifies one side. Each account takes a different feature as explanatory of need.

**Margin, authorship unverified (Source-image transcribed lower-register excerpt · author unverified):**

> قوله فيه خلاف ... ووجه بناء الخلاف على الاختلاف في أن الصانع مختار أو موجب

His statement, 'there is disagreement over this': the disagreement is connected with the dispute over whether the maker is a freely choosing agent or acts by necessitation.

## P1.097 · I:97 · PDF 110

**Arabic anchor:** أنها هي بحسب العقل ... بأن يتحقق الإمكان أو الحدوث

**Provisional English page account:** The question becomes whether the determination of a causal ground is established by pure rational consideration or by facts about the extramental occurrence of what exists. One cannot decide the matter simply by saying the quiddity is possible: that leaves open the relation between the concept of contingency, the temporal origination of an instance, and the actual dependence of that instance on a cause.

## P1.098 · I:98 · PDF 111

**Arabic anchor:** أن الخروج هو الإمكان أظهر ... بأن الاحتياج ثابت للممكن في نفس الأمر

**Provisional English page account:** The position taking possibility rather than temporal origination as the ground of dependence is described as more evident. The possible is held to depend on something beyond itself in the order of reality, independently of the particular occasion at which it began to exist. The marginal argument carefully distinguishes conceptual ascertainment of possibility from an alleged externally subsisting entity named 'possibility'.

**Margin, authorship unverified (Partial image reading, source wording still requires letter-by-letter verification; author unverified):**

> قوله أظهر ... لأن الإمكان أظهر دلالة على ثبوت الاحتياج وأعراها منه

His statement, 'more evident': possibility more evidently indicates the establishment of dependence, and is less entangled with the objections under discussion.

## P1.099 · I:99 · PDF 112

**Arabic anchor:** وإن فرض قدمه ... لو كان الخروج هو الإمكان ... فلا حوج لإضافته

**Provisional English page account:** The debate tests the accounts by imagining a possible existent without a temporal beginning. If the ground of dependence is intrinsic contingency, a thing cannot cease to need a sustaining cause merely because it continues rather than begins. Conversely, if need is grounded exclusively in temporal origination, the explanation of persistence after origination requires further discussion. The page distinguishes the principle that a contingent being needs a cause from the question of what makes its subsequent existence continue.

## P1.100 · I:100 · PDF 113

**Arabic anchor:** معنى الاحتياج إلى المؤثر ... ما توقف الوجود أو العدم ... استمرارها

**Provisional English page account:** The text separates coming to be, continued existence, and recurrence. 'Dependence on an efficient cause' cannot be assumed to have one identical meaning in every argument: it may be taken as the obtaining of an effect, as the continuance of a state, or as the repetition of an originating action. The reader must also avoid making the continued existence of a contingent entity independent of its cause merely because no new temporal beginning is observed.

**Margin, authorship unverified (Selected lower-register note, author unverified; letter-by-letter recheck required):**

> قوله أو توقف استمرارها ... إن معنى الاحتياج إلى المؤثر هو أعم من توقف الوجود والعدم بل من توقف استمرارهما

His statement, 'or the dependence of their continuation': the sense of dependence upon an agent is broader than the dependence of existence or nonexistence alone; it also extends to the dependence of their continuance.
