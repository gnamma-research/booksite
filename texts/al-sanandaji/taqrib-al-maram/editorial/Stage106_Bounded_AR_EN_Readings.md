## I042.upper.02 — The self-subsistence of the Necessary and why it cannot characterize every possible existence

**Source Arabic (selected):**

(قائم بنفسه متميز من غيره مخالف لوجود الممكن في حقيقته) […] فلا يرد أن تجرد الوجود عن الماهية وقيامه بذاته إنما لذاته، فيكون كل وجود مجردا، لأن مقتضى ذات الشيء لا يتخلف عنه، فيكون وجود الممكن مجردا أيضا، وهو باطل لأن ماهية الممكن من حيث هي تقبل العدم […]

**Close English for the selected Arabic:**

“Subsisting through itself, distinguished from what is other than it, differing in reality from the existence of the possible.” […] It cannot be objected that existence’s being separate from quiddity and subsisting through itself necessarily belongs to its own essence; otherwise every existence would be separate, since what follows from a thing’s essence does not fail to accompany it. The possible thing’s existence would then be separate too. But this is false, for the quiddity of the possible, taken as such, admits nonexistence. […]

## I043.lower.01 — Ruled gloss: existence as consideration rather than a second external identity

**Source Arabic (selected):**

(قوله إذ لا يستلزم تجرده إلخ) بناء على أنه ليس الهوية المتحدة مع الماهية في الخارج، لأنه أمر اعتباري لا تحقق له في الخارج، كما هو عند المتكلمين، فافهم. […]

**Close English for the selected Arabic:**

On “since it does not entail its being separate,” etc.: this rests on its not being the identity united with quiddity in external reality, because it is a conceptual consideration (iʿtibārī) having no realization of its own outside the mind, as maintained by the theologians. Reflect. […]

## I043.lower.02 — Lower commentary on necessity and the whiteness–body analogy

**Source Arabic (selected):**

(قوله فلا يكون واجبا إلخ) ضرورة توقف وجوده على تجرده الموقوف على ذلك الغير […] (قوله فإنه قائم به في الخارج إلخ) […] فإنه بعد عروضه له ليس في الخارج شيء واحد يطلق عليه البياض والجسم، بل شيئان كل منهما متحقق على حدة […]

**Close English for the selected Arabic:**

On “it would not be necessary,” etc.: its existence would depend upon its being separate, and that separation in turn upon the other factor. […] On “for it inheres in it externally,” etc.: […] after whiteness comes to qualify the body, there is not one external entity that is called both “whiteness” and “body”; rather there are two, each realized distinctly. […]
