## I:57 — how can the nonexistent be differentiated?

### I057.upper.01 — the blackness/body counterexample

**Arabic anchors:** «كالسواد القائم بالجسم» · «لا جسم مع اتصاف الجسم به» · «الجسم ذو لا جسم» · «الوجود معدوم ولا وجود».

**English.** [The disputed description could attach] as one of the individuals falling under its contradictory—just as blackness inheres in a body. Yet a body qualified by blackness is not, merely for that reason, a nonbody. The reductio is that if one treats the presence of a predicate as the thing's becoming the contrary of itself, one will be forced to say absurdly that the body, precisely while a body, possesses nonbodiness. In the same way, it would be mistaken to infer from a proposed way of speaking about existence that “this existence is nonexistent” or is no existence at all. The point is to test what the claimed opposition actually concerns, not to collapse the levels of predication.

### I057.upper.02 — the differentiability of nonexistents

**Arabic anchors:** «(قالوا)» · «المعدومات متمايزة» · «بعضها متصور وبعضها غير متصور» · «بعضها صادق وبعضها كاذب» · «ولا يعقل التميز بدون الثبوت».

**English.** “They say”: Nonexistent things are **distinguishable from one another**. Some can be conceived and some cannot; some assertions concerning them are true and others false. But differentiation, the argument proceeds, is unintelligible without a kind of fixity or being-established (*ṯubūt*). For one can distinguish two items only insofar as they can be referred to, and reference appears to require something fixed as its object. Thus the opponents attempt to infer a kind of established status for the nonexistent from the fact that it can be thought and described.

### I057.upper.03 — from an object's possibility to its being established

**Arabic anchors:** «ولا يعقل التميز بدون الثبوت» · «(و) كل ممكن ثابت» · «الإمكان» · «ثبوت».

**English.** A second argument is introduced from possibility. Every possible thing, it is said, is “established” in respect of its possibility, even if it is not actually existent. Otherwise possibility and impossibility would have nothing to differentiate them before the thing's realization. This argument requires careful examination, since saying “it is possible that X exists” may be quite different from saying that X already possesses a positive ontological standing. [The final words lead into the objection on I:58.]

### I057.ruled.01 — the force of the blackness analogy

**Arabic anchors:** «(قوله كالسواد الخ)» · «اتصاف الوجود بالعدم» · «اتصاف الجسم بالسواد» · «المعدوم في نفسه».

**English.** On the blackness analogy, the smaller gloss denies that the relation of existence to nonexistence can simply be treated as equivalent to the relation between a subject and the blackness inhering in it. To say that existence receives nonexistence may negate the very existence under discussion, whereas a body's receiving blackness does not negate its being a body. An appeal to an ordinary attribute therefore cannot settle a dispute over the contradictory of existence itself. The note also challenges the move from a description's being conceived to its object being established in itself.

### I057.lower.01 — whether 'the body is a nonbody' follows

**Arabic anchors:** «(قوله كالسواد الخ)» · «اتصاف الوجود بالعدم» · «لا يكون الوجود موجودا» · «لا عذر فيه».

**English.** The longer commentator returns to the proposed example: in a body the qualification by blackness leaves the subject's existence intact, but an attribution of nonexistence to existence is not of that sort. The relevant inference depends on what precisely the subject is said to lack. If the predicate negates the subject considered in its very identity, the analogy breaks down. The objection thus turns not on the grammar alone but on the difference between a separable or accidental predicate and a contradiction in the subject's being.

### I057.lower.02 — does the formula 'not possible' differ from the absence of possibility?

**Arabic anchors:** «(قوله لعدم الفرق بين الإمكان له والإمكان لغيره الخ)» · «الإمكان» · «منفيا في نفسه» · «نفي ثبوته».

**English.** A further gloss distinguishes the negation of possibility from the possibility of negation, as well as “the thing has possibility” from “possibility is affirmed for something else.” The author stresses that an assertion involving the possible does not by itself make the possible object existent. Saying that a thing may exist is consistent with its presently not existing. One cannot, without another premise, move from the stability of a conceptual distinction to the established existence of its subject.

## I:58 — possibility, negation and conceptual distinctions

### I058.upper.01 — the proposed difference between possibility and its negation

**Arabic anchors:** «للفرق بين الإمكان له ولا إمكان له» · «ثبوت الإمكان للشيء» · «وإن لم يكن موجودا في نفسه» · «(ثبت موصوفه)».

**English.** [The point is alleged to be] the difference between “it is possible for it” and “it is not possible for it.” That is, possibility can be truly attributed to a thing even if the thing is not existent in itself. But it does not follow that the difference between the two judgements proves that the thing has an independently existing substrate of possibility. The proof's next step claims that, if the attribute of possibility is established, **its subject must be established**; the commentary asks in which sense the subject is being asserted to be established.

### I058.lower.01 — is the alleged difference already what is being disputed?

**Arabic anchors:** «(قوله يعني أن الفرق الخ)» · «وصف الإمكان بالشيء» · «بناء على اعتقاد الخصم» · «ليس أمرا موجودا في نفسه».

**English.** On “That is, the difference ...”: the intended contrast concerns possibility as a description of a subject and possibility attributed to or denied of that subject. But the distinction does not prove itself. It may presuppose the opponent's thesis that there is a fixed subject in which possibility inheres. The one who denies such a subject can still distinguish a statement of possibility from its negation, because the two statements have different significations without requiring the independently existing thing whose possibility is disputed.

### I058.lower.02 — the substance of a negative proposition

**Arabic anchors:** «الإمكان في حد ذاته» · «منفيا عن الغير أيضا» · «سلب الإمكان» · «وصف ثبوتي».

**English.** The commentator examines what it means to deny possibility “in itself.” If the possibility is a positive attribute, then denying it of another thing does not necessarily deny the very intelligibility of possibility; nor does its absence from some subject make it a nonentity absolutely. But if the dispute is about a nonexistent possible thing, it is precisely illicit to posit the fixed subject as part of the premise. The distinction between positive and negative descriptions must not beg the question of extramental being.

### I058.lower.03 — the form of the objection and the necessary reply

**Arabic anchors:** «ليس فرق بينها» · «التناقض» · «الفرق بين ماهيتين» · «قيل ... عدم الإمكان».

**English.** The gloss presses an alternative: perhaps the meaningful difference is only between affirming and denying an attribution, not between two ontological objects. If so, the opponent has shown a difference between statements, but has not established the subject in the manner required. The author considers the case in which denial of possibility is treated as a real property and the case in which it merely records that the asserted possibility is not available. The latter preserves the distinction without creating a new real existent.

### I058.lower.04 — why a different understanding of the subject changes the argument

**Arabic anchors:** «الممكن» · «في الأذهان» · «الموجود في الأعيان» · «ما لا يمكن».

**English.** It remains open whether the intended subject is the *possible quiddity in the intellect*, a really existing external individual, or something claimed to be fixed while not existing. Those are not equivalent. The marginal author insists that, if the argument changes its sense of “subject” midway, the appearance of a proof from possibility to positive fixity disappears. This opens the next printed page, where the meaning of *thubūt* itself is disputed.

## I:59 — the two meanings of 'established'

### I059.upper.01 — the external sense of existence is not simply being conceded

**Arabic anchors:** «(قلنا)» · «أن (التميز) يقتضي الثبوت في الخارج» · «ممنوع» · «إنما يلزم أن لو كان بحسب الخارج».

**English.** “We reply”: It is denied that the fact of differentiation requires establishment **in external existence**. That consequence would follow only if the difference were itself an external difference between external entities. A distinction made in reason or thought need not have two independently existent objects in the external world corresponding to its two terms. The distinction, therefore, proves less than its advocates require.

### I059.upper.02 — the being of a possible and of an impossible object

**Arabic anchors:** «و(أما)» · «عند العقل» · «اجتماع النقيضين» · «المركبات الخيالية» · «وإن أردت في الذهن».

**English.** At the level of intellect, one may differentiate objects even when they cannot exist externally, such as a combination of contradictories or a composite imagined only in thought. Such differentiation can be meaningful **in the understanding** while its object has no external existence. A thought can therefore distinguish and compare nonexistents without thereby establishing them as external things.

### I059.upper.03 — the meaning of possibility's being fixed

**Arabic anchors:** «(وأن الفرق بين نفي الإمكان والإمكان المنفي)» · «في نفسه» · «على تقدير كونه منفيا» · «ثابت أيضا».

**English.** The alleged difference between denying possibility and possibility considered under negation is likewise a difference in what is affirmed or denied. Even if we grant a stable notion of possibility, that stability may belong to a conceptual judgement rather than to the existence of the possible thing. To infer an existent subject from the conceptual distinction would again be to pass illicitly from the mind to the external world.

### I059.lower.01 — the absolute nonexistent and the nonexistent-at-present

**Arabic anchors:** «(قوله فلا يفيد)» · «العدم المطلق» · «لا وجود له أصلا» · «معدوم».

**English.** On “It does not establish the conclusion”: the reason is that the discussion concerns the absolutely nonexistent, not a thing that once existed, or that exists somewhere else, or that is merely absent at the present instant. One must ask whether a thing's absence is absolute or relative to a particular time or description. An earlier or later existent can be spoken of as nonexistent now without becoming an absolutely nonexistent object.

### I059.lower.02 — the argument from being fixed in oneself

**Arabic anchors:** «(قوله في نفسه)» · «الإمكان» · «غير ثابت أصلا» · «ثبوت معنى».

**English.** The commentator explains that “in itself” may describe the status of a concept under consideration, not an independently established extra-mental entity. If possibility does not exist as an external quality, it is mistaken to infer that the possible object must be established externally. But if the possibility is considered as something intelligible, that does not prove the ontological claim either. The objection turns on precisely this ambiguity.

### I059.lower.03 — why the denial of nonexistence does not yield external existence

**Arabic anchors:** «(قوله وإن أردت في الذهن فلا يفيد)» · «وجود الذهني» · «المعدوم» · «الإمكان المنفي».

**English.** If the required establishment is only in the mind, the conclusion is not the one the opponent sought. The mind can hold a concept of something that has no external being. But if an external existence is required, an argument that appeals merely to differentiation among concepts cannot establish it. Thus the reply distinguishes external fixity, intellectual presence and the truth of a statement concerning the possible.

## I:60 — possible and impossible predication; an affirmative judgement over an absent subject

### I060.upper.01 — why the possibility of a thing need not make the thing 'fixed'

**Arabic anchors:** «فلا يلزم من كونه غير ثبوت سلب الإمكان عن الممكن» · «ثبوت الشيء لنفسه» · «الخارج» · «الممتنع».

**English.** It does not follow from [a particular item's] not having positive fixity that possibility must be denied of the possible. Nor should the proposition “a thing is itself” be taken as proof that every conceivable thing is externally established. The identity at issue can be preserved in a predicative or conceptual judgement without requiring the independent existence of the subject in the manner claimed by the opposing argument.

### I060.upper.02 — judging a subject as existing, nonexistent or merely conceived

**Arabic anchors:** «(ثم كل من الوجود والعدم قد يقع محمولا)» · «وقد يقع رابطة» · «المحمول للموضوع» · «الحكم الإيجابي» · «وجود المحمول للموضوع».

**English.** **Existence and nonexistence may each occur as predicates.** A judgement may also make use of a *link* or copula between subject and predicate. When existence is predicated, the judgement is about existence; when a judgement is affirmative with a different predicate, the existence of the predicate *for the subject* is not necessarily the same as the subject's external existence. The distinction is crucial in statements about nonexistent or imagined subjects: the truth or coherence of the predication cannot simply be reduced to the subject's independent external being.

### I060.lower.01 — preventing a slide from conceptual denial to real impossibility

**Arabic anchors:** «(قوله فلا يلزم من كونه غير ثبوت الخ)» · «إنما ليس من ذلك» · «الصفات الثبوتية والمنفية» · «سلب الشيء».

**English.** The gloss explains that the intended reply is to distinguish the absence of a positive determination from the impossibility of thinking or predicating the thing. Negation may be genuine without itself being another existent positive property. One may deny that something is external or deny a feature of a subject while still entertaining a meaningful proposition concerning that subject. The author therefore resists a reading in which every meaningful negative predicate paradoxically becomes a positive attribute of an existent.

### I060.lower.02 — contrasting contradictory predicates with the subject's actual status

**Arabic anchors:** «الصفات الثبوتية والمنفية» · «الاعتبارات» · «قد يصدق أحدهما» · «كذب الأخرى».

**English.** The difference between contradictories can be expressed by the truth of one statement and falsity of its contrary, without supposing that the contrary is fixed as a second objective quality. Logical opposition belongs to propositions under a given interpretation. The commentary therefore asks whether the negative and affirmative predicates are being used in the same respect, at the same time, and of the same thing; unless these conditions are retained, an alleged contradiction may be only verbal.

### I060.lower.03 — the movement toward affirmative predication in I:61

**Arabic anchors:** «(قوله كما في وجود زيد الخ)» · «الوجود الذهني» · «عدم» · «في الزمان» · «اللاحق».

**English.** The example of Zayd's existence illustrates the difference between an object's existence at a time and the judgement of its existence. A past or future existent can be treated as absent now, although the expression is not senseless. The later and earlier temporal qualifications must not be allowed to alter silently what the predicate *exists* is taken to mean. This prepares the question addressed on I:61: **what unites the two terms in a true affirmative proposition, and what distinguishes their concepts?**
