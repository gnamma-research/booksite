# Taqrīb al-Marām · Volume I · Stage 85: Source-checked excerpt dossier

**Copytext:** Būlāq 1318 AH print, Volume I printed pages 13–48 (combined PDF pp.26–61).

**Scope:** 43 newly entered bilingual excerpts on 36 previously anchor-only body pages. The original Stage84 explanations are preserved in metadata but no longer replace the exact translated snippet in the normal reading view.

**Limits:** None of these pages is a complete all-register translation; unentered phrases, extensive lower ḥawāshī, and independent second proof remain open. No manuscript has been collated. Phrases are selected from legible spans of the printed facsimile; uncertain readings are not silently filled in.

## I013.upper.01 · printed I:13 · upper

**Arabic:** ويمكن أن يقال المراد بالعقائد الدينية المحمولات المنسوبة إليها

**English:** It may be said that what is meant by religious doctrines is the predicates attributed to them.

*Boundary note: The sentence has material on either side; no claim of exhaustive transcription.*

## I013.upper.02 · printed I:13 · upper

**Arabic:** وكأن المصنف رأى أنه من العلوم التي غايتها أنفسها فاكتفى ببيان حده

**English:** It seems that the author regarded this as one of the sciences whose ends reside in themselves, and so contented himself with stating its definition.

*Boundary note: The opening of the sentence and intervening argument are not completely transcribed.*

## I013.lower.01 · printed I:13 · lower

**Arabic:** من الذهني وغيره يساوي الموجود المعدوم في العموم

**English:** [The knowable], whether mental or otherwise, is as general as the existent and the nonexistent.

*Boundary note: Excerpt starts mid-sentence; this is not the entire lower note.*

## I014.upper.01 · printed I:14 · upper

**Arabic:** والعلم لا يحد لوضوحه واختاره الإمام الرازي لوجهين: الأول أن علم كل أحد بأنه موجود ضروري

**English:** Knowledge is not strictly defined because of its evidentness. Imām al-Rāzī chose this view for two reasons. First, every person’s knowledge that he exists is immediate.

*Boundary note: Selected first reason; remainder and ensuing dispute omitted.*

## I014.upper.02 · printed I:14 · upper

**Arabic:** والثاني أن غير العلم يعلم بالعلم فلو لم يعلم العلم لزم الدور

**English:** Second, things other than knowledge are known by means of knowledge. If knowledge itself were not known, a circle would result.

*Boundary note: The printed qualifying reply is still outstanding.*

## I015.upper.01 · printed I:15 · upper

**Arabic:** وغير العلم إنما يعلم بحصول علم جزئي لا بتصور العلم على تقدير اكتسابه

**English:** Things other than knowledge are known through the occurrence of a particular act of knowing, not by forming a conception of knowledge, even if such a conception has to be acquired.

*Boundary note: Part of the reply to the second reason on previous leaf.*

## I015.ruled.01 · printed I:15 · ruled

**Arabic:** هذا إن كان تصوره لازم حصوله كما في سائر الوجدانيات

**English:** That is so only if conceiving it necessarily accompanies its occurrence, as with other immediate experiences.

*Boundary note: Very short bounded ruled gloss; remainder not read as a continuous span.*

## I017.upper.01 · printed I:17 · upper

**Arabic:** وإنما فسرنا التجلي بالانكشاف التام الذي لا يشمل الظن لأن العلم عند القائلين بهذا التعريف مقابل الظن

**English:** We explained “disclosure” as complete unveiling, which does not include conjectural belief, because those who give this definition contrast knowledge with conjecture.

*Boundary note: The definition’s preceding terms are treated elsewhere; not a whole leaf.*

## I018.upper.01 · printed I:18 · upper

**Arabic:** وأما الترتيب الذي ذكروه في تعريفه فهو لازم من لوازمه

**English:** As for the ordering mentioned in their definition of inquiry, it is one of the things necessarily attendant upon it.

*Boundary note: The account of mental movement before this sentence is not fully entered.*

## I019.upper.01 · printed I:19 · upper

**Arabic:** (ولو في الإلهيات) خلافا للقائل بأنه لا يفيد العلم فيها لأن الحقائق الإلهية من ذاته وصفاته لا تتصور والتصديق بها فرع التصور

**English:** “Even in theology”: contrary to the person who says that inquiry produces no knowledge there, since the divine realities—His essence and attributes—cannot be conceived, whereas assent about them depends upon conception.

*Boundary note: Historical objection being reported, not an editorial assertion.*

## I020.upper.01 · printed I:20 · upper
