**Rapid English rendering (not a literal complete-page translation)**

The page continues the question of dependence at the stage of persistence. It distinguishes dependence associated with a thing's existence or nonexistence from the claim that the possible, considered in itself, has an intrinsic preference for one alternative. ### P1.106 · Necessity through choice is not the negation of choice

**Source:** printed vol. I:106; physical PDF p.119 (main upper register; note boundary beneath rule).

**Selected Arabic text (not complete transcription)**

> وهذا لا ينافي الاختيار لأن الاختيار من تمام العلة فلا يتحقق الوجوب إلا به

**Rapid English rendering (not a literal complete-page translation)**

The impossibility of nonexistence while the existent actually exists does not negate divine or creaturely choice: choice itself is part of what completes the cause, and the necessity that follows from a completed cause is not established without that choice. The text accordingly says that making the effect necessary **through choice** does not contradict choice; rather, it confirms its role in producing the effect. The discussion then moves from the three modes—necessity, impossibility and possibility—to more general modes of attribution.

### P1.115 · Five kinds of precedence and succession

**Source:** printed vol. I:115; physical PDF p.128 (main upper register; note boundary beneath rule).

**Selected Arabic text (not complete transcription)**

> تكون بالعلة والطبع أو الزمان أو الشرف أو الرتبة

**Rapid English rendering (not a literal complete-page translation)**

Priority and posteriority are distinguished according to cause, nature, time, excellence or rank. The motion of a hand is causally prior to the motion of the key it turns; this does not require the hand's movement to occur in an earlier interval. Priority in nature concerns asymmetric dependence, while temporal order refers to before and after in time. Excellence and rank constitute further orders that should not be conflated with either causation or chronology. The annotations test whether these kinds can be reduced to one another.

### P1.125 · Conceptual separability does not imply separate extramental existence

**Source:** printed vol. I:125; physical PDF p.138 (main upper register; note boundary beneath rule).

**Selected Arabic text (not complete transcription)**

> والمراد جواز الانفكاك بحسب التعقل دون الخارج

**Rapid English rendering (not a literal complete-page translation)**

The intended possibility of separation is **according to intellectual consideration**, not separation in extramental reality. Hence, although the world and its maker are distinct, this does not imply that the world can exist without its maker. One can consider the maker without considering the world; one can likewise frame the concept of the world without explicitly considering its maker, even if the maker's reality must then be established by argument. This is not the claim that an actually existent world could dispense with its necessary cause.
