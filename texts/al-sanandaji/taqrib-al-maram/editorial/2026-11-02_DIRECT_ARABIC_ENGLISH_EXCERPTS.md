# Four first-pass direct source passages: Sanandajī, Taqrīb al-Marām

**Date:** 2 November 2026. Four *continuous selected clauses* from the upper printed register of the user-supplied PDF, checked visually at physical PDF pages 119, 138, 152 and 153. Unlike the 40 page-oriented interpretations, these passages are translated directly from transcribed Arabic, although independent second-pass collation is not yet complete. The selections do **not** constitute complete printed pages; surrounding text and marginal ḥāshiya remain outside these snippets.

## L.I.106.01 · Choice is part of the complete cause

**Source:** Taqrīb al-Marām I:106, supplied source facsimile PDF p.119; continuous selected upper-register clauses; transcription first-pass

**Arabic**

> وهذا لا ينافي الاختيار لأن الاختيار من تمام العلة فلا يتحقق الوجوب إلا به وكون المعلول واجبا بالاختيار لا ينافي الاختيار بل يحققه

**English translation**

This does not conflict with choice, because choice is part of the completeness of the cause; necessity is therefore not realized without it. And the effect’s becoming necessary through choice does not conflict with choice—rather, it establishes it.

**Editorial note:** Continuous upper running commentary; question of 'necessity through another' and human/divine choice. Only this excerpt is transcribed and translated, not the whole page.

## L.I.125.01 · Mental separability is not separate existence

**Source:** Taqrīb al-Marām I:125, facsimile PDF p.138; selected continuous upper-register clause; provisional source-based transcription

**Arabic**

> والمراد جواز الانفكاك بحسب التعقل دون الخارج فلا يرد أن العالم والصانع متغايران ولا يجوز انفكاكهما لامتناع وجود العالم بدون الصانع

**English translation**

What is intended is the possibility of separation in intellectual consideration, not in external reality. Hence there is no objection that the world and its Maker are distinct yet cannot be separated, because the existence of the world without its Maker is impossible.

**Editorial note:** Separability of concepts under the act of considering them is not the same as the possible independent external existence of their referents. Do not read the former as a claim that an actually existing world could lack a maker.

## L.I.139.01 · The incomplete cause divides into internal and external

**Source:** Taqrīb al-Marām I:139, physical PDF p.152, upper running text; contiguous selected first-pass transcription, not the full page

**Arabic**

> وهي إما تامة أو ناقصة والناقصة ما كان جزءا للشيء أي داخلا فيه أو أمر خارج عنه فإن كانت داخلة ووجود الشيء معها بالفعل كالهيئة للسرير فهي علة صورية وإما بالقوة كالخشب للسرير فهي علة مادية

**English translation**

A cause is either complete or incomplete. An incomplete cause may be a constituent of the thing—that is, internal to it—or something outside it. If it is internal and the thing exists actually in conjunction with it, as the arrangement of a bed, it is a formal cause; if [the thing exists in conjunction with it] potentially, as the timber of a bed, it is a material cause.

**Editorial note:** The original argument continues with qualifications about a completed form, material potentiality, and the efficient cause. Bracketed words in English clarify an implicit subject; they are not directly printed Arabic.

## L.I.140.01 · Causes of quiddity and causes of existence

**Source:** Taqrīb al-Marām I:140, physical PDF p.153; selected running-text argument; first-pass direct source reading

**Arabic**

> ويخص الأوليان أعني الصورية والمادية باسم علل الماهية لأن الشيء يفتقر إليهما في ماهيته لا في وجوده فقط إذ لا يعقل إلا بهما والآخران أعني الفاعلية والغائية باسم علل الوجود لأن الشيء يفتقر إليهما في الوجود فقط ويعقل بدونهما

**English translation**

The first two, namely the formal and the material, are specifically called causes of quiddity, because the thing depends on them in what it is, not merely for its existence, since it cannot be conceived without them. The other two, namely the efficient and the final, are called causes of existence, because the thing depends on them for its existence alone, while it can be conceived without them.

**Editorial note:** The claims here concern the explanatory order of quiddity versus existence, not a universal denial that efficient causes may also account for formal features or the order of ends. Internal and external causal roles must be kept distinct.
