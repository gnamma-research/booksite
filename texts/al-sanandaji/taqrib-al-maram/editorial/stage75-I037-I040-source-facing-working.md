## I:37 — The three modes of existing

### I037.upper.01 — Shared meaning and division
**Arabic anchors:** «أن اشتراك الموجودات في الوجود معنوي أو لفظي»؛ «بأنه على اشتراك معنوي»؛ «وجود (الواجب) ووجود (غيره)»؛ «التقسيم المشترك لا يقال للوجود».

**English:** The question is whether the things said to exist share the word “existence” merely by homonymy or share an intelligible **meaning**. The position defended here is that existence is said of them through a common meaning, though the particular modes of existence differ. The text identifies several ways to analyze this distinction and warns that the common meaning should not be confused with one numerically identical extramental entity distributed among individuals. The Necessary and what is other than it must both fall under the relevant expression if the division is to be intelligible.

### I037.upper.02 — The threefold division
**Arabic anchors:** «(بنيه على اشتراك معنوي)»؛ «(صح التقسيم إلى) وجود (الواجب) ووجود (غيره)»؛ «وجود القسمة مشترك بين جميع أقسامها»؛ «كالجوهر والعرض».

**English:** The proof takes the form of a valid **division**: existence is divided into the existence of the Necessary and the existence of others, and the latter admits further divisions, such as substance and accident. A division requires that the divided term be intelligibly common to its members; it does not prove that the thing divided exists as a separate reality alongside all the members. The distinction between a logical common notion and an independent common entity is therefore crucial.

### I037.lower.01 — Three kinds of commonality
**Arabic anchors:** «الموجودات وجوه ثلاثة أحدها أن يلزم وجود أمر مشترك مع التردد في كل من الخصوصيات»؛ «فإذا نظرنا إلى وجود الممكن ... ممكناً»؛ «المعدوم»؛ «التقسيم إلى الوجود الواجب ووجود الممكن».

**English:** The marginal author says the argument from division may be understood in more than one way. When one attends to a possible thing, one can consider its particular manner of being, and also what it shares with others; when one attends to the Necessary, its being has a different determination. The fact that we can divide both under the expression “existence” requires a common *meaning*, not necessarily a common existent genus. Moreover, the distinction between existence and nonexistence must not itself be described as another division into independently existing species of a thing. The different logical statuses of the members must be retained.

### I037.lower.02 — True shared meaning and verbal equivocation
**Arabic anchors:** «(قوله معنى)»؛ «أن الوجود عند المتكلمين حقيقة واحدة تختلف بالإضافة إلى الماهيات المختلفة»؛ «وعند الفلاسفة حقيقة مختلفة بالذات»؛ «(قوله أو لفظي)»؛ «نسب إلى الشيخ أبي الحسن الأشعري».

**English:** On “meaning”: according to one account associated here with certain theologians, existence is one common intelligible content differentiated by its relations to distinct quiddities. Philosophers, the gloss reports, may instead understand particular modes of existence to differ in reality while sharing an abstract predicate. The author distinguishes such positions from the thesis that the term “existence” is **merely homonymous**. He reports an attribution of a stronger difference in the meaning of existence to al-Ashʿarī and asks how it is to be understood, rather than simply accepting that the two doctrines are equivalent. The closing remark on what it means to “join the special to the common” goes on to I:38.

## I:38 — Specialization and the common predication of existence

### I038.upper.01 — What is shared and what differs
**Arabic anchors:** «لا تتوقف على الوضع والعلم ولذلك لا تختلف باختلاف اللغات»؛ «مع الاشتراك اللفظي»؛ «(الأمر الثاني)»؛ «(يلزم مع التردد في الخصوصية)».

**English:** What is shared in meaning is not established simply by a convention of language; cognition of existence, on this argument, is not confined to the accidental distinctions among languages. Nevertheless, the fact that a term is used in several cases does not by itself prove that those cases contain one and the same external entity. The second argument considers the intelligibility of differences among the **specializations** of existence and the possibility of reflecting on them while retaining a general notion.

### I038.upper.02 — Possible, necessary and nonexistent
**Arabic anchors:** «(تمام الحصر في الموجود والمعدوم)»؛ «ولو لم يكن الوجود مشتركا معنويا»؛ «لزم الحصر في قولنا الإنسان إما موجود أو معدوم»؛ «المعدوم».

**English:** An exhaustive contrast such as “the human being is either existent or nonexistent” presupposes that we understand the negation being made. The opponent cannot explain that opposition solely by ascribing unrelated meanings to “existence” in the cases to which it applies. The text also considers the division of what exists into Necessary and possible. But this reasoning must be handled carefully: the terms of a division can be intelligible in the mind even where not every term names something actual outside it.

### I038.lower.01 — No external universal is established by common predication
**Arabic anchors:** «(قوله بل كان له في كل معنى الخ)»؛ «على تقدير أن يكون مشتركا لفظيا»؛ «خصوصية مختلفة»؛ «المفهوم»؛ «مطلق الماهية والتشخص».

**English:** The gloss differentiates the sharing of an **expression** from the sharing of an intelligible content. A single word may designate entirely different objects, but then it does not provide the common content needed for the proposed division. Conversely, when one grasps the common content of “existence,” one has not thereby established a universal existence outside the mind, over and above the particular existents. The author asks whether specialization is something added in the concept or something constituting each existing thing as that thing. Merely treating particularity as an accident of an independently existing universal would beg the question.

### I038.lower.02 — Two questions hidden in “existence”
**Arabic anchors:** «(قوله ما اتصف بالوجود)»؛ «(قوله فأي مفهوم من معانيه)»؛ «الوجود بأحد المعاني»؛ «المعنى الاعتباري»؛ «الإنسان».

**English:** On “whatever is qualified by existence”: the question is **which sense** of existence is being predicated. If one means a mental or relational meaning, this can be commonly conceived; if one means the reality by which the individual is actual, it does not follow that every individual actualizes one numerically identical external being. The gloss contrasts those possibilities through the example of “human being” and asks which predicate makes it proper to say that a person exists rather than does not. The final objection continues onto I:39.

## I:39 — Additional existence and the negation of existence

### I039.upper.01 — A shared meaning does not prove that every nonexistent is a thing
**Arabic anchors:** «يجوز الاتصاف بالوجود بمعنى آخر فلم يكن معدوما ولا موجودا بالمعنى الذي أريد»؛ «(على زيادته على الماهية ذهنا)»؛ «أن العقل يلاحظ الوجود دون الماهية»؛ «ثم الوجود له (عنها)».

**English:** It is possible for one and the same thing to be spoken of under different senses of “existent”; this does not mean that a thing is at once existent and nonexistent *in the same respect*. An argument that existence is **additional to quiddity in the mind** starts from the intellect's ability to consider existence without considering the particular quiddity, and vice versa. This difference of conception is not yet a proof of two extramental realities.

### I039.upper.02 — Two arguments for mental distinction
**Arabic anchors:** «(الأول)»؛ «(صح تسلبه) أي الوجود (عنها)»؛ «أي عن الماهية»؛ «فلو كان عينها لما جاز ذلك لامتناع سلب الشيء عن نفسه»؛ «(و) الثاني».

**English:** One argument is that the mind can **deny existence** of a quiddity: a quiddity can be conceived as nonexistent. If existence were identical with the quiddity in every sense, denying it would seem to be denying the thing of itself, which is impossible. The second argument appeals to the distinction between what the mind understands by the quiddity and what it understands by existence; the significance of that distinction is considered across I:39–40. The upper stream is explicit that the thesis under investigation concerns **addition in intellect**, not necessarily an added external accident.

### I039.lower.01 — What does the denial actually deny?
**Arabic anchors:** «(قوله يجوز الاتصاف بالوجود بمعنى آخر الخ)»؛ «مفهوم العدم»؛ «الوجود والماهية هو تغايرهما بحسب المفهوم دون الهوية»؛ «(قوله أي عن الماهية الخ)»؛ «سلب مبدا الاشتقاق».

**English:** The lower commentator says the argument depends on **what it is that is denied**. To say that a possible quiddity is nonexistent is not to deny that the concept of that quiddity can be thought, nor to place an external thing called “nonexistence” where actual existence should be. The difference in meaning between existence and quiddity may warrant denying one predicate of the other in thought, without proving an external quiddity lacking existence. The gloss distinguishes denial of a **derived predicate** from denial of the underlying source or principle of the predicate, and asks whether the inference from the former to the latter is licensed.

### I039.lower.02 — Necessary and possible existence remain different
**Arabic anchors:** «الفرق بين معنى الوجود والماهية»؛ «سلب الوجود عن الماهية»؛ «المعنى المصدري الانتزاعي»؛ «المحقق»؛ «الوجود المقابل للعدم».

**English:** The writer returns to the distinction between the abstract verbal or intellectual meaning of existence and the actuality that stands opposite nonexistence. If existence is understood in the abstract derivative sense, it can be conceptually set aside; doing so does not itself establish that the thing's concrete reality is removed or that the thing remains externally real in its absence. If the sense is actual existence, the matter is different. The objection is directed against sliding between these senses while asserting that one and the same entity can be both present and absent. [The final sentence runs into the causal argument on I:40.]

## I:40 — The third argument for addition and the causal regress

### I040.upper.01 — Causation presupposes priority
**Arabic anchors:** «(إفادة محله عليها)»؛ «ولو كان عينها لأفاد المحل الشيء على نفسه»؛ «(و) الثالث (اكتساب ثبوتها)»؛ «يقتقر إلى كسب الوجود»؛ «ثبوت الماهية».

**English:** The third argument concerns the quiddity's acquiring the establishment or actuality which it lacks in itself. If existence were simply the quiddity, would not the cause be causing the thing **as itself**, a peculiar causal self-reference? The argument appeals to the way the thing acquires existence; the analysis of what it is for a cause to confer existence must distinguish a thing's receptive capacity and an efficient agent. The text invokes the notion of “acquiring” being, but it does not follow that a pre-existing quiddity must already occupy the external world as its own causal recipient.

### I040.upper.02 — The theologians' response concerning the Necessary
**Arabic anchors:** «واستدل بها ... اختلاف ثبوت الماهية»؛ «(والحكم على أن حقيقة الواجب وجوده خاص)»؛ «فالوجود عندهم نفس الماهية في الواجب»؛ «قالوا لو كان الوجود في الواجب زائدا على الماهية».

**English:** The theologians have been represented as holding that the reality of the Necessary is its **special existence**. On that interpretation, in the Necessary existence is not additional to a separate quiddity. They argue: if its existence were an added feature of a quiddity, it would depend on something other than that quiddity, which would destroy the claim that it is necessary through itself. The discussion thus turns on whether necessity is explained by the thing's actual being or by an additional attribute that itself needs explanation. The last sentence joins I:41.

### I040.lower.01 — Does giving existence cause a regress?
**Arabic anchors:** «(قوله لأن جعل الشيء على نفسه الخ)»؛ «الحمل هنا أيضا هو الحمل الاشتقاقي»؛ «لا حمل المواطأة»؛ «حقيقة الوجود ...»؛ «الوجود الزيادة».

**English:** The marginal writer says the alleged self-predication is not always a case of strict identity: the predication in question may be **derivative**, not a claim that the subject and predicate are one in every respect. A quiddity's being qualified by existence is therefore not exactly the same sort of proposition as the quiddity's being identical with itself. Yet if one makes a distinct extramental “existence” into a further thing, it must in turn have existence and the regress returns. The distinction must be located at the level of explanation rather than by multiplying external entities.

### I040.lower.02 — Why the possible is not thereby Necessary
**Arabic anchors:** «لزم احتياجه إلى غيره والاحتياج إلى غيره ممكن»؛ «الماهية لا يمكن أن تكون من غير الوجود ...»؛ «وإذ ذاك يكون وجوده نفس الماهية»؛ «فيكون المعنى ...».

**English:** The gloss presses the objection: if a possible thing's “existence” is merely an intellectual addition, why does it still require an efficient cause, whereas the Necessary does not? The reply must distinguish **dependence on an external cause** from dependence on an independently existing accident named existence. An object whose actuality is given by another is possible, even though its quiddity and concrete existence are not two separately actual items. The Necessary is understood as having no such external causal dependence. The author notes that a purely terminological answer will not suffice; he proceeds to the problem of the Necessary's quiddity and the causal priority of essence in I:41.
