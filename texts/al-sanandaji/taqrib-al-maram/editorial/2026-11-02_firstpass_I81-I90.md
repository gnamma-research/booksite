## P1.081 · I:81 · PDF page 94

Arabic anchor (selected words, not complete transcription):

> إنما يستند إلى الفاعل القادر ... أو إلى الوجود الخارجي

English first-pass page account:

The page asks how a universal quiddity comes to be this individual. Even when several descriptions are conjoined, each can still be understood universally; their conjunction alone therefore does not explain numerical individuation. Two accounts are reported: that it rests with the freely choosing agent, or that it follows from actual existence outside the intellect. The argument distinguishes a nature's conceptual commonality from the determinate existence of its instances.

Lower marginal note, **author not verified**:

> قوله لأن كلا من المنضم والمنضم إليه

The lower-register note presses the objection that the constituent and the item conjoined to it can each remain universal.

## P1.082 · I:82 · PDF page 95

Arabic anchor (selected words, not complete transcription):

> أو إلى الوجود الخارجي ... تعين الماهية

English first-pass page account:

The explanation of extramental individuation continues. If a quiddity is considered in its common meaning, it is not yet made numerically singular by appending a second conceptual qualification. To appeal to yet another determiner risks renewing the question at each level. This section distinguishes the quiddity, its instantiation, and the account offered for its being this particular.

## P1.083 · I:83 · PDF page 96

Arabic anchor (selected words, not complete transcription):

> أو إلى المادة المتشخصة ... وإما باعتبار العوارض ... بحسب تعاقب الاستعدادات

English first-pass page account:

Another reported explanation refers to already individuated matter, while a further account appeals to accompanying accidents and the succession of predispositions that bring them about. Neither is simply identical to the account according to which determinate things depend directly on the freely choosing agent. The problems of individuating a material composite and individuating a nonmaterial entity need not have the same solution.

## P1.084 · I:84 · PDF page 97

Arabic anchor (selected words, not complete transcription):

> فصل في بيان الوجوب والامتناع والإمكان ... هي من الأمور العارضة للماهية بالقياس إلى الوجود

English first-pass page account:

Necessity, impossibility, and possibility are discussed as intelligible qualifications arising when a quiddity is considered with respect to existence. The attribution may concern existence simply or a predicate's occurrence in a subject. These qualifications are therefore not treated as three concrete objects separately added to the subject; their meaning depends on the relation under consideration.

Lower marginal note, **author not verified**:

> قوله هي من الأمور العارضة للماهية

The marginal discussion asks in what sense these modalities qualify a quiddity, particularly when impossibility is formulated as the necessary negation of existence.

## P1.085 · I:85 · PDF page 98

Arabic anchor (selected words, not complete transcription):

> الإمكان اللفظي ... ويقسم كل من الأولين ... إلى الذاتي والغيري

English first-pass page account:

A loose or verbal use of the word possible must be distinguished from a technical modal judgment. A failure to establish impossibility is not automatically the same as the possibility of a thing in itself. The discussion then distinguishes intrinsic and extrinsic modes of necessity and impossibility, asking whether the modal character belongs to the essence itself or through something other than it.

Lower marginal note, **author not verified**:

> قوله يقصد به تفسير مدلول اللفظ

The marginal note differentiates an explanation of a word's meaning from a substantive judgment about a thing's intrinsic modal status.

## P1.086 · I:86 · PDF page 99

Arabic anchor (selected words, not complete transcription):

> واجب الوجود لذاته ... وهو الله تعالى ... وواجب الوجود لغيره

English first-pass page account:

The necessary through itself is that whose existence is necessary by its very essence; the intended example is God. What is necessary through another depends on a distinct condition. A parallel distinction applies to impossibility. No inference from a nature's possibility to its actual occurrence is licensed merely by these modal divisions; the subject's necessity must not be confused with that of a predicate attributed to it.

Lower marginal note, **author not verified**:

> قوله لذاته

The marginal note examines the force of 'through itself' in the modal distinction.

## P1.087 · I:87 · PDF page 100

Arabic anchor (selected words, not complete transcription):

> امتناع الوجود ... أو ضرورة العدم ... من الممتنع

English first-pass page account:

Impossibility can be expressed by the necessity of the absence of existence. The discussion analyzes the contraries and contradictories of necessity, impossibility and possibility, warning that a bare negation of one expression is liable to equivocation if the precise sense of possibility is not fixed. The modal predicates are considered by reference to being and non-being.

## P1.088 · I:88 · PDF page 101

Arabic anchor (selected words, not complete transcription):

> الإمكان بالنظر إلى الاستقبال

English first-pass page account:

The upper running text turns to possibility with respect to what lies in the future. The longer smaller-type marginal discussion distinguishes unrestricted possibility from the temporal judgment that something can occur after its present nonexistence. This is only an abbreviated guide to the opening of that discussion, not a full translation of the lower-register dialectic.

Lower marginal note, **author not verified**:

> الإمكان بالنظر إلى الاستقبال

Most of this printed page belongs to a lower-register debate on temporal possibility. Its exact argument and authorial identity require further collation.

## P1.089 · I:89 · PDF page 102

Arabic anchor (selected words, not complete transcription):

> ويسمى بالإمكان الاستقبالي ... الاستعداد

English first-pass page account:

Possibility with reference to the future is distinguished from an assessment that relies on what has already occurred or presently exists. Preparedness or aptitude (istiʿdād) is then discussed in relation to the proximate conditions of occurrence. Such preparedness admits degrees of proximity, unlike the merely abstract absence of intrinsic impossibility.

## P1.090 · I:90 · PDF page 103

Arabic anchor (selected words, not complete transcription):

> محل الإمكان ... تحتاج إلى مادة أخرى ... وإمكان

English first-pass page account:

The discussion relates aptitude to its underlying subject. For events arising in material things, one must ask where the readiness to receive them is located before occurrence. A subject's receptive potentiality is distinguished from the broad logical claim that an event is not intrinsically impossible. The argument requires closer comparison of the notions of matter, accident and alteration.
