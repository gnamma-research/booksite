### Editorial disclaimer and authorial layers

This stage delivers **40 page-level interpretive English guides**, **21 newly selected short Arabic–English source phrases**, **four source-aligned matn/sharḥ records**, and **one translated lower-register passage of presently unknown individual author**. **It does not translate forty pages in full**: most source clauses and marginal lines remain untranslated. Parenthetical base-text words are al-Taftāzānī lemmata quoted *within* Sanandajī; Sanandajī's running prose is a different layer. The lower register includes Wasīm and other authors; no new individual note is positively authenticated as Wasīm. The previously attributed Wasīm II:117 excerpt is **not copied or retranslated**.

### Important source-image correction

An initial contact-sheet impression suggested divine speech continued through II:153 and the divine vision argument began only at II:154. **Larger original images correct this**. **II:149–150** contain a separate discussion of *baqāʾ* (divine subsistence) and *takwīn* (bringing into existence); **II:151** actually begins the vision discussion under the original **«فصل في أحواله»**. The direct source quotations and page records below are corrected to match, not silently left under the initial topical misclassification.

### Argumentative structure

- **II:141–148:** *kalām nafsī* versus expression in letters and sounds; whether the divine speech is an eternal perfection or an originated audible event; language, command, report, writing and gesture.
- **II:149–150:** the divine attribute of subsistence (*baqāʾ*) and the theological disagreement over *takwīn*, especially whether production is an additional intrinsic quality or a conceptual relation grounded in agent and effect.
- **II:151–165:** the original chapter *fī aḥwālihi* and the vision of God (*ruʾya*), Moses' request, the meaning of `lan tarānī`, and objections about direction, face-to-face confrontation, comprehension and bodily senses.
- **II:166:** the original **«فصل في أفعاله»** opens the dispute over divine creation of the acts of servants.
- **II:167–180:** divine will versus approval/command, human agency and choice, *kasb* (acquisition), the Muʿtazilī objection from praise and blame, and whether power/agency can be truly attributed to the creature without independent origination.

### G.II.177.01 — Acquisition (kasb) does not itself require origination

**Source:** II:177, PDF 472, SMALL TYPE under the first horizontal divider.
