# Taqrīb al-Marām — cumulative parallel commentary selection

**ʿAbd al-Qādir al-Sanandajī** · Working Arabic–English excerpts linked to Tahdhīb al-Kalām.

Only source-identified selections and short sections are represented here. These passages **do not constitute a full translation** of the two-volume commentary; Arabic ellipses are genuine omissions. The 50-page preliminary English draft for I:13–62 is preserved [separately](editorial/2026-10-31_recovered_I13-I62_english_working.md).

## S.00 — Sanandajī’s opening words

*Opening of the commentator’s own preface · vol. I, printed pp. 2–3 (PDF pp. 15–16)*

**Arabic witness excerpt:**

بسم الله الرحمن الرحيم. الحمد لله الذي هدانا لدين الإسلام، ووفقنا على تحرير المقاصد الدينية وتهذيب الكلام، والصلاة والسلام على سيدنا ومولانا محمد خير الأنام، وآله وصحبه ... وبعد، فيقول العبد الفقير إلى ألطاف ربه الغني عبد القادر ابن الشيخ محمد السعيد التختي: لما كان قسم التهذيب من الكلام الذي صنفه قدوة الأنام العلامة الثاني السعد التفتازاني، قدس سره، مع رصانته وجزالته مشتملاً على غرائب أفكار ...

**English:**

In the name of God, the Merciful, the Compassionate. Praise belongs to God, who guided us to the religion of Islam and granted us success in refining religious aims and in ordering discourse in kalām. May blessing and peace be upon our master Muḥammad, the best of humankind, and upon his family and companions. To proceed, the servant in need of the gracious kindness of his self-sufficient Lord, ʿAbd al-Qādir ibn al-Shaykh Muḥammad al-Saʿīd al-Takhtī, says: The kalām portion of the Tahdhīb composed by the exemplar of scholars, the second master, Saʿd al-Dīn al-Taftāzānī—may God sanctify his spirit—is, despite its firm composition and concentrated expression, filled with rare intellectual insights …

- The author writes in rhymed, ornamented prose. The English aims to preserve the sense and authorial voice without reproducing every rhyme or repeating untranslated scholastic epithets.

## S.021 — Knowledge, inquiry and proof as the introduction to kalām

*Taqrīb al-Marām I:8 · PDF p. 21 · main commentary*

**Arabic witness excerpt:**

الكلام عامة بمباحث العلم والنظر والدليل، لأن تحصيل العقائد بطريق النظر والاستدلال والرد على منكري حصول العلم مطلقا أو استفادته من النظر مطلقا أو في الإلهيات خاصة متوقف على ذلك. وبدأ بها رعاية للقاعدة في كل علم لتكون مقدمة الشروع، فقال: «الكلام هو العلم بالعقائد»، وهي الأحكام التي يتعلق الغرض بمجرد اعتقادها بالقلب، وأما التي يتعلق الغرض بعملها وإن كان

**English:**

The general discussion of kalām opens with knowledge, rational consideration and evidence. Acquiring doctrines by rational investigation and inference depends upon these subjects, as does answering those who deny that knowledge is obtained at all, or that inquiry yields knowledge either generally or in divine matters particularly. He begins with them in keeping with the rule that a discipline begins with what it requires as a preliminary. Hence the words, ‘Kalām is knowledge of doctrines’: doctrines are judgments whose intended end is that they simply be assented to in the heart. But judgments whose intended end is practical performance, even where assent to them is also intended—

- Kalām and jurisprudence are differentiated primarily by the end to which their respective inquiries are directed, not by the impossibility of either discipline making use of truths considered in the other.

## S.022 — The distinction from jurisprudence and the qualification ‘religious’

*Taqrīb al-Marām I:9 · PDF p. 22 · main commentary*

**Arabic witness excerpt:**

الاعتقاد فيها أيضا مقصودا فالعلم بها علم الفقه. والمراد بالعقائد «الدينية» المنسوبة إلى دين نبينا محمد صلى الله عليه وسلم، سواء بطريق الشريعة أو الحقيقة، وسواء كانت من الدين في الواقع ككلام أهل الحق أم لا ككلام الفرق المبتدعة الضالة، كذا في شرح المقاصد. فإذا كان المراد من العلم مطلق التصديق سواء طابق الواقع أم لا ليشمل إدراك الظني فلا يصح قوله «عن الأدلة اليقينية» أي المكتسب

**English:**

—are studied in jurisprudence. By ‘religious’ he means doctrines ascribed to the religion of our Prophet Muḥammad, blessings and peace be upon him, whether considered through the revealed Law or the truth of reality, and whether truly part of the religion, as in what he calls the discourse of the people of truth, or only claimed to be so, as in the discourse of errant innovative sects. So it is stated in the Sharḥ al-Maqāṣid. But if ‘knowledge’ is taken to mean assent as such, whether it corresponds to reality or not, so that mistaken apprehension is included, his phrase ‘from certain proofs’—that is, [knowledge] acquired—

- The qualification ‘religious’ identifies the kind of doctrinal claim being examined. It does not imply that every claim represented as religious is true.

## S.023 — Conclusive evidence, certainty and imitation

*Taqrīb al-Marām I:10 · PDF p. 23 · main commentary*

**Arabic witness excerpt:**

ذلك العلم عن الأدلة على أن المراد منه العلم اليقيني، لأن الدليل اليقيني ما يفيد اليقين، إلا أن يقال المراد بالأدلة اليقينية بزعم المستدل. ثم إنهم اعتبروا في أدلتها اليقين لأنه لا عبرة بالظن في الاعتقادات خلافا للعمليات. تخرج عن التعريف علم الله وعلم الرسول بالاعتقادات وكذا اعتقاد المقلد، ويدخل

**English:**

—does not by itself demonstrate that what is intended is certain knowledge, because a certain proof yields certainty, unless what is meant by certain proofs is ‘those held certain by the person adducing them’. They stipulate certainty for doctrinal proofs, since conjecture has no authority in matters of doctrine as it can in practical matters. By the definition so delimited, God’s knowledge of the doctrines and the Messenger’s knowledge of them are excluded, as is the belief of one who simply imitates. Included, however, is—

- This is a restriction on the technical definition of demonstratively acquired theological knowledge. It does not imply that God or the Prophet lacks the knowledge of doctrine.

## S.024 — The Companions and the subject of the science

*Taqrīb al-Marām I:11 · PDF p. 24 · main commentary*

**Arabic witness excerpt:**

علم الصحابة وإن لم يستدلوا في ذلك الزمان بالكلام كما أن علمهم بالعمليات فقه وإن لم يسموه به. «وموضوعه المعلوم من حيث يتعلق بذلك».

**English:**

—the knowledge of the Companions, even though at that time their knowledge was not called kalām, just as their knowledge of practical matters was jurisprudence though it was not given that name. [Al-Taftāzānī says:] ‘Its subject is the knowable insofar as it relates to that [aim].’

- The historical existence of knowledge is distinguished from the subsequent technical naming of a discipline. The quoted matn wording has been independently confirmed from the Cairo 1912 original.

## S.025 — The knowable, including questions of nonexistence

*Taqrīb al-Marām I:12 · PDF p. 25 · main commentary*

**Arabic witness excerpt:**

أي بالعقائد الدينية، لأنه يبحث فيه عن أحوال العالم من القدم والوحدة وغيرها، وعن أحوال الجسم والعرض والحدوث والتركيب من الجواهر الفردة وقبول الفناء ونحو ذلك، وعن أحوال المعدومات من الانتفاء وعدم الاحتياج إليها في اعتقاد كون صفاته تعالى متعددة موجودة، إلى غير ذلك مما هي عمدة المسألة ودليل لها، والشامل لموضوعات هذه المسائل هو المعلوم المتناول للموجود والمعدوم، وقد يقال المعلوم...

**English:**

That is, insofar as it concerns religious doctrines. For the discipline investigates the world with respect to origination, unity and other such conditions; bodies and accidents, their origination, the composition of indivisible substances and their susceptibility to annihilation; and nonexistents in respect of nonbeing and the question of whether positing them is required to affirm the multiplicity and existence of God’s attributes. These and similar matters constitute grounds and proofs for the theological propositions at issue. The subject encompassing such investigations is therefore the knowable, which extends to both what exists and what does not exist. It might also be argued that ‘the knowable’—

- The possible object of knowledge is wider than the class of existing things. That fact alone does not settle the separate question whether a nonexistent possesses independently established thinghood.

## S.20 — Why theology opens with knowledge and demonstration

*Taqrīb al-Marām, I:8 · scanned PDF p. 21 · running commentary*

**Arabic witness excerpt:**

الكلام عامة بمباحث العلم والنظر والدليل، لأن تحصيل العقائد بطريق النظر والاستدلال، والرد على منكري حصول العلم مطلقا أو استفادته من النظر مطلقا أو في الإلهيات خاصة، متوقف على ذلك؛ وبدأ بها رعاية للقاعدة في كل علم لتكون مقدمة الشروع.

**English:**

The discussion of kalām begins with the subjects of knowledge, rational consideration, and proof. This is because the acquisition of doctrines by way of investigation and inference, and the reply to those who deny the attainment of knowledge altogether or deny its derivation from investigation—either in general or specifically in divine matters—depend upon those subjects. He therefore begins with them, observing the rule that each discipline is preceded by what it requires as an introduction.

- Sanandajī explains the order of al-Taftāzānī's introduction: epistemology and proof are prerequisites for theology, not detachable preliminary ornament.

## S.01 — What belongs to kalām

*Direct image-based selected translation · vol. I, printed pp. 8–9 (PDF pp. 21–22)*

**Arabic witness excerpt:**

والكلام هو العلم بالعقائد، وهي الأحكام التي يتعلق الغرض بمجرد اعتقادها بالقلب ... والمراد بالعقائد (الدينية) المنسوبة إلى دين نبينا محمد صلى الله عليه وسلم.

**English:**

Kalām is knowledge of doctrines: judgments whose purpose is simply that they be believed in the heart. By ‘religious’ doctrines are meant those attributed to the religion of our Prophet Muḥammad.

- Sanandajī distinguishes what must be believed as a doctrine from practical rulings whose purpose is performance. Such a ruling may also be believed, but it belongs formally to jurisprudence.

## S.02 — The immediate conception of existence

*Direct image-checked lemma and explanation · I:33–34 (PDF pp. 46–47)*

**Arabic witness excerpt:**

(تصور الوجود) … ضروري

**English:**

“The conception of existence is immediate [not acquired through demonstration].”

- The two words of this lemma straddle printed pages I:33–34. The running explanation treats the statement “a thing is either existent or nonexistent” as presupposing intelligible concepts of existence and nonexistence. The commentary should not be made to imply that all ontological doctrines are self-evident.

## S.03 — When a producing cause is complete

*Direct image-checked mixed lemma and explanation · I:141–142 (PDF pp. 154–155)*

**Arabic witness excerpt:**

(وعند تمام الفاعل) بجميع جهات التأثير من الشرائط والآلات والقابل (يجب وجود المعلول)، فإنه لو جاز عدمه لكان وجوده بعد ذلك ترجيحاً بلا مرجح

**English:**

“When the producing cause is complete”—with respect to every determinant of its operation, including its conditions, instruments and receptive subject—“the effect must exist”; for if its nonexistence remained possible, its subsequent existence would amount to the preference of one alternative without a determinant.

- The bracketed portions in parentheses are lemmata of the matn as presented by the printed commentary; the intervening and subsequent explanation belongs to the running commentary. This passage supports the analysis of a complete causal nexus, NOT by itself a particular doctrine of secondary natural efficiency or a necessity constraining the divine will.

## S.04 — Separate intellects, angels and jinn: a reported doctrine

*Direct image-checked report · II:100–101 (PDF pp. 395–396)*

**Arabic witness excerpt:**

(وزعموا أن الملائكة هم العقول المجردة والنفوس الفلكية) … (وزعموا) (أن الجن أرواح) هي جواهر (مجردة لها تصرف) وتأثير (في الأجسام العنصرية)

**English:**

“They claimed that the angels are separate intellects and celestial souls” … and “they claimed that jinn are spirits,” namely substances “separate [from matter], possessing activity” and influence “upon elemental bodies.”


## S.05 — Wisdoms and ends in certain divine acts

*Direct image-checked matn and explanatory prose · II:201 (PDF p. 496)*

**Arabic witness excerpt:**

ذهب المعتزلة إلى تعليل أفعاله تعالى بالأغراض والأشاعرة إلى عدمه. (الحق أن تعليل بعض أفعاله) سيما الأحكام الشرعية كما في الحدود والكفارات وتحريم المسكرات (بالأغراض) أي الحكم الراجعة إلى العباد ظاهر (ثابت بالنص) … (والإجماع) … (وعليه مبنى) حجة (القياس)

**English:**

The Muʿtazila maintained that God’s acts are explained by purposes, whereas the Ashʿarīs denied this. “The correct position is that the explanation of some of His acts”—especially legal rulings, such as punishments, expiations and the prohibition of intoxicants—“by purposes,” meaning wisdoms that benefit servants, is manifest and “established by revelation” and “consensus”; “upon this rests” the evidential force of “analogical reasoning.”

- The examples—penalties, expiations and prohibition of intoxicants—help define ‘purposes’ here as wisdoms benefiting servants, not a gain or completion occurring in God.

## S.06 — The different purposes of doctrine and practical ruling

*Selective, image-checked Sanandajī explanation · I:8–9, PDF pp. 21–22*

**Arabic witness excerpt:**

[العقائد] وهي الأحكام التي يتعلق الغرض بمجرد اعتقادها بالقلب، وأما التي يتعلق الغرض بعملها وإن كان الاعتقاد ...

**English:**

Doctrines are judgments whose intended end is that they should simply be assented to in the heart. As for judgments whose intended end is practical performance—even though assent to them also matters—…

- This is a disciplinary distinction according to the primary purpose of inquiry. It does not deny the obligation to believe that particular legal rulings are true.

## S.07 — Religious as an identifying restriction

*Directly checked lexical explanation · I:9, PDF p. 22*

**Arabic witness excerpt:**

(الدينية) المنسوبة إلى دين نبينا محمد صلى الله عليه وسلم ...

**English:**

‘Religious’ means belonging to the religion of our Prophet Muḥammad—may God bless him and grant him peace …

- The point is an identifying qualifier for the doctrines studied in Islamic kalām, not a definition of religion as knowledge alone.

## S.08 — Certainty is not merely doctrinal vocabulary

*Directly checked excerpt · I:10, PDF p. 23*

**Arabic witness excerpt:**

العلم اليقيني، لأن الدليل اليقيني ما أفاد اليقين ...

**English:**

Certain knowledge: for a conclusive proof is one that yields certainty …

- The qualifier yaqīnī applies to the evidential warrant of doctrinal knowledge. This matters when distinguishing firm, true assent from mere psychological conviction.

## S.09 — Why the subject is the knowable

*Image-checked abbreviated explanation · I:12, PDF p. 25*

**Arabic witness excerpt:**

المعلوم ... [يتناول] الموجود والمعدوم ...

**English:**

The knowable extends to what exists and what does not exist …

- Sanandajī’s distinction allows kalām to ask whether a proposed object exists at all. It does not commit the text to the real extramental existence of every object conceivable to thought.

## S.10 — The end and benefit of theological inquiry

*Image-checked phrase within discussion · I:13–14, PDF pp. 26–27*

**Arabic witness excerpt:**

الفوز بسعادة الدارين ...

**English:**

Attaining felicity in the two abodes [this life and the hereafter] …

- This is the wider benefit of confirmed religious conviction, not a substitute for the discipline’s proximate aim of rationally establishing the doctrines.

## S.12 — Why inference involves an outward and a returning movement

*Direct page-image selected translation · Taqrīb I:17–18, facsimile PDF pp. 30–31*

**Arabic witness excerpt:**

(وحقيقة النظر حركة النفس) ... (في المعقولات) ... (عودًا) ... (على بدء) ... (لتحصيل المطلوب المجهول)

**English:**

In speaking of inquiry as the soul’s movement among intelligibles, two movements are distinguished. The mind first seeks the appropriate mediating principle for the question it faces; it then returns from that principle to what was sought, obtaining the conclusion previously unknown.


## S.13 — Customary concurrence: the Ashʿarī explanation

*Direct page-image selected translation · Taqrīb I:24, PDF p. 37*

**Arabic witness excerpt:**

(بطريق العادة) ... هذا مذهب الأشعري، حيث قال إن حصول العلم عقيب النظر الصحيح بإجراء العادة ...

**English:**

The words ‘by customary concurrence’ express the Ashʿarī account: knowledge follows sound consideration through the customary order established by God. The inquiry does not of itself produce knowledge as an independently efficacious cause.


## S.14 — Generation (tawlīd) and action through an intermediary

*Direct page-image selected translation · Taqrīb I:24–25, PDF pp. 37–38*

**Arabic witness excerpt:**

(التوليد) ... وهو أن يصدر من الفاعل فعل بواسطة فعل آخر صادر منه، كحركة المفتاح بواسطة حركة اليد ...

**English:**

Generation (*tawlīd*) means that one action proceeds from an agent through another action proceeding from that agent—for example, the movement of a key through the movement of the hand. Applied to inquiry, the view treats the resulting knowledge as an effect generated through the agent’s act of considering.


## S.15 — The necessity account of knowledge following inquiry

*Direct page-image selected translation · Taqrīb I:25, PDF p. 38*

**Arabic witness excerpt:**

(أو) بطريق (الوجوب) ... وجوب حصول العلم عقيب النظر الصحيح ... عند الاستعداد التام ...

**English:**

The third account appeals to necessity: with the relevant conditions completely prepared, knowledge necessarily follows a sound inference. This differs from the claim that an agent, by an act of inquiry, produces knowledge through generation.


## S.16 — Duty itself and knowing that one is under a duty

*Direct page-image selected translation · Taqrīb I:31, PDF p. 44*

**Arabic witness excerpt:**

... التوقف على ثبوت الشرع عند المكلف (هو العلم بالوجوب) أي وجوب النظر (لا نفس الوجوب) ...

**English:**

What depends upon the proof of revelation, so far as the person subject to the command is concerned, is knowledge of the obligation to inquire, not the obligation itself. The obligation may obtain in reality through the revealed command even before the addressee knows that it obtains.


## S.17 — A proof and the distinction between demonstration and indication

*Direct page-image selected translation · Taqrīb I:32, PDF p. 45*

**Arabic witness excerpt:**

(والدليل) هو (ما يمكن أن يتوصل بالنظر فيه إلى حكم) جازم أو غير جازم (وقد يخص) بالحكم (الجازم فيقابل بالإمارة) ...

**English:**

A proof is something through consideration of which one can arrive at a judgment, whether the judgment is certain or merely probable. The term ‘proof’ is sometimes restricted to what yields firm judgment; on that usage it is contrasted with an indicative sign (*imāra*).

## S.063 — Quiddity and the question ‘What is it?’

*Taqrīb al-Marām I:63 (PDF 76) · principal commentary*

**Arabic witness excerpt:**

فصل في الماهية ... ماهية الشيء ما يجاب به عن السؤال ما هو

**English:**

The chapter on quiddity follows the inquiry into existence and nonexistence, because consideration of quiddity as a subject of those questions is subsequent to them in that respect. The term māhiyya derives from the question mā huwa—‘What is it?’ A thing’s quiddity is what is given in answer to that question.

- An explanation of scholastic terminology, not proof of an independently subsisting universal.

## S.064 — Quiddity considered with accidents

*Taqrīb al-Marām I:64 (PDF 77) · principal commentary*

**Arabic witness excerpt:**

قد تؤخذ بشرط شيء ... تسمى المخلوطة ... ولا خفاء في وجودها

**English:**

Quiddity may be considered on the condition that something else accompanies it, namely its concomitant accidents. It is then called ‘mixed’, because it is mingled with those accidents. Its existence is not doubtful, since individuals such as Zayd and ʿAmr exist externally as instances of human nature.

- The mixed nature is the nature as instantiated, not an additional thing distinct from its instances.

## S.065 — Quiddity taken on condition of nothing else

*Taqrīb al-Marām I:65 (PDF 78) · principal commentary*

**Arabic witness excerpt:**

تؤخذ بشرط لا شيء ... وتسمى المجردة ... ولا توجد في الأذهان فضلا عن الأعيان

**English:**

Quiddity may instead be considered on the condition of no further thing, that is, without accidents and concomitants. It is called ‘abstracted’ because it is detached from them. Under that strict restriction it is found neither in minds nor, a fortiori, in external things: mental existence itself can count as an accidental concomitant.

- The restrictive consideration bi-sharṭ lā must not be confused with the unrestricted consideration lā bi-sharṭ.

## S.066 — Mental consideration and mental occurrence

*Taqrīb al-Marām I:66 (PDF 79) · principal commentary*

**Arabic witness excerpt:**

الذهن ... عارضا بلا ملاحظة ... الحكم عليها باستحالة وجودها

**English:**

One can intentionally consider an object without considering an accident as belonging to it, yet that very consideration may actually occur in the mind. Being in the mind can thus belong to what is considered as a fact, without being included among the properties attended to in that consideration. To judge a consideration impossible does not entail that it cannot be conceived.

- The intentional content of a conception and its actual mental occurrence are different respects.

## S.067 — The abstracted and the unrestricted quiddity

*Taqrīb al-Marām I:67 (PDF 80) · principal commentary*

**Arabic witness excerpt:**

من حيث ذاتها مجردة ... وقد تؤخذ لا بشرط شيء ... وهي أعم من المخلوطة

**English:**

From the standpoint of its own essence the nature is abstracted and opposed to the mixed nature; from the standpoint of its occurrence in the mind, however, it is a case of a nature accompanied by a concomitant. One may also consider quiddity ‘without condition’, without stipulating either association with accidents or abstention from them. This unrestricted consideration is more general than the mixed case.

- The three considerations are alternatives of analysis, not three independently existing natural objects.

## S.068 — Unrestricted quiddity and external existence

*Taqrīb al-Marām I:68 (PDF 81) · principal commentary*

**Arabic witness excerpt:**

توجد الماهية لا بشرط في الخارج ... لكونها نفسها ... لا جزءا منها

**English:**

The unrestricted quiddity is also more general than the abstracted one, for what is unrestricted may be truly predicated of either restricted consideration. It exists externally because it is identical in nature with what exists there alongside accidents, rather than a separate part added to it.

- The unrestricted nature exists as instantiated; the universality itself is not thereby another external constituent.

## S.069 — The natural universal

*Taqrīb al-Marām I:69 (PDF 82) · principal commentary*

**Arabic witness excerpt:**

التمييز بين المطلق والمقيد في العقل ... معروضة للكل ... الكلي الطبيعي

**English:**

The unrestricted and restricted considerations are distinct in the intellect, not necessarily in the external object. The quiddity considered as the recipient of universality is the natural universal. What exists externally is the nature that receives this predication, not the logical attribute of being universal considered in abstraction.

- Natural universal and logical universal refer to different aspects of the same act of analysis.

## S.070 — Individual natures and the intellectual universal

*Taqrīb al-Marām I:70 (PDF 83) · principal commentary*

**Arabic witness excerpt:**

وهي الأفراد ... الوجود حقيقة المخلوطة ... والقائل ابن سينا

**English:**

The unrestricted nature exists externally when individuation accompanies it; in this concrete respect it is the mixed quiddity and constitutes individuals. The nature taken together with the logical attribute of universality, however, is an intellectual construct, not an additional whole outside the mind. A different analysis attributed to Ibn Sīnā is then introduced.

- The text distinguishes natural, logical and intellectual universality.

## S.071 — Ibn Sīnā on quiddity as a constituent

*Taqrīb al-Marām I:71 (PDF 84) · principal commentary*

**Arabic witness excerpt:**

وتكون مادة وجزءا للشخص ... متقدمة عليه في الوجودين

**English:**

According to this account, the quiddity can be taken without including further attributes among what belongs to its definition, even when such attributes are subsequently added. It then serves as a constituent or matter of the individual, prior to the constituted individual in both mental and external order, since a whole presupposes its constituent. This must be distinguished from predicating the species of the complete individual.

- ‘Matter’ here designates a constituent under a specific metaphysical consideration, not necessarily prime matter.

## S.072 — Species and genus; transition to simple quiddity

*Taqrīb al-Marām I:72 (PDF 85) · principal commentary*

**Arabic witness excerpt:**

والثاني نفس الإنسان والثالث جنس محمول عليه ... ثم الماهية البسيطة

**English:**

The relevant second consideration concerns human nature itself, and the third a genus predicated of it. The analysis now turns to simple quiddity.

- Only the principal large-type text on this page is translated here; the other printed register is separate.

## S.073 — Avicenna and the three ways of considering quiddity

*Taqrīb I:73 · PDF p. 86 · principal text*

**Arabic witness excerpt:**

الماهية المأخوذة بشرط لا شيء ... فلا يكون ذلك المعنى مقولا على المجموع المركب حال المقارنة بل جزء منه ومادته

**English:**

The exposition returns to Ibn Sīnā’s account in the Shifāʾ and its clarification in the commentary on the Ishārāt. A quiddity considered with the exclusion of additional features is understood in its meaning alone; in a composite it is a constituent, not a predicate naming the complete whole. Taken with a further qualification it can name the constituted whole. Taken without either stipulation it can be predicated more generally. Thus animality in one consideration is a constituent of the human being, in another is tied to the specific nature of the human, and in a third is a genus predicable of human beings and other animals. The point is to compare the later technical distinctions with the different uses suggested by Ibn Sīnā.

- Predication of a nature must be distinguished from the nature's status as a constituent of a composite.

## S.074 — Real composition and the reduction of plurality

*Taqrīb I:74 · PDF p. 87 · principal text*

**Arabic witness excerpt:**

ولا يخفى وجود الماهية المركبة ... ولا بد من انتهائها إلى البسيطة ... ثم التركيب قد يكون حقيقيا ... وقد يكون اعتباريا

**English:**

There can be no doubt that composite quiddities exist. Their analysis must terminate in simple units, since unity is a principle of plurality: neither a finite nor an infinite multitude could exist without units. Composition is real when several things constitute one nature with its own consequences. It is considerational when the intellect treats a plurality as one without an additional externally existing whole, as when soldiers are counted as an army. The parts of a genuinely real composite are related by actual dependence.

- The principle of unity need not be identified with indivisible physical atoms. The distinction is between real constitution and intellectual grouping.

## S.075 — Dependence in a compound: one-sided or reciprocal

*Taqrīb I:75 · PDF p. 88 · principal text*

**Arabic witness excerpt:**

ثم الاحتياج قد يكون من جانب واحد ... وقد يكون من الجانبين كالهيولى والصورة ... بخلاف المركب الاعتباري

**English:**

In a real composite the need among constituents can be one-sided, as when a mineral, vegetal or animal form depends upon underlying elemental materials. It may instead be reciprocal, as is said of matter and form: matter persists through form, while a material form is individuated through matter. This does not mean that the two are separately complete things subsequently placed alongside one another. The merely considerational composite differs because its unity is due to an intellectual grouping of members.

- Persistence of matter and individuation of a material form belong to different respects of mutual dependence.

## S.076 — The army and the question of made quiddities

*Taqrīb I:76 · PDF p. 89 · principal text*

**Arabic witness excerpt:**

ليس من العسكر في الخارج إلا تلك الأفراد ... المَجعولية الاحتياج إلى الفاعل ... احتياج الممكن إلى الفاعل من لوازم الماهية

**English:**

The grouping of soldiers does not introduce a further independent external substance called an army. If the collective arrangement itself is merely notional, making it an additional constituent does not establish an independently existing compound. The commentator then asks what it means to say that a quiddity is made. If made-ness means dependence upon an agent, that dependence follows necessarily from the contingent thing. This must still be distinguished from the claim that the bare meaning of a quiddity is itself another product existing alongside the realised individual.

- The dispute over jaʿl does not disappear merely by noting a contingent thing's dependence; it concerns precisely what is said to be made.

## S.077 — Essential concomitants and the conclusion of the dispute

*Taqrīb I:77 · PDF p. 90 · principal text*

**Arabic witness excerpt:**

كزوجية الأربعة ... فإن احتياج الممكن إلى العلة ضروري ... فعلى ما ذكر يكون الخلاف لفظيا

**English:**

A particular finite magnitude is not part of what is meant by body, for a body can be conceived without that magnitude and still count as body. That differs from the evenness of four: were four conceived as not even, it would not be four. If the opponents mean no more than the distinction already explained, the disagreement is verbal, since the dependence of the contingent upon a cause is necessary. The treatise now passes from existence and quiddity to their concomitants.

- A feature may necessarily accompany a realised thing without being included in its essential definition.

## S.078 — The opening of determination and individuation

*Taqrīb I:78 · PDF p. 91 · principal text*

**Arabic witness excerpt:**

فصل في التعين ... غير ماهيته ووجوده ووحدته ... وإن كان التعين مفهوما كليا

**English:**

The individual determination discussed in the Sharḥ al-Maqāṣid differs from a thing's quiddity, existence and unity, since the concepts of these can be shared, whereas the individual's own determination is proper to it. The concept of determination considered generally can itself be universal; that does not make any one individual's determination numerically common to many. The argument continues into the next page.

- A common concept of determination is not an individual determination common in number to every instance.

## S.079 — Determination as a common concept and an individual reality

*Taqrīb I:79 · PDF p. 92 · principal text*

**Arabic witness excerpt:**

أن التعين وجودي أو عدمي حقيقي أو اعتباري ... لا بد في تحقيقه أولا من بيان ما هو المراد من هذه الألفاظ

**English:**

The discussion distinguishes the common concepts of determination and individuation from the particular determination of an individual. Each individual is determinate in itself and distinguishable from another, though general terms can be applied to both. Before deciding whether determination is positive or negative, real or merely considerational, the meanings of those paired alternatives must be made clear.

- The classification of determination as real or considerational cannot precede a definition of those terms.

## S.080 — Privation, positive determination and nafs al-amr

*Taqrīb I:80 · PDF p. 93 · principal text*

**Arabic witness excerpt:**

التعين والتشخص ... العَدَمي ... الحقيقي ... في نفس الأمر ... الاعتباري بخلافه

**English:**

Determination can name the individual's inability to be numerically shared, or what is held to account for that inability. A negative condition can mean simple nonexistence, a specified privation, or a concept that includes negation. A real condition is what obtains in nafs al-amr rather than solely through an act of intellectual stipulation. These distinctions allow the commentator to test whether an alleged individuating principle is genuine.

- Nafs al-amr denotes the way the matter stands independent of arbitrary stipulation; it need not name an additional physical or mental region.
