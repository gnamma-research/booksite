## 833 · The possibility of essential knowledge; transmitted testimonies

### Arabic

الرجوع إليهم في معانيها، فكذا للصوفية مصطلحات ينبغي أن يرجع إليهم في معاني حقائقها كلفظ البقاء، والفناء، والمقام، والحال، والجمع، والتفرقة، والفقر، والغنى، والإثبات، والمحو، والعلم، والمعرفة، فالحكم عليهم قبل الاستفسار عن المراد بالزندقة والإلحاد رمي في عمائه.

البحث الثاني: في بيان أن العلم بحقيقة الله، هل يقع أم لا، وكذا هل يجوز أم لا؟
قال بعض المتصوفة، وبعض أصحاب الشافعي كالغزالي وإمام الحرمين، وجمهور المحققين، وكذا الفلاسفة: إن حقيقة الله تعالى غير معلومة للبشر، وقد خالف فيه كثير من المتكلمين، ودلائل الفريقين مذكورة في الكتب الكلامية مستوفاة طويناها مخافة الإملال.
أما في جواز العلم بحقيقة الله ففيه خلاف الفلاسفة، وهم لا يجوزون، لأن المعقول إما بالبديهة، وليست ببديهية، وإما بالنظر، والنظر إما في الرسم ولا يفيد الحقيقة، وإما في الحد، ولا يمكن تحديدها لعدم التركيب، الجواب: منع حصر المدرك بالكنه في البديهة، والحد لجواز خلق الله تعالى علما متعلقا بما ليس ضروريا في شخص بلا سابقة نظر، إما بالإلهام والوحي، أو بتصفية الباطن، أو بقلب النظري ضروريا، وأيضا فالرسم وإن لم يجب أن يفيد الحقيقة فلا يمتنع أن يفيد، ومن المحققين من توقف كالقاضي أبي بكر، وضرار بن عمرو من المتقدمين، وروي عن ضرار أنه يزعم أن الله تعالى ماهية لا نعرفها الآن، لكن في قدرته أن يخلق حاسة سادسة ندرك بها خاصة ذاته تعالى.

الفصل الأول:
في النقل عن الله تعالى وعن رسوله وعن الثقات بأن العقول قاصرة عن معرفة الله تعالى بالكنه
قال الله تعالى: ﴿ولا يحيطون به علما﴾ [طه: 110]، وقال النبي عليه السلام: لو عرفتم الله حق معرفته لعلمتم العلم الذي ليس معه جهل، وما بلغ أحد ذلك، وقيل: ولا أنت؟ فقال عليه السلام: ولا أنا، الله أعظم من أن يبلغ أحد أمره، وقال داود عليه السلام: «سبحان من جعل اعتراف العبد بالعجز عن شكره شكرا كما جعل اعترافه بالعجز عن معرفته معرفة».
وروي عن سهل بن عبد الله التستري أنه سئل عن ذات الله تعالى فقال: «ذات الله موصوفة بالعلم غير مدركة بالإحاطة»، «فقد حجب الخلق عن معرفة كنه ذاته، ودلهم عليه بآياته»، وعنه أيضا أنه قال: «غاية المعرفة الدهشة والحيرة»، ونقل الإمام القشيري عن يوسف بن حسين أنه قال: «سمعت ذا النون المصري يقول: وقد سئل عن التوحيد مهما تقول في نفسك شيء فالله بخلافه، وقال أيضا: أعرف الناس بالله أشدهم تحيرا»، ولهذا قال

### English

—be sought from them, so the Sufis have technical expressions whose true meanings should be sought from them: subsistence, annihilation, station, state, gathering, differentiation, poverty, richness, affirmation, effacement, knowledge, and recognition. To judge them guilty of heresy and deviation before asking what they intend is to shoot blindly.

Second inquiry: whether knowledge of God's very reality occurs, and likewise whether it is possible.

Some Sufis, some followers of al-Shāfiʿī, such as al-Ghazālī and the Imam of the Two Sanctuaries, the majority of the verifying scholars, and likewise the philosophers say that God's reality is not known to human beings. Many theologians have disagreed. The arguments of both parties are set out fully in books of theology; we omit them here for fear of causing weariness.

Concerning the possibility of knowing God's reality, the philosophers dissent and do not allow it. An intelligible object, they argue, is known either immediately—and this reality is not immediately evident—or through inquiry. Inquiry proceeds either through a description, which does not yield the reality, or through a definition; but this reality cannot be defined, because it lacks composition. The answer is to deny that apprehension of a thing in its very essence is confined to immediate knowledge and definition. God may create in a person knowledge concerning what is not necessarily known, without preceding inquiry: through inspiration and revelation, purification of the inward being, or conversion of inferential knowledge into immediate knowledge. Moreover, although a description need not yield the reality, it is not impossible that it should. Some verifying scholars have suspended judgement, such as Qāḍī Abū Bakr and, among the early figures, Ḍirār b. ʿAmr. It is reported that Ḍirār maintained that God has a quiddity we do not now know, but that it lies within His power to create a sixth sense by which we apprehend what is distinctive to His Essence.

CHAPTER ONE
Transmissions from God, His Messenger, and trustworthy authorities showing that intellects are inadequate to knowing God in His very essence.

God says: “They do not encompass Him in knowledge” [Q 20:110]. The Prophet is reported to have said: “Were you to know God as He deserves to be known, you would possess knowledge accompanied by no ignorance. No one has attained that.” It was asked: “Not even you?” He replied: “Not even I. God is too great for anyone to attain His affair.” David is reported to have said: “Glory to Him who made the servant's acknowledgement of inability to thank Him a thanksgiving, just as He made acknowledgement of inability to know Him a recognition.”

Sahl b. ʿAbd Allāh al-Tustarī, asked about God's Essence, is reported to have answered: “God's Essence is characterized by being known, but is not apprehended by encompassing it”; “He has veiled creatures from knowing the ultimate reality of His Essence and has pointed them toward Him through His signs.” He also said: “The limit of recognition is astonishment and bewilderment.” The Imam al-Qushayrī transmits from Yūsuf b. Ḥusayn: “I heard Dhū l-Nūn al-Miṣrī, asked about unification, say: Whatever thing you formulate within yourself, God is other than it.” He also said: “Those who know God best are the most bewildered.” For this reason he [the Prophet] said—

### Editorial notes

1. Historical reports are translated as attributed by this treatise, not certified as authentic Prophetic hadith. The earlier reconstruction on p. 833 invented a different report beginning “Were You to punish”; it is superseded here.
2. «موصوفة بالعلم» is rendered in context as “characterized by being known,” contrasting knowledge with encompassing apprehension; “characterized by knowledge” is also grammatically possible.
3. Actual attainment and logical possibility are distinct questions in the source. The reply to the philosophers denies an impossibility argument; it does not prove actual unrestricted comprehension.

## 834 · The sayings on bewilderment and the limits of intellect

### Arabic

عليه السلام: «رب زدني فيك تحيرا»، وقال يوسف بن حسين: «من وقع في بحار التوحيد لا يزداد على مر الأيام إلا ظماء»، ووقف رجل على حسين بن منصور الحلاج فقال: «من الحق الذي تشيرون، فقال مولى الأنام ولا يعقل».
وقيل للشبلي: «أخبرنا عن توحيد مجرد بلسان حق مفرد، فقال: من أشار إليه ثنوى، ومن كيفه وثني، ومن نطق فيه فهو غافل، ومن سكت عنه فهو جاهل، ومن وهم أنه واصل فليس له حاصل، ومن ظن أنه قريب فهو بعيد، ومن توهم أنه واجد فهو فاقد، وكل ما ميزتموه بأوهامكم، وأدركتموه بعقولكم في أتم معانيكم، فهو مصروف مردود إليكم محدث مصنوع مثلكم».
وقال الشبلي أيضا: «ما شم روائح التوحيد من تصور عنده التوحيد». قال ابن عطا رضي الله عنه: «العقل خلق آلة للعبودية، لا للإشراف على الربوبية».
وقال غيره: «العقل يجول حول الكون، فإذا نظر إلى الملكوت ذاب». وقال الإمام الرازي: «اعلم أن الكمل من أهل الحقيقة لم يتكلموا في المعرفة بأكثر من الاعتراف بالعجز عنها». ولهذا قال بعضهم: «الحق لا يعرفه سواه». كما قال أبو بكر الصديق رضي الله عنه: «الحمد لله الذي لم يجعل للخلق سبيلا إلى معرفته إلا بالعجز عن معرفته».
وقال الواسطي رضي الله عنه: «لا تفتح المعرفة للعبد، وفيه افتقار إلى الله تعالى أو استغناء به؛ لأنهما من أمارات بقاء العبد والعارف فناء كله». وقال قطب الأقطاب عبد القادر الجيلي:
«حجب الألباب برداء كبريائه عن معرفة كنه ذاته، وحسر الأبصار بنور بقائه عن إدراك حقيقة أحديته». وقال أيضا: «لا يتصوره الأوهام، ولا يقدره الأفهام، ولا يدرك بالقياس، ولا يمثل بالناس، ولا يكيفه العقول، ولا يحده الأذهان، جل أن يشبه بما صنعه، أو يضاهي إلى ما اخترعه، وقال أيضا: الإيمان يثبت الحق بعلم اليقين تصديقا، والاطلاع على علم حقيقته تعالى غيب لا مجال للعقل في إدراكه، وكلما حكاه الوهم، أو جلاه الفهم، أو تخيله العقل وتصوره الذهن، فعظمة الله وكبرياؤه بخلاف ذلك، هو الأول، والآخر، والظاهر، والباطن، وهو بكل شيء عليم». وقال علي كرم الله وجهه: إن المعرفة أن تعرف أن كل ما تصور في وهمك فالله تعالى بخلافه.

### English

—“My Lord, increase me in bewilderment concerning You.” Yūsuf b. Ḥusayn said: “Whoever falls into the seas of unification gains, as the days pass, only greater thirst.” A man approached al-Ḥusayn b. Manṣūr al-Ḥallāj and asked: “Who is the Real to whom you point?” He answered: “The Lord of creatures, who is not comprehended by intellect.”

Al-Shiblī was asked: “Tell us about unification stripped of all else, in the language of a uniquely true account.” He replied: “Whoever points toward Him is a dualist; whoever assigns Him a modality is an idolater; whoever speaks about Him is heedless; whoever remains silent about Him is ignorant. Whoever imagines he has arrived has attained nothing; whoever thinks he is near is far; whoever supposes he has found Him has lost Him. Everything you distinguish in your imaginations and apprehend by your intellects in your most complete meanings is turned back and returned to you: originated and made, like yourselves.”

Al-Shiblī also said: “Someone for whom unification has become a conceived object has not smelled its fragrances.” Ibn ʿAṭāʾ—may God be pleased with him—said: “Intellect was created as an instrument of servanthood, not to look down upon lordship.”

Another said: “Intellect ranges around the cosmos; when it looks toward the spiritual kingdom, it melts.” The Imam al-Rāzī said: “Know that the perfected among the people of realization have said no more concerning recognition than to acknowledge their inability to attain it.” Hence someone said: “None knows the Real but He Himself.” Similarly, Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq is reported to have said: “Praise belongs to God, who has given creatures no path to recognizing Him except their inability to recognize Him.”

Al-Wāsiṭī—may God be pleased with him—said: “Recognition is not opened to the servant while there remains in him need for God or sufficiency through Him, for both are signs of the servant's remaining, whereas the recognizer is wholly passing-away.” The Pole of poles, ʿAbd al-Qādir al-Jīlī, said: “By the mantle of His greatness He veiled the intellects from knowing the ultimate reality of His Essence, and by the light of His abiding He exhausted the eyes, preventing their apprehension of the reality of His unicity.” He also said: “Imaginings do not conceive Him; understandings do not measure Him. He is not apprehended through analogy, compared with human beings, assigned a modality by intellects, or bounded by minds. Exalted is He above being likened to what He has made or compared with what He has originated.” He also said: “Faith affirms the Real through the knowledge of certainty, by assent. Access to knowledge of His reality is an unseen matter that intellect has no scope to apprehend. Whatever imagination recounts, understanding discloses, intellect imagines, or mind conceives, God's greatness and majesty are other than it. He is the First and the Last, the Manifest and the Hidden, and He knows everything.” ʿAlī—may God ennoble his face—is reported to have said: “Recognition is to know that whatever takes shape in your imagination, God is other than it.”

### Editorial notes

1. The text presents “My Lord, increase me in bewilderment” as Prophetic speech; this translation records, but does not authenticate, that attribution.
2. «ثنوى» is rendered “dualist”; its force is stronger than merely making a distinction. «لا للإشراف على الربوبية» contrasts instrumentality in service with a claimed commanding overview of lordship.
3. The apparently paradoxical al-Wāsiṭī quotation explicitly negates both need and sufficiency as conscious states of a remaining claimant. It must not be rewritten as a commonplace affirmation of need.
