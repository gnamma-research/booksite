## 849 · Poetic speech, qualified identity, and differing reports after annihilation

### Arabic

حضوره عيانا أعجبا للمحب الحقيقي المستهتر الهائم أن لا يرى غيره تعالى عند ظهور إشراق أنواره القاهرة الباهرة للإدراكات والأفهام، ويقول أمثال ما ذكر من الكلام، ويشاهد الحق تعالى في كل آياته ومصنوعاته كما قال الصديق الأكبر: «ما رأيت شيئا إلا ورأيت الله فيه قبله»، وبعض قال: إلا ورأيت الله معه، وعن علي كرم الله وجهه: «لا أعبد ربا لم أره»، وأمثال هذا أكثر من يحصى، ولا يبعد أيضا أن يصدر من أهل الإفاقة من المتصوفة نظائر هذه الرموز على طريق المتشابهات القرآنية والحديثية أو على سبيل البلاغة تجوزا واستعارة أو مبالغة، نحو: لسان الوزير لسان الأمير، وأبو يوسف أبو حنيفة.

فوالله ما أدري أبالخمر أسبلت / جفوني أم من عبرتي كنت أشرب

وقد ينزل الناقص منزلة العدم أو الفرد الكامل منزلة كل الحقيقة، وكذا ينزل وجود الشيء منزلة عدمه كما في قوله تعالى: ﴿وما رميت إذ رميت﴾ [الأنفال: 17]، فلا يبعد أن يكون مثل قول «أنا الحق» بناء على التخلق بأخلاق الله والتحقق بصفات الله، كما نطق حديث كنت سمعه وبصره وبي يسمع وبي يبصر، ويحتمل عند هذا الفقير أن يكون هذا الاختلاف بين أهل الحقيقة مثل اختلاف المتكلمين أن الضوء شرط الإبصار أو شرط الوجود، وكذا بقاء الأعراض بتجدد الأمثال أو باستمرار الوجود، يعني أن كلا الفريقين سواء في عدم الإبصار في الظلمة والإبصار في الضوء، وكذا الفريقان سيان في مشاهدة البقاء حسا، لكنهم يختلفون على حسب آرائهم المرضية عندهم في قواعدهم، وكذا المحققون كل وصل إلى فناء الفناء، أو لا حكم هناك بالعينية أو بالغيرية لطمس العلوم هناك، أما إذا ردوا إلى أنفسهم لطفا من الله لترويحهم وإبقاء وجودهم إلى أجل مسمى، فكل يقول على حسب مقتضى شاكلته وموجب استحسان فطرته وسليقته، وكل كالمجتهد معذور في كلامه، للمخطئ أجر، وللمصيب أجران، والصواب ما هو الموافق للشريعة الغراء والملة السمحة السهلة البيضاء، قال الله تعالى: ﴿وأن هذا صراطي مستقيما فاتبعوه ولا تتبعوا السبل فتفرق بكم عن سبيله﴾ [الأنعام: 153].

### English

—that the beloved is visibly present. Is it then astonishing that the true lover, passionately absorbed and love-struck, should see nothing but God when the radiance of His lights appears, overpowering and dazzling apprehension and understanding? Or that he should utter expressions such as those mentioned and witness the Real in all His signs and handiwork? As the greatest Truthful One is reported to have said: “I saw nothing without seeing God in it, before it.” Another said, “without seeing God with it.” And ʿAlī—may God ennoble his face—is reported to have said: “I do not worship a Lord whom I have not seen.” Such sayings are too numerous to count.

Nor is it improbable that comparable allusions should issue from Sufis who have returned to sobriety, in the manner of the ambiguous expressions of Qurʾān and hadith, or rhetorically through figurative usage, metaphor, or hyperbole: for example, “The minister's tongue is the ruler's tongue,” or “Abū Yūsuf is Abū Ḥanīfa.”

Verse:
“My flowing tears and my wine grew alike;
my eye pours out something like what fills the cup.
By God, I do not know whether my eyelids let wine fall,
or whether it was from my tears that I was drinking.”

Another:
“The glass grew fine, and the wine grew fine;
they resembled each other, and the matter became confused.
It seemed to be wine without a cup,
and seemed to be a cup without wine.”

The deficient may be treated as nonexistent, or the perfect individual as the whole reality of his kind. Likewise, a thing's existence may be treated as its nonexistence, as in God's words: “You did not throw when you threw” [Q 8:17]. It is therefore not improbable that an expression such as “I am the Real” should rest upon assuming divine character traits and realising divine attributes, as the report says: “I become his hearing and sight; through Me he hears and through Me he sees.”

To this poor servant it also seems possible that this disagreement among the people of reality resembles the theologians' disagreement whether light is a condition of seeing or a condition of existence, and whether accidents persist by the renewal of their like or by the continuation of their existence. Both parties agree that there is no seeing in darkness and that there is seeing in light; both likewise agree in sensibly witnessing persistence. Yet they differ according to the positions they accept within their theoretical principles. So too the realised knowers: each reaches annihilation of annihilation; there is no judgement there of identity or otherness, because cognitions are effaced there. But when, through God's kindness, they are returned to themselves for refreshment and for their existence to continue until an appointed term, each speaks according to the requirements of his own disposition and what his natural constitution and temperament find appropriate. Each is excused in what he says, like a jurist exercising independent judgement: the one who errs has one reward, and the one who is right two. What is right is what accords with the radiant religious law and the generous, easy, and pure religion. God says: “This is My straight path: follow it, and do not follow other paths, lest they divide you from His path” [Q 6:153].

### Editorial notes

1. All four Arabic couplets are translated, not merely described. The cup/wine image is an analogy for the ambiguity of appearance, not an unqualified assertion of ontological identity.
2. The source’s unusual “in it, before it” in the saying ascribed to Abū Bakr is preserved. Attribution of these sayings and the wording of the proximity report remain separate from the task of translating this treatise.
3. The printed phrase أو لا حكم there is syntactically awkward. The translation follows the immediately clear sense that identity/otherness is not judged during the effacement of cognitions; it does not claim a manuscript-supported emendation.
4. The analogy concerning light preserves the source’s “condition of existence” without silently adding an unspecified object. The point is disagreement in explanatory theory despite a shared observed phenomenon.

## 850 · The authority of clear texts; conclusion on the meaning of knowledge and faith

### Arabic

لا يقال كل من أهل المذاهب الأصلية كالجبرية والقدرية والمجسمة والمعتزلة والخوارج والروافض وغيرهم، والفرعية كالشافعية والمالكية والحنابلة مستندهم وحجتهم أيضا من الكتاب والسنة، ولكن العمد بالنصوص الظاهرات المحكمات القطعية الدلالات، لا بالمتشابهات ولا بالمؤولات على خلاف المحكمات، والقاعدة عند التعارض الرجوع إلى المحكمات على ما فهمه أهل اللغة والمجتهدون عن ابن عباس رض أنه قال: قال رسول الله عليه السلام: «الأمر ثلاثة، أمر بين رشده فاتبعه، وأمر بين غيه فاجتنبه، وأمر اختلف فيه، فكله إلى الله عز وجل». والله تعالى أنزل المحكمات على لسان عربي مبين، لا تعمية ولا ألغاز، وأما المتشابهات فلا يعلم تأويله إلا الله، والراسخون في العلم يقولون آمنا به كل من عند ربنا وأما الذين في قلوبهم زيغ فيتبعون ما تشابه منه، فيكون فتنة للعوام المقلدين وغير الراسخين في العلم نعوذ بالله، قال النبي عليه السلام: «يكون في آخر الزمان دجالون كذابون يأتونكم من الأحاديث بما لم تسمعوا أنتم ولا آباؤكم، وإياكم وإياهم لا يضلونكم ولا يفتنونكم». قال الله تعالى: ﴿قل كل يعمل على شاكلته فربكم أعلم بمن هو أهدى سبيلا﴾ [الإسراء: 84]، كل حزب لديهم فرحون وإذ لم يهتدوا فسيقولون هذا إفك قديم يهدي الله لنوره من يشاء، ومما يدل على حقيقته وصوابيته أن الظهور التام لهذا الدين القويم الظاهر كالشمس وقت الضحى، وقال الله تعالى: ﴿هو الذي أرسل رسوله بالهدى ودين الحق ليظهره على الدين كله ولو كره المشركون﴾ [الصف: 9]، وهؤلاء الفرقة هم الغالبون على جميع أهل الملك والأديان، والعالمون المنصورون على كل أهل الزيغ والبطلان والطغيان، وفي أيديهم شعائر الإسلام من الإمامة والإمارة والخطابة والأذان والتدريس والقضاء والألوية والأعلام، وكفى به حجة لأهل الإنصاف المائل عن الاعتساف. الحمد لله الذي هدانا لهذا وما كنا لنهتدي لولا أن هدانا الله.

### English

One should not object that all the adherents of fundamental doctrinal schools—such as the proponents of compulsion and of independent power, the corporealists, Muʿtazilites, Khārijites, Rāfiḍites, and others—and of the subsidiary legal schools, such as the Shāfiʿīs, Mālikīs, and Ḥanbalīs, also take their grounds and proofs from the Book and the Sunnah. Reliance is upon the manifest, unambiguous texts whose indications are decisive, not upon ambiguous texts or interpretations that contradict the unambiguous ones. When there is conflict, the rule is to return to the unambiguous texts according to the understanding of the specialists in the language and the jurists qualified to exercise independent judgement.

Ibn ʿAbbās—may God be pleased with him—is reported to have said that God's Messenger said: “Matters are of three kinds: one whose right guidance is clear, so follow it; one whose error is clear, so avoid it; and one about which there is disagreement, so entrust it to God, mighty and majestic.” God sent down the unambiguous passages in a clear Arabic tongue, not as obfuscation or riddles. As for the ambiguous passages, none knows their interpretation except God; those firmly grounded in knowledge say, “We believe in it: all is from our Lord.” Those in whose hearts there is deviation follow what is ambiguous, thereby becoming a trial for ordinary people who follow authority and for those not firmly grounded in knowledge. We seek refuge in God.

The Prophet—peace be upon him—is reported to have said: “At the end of time there will be impostors and liars who bring you reports that neither you nor your fathers have heard. Beware of them, lest they lead you astray or put you to trial.” God says: “Say: each acts according to his disposition, and your Lord knows best who is better guided in the way” [Q 17:84]. Each party rejoices in what it possesses; because they have not been guided by it, they will say, “This is an ancient fabrication”; God guides to His light whom He wills.

Among the indications of its truth and correctness is the complete ascendancy of this upright religion, manifest like the sun in the forenoon. God says: “He it is who sent His Messenger with guidance and the religion of truth, to make it prevail over every religion, though the associators dislike it” [Q 61:9]. This community prevails over the holders of dominion and the adherents of other religions; its learned members are supported against the people of deviation, falsehood, and transgression. In its hands are the institutions and public signs of Islam: the imamate, governance, preaching, the call to prayer, teaching, judging, banners, and standards. This suffices as evidence for fair-minded people who turn away from arbitrariness. Praise belongs to God who guided us to this; we would not have been guided had God not guided us.

CONCLUSION

It contains two chapters.

CHAPTER ONE: Explaining the basis of the judgement of error and disclosing what is correct.

Know that this judgement rests upon the doctrine that faith neither increases nor decreases according to the Lamp of the Community and Light of the Imams, the Greatest Imam Abū Ḥanīfa—may God be pleased with him. The required state is certainty, which, it is argued, does not admit degrees: any difference would consist only in the possibility of the contradictory, and such a possibility, however remote, contradicts certainty. Consequently, the meaning assigned to “We have not known You as You deserve to be known” would be “My knowledge has not attained firm and certain assent”; on that construction the expression is judged erroneous.

I say: in Sufi terminology, what is intended by recognition here is not assent to the existence and unity of the Real. It is conception and acquaintance with the ultimate reality of God's Essence, Names, attributes, and acts, through witnessing rather than through certain knowledge of an unseen object, as explained in the first inquiry of the introduction when setting out their terminology. Every group has its own terminology, and one must refer to it when discussing matters with them.

Even were it conceded that assent is intended, assent also admits increase and decrease according to the Greatest Imam, with respect to its object. ### Editorial notes

1. The opening anticipates an objection and answers it by distinguishing decisive texts from ambiguous or counter-textual interpretations. It does not claim that all scriptural appeals are equally authoritative.
2. The collective ascendancy argument and sectarian names are the author’s own historical-theological rhetoric, not independent contemporary claims by the translator.
3. The sequence of allusions after Q 17:84 draws on Qurʾānic phrases, including rejoicing in one’s party, calling revelation an ancient fabrication, and guidance to divine light; the source does not supply individual verse numbers there.
4. The account that certainty cannot vary is first presented as the basis of the accusation being answered. The next page expressly contests the assumption that all differences require the possibility of the contradictory. This distinction corrects the earlier interpretive draft.
5. The page ends mid-sentence; the word “fruit” at the start of p. 851 completes the final phrase.
