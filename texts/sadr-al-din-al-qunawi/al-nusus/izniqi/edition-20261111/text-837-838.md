### English

Eighth: They say that when a man is in a vast desert, the heat becomes intense, no water is present, and thirst and deprivation overwhelm him, he imagines there is water and drink in whichever direction he looks. When he reaches it, he finds nothing and discovers that it was a mirage. Similarly, human intellects and souls find themselves in the wilderness of His greatness and the desert of His dazzling splendour. The shining lights become immense; the rays' illuminations reflect against one another; the heat of yearning and the blaze of longing to reach the divine Presence prevail. Whichever way a person looks, he supposes water is there. Those who strip God of attributes look toward transcendence, those who liken Him to creatures toward affirmation; likewise the proponents of compulsion and those of creaturely determination. In short, each of the religious parties turns in some direction, supposing the water to be there. When they reach it on the Day of Resurrection, they find nothing there of this matter, as God says: “We shall turn to the deeds they have performed and make them scattered dust” [Q 25:23].

The strong, however, recognizing humanity's incapacity and intellect's bewilderment in the immense desert, flee from themselves and seek God's help and guidance. The Friend [Abraham] said: “My Lord, grant me judgement” [Q 26:83]; Joseph: “Take me in death as one who submits” [Q 12:101]; the one addressed [Moses]: “My Lord, expand my breast” [Q 20:25]; Jesus: “You know what is in myself, and I do not know what is in Yourself” [Q 5:116]; and the Beloved: “Show us things as they are.”

Ninth: They say that a child looks into a mirror and sees an image. Desiring it, he approaches and sees it approaching him. The nearer it appears, the stronger his desire to seize it. He reaches out his hand, but on doing so sees a barrier of iron, while the image becomes concealed. Similarly, the intellectual faculty looks into the mirror of “Whoever knows himself,” and the self-disclosure of the lights of “knows his Lord” gleams before it. The more intently the eye looks into this mirror, the greater and more complete the disclosure. Intellect longs to reach the ultimate reality of His self-sufficiency and extends the hand of seeking. It then sees between itself and what it seeks a curtain of pre-eternity, beginninglessness, and necessity. It remains powerless and bewildered behind this barrier; because it has extended the hand of appropriation, that disclosure disappears behind the pavilions of divinity and the veils of greatness, majesty, and overwhelming power.

A subtle point. When the Real's light discloses itself upon the mirrors and glass of receptive loci, His light is free from colours and restrictions, unlike the mutually opposed recipients, coloured and restricted by individuations, attributions, and relations. It necessarily takes on the colouring demanded by these loci and sites of disclosure, becomes restricted according to them, and assumes their qualities. The mirror therefore does not convey the Beloved's beauty as it deserves. Your determinate existence is a veil for you. Hence the verifying masters say: “Your existence is a sin with which no other sin can be compared.” Understand the following Persian and Arabic verses illustrating this point.

Verse:
“Because His beauty had a hundred thousand faces,
in every atom there was a different vision.
Inevitably He showed each atom, in turn,
a different countenance of His own beauty.”

Another:
“All are one light, but its colours differ,
casting a difference between this and that.”

### Editorial notes

1. The ninth argument’s printed reading is «مخفية», confirmed by the PDF text layer; the variants are not silently substituted. The “iron” is a mirror barrier, not necessarily its frame.
2. The two Persian poems are translated in full. The parent text makes no authorial attribution for them here; this edition does not repeat the prior draft’s unsupported assignments to ʿAṭṭār or Rūmī.
3. The sectarian generalization in argument eight belongs to the historical author. It has not been softened by adding “only when absolutized,” a qualification absent from this sentence.

### English

Another verse:
“The reality is one, while the determinations differ:
a secret disclosed to the people of knowledge.”

Tenth: Undoubtedly, the more completely someone's attention is severed from what is other than the Real, and the more fully he is absorbed in knowing God, the more complete his recognition. Hence it is said that recognition of the Lord is attained in proportion to estrangement from the self. There is no doubt that Muḥammad—peace be upon him—on the night when he reached the utmost limit in this matter possessed the most complete recognition. Nevertheless, he acknowledged inability to know and said: “I cannot enumerate praise of You.” If he, despite perfect nearness, did not know Him as He deserves, how could someone below him do so? This is supported by his reported saying: “Do not reflect upon God's Essence; reflect upon the sign,” and by God's words: “Above every possessor of knowledge is one who knows” [Q 12:76].

CHAPTER THREE
Explaining that the cause of falling short of recognition is His extreme manifestness.

The Proof of Islam, Abū Ḥāmid al-Ghazālī—may God have mercy upon him—said: “Know that the cause of creatures' understandings falling short of knowing God, although He is the most evident and manifest of existents—since every existent proclaims through the language of its state that He brings it into existence and governs it—is His extreme manifestness.

Verse:
‘In every thing there is a sign of Him,
indicating that He is One.’

“This is particularly so in our existence, our selves, our qualities, the changes of our states and hearts, and all our faculties of apprehension. The sight of bats is weak and dazzled by the sun's light when it shines. Just as the intensity of its appearance combined with the weakness of bats' eyes prevents their seeing, so too our intellects are weak while the beauty of the divine Presence is supremely radiant and luminous, supremely pervasive and comprehensive, so that not one atom in the kingdom of the heavens and earth falls outside it: this causes His hiddenness. Glory to Him who is veiled from intellects by the radiance of His light and concealed from eyes and others by the perfection of His manifestation! Do not be surprised at this. Things become evident through their opposites; what is so universally existent that it has no opposite is difficult to apprehend. The sunlight is an example. If the sun shone continually upon every place and body, we would suppose bodies had no feature except their colour, since we see blackness in the black and whiteness in the white.
