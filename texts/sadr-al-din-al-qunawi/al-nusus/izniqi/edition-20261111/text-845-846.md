## 845 · Ibn al-Fāriḍ and the complete Persian verse citations

### Arabic

وإني التي أحببتها لا محالة / وكانت لها نفسي علي محيلتي
لها صلواتي بالمقام أقيمها / وأشهد فيها أنها لي صلت
كلانا مصل واحد ساجد إلى / حقيقته بالجمع في كل سجدة
وما كان لي صلى سواي ولم تكن / صلاتي لغيري في أدا كل ركعة
خرجت بها عني إليها فلم أعد / إلي ومثلي لا يقول برجعة
وأفردت نفسي عن خروجي تكرما / فلم أرضها من بعد ذاك لصحبتي
وغبت عن إفراد نفسي بحيث لا / يزاحمني إبداء وصف بحضرتي
كذا كنت حينا قبل أن يكشف الغطا / من اللبس لا أنفك عن ثنوية
أروح بفقد بالشهود مؤلفي / وأغدو بوجد بالوجود مشتتي
يفرقني لي التزاما بمحضري / ويجمعني سلب اصطلاما بغيبتي
أحال حضيضي الصحو والسكر معرجي / إليها ومحوي منتهى قاب سدرتي
فلما جلوت العين عني اجتليتني / مفيقا ومني العين بالعين قرت
كـ«خواجه عطار»:
درنگر كين عالم وآن عالم اوست / نيست غير او وگر هست آن هم اوست
جمله يك ذاتست اما متصف / جمله يك حرف و عبارت مختلف
يكيست اين جمله چه آخر چه اول / ولي بيننده را چشم است احول
زهي اسم و زهي معني همه تو / همي گويم كه اي تو اي همه تو
ومولانا جلال الدين الرومي:
آن پادشاه اعظم در بسته بود محكم / پيوسته چرا باشد اين ناله وافغانم
آن پادشاه اعظم بنشسته بود محكم / پوشيد دلق آدم امروز بر درآمد
وسلطان ولد:
گفتار مرا زمن مدان چون عدمم / از حق شود مدام هر دم كه دمم
حق ذات قديمست منم فاني او / حادث مشمر مرا كه نور قدمم

### English

“And I am, assuredly, the one I loved;
it was my own self that referred me to her.

My prayers at the station I establish for her;
in them I witness that she has prayed to me.

We two are one worshipper, prostrating toward
its own reality in gathering at every prostration.

None other than myself has prayed to me;
in performing each cycle, my prayer was not to another.

Through her I left myself for her and did not return
to myself; one like me does not profess a return.

Generously I excluded my self from my departure;
after that I did not deem it worthy of my companionship.

I became absent even from setting my self apart,
so that no disclosed attribute could crowd my presence.

Thus I was for a while, before the covering of confusion
was lifted: I was not yet free of duality.

At evening I go in loss, with witnessing gathering me;
at morning I go in finding, with existence scattering me.

It divides me by binding me to myself in my presence;
negation gathers me by sweeping me away in my absence.

Sobriety made my lowest point; intoxication was my ascent
to her; my effacement, the furthest bow-length of my Lote-tree.

When I cleared the eye of myself, I beheld myself,
restored to sobriety; through me, the eye found repose in the very eye.”

As [in the words of] Khwāja ʿAṭṭār:

“Look: this world and that world are He.
There is none but He; if there is, that too is He.
All is one Essence, though qualified by attributes;
all is one letter, though its expressions differ.”

“All this is one, whether last or first;
but the beholder's eye sees double.
How wondrous the name, how wondrous the meaning—all You!
I keep saying: O You, O You who are all!”

And our master Jalāl al-Dīn al-Rūmī:

“That greatest King had firmly shut the door.
Why should this crying and lament of mine continue?
That greatest King had been firmly seated;
today He put on Adam's patched cloak and came forth.”

And Sulṭān Walad:

“Do not take my speech as coming from me, for I am nothing.
From the Real comes every breath that I breathe.
The Real is the eternal Essence; I am one effaced in Him.
Do not count me as originated: I am the light of beginninglessness.”

### Editorial notes

1. Every cited verse on the page is translated; these are intentionally close verse renderings, not metrical imitations.
2. The Ibn al-Fāriḍ text is a selected sequence, not the whole Tāʾiyya. Its deliberately paradoxical first-person declarations are retained. The following prose places constraints on how such utterances are interpreted.
3. The condensed verse «أحال حضيضي الصحو ...» and the repeated «العين» carry syntactic and lexical play. “Eye” is retained in English, with possible resonances of the very reality/entity; the translation is not claimed to exhaust those resonances.
4. The printed Rūmī line «پيوسته چرا باشد ...» is translated as a question. Comparison with a separately edited Persian witness remains desirable; no absent lines are supplied.

## 846 · Awhad al-Dīn’s verse; reason, revelation, and ontological dependence

### Arabic

والشيخ أوحد الدين الكرماني:
چندان برو اين ره كه دوي برخيزد / گر هست دوي، به رهروي برخيزد
تو او نشوي، ولي اگر جهد كني / جايي برسي كز تو، تويي برخيزد
إذا عرفت هذا فاعلم أن الاعتقاد الحق الواجب القبول، الموافق للمعقول والمنقول، والمأخوذ من سيدنا وسندنا قطب وقته وعصره، وفريد حينه ودهره سلطان الأولياء، وارث خاتم الأنبياء، شيخ الإسلام والمسلمين، جامع الفضائل الآخرين، الشيخ زين الدين الخافي قدس الله سره:
«هو أن الله منزه عن الحلول في الأشباح، مقدس عن السريان في الأرواح، من قال: اتحد بالكون فقد ألحد، ومن قال: إنه ليس له تعين في ذاته إلا في الكون فقد أفسد العقائد وأجحد، وهو كان في ذاته متعينا في ذاته قبل كائناته، عالما بذاته، وبما ظهر من مخلوقاته على مقتضيات صفاته، تجلى بذاته على ذاته قبل ظهور مظاهر صفاته، فأراد إظهار كمالاته على صفحات الأرواح والأجسام من مكوناته، فأظهر أولا مظهر المظاهر روح حبيبه من فيض أنواره صفاته الذاتية، ثم أظهر من فيض نوره ما أظهر من عوالم الأرواح والأنوار».
ثم وثم إلى آخر المراتب من المظاهر، هذا هو معتقد أهل السنة والجماعة بالنقل الصريح المتواتر من لدن رسول الله عليه السلام إلى يومنا هذا، والأئمة الأربعة المجتهدون رضوان الله عليهم أجمعين مصرحون مجتمعون على هذا الاعتقاد بلا ريبة ومرية، وهو المنصوص عليه في القرآن العظيم والحديث الكريم، كم من آية وحديث دلا على تكوين الحادث ووجوده وإيجاده وخلقه وذرئه وتصويره وإحيائه وإعدامه وإفنائه وإماتته وإعادته، فإذن القول بأن وجود الحادث عبارة عن أمر وهمي خيالي واعتباري كالسراب وكما في عين الأحول وكما في المرآة سفسطة، وتأويل زائغ مخالف للنصوص وعقائد أهل السنة والاستدلال على هذا بأن القول بوجود الحادث شرك، ليس بصواب؛ لأن الشرك إنما يلزم لو كان وجود الحادث واجبا مستقلا بذاته، أما إذا كان محتاجا في حدوثه وبقائه وغير مستقل وعدما في حد ذاته فلا، وقولهم: «العقل بمعزل عن هذا، لأن هذا طور وراء طور العقل مسلم»، لكن ما يدل العقل على خلافه مردود، وهذا من خلاف ما دل العقل، فكم بينهما، الأول مسلم والثاني مردود، صرح بهذا أقطب الأقطاب عبد القادر الجيلي: «لأن العقل من حجج الله، فإذا كان ما يرده مقبولا لا يؤمن عليه، ولكنه يجوز أن يكون وراء طوره، لأن الولاية وراء العقل، والنبوة وراء طور الولاية»، هذا، وكذا الشرع يرد ما قالوا، وما رده الشرع مردود وضلال وبدعة.
قالت عائشة رضي الله عنها: «تلا رسول الله عليه السلام: ﴿هو الذي أنزل عليك الكتاب منه آيات محكمات﴾ [آل عمران: 7]، ثم قال: فإذا رأيت الذين يتبعون ما تشابه منه فأولئك الذين سمى الله، فاحذروهم». وقال عليه السلام: «ولا يجتمع أمة محمد على الضلالة، ويد الله على الجماعة، ومن شذ شذ في النار»، وقال عليه السلام: «فإنه من يعش منكم بعدي فسيرى اختلافا كثيرا، فعليكم بسنتي وسنة الخلفاء الراشدين المهديين، تمسكوا بها

### English

And Shaykh Awhad al-Dīn al-Kirmānī:

“Travel this path until duality falls away;
if duality remains, walking the path will remove it.
You do not become He; but if you strive,
you reach a place where your you-ness falls away from you.”

Having understood this, know that the true belief which must be accepted, agreeing with reason and transmission and received from our master and support—the Pole of his time, unique in his age, Sultan of saints, inheritor of the Seal of prophets, Shaykh of Islam and Muslims, gatherer of the virtues of the later generations, Shaykh Zayn al-Dīn al-Khāfī, may God sanctify his secret—is:

“God is free from indwelling in bodies and sanctified beyond permeation through spirits. Whoever says He has become united with the cosmos has deviated; whoever says He has no determination in Himself except in the cosmos has corrupted belief and denied the truth. Before His creatures, He was determined in Himself, in His own Essence, knowing Himself and what appears of His creatures according to the requirements of His attributes. He disclosed Himself through Himself to Himself before the appearances of His attributes emerged. He then willed to display His perfections upon the pages of spirits and bodies among His creatures. First He brought forth the manifestation of manifestations, the spirit of His Beloved, from the effusion of the lights of His essential attributes. Then, from the effusion of its light, He brought forth what He brought forth of the worlds of spirits and lights.”

And so on, through the remaining ranks of manifestation. This is the belief of the People of the Sunnah and the Community, according to explicit, recurrent transmission from God's Messenger to our own day. The four interpreting Imams—God be pleased with them—explicitly agree on this belief without doubt or dispute. It is stated in the mighty Qurʾān and noble hadith. How many verses and reports affirm the coming-to-be, existence, bringing into existence, creation, origination, forming, vivification, making nonexistent, annihilation, putting to death, and restoration of originated things! To say, therefore, that the existence of an originated thing is merely imaginary, fanciful, or a consideration—like a mirage, what appears to a person who sees double, or an image in a mirror—is sophistry and a deviant interpretation contrary to the texts and the beliefs of the People of the Sunnah. Nor is it sound to argue for this by claiming that affirming the existence of originated things is associationism. Associationism would follow only if the originated thing's existence were necessary and independently subsistent. It does not follow when that thing needs another for its origination and continuance, is not independent, and is nonexistent considered in itself.

Their statement, “Intellect has no jurisdiction here, since this is a domain beyond intellect,” is conceded. But what intellect demonstrates to be false is rejected; the position in question contradicts what intellect demonstrates. How different these two cases are! The first is conceded; the second rejected. The Pole of poles, ʿAbd al-Qādir al-Jīlī, stated this explicitly: “Intellect is among God's proofs. If what it rejects were accepted, it could not be trusted. But something may lie beyond its domain, for sainthood lies beyond intellect and prophethood beyond sainthood.” Furthermore, the religious law rejects what they say, and whatever the law rejects is rejected, error, and innovation.

ʿĀʾisha—may God be pleased with her—said: “God's Messenger recited: ‘He it is who sent down the Book to you; in it are unambiguous verses’ [Q 3:7]. Then he said: ‘When you see those who follow what is ambiguous in it, those are the people God has named: beware of them.’” He also said: “The community of Muḥammad will not agree upon error; God's hand is with the community; whoever separates himself separates himself into the Fire.” And: “Whoever among you lives after me will see much disagreement. Hold to my practice and that of the rightly guided successors; cling to it—

### Editorial notes

1. The anti-illusionist argument is explicit and strong: the author rejects treating originated existence as merely imaginary. This is not reduced to a generic rejection of incarnation.
2. The assertions of consensus and doctrinal continuity are al-Iznīqī’s claims. The translation does not certify them as independently established historical conclusions.
3. The source distinguishes what lies beyond intellect from what contradicts an intellectual demonstration. The earlier summary omitted much of this argument.
4. The awkward printed phrase about an untrustworthy intellect is rendered contextually. Prophetic quotations remain attributed quotations, with authentication a separate task.
