Exposition · The transmitted opening

In the Name of God, the Compassionate, the Merciful. God bless our master Muḥammad, his family and his Companions, and grant them peace.

The shaykh, imam, eminent and resolute scholar, God-knowing master and divinely sustained pole, exemplar of wayfarers and beacon of those who have arrived, ocean of gnosis and dawning-place of the sun of direct vision, broad highway of the Path, who unites the Sacred Law with the ocean of Reality—Abū al-ʿAbbās Aḥmad ibn Muḥammad ibn ʿAjība al-Ḥasanī, may God be pleased with him and grant him His good pleasure, amen—said:[P01]

Theology · The divine speech and the sciences of interpretation

We praise You, O You who disclose Yourself to Your servants in Your speech, in the perfection of Your splendour and beauty; who loose the tongues of consummate scholars to bring forth its pearls and precious gems, and cause springs of wisdom to gush from their hearts, sustained by its principles and foundations. As they dive into its current, they draw benefit from its unique treasures and reiterated teachings. With its dazzling signs and its manifest, overwhelming proofs, they refute the doubts of those who oppose and resist it. Yet all acknowledge their inadequacy, each drawing from the ocean of its mysteries and meanings according to the understanding and facility granted him. It is the surging ocean whose bottom cannot be reached, the delightful garden never without blossom or flower. And how could it be otherwise, when it is the speech of our Master, who knows hidden things, what has been, what now is, and what is to come?

Blessings and peace be upon our master and patron Muḥammad, the manifestation of mercies, sent with wonders that breach the customary order (خوارق العادات) and the radiance of clear proofs; and upon his family and Companions, the possessors of generosity and open-handedness, mountains of certainty amid intensifying crises and ever more difficult perplexities.

To proceed: the science of interpreting the Qurʾān (علم تفسير القرآن) is among the noblest sciences, and among the best pursuits upon which to expend the fruits of reflection and the inventive powers of understanding. Yet none should advance to this momentous undertaking except the consummate scholar whose feet are firmly planted in the outward sciences (العلوم الظاهرة) and whose thought has ranged through the Qurʾān’s resplendent meanings. He must first have drunk deeply of outward learning: Arabic grammar, morphology, lexicography and rhetoric, jurisprudence, hadith and history—having received these from the mouths of men. He must then have plunged into the sciences of Sufism (علوم التصوف), in tasting, state and station (ذوقًا وحالًا ومقامًا), through the companionship of perfected people of spiritual tasting (أهل الأذواق). Otherwise, silence concerning this tremendous matter is safer for him, and occupation with whatever he can manage of the outward Sacred Law is more fitting.

Know that the mighty Qurʾān has an outward aspect (ظاهر) for the people of the outward and an inward aspect (باطن) for the people of the inward. The interpretation offered by the people of the inward is tasted only by the people of the inward: others neither understand it nor taste it. Nor is it sound to set it forth before establishing the outward meaning. Thereafter one may indicate inward knowledge through a delicate expression and a subtle allusion (إشارة). Let anyone whose understanding does not reach the tasting of those mysteries yield assent, rather than hasten to deny them. For the knowledge of spiritual tastings (علم الأذواق) lies beyond the compass of discursive intellects and is not attained through the successive transmission of reports.[P02]

Exposition · The inward sense does not abolish the outward sense

He says in Laṭāʾif al-minan: “Know that this community’s interpretation”—meaning the Sufis—“of the speech of God and of His Messenger, God bless him and grant him peace, through unfamiliar meanings is not a diversion of the outward sense from its outward meaning. Rather, the outward aspect of the verse conveys what the verse was revealed for and what it indicates in the language’s expression.

“There are also inward understandings apprehended on hearing a verse or a hadith by those whose hearts God has opened. It has been related that he, blessings and peace be upon him, said: ‘Every verse has an outward aspect, an inward aspect, a limit, and a point of ascent (ظاهر وباطن وحدّ ومطّلع).’ Do not let a contentious opponent turn you away from receiving unfamiliar meanings from them by saying, ‘This diverts the speech of God, mighty and majestic, and the speech of His Messenger, God bless him and grant him peace.’ It is no such diversion. It would be a diversion of God’s speech only were they to say, ‘The verse has no meaning except this.’ They do not say that. Rather, they confirm the outward senses in their outward meanings, intentions and proper applications, and understand from God what He has enabled them to understand.” End of quotation.[P03]

Saʿd al-Dīn says in his commentary upon al-Nasafī’s Creed, after refuting impious distortion (الإلحاد): “As for the view of certain realized authorities that scriptural texts retain their outward meanings, while also containing hidden allusions to realities unveiled to the people of wayfaring, which can be reconciled with the intended outward senses, this is of the perfection of faith and pure gnosis.”[XVI-N001] His words “can be reconciled,” and what follows, mean that these realities may be indicated within the inward dimension of the discourse in such a way that the discourse’s secret sense does not reject them, and the wording is not wholly remote from them, which would amount to distortion (تحريف).

Shaykh Zarrūq, may God be pleased with him, said: “The Sufi’s consideration is more particular than that of the exegete and the interpreter of hadith: each of those considers the ruling and the meaning, and no more, whereas the Sufi adds the search for the allusion after affirming what they have affirmed. Otherwise he is an esotericist (باطني), outside the Sacred Law, let alone the Sufis. And God knows best.” End of quotation.

Language · Outward aspect, inward aspect, limit and point of ascent

Concerning his saying, blessings and peace be upon him, “Every verse has an outward aspect, an inward aspect, a limit, and a point of ascent”: the outward aspect belongs to those concerned with the wording’s outward form, such as the grammarians, lexicographers and morphologists. The inward aspect belongs to those concerned with the wording’s meaning and what the discourse indicates of command, prohibition, narratives, reports, the affirmation of divine unity (التوحيد), and the other sciences of the Qurʾān. This is the exegetes’ consideration. The limit (الحدّ) belongs to those concerned to derive rulings from it—the jurists—who stop at what the wording indicates and the purpose for which it is expressed, without adding to it. The point of ascent (المطّلع) belongs to the people of realities (أهل الحقائق), the great Sufis: they look through the verse’s outward aspect into its inward aspect, and mysteries, sciences and subtle matters are unveiled to them, disclosed when they exercise contemplative reflection (الفكرة) upon it.[P04]

He says in al-Ṣiḥāḥ: “In the report, ‘from the terror of the place of arrival (المطّلع),’ the affairs of the Hereafter upon which one is about to look are likened to a muṭṭalaʿ, that is, a place of approach. One says, ‘Where is the muṭṭalaʿ of this affair?’ meaning, ‘Where is its point of approach?’ It is the place from which one looks down from a height.” End of quotation. For the people of realities look from the verse’s outward aspect into the mysteries of its inward aspect, and dive into the surging depths of its ocean. And God Most High knows best.

Exposition · Introduction: names and verse-counting

I:52

It is Meccan. It has ten names: the Opening (الفاتحة); the Complete (الوافية); the Sufficient (الكافية); the Healing (الشافية); and the Seven Oft-Repeated (السبع المثاني). It is called the Seven Oft-Repeated because, according to al-Shāfiʿī, it consists of seven verses with the basmala counted among them, whereas Mālik does not count the basmala and makes “not those upon whom wrath has fallen, nor those who are astray” the seventh verse; or because it is repeated in every prayer; or because it contains praise of God (الثناء), with an evident play upon the name al-mathānī. It is also called the Mother of the Qurʾān (أم القرآن), because it is the Qurʾān's opening and beginning, or because it contains in summary what the Qurʾān contains, as will be explained; the Sūrah of Praise and Thanksgiving; the Sūrah of Teaching How to Ask; the Sūrah of Prayer, because it is repeatedly recited therein; and the Foundation of the Qurʾān, because it is its root and beginning, upon which the rest is built.

Cairo I:52–53

“In the Name of God, the Compassionate, the Merciful.”

Legal · The basmala: Qurʾānic status and evidence

The written copies of the Qurʾān agree in opening it with “In the Name of God, the Compassionate, the Merciful.” The imams disagree concerning this formula. Mālik said that it is not a verse of al-Fātiḥa or of any other sūrah, except in al-Naml alone. Al-Shāfiʿī said that it is a verse of al-Fātiḥa alone. Ibn ʿAbbās said that it is a verse of every sūrah.

Mālik's evidence is the authentic report in which the Prophet, God bless him and grant him peace, said: “A sūrah has been sent down to me whose like is not in the Torah, the Gospel, or the Criterion.” He then said, “Praise belongs to God, Lord of the worlds,” without mentioning the basmala. There is likewise the authentic report that God says: “I have divided the prayer between Myself and My servant into two halves. The servant says, ‘Praise belongs to God, Lord of the worlds.’” He thus begins with this, without the basmala.

Al-Shāfiʿī's evidence is the authentic report that the Messenger of God, God bless him and grant him peace, used to recite: “In the Name of God, the Compassionate, the Merciful. Praise belongs to God, Lord of the worlds.” Ibn ʿAbbās's evidence is that the basmala is written with every sūrah in the written copy of the Qurʾān, despite the Companions' care not to introduce anything into it other than God's speech. They said: “What is between the two covers is God's speech.”

Legal · Recitation and prayer rulings

When you begin at the opening of a sūrah, recite the basmala, except in Barāʾa [al-Tawba], which will be discussed in its place. When you begin within a sūrah, the majority give you a choice. When you complete one sūrah and begin another, the Qurʾānic readers differ over whether to recite or omit the basmala.

As for its ruling in prayer, Mālik said that it is disliked in obligatory prayer, but not in voluntary prayer. Al-Shāfiʿī said that it is obligatory and that omitting it invalidates the prayer. According to him, therefore, one recites it aloud in audible prayer and silently in silent prayer. With Abū Ḥanīfa it is likewise, except that he recites it silently in every case. Mālik's evidence that it is not a verse is the authentic report from Anas, who said: “I prayed behind the Prophet, God bless him and grant him peace, Abū Bakr, ʿUmar, and ʿUthmān, and they used to begin with ‘Praise belongs to God, Lord of the worlds,’” not mentioning the basmala at all. Al-Shāfiʿī's evidence that it is a verse, in his view, is the report of its recitation already mentioned.

Exposition · History of the written formula

I:53

The basmala was not in use before Islam. People used to write, “In Your Name, O God,” until “In the Name of God is its sailing” [11:41] was revealed, whereupon they wrote, “In the Name of God.” Then “or call upon the All-Merciful” [17:110] was revealed, and they wrote, “In the Name of God, the All-Merciful.” Finally, “and it is: In the Name of God, the All-Merciful, the Ever-Merciful” [27:30] was revealed, and they wrote it in this form.

Language · Grammar and derivation of the basmala

The alif of ism in bism is omitted because of frequent usage. The preposition bāʾ is connected to an elided governing element (متعلِّقة بمحذوف). According to the Basrans, the ellipsis is nominal: “My beginning is [made] in the Name of God” (ابتدائي كائن بسم الله), so its syntactic locus is nominative (موضعها الرفع). According to the Kufans, the ellipsis is verbal: “I begin” or “I recite,” so each speaker supplies the action for which the basmala is made the beginning, and its syntactic locus is accusative (موضعها النصب). The omitted element is understood after the basmala in order to convey restriction (الحصر) and exclusive specification (الاختصاص).

According to the Basrans, ism (“name”) is derived (مشتق) from sumuww (“elevation”), with the final radical - the lām of the root (لام الكلمة) - omitted. According to the Kufans it is derived from sima (“mark, sign”; السِّمة، أي العلامة), with the first radical - the fāʾ of the root (فاء الكلمة) - omitted. The Basrans appeal to the diminutive (التصغير) and the broken plural (التكسير): one says asmāʾ, not awsām, and sumayy, not wusaym.

“God” (الله) is a proper name (عَلَم) for the Essence that is Necessary in Existence (الذات الواجبة الوجود) and deserving of every praise. Whether the name is derived (مشتق) or independently coined (مرتجل) is disputed. The two positions will be discussed under “Praise belongs to God,” as will the names al-Raḥmān and al-Raḥīm.

“Praise belongs to God, Lord of the worlds, the Compassionate, the Merciful, Master of the Day of Recompense.”

Language · Al-ḥamd: syntax, definiteness and readings

I say: “Praise” (الحمد) is the subject (مبتدأ), and “belongs to God” (لله) is the predicate (خبر). Its underlying construction is accusative (النصب), and it has in fact been recited that way; the underlying expression is “I praise God with praise” (أحمد الله حمداً). It was shifted from the accusative to the nominative (الرفع) in order to signify the universality and abiding stability of praise, rather than its renewed occurrence and origination. The form also teaches the very words to be uttered while intimating God's independence of their praise: praise belongs to God even if you do not praise Him. Had He said, “I praise God,” this meaning would not have been conveyed. Ḥamd is one of those verbal nouns (المصادر) that are put in the accusative by implicit verbs (أفعال مضمرة) which are scarcely ever expressed alongside them.

The definite article (التعريف) may be generic (للجنس): it then denotes the reality as such, without making its universal extension a condition, and points to what everyone already recognises praise to be. Or it may be exhaustive (للاستغراق): in reality every praise belongs to God, since there is no good that He does not bestow, either through an intermediary or without one, as He says, “Whatever blessing you have is from God” [16:53]. Or it may be referential (للعهد), the referent being God's praise of Himself in His beginningless eternity.

“Praise belongs to God” has also been recited with the vowel of the dāl made to follow that of the lām, and conversely (بإتباع الدال للام وبالعكس), treating the two, because of their habitual conjunction, as though they were a single word.

Language · Praise and thanksgiving: technical meanings

I:53–54

In the language (في اللغة), praise (الحمد) is commendation of what is beautiful, intended as magnification and reverence. In technical usage (في العرف), it is an act that discloses magnification of a benefactor precisely insofar as he is a benefactor. Thanksgiving (الشكر), in the language, is an act that signifies magnification of the benefactor and is therefore synonymous with ḥamd in that technical sense. In technical usage, however, shukr is the servant's directing everything God has bestowed upon him - hearing, sight, and the rest - toward the purpose for which it was created and bestowed. See our larger commentary on al-Fātiḥa for the relations (النِّسَب) among these meanings, which we set out there in verse and prose.

Language · Allāh: the name and its derivation

I:54

“God” (الله) is, on this view, an independently coined (مرتجل), underived (جامد) name; the alif and lām are integral to the word and are not the definite article. Al-Wāḥidī said: “It is a name with which the Creator - glory be to Him - is uniquely named; in describing Him it functions as a proper name, and no derivation is known for it.” Al-Uqlīshī said: “If this name is not derived, it signifies the very Essence without considering in it any particular attribute. It is not a name derived from an attribute, like the Knowing, the Real, the Creator, or the Provider.” On this account the alif and lām in Allāh belong to the word itself, just as the zāʾ belongs to Zayd. A number of scholars held this view; al-Ghazālī preferred it and said that everything proposed by way of derivation is strained.

It has also been said that Allāh is derived from taʾalluh (التألّه), worship or devotion; or from walahān (الولهان), bewilderment, because intellects are bewildered concerning Him. Another view is that its original form was al-ilāh: the hamza was omitted, its vowel transferred to the lām, assimilation (الإدغام) then occurred, and the lām was pronounced emphatically in magnification, except when preceded by kasra.

Language · Rabb: nurture, lordship and meaning

“Lord” (ربّ, rabb) is an adjective qualifying (نعت) “God.” In origin it is a verbal noun (مصدر) meaning nurture or bringing-up (التربية): bringing a thing to its perfection little by little. It is then used descriptively for intensification, as are ṣawm (“fasting”) and ʿadl (“justice”). It is also said to be a descriptive form from rabbahu yarubbuhu, “he tended it / nurtured it”; its original form is rabab, after which assimilation (الإدغام) occurs. An owner is called rabb because he preserves what he owns and nurtures it. The term is not applied to anyone other than God without qualification, as in “Return to your lord” [12:50]. Ibn Juzayy said: “It has four meanings: deity, master, owner, and one who sets things right. All four are suitable in ‘Lord of the worlds,’ though the strongest meaning is ‘deity,’ because that is peculiar to God Most High.”

Language · ʿĀlam: the cosmos as a sign of its Maker

“The worlds” (العالمين) is the plural of ʿālam (عالَم). Ibn ʿAjība explains the word morphologically: al-ʿālam is “a noun for that by which something is known” (اسم لما يُعلَم به), just as khātam is that by which something is sealed and ṭābiʿ that by which something is stamped. The term came to be used predominantly for that by which the Maker is known (ما يُعلَم به الصانع): everything other than Him, comprising substances (الجواهر) and accidents (الأعراض). Because they are contingent or possible (لإمكانها) and stand in need of an efficient cause (مؤثِّر) that is Necessary in Itself (واجب لذاته), they signify His existence. Thus the cosmos functions as a sign (علامة) of its Maker, although Ibn ʿAjība's wording here is literally “that by which [He] is known.” The plural is used to include the differing genera contained beneath the term. Rational beings are given grammatical precedence, so it is pluralised with yāʾ and nūn like their other descriptions. It is therefore a true plural (جمع), not a collective noun (اسم جمع), contrary to Ibn Mālik.

It is also said that the term was coined for beings possessed of knowledge - the angels and the two weighty species (الثقلان), humankind and jinn - and that it includes other beings only by concomitant extension (على سبيل الاستتباع). Another view is that humankind is intended here: every human being is a world (عالَم), since he contains counterparts of what exists in the greater world (العالم الكبير). Hence revelation places contemplation of the two on the same footing: “And in your own selves - do you not see?” [51:21].

Language · Al-Raḥmān and al-Raḥīm: form and meaning

Al-Raḥmān (الرَّحمن, “the Compassionate”) and al-Raḥīm (الرَّحيم, “the Merciful”) are two intensive forms (اسمان بُنيا للمبالغة) derived from raḥima, “to have mercy,” as ghaḍbān is formed from ghaḍiba and ʿalīm from ʿalima. In ordinary language, mercy (الرحمة) is tenderness of heart and an inclination that entails graciousness and beneficence; from the same root comes raḥim, “womb,” because of its tender enclosing of what is within it. The divine Names, however, are taken with regard to their ends (الغايات), which are acts, not their beginnings (المبادئ), which in creatures would be passive affections (انفعالات).

Al-Raḥmān (الرَّحمن) is more intensive than al-Raḥīm (الرَّحيم), because increase in morphological form indicates increase in meaning (زيادة المبنى تدل على زيادة المعنى), as with qaṭṭaʿa and qaṭaʿa. This increase may be understood quantitatively or qualitatively. Quantitatively one says: “Compassionate in this world,” because that mercy encompasses believer and unbeliever, “and Merciful in the hereafter,” because that mercy is particular to believers. Qualitatively one says: “Compassionate in this world and the hereafter, and Merciful in this world,” because all blessings of the hereafter are immense, while worldly blessings include both great and small.

Al-Raḥmān is placed first, although the normal progression (الترقي) would be from the lesser to the greater, because the mercy of this world comes first and because al-Raḥmān has become like a proper name (كالعَلَم), inasmuch as none other is described by it. Its meaning is the true Benefactor whose mercy reaches its utmost limit, and this is true of none but God Most High. See al-Bayḍāwī. The two Names will be discussed again in the running interpretation.

Language · Malik and mālik: readings and annexation

“King” (مَلِك, malik) is an adjective (نعت) qualifying what precedes. The majority recite it without alif, from mulk (المُلك, sovereignty), with ḍamma; ʿĀṣim and al-Kisāʾī recite mālik (مالِك, master/owner) with alif, from milk (المِلك, possession), with kasra. On the latter reading the ellipsis is understood as “Master of the coming of the Day of Recompense,” or “Master of the affair on the Day of Recompense.” Ibn ʿAjība prefers the majority reading for three reasons: first, a king is greater than an owner, for anyone may be called owner of his property whereas the king is master of the people; second, “His is the sovereignty on the day the Trumpet is blown” [6:73]; and third, the reading malik requires no ellipsis (حذف), whereas ellipsis is contrary to the default (خلاف الأصل).

“The Day of Recompense” (يوم الدين) is an adverbial expression (ظرف) annexed (مضاف) to what precedes by an extended construction (على طريق الاتساع). The adverbial is treated syntactically like a direct object (مفعول به), while the meaning remains adverbial: “the King on the Day of Recompense.” Or, on the mālik reading, the sense is “Master of the affair on the Day of Recompense,” in which case an ellipsis (حذف) is posited. Both readings - the short form malik and the long form mālik - are transmitted from the Prophet, God bless him and grant him peace.

I:56

Malik has been recited in several other ways which Ibn ʿAjība omits because they are anomalous (شاذة). If one objects: “Malik and mālik are indefinite (نكرة), because an active participle's annexation (إضافة اسم الفاعل) does not make it specific; how then can either qualify al-Raḥmān al-Raḥīm, which are definite (معرفة)?” The answer is that an active participle's annexation fails to specify only when it denotes present or future time, for then the annexation is non-pure (إضافة غير محضة). Here the meaning is continuously and eternally true, so the annexation is pure (إضافة محضة). This is Ibn Juzayy's explanation.

Exposition · Praise and lordship

The Real, glorious is His majesty, teaches His servants how to praise and magnify Him and then to ask of Him: “My servants, say: ‘Praise belongs to God, Lord of the worlds.’” That is: beautiful praise is deserved only by the Magnificent, the Majestic. None other deserves praise, since in reality there is no benefactor but God: “Whatever blessing you have is from God” [16:53]. Or: all praises whatsoever belong to God. Or: the praise already known to the mind is God's praise of Himself in His beginningless eternity, before He brought His creation into existence. When He brought His creation into existence, He said to them, “Praise belongs to God”: that is, “Praise Me with that praise already known in beginningless eternity.”

He alone deserves praise because He is “Lord of the worlds.” It is as though someone asked Him, “Why does praise belong exclusively to You?” and He answered: “Because I am Lord of the worlds. I brought them into existence through My mercy and sustain them through My bounty. There is no benefactor but Me, and so I alone deserve praise. From Me comes their being brought into existence, and upon Me rests their continually renewed sustenance. I am the Lord of the servants. All the worlds, with their many genera and differing species, are in My grasp and under My nurture and care.”

Exposition · Reports on the worlds and the angelic hosts

Some have said that God created eighteen thousand worlds, half on land and half in the sea. Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī said: “It has been related that the children of Adam are one tenth of the jinn; the children of Adam and the jinn together are one tenth of the land animals; all these are one tenth of the birds; all these are one tenth of the sea animals; all these are one tenth of the angels of the earth charged with the children of Adam; all these are one tenth of the angels of the lowest heaven; and all these are one tenth of the angels of the second heaven. This progression continues to the angels of the seventh heaven.

“All of these, in comparison with the Footstool, are very few. These in turn are one tenth of the angels of a single pavilion among the hundred thousand pavilions of the Throne. Were the heavens and the earth, and all within and between them, compared with the length and breadth of one pavilion, they would be a very small and trifling thing. There is not a handspan of space without an angel prostrating, bowing, or standing, with a resounding voice glorifying God and declaring His unity. Then all these, in comparison with those who move around the Throne, are like a drop in the sea. None but God Most High knows their number: ‘None knows the hosts of your Lord but He’ [74:31].” Here ends the quotation.

Wahb ibn Munabbih said: “The Throne has three hundred and sixty-six supports. Between each pair of supports are sixty thousand deserts; in each desert are sixty thousand worlds; and each world is the size of the two weighty kinds.”

I:56–57

All these worlds are in the grasp of the Real, beneath His nurture and preservation. He conveys sustaining influx (المدد) to each in its settled abode and place of deposit: to its spiritual dimension through the strength of sciences and gnoses (العلوم والمعارف), and to its bodily dimension through the strength of corporeal forms (الأشباح). From the Throne to the earth, their provisions are apportioned, their lifespans delimited, their bodily forms preserved, and their locations known. Nothing on earth or in heaven is hidden from Him; He is the All-Hearing, the All-Knowing.

Exposition · Mercy, origination and sustaining influx

I:57

This nurture (التربية) by which He nurtures His creation is mercy and beneficence from Him, not something binding or obligatory upon Him. Hence He joins to “Lord of the worlds” the words al-Raḥmān al-Raḥīm: the Compassionate through the blessing of bringing-into-existence (نعمة الإيجاد), the Merciful through the blessing of sustaining influx (نعمة الإمداد). As the Ḥikam says: “Two blessings no existent is without, and every originated being must have them: the blessing of being brought into existence and the blessing of sustaining influx. He first favoured you with existence, then followed it with successive sustaining influx.”

His Name al-Raḥmān entails the bringing of things into existence and their manifestation (الإيجاد والإبراز); His Name al-Raḥīm entails their nurture and sustaining influx (التربية والإمداد). Hence the Name al-Raḥmān may not be applied to anyone else, and no one has been named by it, because bringing into existence is not truly possible from other than Him. Al-Raḥīm differs: it may be applied to another, since the issuing of sustaining assistance from some creatures appears, metaphorically and by borrowed attribution, to be shared.

Or: He is al-Raḥmān in this world and the hereafter, and al-Raḥīm in the hereafter, because the mercy of the hereafter is particular to believers. Or: al-Raḥmān with respect to the great blessings (جلائل النعم) and al-Raḥīm with respect to their subtleties (دقائقها). The great blessings include Islam, faith (الإيمان), spiritual excellence (الإحسان), gnosis (المعرفة), guidance, unveiling the veil, opening the door, and entrance among the beloved; subtler blessings include health, well-being, lawful wealth, and the other things to be mentioned under “those upon whom You have bestowed favour.”

Exposition · Sovereignty on the Day of Recompense

He from whom bringing into existence and sustaining truly proceed deserves to be King over all the servants. This is therefore mentioned immediately afterwards: “King of the Day of Recompense.” He disposes of His servants as He wills: none can repel what He decrees, and none can withhold what He gives. He is King of kings and Lord of lords in this abode and the next.

Sovereignty is associated particularly with the Day of Recompense, the day of requital, because on that day God's sovereignty becomes plainly manifest to all creation. God Most High discloses Himself to judge between His servants, so that believers see Him directly. In this world, by contrast, His disposal of affairs is understood only by the perfected among the believers. Many of the ignorant have therefore claimed sovereignty and attributed it to themselves. On the Day of Resurrection, God's sole sovereignty is evident to both the elect and the common people. God Most High says: “Whose is the sovereignty today? God's, the One, the All-Subduing” [40:16].

Spiritual · Divine self-disclosure and self-praise

When the Real, glory be to Him, disclosed Himself (تجلّى) from the realm of jabarūt (عالم الجبروت) into the realm of malakūt (عالم الملكوت) - or, one may say, from the realm of the unseen into the realm of witnessing - He praised Himself by Himself, glorified Himself by Himself, and declared His own unity by Himself. How fine are al-Harawī's words:

Thus, affirming His own unity by Himself, and expressing Himself by Himself, He said: “Praise belongs to God, Lord of the worlds.” It is as though He says in the heading of His Book and the secret of His address: “I am the Praiser and the Praised. I sustain every existent. I am Lord of lords and, for whoever understands the address, the Cause of causes. I am Lord of the worlds, Sustainer of the heavens and the earths. Indeed, I am alone in My existence, disclosing Myself to My servants through My generosity and bounty. All the worlds are established through My establishing them, and effaced through the absolute oneness of My Essence.”

A man said in al-Junayd's presence, “Praise belongs to God,” without adding “Lord of the worlds.” Al-Junayd said to him, “Complete it, my brother.” The man replied, “What standing do the worlds have, that they should be mentioned with Him?” Al-Junayd said: “Say it, my brother. When that which has come to be is placed alongside the Beginningless, that which has come to be vanishes, and the Beginningless remains.”

He says, glory be to Him [in this spiritual interpretation]: “O you who are near to Me, ponder My secret, for it is wondrous. I am the Lover and I am the Beloved; I am the Near and I am the Answerer. I am the Ever-Merciful, the All-Merciful; I am the King, the Requiter. I am All-Merciful through the blessing of bringing into existence, and Ever-Merciful through the succession of sustaining gifts. From Me comes the bringing into existence, and upon Me rests continual sustaining. I am Lord of the servants. I am the King, the Requiter, rewarding goodness with goodness. I am King without qualification — were it not for the ignorance of the people of obstinacy and dissension! For those of humankind who understand Us, command belongs to Us perpetually.”

He says in the Greater Epistles: “What counts is not the outward appearances of things, but the hidden secret. This consists in the manifestation of the affair of the Real, the lifting of its covering, and the removal of its veils and concealment. When that self-disclosure and manifestation are realised, when effacement and passing away overcome things, and darkness is dispersed by the shining of the light, there the eye of certainty appears and the manifest Truth stands established. Then the claims of the claimants are nullified, just as ordinary people understand their nullity on the Day of Recompense, when sovereignty belongs to God, Lord of the worlds. Would that I knew at what time sovereignty belonged to another, so that it should be qualified by His saying, ‘Sovereignty on that day belongs to God’ [22:56], or ‘Command on that day belongs to God’ [82:19]! Were it not for the vast claims of diseased hearts!” Here ends the quotation.

عالَم، نصفها في البر ونصفها في البحر. وقال الفخرُ الرازي: رُوِيَ أن بني آدم عُشْرُ الجن، وبنو آدم والجنُ عُشْرُ حيوانات البر، وهؤلاء كلُّهم عشر الطيور، وهؤلاء كلهم عشر حيوانات البحار، وهؤلاء كلهم عشر 1:5 · Al-Fātiḥa 1:5 — commentary

Cairo I:59–61

“You alone we worship, and from You alone we seek help.” [1:5]

Language · The fronting of iyyāka and the worshipper’s orientation

I say: “You alone” (إياك, iyyāka) is the direct object (مفعول) of “we worship” (نعبد). It is fronted (قُدِّم) for magnification and special attention, and to indicate restriction or exclusivity (الحصر); hence Ibn ʿAbbās said: “We worship You and worship none other with You.” Its fronting also places first what is first in existence: the Sovereign who is worshipped (الملك المعبود). It alerts the worshipper that his regard should be directed first and essentially to the One worshipped (المعبود), and only from Him to the worship - not considering it merely as an act that proceeds from himself, but as a noble relation (نسبة شريفة) to Him and a bond (وصلة) between himself and the Real. The gnostic's arrival (وصول العارف) becomes true only when he is absorbed in attending to the Holy Presence and absent from everything besides It, so that he observes neither himself nor any state of himself except insofar as it is a self-disclosure (تجلٍّ) from His self-disclosures and a locus manifesting His Lordship (مظهر لربوبيته). For this reason what God relates of His Beloved - “Do not grieve; God is with us” [9:40] - is superior to what He relates of His interlocutor Moses - “Indeed, my Lord is with me; He will guide me” [26:62] - insofar as the latter explicitly states the object sought.

Likewise, “You alone” (إياك) is the direct object (مفعول) of “we seek help” (نستعين), and it too is fronted for exclusive specification (الاختصاص) and attention. The pronoun is repeated instead of saying, “You alone we worship and seek help,” because making it explicit a second time is stronger in manifesting reliance upon God, more decisive in bringing attachment and turning to God into presence, and more perfect in praise. Thus “Through You I prevail, through You I seek protection, and through You I attain my aims” is more forceful and laudatory than “Through You I prevail and seek protection,” and so forth.

Exposition · Why worship precedes asking for help

Worship is placed before seeking help so that the endings of the verses correspond, and to make it known that putting forward the means of approach before asking for one’s need is more conducive to receiving an answer. One who has entered the king’s service and begun to perform it to the extent of his capacity, then asks the king’s assistance in it, receives what he asks. This differs from one whom the king charges with his service and who says, “Give me what will help me perform it”: that is discourtesy. Moreover, one who brings those mighty attributes to mind cannot but hasten to submission and worship. Again, when the speaker ascribes worship to himself, this might suggest boasting and self-congratulation over what proceeds from him. He therefore follows it with “and You alone we ask for help,” to dispel that suggestion.

Language · Worship, assistance and the plural “we”

Worship (العبادة, ʿibāda) is the utmost limit of submission and self-abasement; hence ṭarīq muʿabbad means “a road made low and submissive [by being trodden].” Seeking help (الاستعانة, istiʿāna) is requesting assistance. What is intended is assistance in all important affairs, or specifically assistance in performing acts of worship.

The pronoun implicit in the two verbs refers to the reciter together with the guardian angels accompanying him and those present at the congregational prayer; or to him together with all other existing beings. He incorporates his worship into the midst of theirs, and mingles his need with theirs, in the hope that his worship will be accepted through its blessing and his need answered. It is for this reason that congregational prayer was prescribed. So said al-Bayḍāwī. Exposition · Worship and dependence upon God

I:60

The Real, glorious is His majesty, says, completing His instruction of His servants: “When you have praised Me, glorified Me, and magnified Me, acknowledge My lordship, manifest servanthood in yourselves, and ask My assistance at every moment. Say: ‘You alone we worship, and You alone we ask for help.’” It is as though, when He mentioned that He deserves all praise, from eternity and in time, because He is the Lord and Sustainer of the worlds, the origin of all roots and their branches, who first bestowed existence upon them and then a continuous supply of support, and who therefore possesses them absolutely, He went on to declare that none besides Him deserves worship. For there is in reality no benefactor but God. He therefore has the greatest claim to be worshipped, and is most worthy to be made the sole object of orientation and intent. He is wholly independent and draws support from no other. The sustaining supply comes from the very fountain of munificence; were that supply cut off, existence would cease.

Spiritual · The shift to direct address and spiritual presence

Al-Bayḍāwī said: “When the One truly deserving praise had been mentioned and described with mighty attributes by which He is distinguished from all other essences, knowledge became attached to a determinate object of knowledge. He was therefore addressed directly: ‘O You whose affair is thus, we single You out for worship and for seeking help.’ This more strongly indicates exclusive specification (الاختصاص), and it marks an ascent from absence/third-person reference (الغيبة) to witnessing (الشهود): as though what had been known became directly seen, what had been intellectually apprehended became witnessed, and absence became presence. The opening of the discourse is built upon the beginnings of the gnostic's state: remembrance (الذكر), reflection (الفكر), contemplation of His Names, consideration of His favours, and inference from His works to the greatness of His affair and the splendour of His sovereignty. It is then followed by the culmination of his affair: that he plunge into the sea of arrival (لجة الوصول), become one of the people of witnessing (أهل المشاهدة), see Him directly, and converse intimately with Him face to face. O God, make us among those who arrive at the Reality itself (العين), not those who merely follow the trace (الأثر).” Source comparison: Cairo I:60 has دون التابعين للأثر, “rather than those who follow the trace.” The inspected electronic text of al-Bayḍāwī, Anwār al-tanzīl, I:29, on Q1:5, reads دون السامعين للأثر, “rather than those who hear of the trace.” Both contrast arrival with an indirect relation to its evidence, but the source witness specifies hearing where Cairo specifies following. The translation retains Cairo’s wording and records the difference without choosing an unverified authorial reading.

“It is the Arabs’ custom to vary their speech and pass from one mode of expression to another, refreshing and enlivening the hearer. Thus they move from direct address to the third person, and from the third person to the first, as in His saying, ‘Until, when you are in ships and they sail with them upon a wind …’ [10:22]. He did not say ‘with you.’ And in His saying, ‘He sent the winds, which stir up clouds, and We drove them to a land …’ [35:9]”: that is, He did not say, “and He drove them.” Consult his discussion in full.

The rhetorical shift (الالتفات, iltifāt) here occurs in “You alone we worship.” He did not say “Him alone we worship,” although the apparent continuation of the preceding discourse would have been in the third person (الغيبة). The beauty of the shift lies in the fact that the One described has now become determinate and present.

Theology · Divine aid and human action

Al-Uqlīshī said: “This is the verse concerning which the Prophet, God bless him and grant him peace, said: ‘When the servant says, “You alone we worship, and You alone we ask for help,” God Most High says, “This is between Me and My servant, and My servant shall have what he asks.”’ Its meaning is: ‘Whichever servant turns to Me in worship and asks My assistance in it, his worship is accepted, and My assistance in it is assured to him, so that he may perform it as it should be performed.’ Worship is the servant’s attribute, and assistance is from God Most High to the servant. For this reason He said, ‘This is between Me and My servant.’”

Ibn Juzayy said: “That is, we ask Your assistance in worship and in all our affairs. This is evidence of the falsity of the positions of the Qadariyya and the Jabriyya, and that the truth lies between the two.”

Spiritual · Wisdom and power; effacement and subsistence

I:61

When the Real, glorious is His majesty, disclosed Himself from the realm of jabarūt (عالم الجبروت) to the realm of malakūt (عالم الملكوت), and praised Himself by Himself, He also disclosed Himself and descended from the realm of malakūt to the realm of mulk (عالم الملك) through His power (القدرة) and wisdom (الحكمة), in order to manifest the traces of His Names and Attributes. He manifested servanthood (العبودية) and concealed Lordship (الربوبية); He manifested wisdom and concealed power. He thus made the realm of wisdom (عالم الحكمة) address the realm of power (عالم القدرة), submit to it, worship, draw from it assistance and guidance, and seek protection from the path of error and misguidance.

The realm of wisdom (عالم الحكمة) is the locus of legal-moral responsibility (محل التكليف), while the realm of power (عالم القدرة) is the locus of divine disposal (محل التصريف). The realm of wisdom is the realm of bodily forms (عالم الأشباح), and the realm of power the realm of spirits (عالم الأرواح). “You alone we worship” belongs to the people of the realm of wisdom; “from You alone we seek help” to the people of the realm of power. Hence Shaykh Abū al-ʿAbbās al-Mursī, may God be pleased with him, said: “‘You alone we worship’ is Sacred Law (شريعة); ‘from You alone we seek help’ is Reality (حقيقة). ‘You alone we worship’ is islām; ‘from You alone we seek help’ is iḥsān. ‘You alone we worship’ is worship (عبادة); ‘from You alone we seek help’ is servanthood (عبودية). ‘You alone we worship’ is distinction/separation (فرق); ‘from You alone we seek help’ is gathering/unification (جمع).”

Or, if you wish: “‘You alone we worship’ belongs to those who act for God (العمل لله), the sincere; ‘from You alone we seek help’ belongs to those who act through God (العمل بالله), the people of tawḥīd. Acting for God brings reward; acting through God brings nearness. Acting for God brings realisation of worship; acting through God brings rectification of the will. Acting for God is the attribute of every worshipper; acting through God is the attribute of every seeker. Acting for God is standing by the rulings governing outward things; acting through God is standing by the rectification of inward consciences.” So said al-Qushayrī.

People are three classes with respect to witnessing power (شهود القدرة) and wisdom (الحكمة). One class is veiled by wisdom from witnessing power: these are the veiled among the heedless, who stop with “You alone we worship.” A second class is veiled by witnessing power from wisdom: these are the people of effacement (أهل الفناء), who stop with “from You alone we seek help.” A third class is veiled neither by wisdom from power nor by power from wisdom: they give every possessor of a right his right and every possessor of a share his due. These are the perfected among the people of subsistence (أهل البقاء), who combine “You alone we worship” with “from You alone we seek help.” Success is through God.

Language · Guidance and the straight path: language and readings

I:61–62

I say: Guidance (الهداية, hidāya) originally means indicating or directing with gentleness (الدلالة بلطف), and for that reason it is normally used of what is good. “Guide them to the path of Hell” [37:23] is therefore ironic or derisive (على التهكم). The verb is hadā (هدى). Its normal construction takes the preposition li- or ilā; here it governs a direct object without a preposition, by analogy with “Moses chose his people” [7:155]. Ṣirāṭ (الصراط), in the language, means “road.” It is derived from saraṭa al-ṭaʿām, “he swallowed the food,” as though the road swallowed its travellers, those passing along it. The sīn was changed to ṣād to harmonise with the emphatic ṭāʾ (الإطباق); it may also be pronounced with an admixture of zāy because their points of articulation are close. Mustaqīm (المستقيم) means “having no crookedness.” What is intended is the path of the Real (طريق الحق) that brings one to God.

Spiritual · The straight path: Sacred Law and Reality

I:62

The Real, glorious is His majesty, says, teaching His servants how to seek Him and what they ought to seek: Say, “Guide us” - direct us to the straight path that brings one to the Presence of bliss (حضرة النعيم). The straight path is outwardly to travel according to the Muḥammadan Sacred Law (الشريعة المحمدية), and inwardly to divest oneself of any claim to power or strength of one's own (التبرّي من الحول والقوة). Or say: it is that your outward be Sacred Law (شريعة) and your inward Reality (حقيقة); your outward servanthood (عبودية) and your inward freedom (حرية); distinction (الفرق) present outwardly, and gathering (الجمع) witnessed inwardly. In the Ḥikam: “When He makes you outwardly obedient to His command and inwardly surrendered to His overpowering might, He has bestowed an immense favour upon you.”

The straight path that the Real commands us to seek is the conjunction of Sacred Law and Reality (الجمع بين الشريعة والحقيقة), understood from “You alone we worship, and from You alone we seek help.” Hence He joins the petition for guidance directly to those words. It is as though the Real, glorified is He, says: “My servants, praise Me and glorify Me, make Me alone your aim, and single Me out in worship. Outwardly occupy yourselves with My worship; inwardly seek assistance through My power and strength. Or outwardly observe the courtesies of My service; inwardly witness My power and the majesty of My Lordship.”

Our master ʿAlī, may God ennoble his countenance, said: “The straight path here is the Qurʾān.” Jābir, may God be pleased with him, said: “It is Islam,” meaning the generous, upright religion (الحنيفية السمحاء). Sahl ibn ʿAbd Allāh said: “It is the path of Muḥammad, God bless him and grant him peace,” meaning following what he brought. The substance of these statements is what preceded: rectifying the outward through Sacred Law (الشريعة) and the inward through Reality (الحقيقة). This is the straight path; whoever travels it is among those who arrive and are brought near (الواصلين المقربين), together with the prophets and the utterly truthful.

Spiritual · Why the guided continue to ask for guidance

If you ask: when the servant is already proceeding upon this straight way, how can he ask for what he already possesses? The answer is that he asks for firmness in what he has attained and guidance to what he has not attained. The people of the station of islām ask for steadfastness in the islām already attained and ascent (الترقي) to the station of īmān not yet attained. This follows Sufi usage, which assigns outward action specifically to the station of islām and inward action to the station of īmān. The people of īmān ask for steadfastness in the īmān attained and ascent to the station of iḥsān not yet attained. The people of iḥsān ask for steadfastness in iḥsān and ascent into the endless unveilings of gnosis (كشوفات العرفان): “Above every possessor of knowledge is one more knowing” [12:76].

I:62–63

Shaykh Abū al-ʿAbbās al-Mursī, may God be pleased with him, said: “‘Guide us upon the straight path’: by making us firm in what is attained, and directing us towards what is not attained.” Then he said: “The generality of believers say, ‘Guide us upon the straight path,’ meaning: by making us firm in what is attained and directing us towards what is not attained. They have attained the affirmation of unity, but the degrees of the righteous have escaped them. The righteous say, ‘Guide us upon the straight path,’ meaning: we ask You to make us firm in what is attained and direct us towards what is not attained. They have attained righteousness, but the degrees of the martyrs have escaped them. The martyrs say, ‘Guide us upon the straight path,’ meaning: by making us firm in what is attained and directing us towards what is not attained. They have attained martyrdom, but the degrees of the utterly truthful have escaped them. The utterly truthful say, ‘Guide us upon the straight path,’ meaning: by making us firm in what is attained and directing us towards what is not attained. They have attained the degrees of the utterly truthful, but the degrees of the Pole have escaped them. The Pole says, ‘Guide us upon the straight path’: by making him firm in what is attained and directing him towards what is not attained. He has attained the rank of the Pole, but there remains knowledge which, if God wills to acquaint him with it, He acquaints him with.” Here ends the quotation.

I:63

Someone said: “Guidance is either to the Reality itself (العين) or to the trace (الأثر) that indicates the Reality; the first has no end.” I say: the first belongs to the people of witnessing and direct vision (أهل الشهود والعيان), the second to the people of proof and demonstration (أهل الدليل والبرهان). Guidance to the Reality itself is direction to God; guidance to the trace is direction to works. “Whoever directs you to God has counselled you sincerely; whoever directs you to works has wearied you.” The first has no end because ascent (الترقي) after gnosis (المعرفة) has no end. Direction to the trace is otherwise: its end is arrival (الوصول) at the Reality itself, provided the guide knows the path.

Exposition · Al-Bayḍāwī’s four kinds of guidance

Al-Bayḍāwī said: “God’s guidance takes forms beyond numbering: ‘If you count God’s blessing, you will not enumerate it’ [14:34]. Yet these fall within successively ordered kinds:

“The first is the bestowal of the faculties through which a person is enabled to find his way to what benefits him, such as the intellectual faculty, the internal senses, and the external organs of perception.

“The second is the establishment of indications distinguishing truth from falsehood, and soundness from corruption. This is indicated by His saying, ‘We guided him upon the two high ways’ [90:10], and ‘We guided them, but they preferred blindness to guidance’ [41:17].

“The third is guidance by sending messengers and sending down scriptures. This is meant by His saying, ‘We made them leaders, guiding by Our command’ [21:73], and ‘This Qurʾān guides towards that which is most upright’ [17:9].

“The fourth is that He should uncover the mysteries from their hearts and show them things as they are, through revelation, inspiration, and true dreams. The attainment of this is particular to the prophets and the saints. This is meant by His saying, ‘Those are they whom God guided; so follow their guidance’ [6:90], and ‘Those who strive in Us, We shall surely guide them upon Our ways’ [29:69].

“What is sought is therefore either an increase in the guidance already bestowed upon them and steadfastness in it, or the attainment of the degrees consequent upon it. When the gnostic who has arrived says it, he means by his words: ‘Direct us upon the path of journeying in You, that You may efface from us the darknesses of our states and remove the coverings of our bodies, so that we may be illumined by the light of Your holiness and see You through Your light.’” Here ends the quotation.

Exposition · Ibn ʿAjība’s clarification: journeying, arrival and ascent

I say: His wording under “the fourth” is awkward (في عبارته قلق) and overly compressed. The sounder expression would be: “The fourth is that He unveil from their hearts the darknesses and all other-than-God (الظُّلَم والأغيار), cause lights and mysteries (الأنوار والأسرار) to shine upon them, and show them things as they are through revelation and inspiration, and through employing reflection upon the majesty of the all-knowing King, until the lights of the meanings (أنوار المعاني) dominate the sensible aspect of the vessels (حسّ الأواني).” Then one should say: “This is a kind whose attainment is particular to prophets and saints.”

As for his words, “When the gnostic who has arrived says it...,” the sounder wording is: “When the disciple who is journeying (المريد السائر) says it.” For from one who has arrived (الواصل), all darknesses, coverings, and other turbidity have been effaced: God Most High has covered his description with His Description and his attribute with His Attribute, so no dark attribute remains to him. Moreover, “Direct us to the path of journeying (طريق السير)” suits the traveller (السائر), not one who has arrived. The one who has arrived has nothing left but ascent (الترقي); in Sufi technical usage (اصطلاح الصوفية), “journeying” (السير) is used only for what precedes arrival (الوصول). God Most High knows best.

1:7 · Al-Fātiḥa 1:7 — commentary

Cairo I:64–70

“The path of those whom You have blessed, not those upon whom wrath has fallen, nor those who are astray.” [1:7]

Language · Substitution, qualification and negation

I say: the second “path” (صراط) is a substitute (بدل) for the first - specifically, substitution of the whole for the whole (بدل الكل من الكل). It is, in effect, like repeating the grammatical governor (العامل), since the substitute is what is ultimately intended by the syntactic relation (المقصود بالنسبة). Its function is confirmation (التوكيد) and explicit specification (التنصيص), in the strongest and most emphatic manner, that the path of the Muslims is the path attested to be straight. He makes the second phrase an explanation and clarification of the first, as though this were self-evident and without obscurity: the straight path is the path of the believers. “Not those upon whom wrath has fallen” (غير المغضوب عليهم) may itself be a substitute (بدل) for “those” (الذين), meaning that those blessed are precisely those kept safe from wrath and error. Or it may be an explanatory or restrictive adjective (صفة مبيِّنة أو مقيِّدة): they combine the unrestricted blessing - the blessing of faith - with safety from wrath and error. This grammatical analysis is sound only on one of two interpretations: either the relative expression (الموصول) is treated like an indefinite (النكرة), since no previously identified group (معهود) is intended, as with the formally definite expression in the poet's words, “Indeed, I pass by the base fellow who reviles me...”

Or ghayr (غير, “other than/not”) is treated as definite (معرفة) because it is annexed to something having a single contrary - namely, the one blessed - and so it becomes determinate in the way “motion, other than rest” is determinate. Otherwise the construction would require a definite noun (المعرفة) to be qualified by an indefinite adjective (النكرة). Reflect upon it.

I:65

Wrath (الغضب) is the soul's agitation with the will to take vengeance. When attributed to God Most High, what is intended is its end-result (غايته): punishment. “Upon them” (عليهم) stands as the deputy subject (نائب فاعل) of the passive participle al-maghḍūb. The lā in “nor” is pleonastic/additional (مزيدة) for strengthening the negation already contained in ghayr, as though He had said: “neither those upon whom wrath has fallen nor those who are astray.” ʿUmar, may God be pleased with him, recited, “and other than those who are astray.” Error (الضلال) is turning aside from the even path, deliberately or mistakenly. It has a very broad extension, and the distance between its lowest and highest degrees is great. So said al-Bayḍāwī.

The bestowal of favour is ascribed to God, whereas wrath is expressed in the passive, to teach courtesy: “Whatever good befalls you is from God …” [4:79], and the rest of the verse.

Exposition · The recipients of divine favour

The Real, glorious is His majesty, says, explaining the straight path: “It is the path of those upon whom I have bestowed favour through guidance, uprightness, and general and special gnosis: the prophets, the utterly truthful, the martyrs, and the righteous.” “Those upon whom favour has been bestowed” is unrestricted in the verse. It applies to everyone favoured with gnosis and uprightness in his religion, such as the Companions and their like. It has been said that the followers of our master Moses, peace be upon him, before the distortion are intended. It has also been said that the followers of our master Jesus before the alteration are intended. The sound determination is that its meaning is general.

Exposition · Kinds of blessings

Al-Bayḍāwī said: “Although God’s blessings are innumerable, as God says, ‘If you count God’s blessing, you will not enumerate it’ [14:34], they fall into two kinds: worldly and otherworldly.

“The first, the worldly, has two divisions: bestowed and acquired. The bestowed has two divisions. One is spiritual: the breathing of the spirit into a person and his illumination by the intellect and the faculties consequent upon it, such as understanding, thought, and speech. The other is bodily: the formation of the body with the power inhering in it and the conditions that come upon it, such as health and completeness of its members. Acquired blessings include purifying the soul of vices, adorning it with good character and virtuous dispositions, embellishing the body with pleasing appearances and comely ornaments, and acquiring standing and wealth.

“The second, the otherworldly, is that He should forgive a person’s previous faults, be pleased with him, and give him a dwelling in the highest heights, with the angels brought near, for ever and ever. It is this last division that is intended, together with whatever in the first division serves as a means of attaining it. In other things, believers and unbelievers share.” Here ends the quotation.

Spiritual · Degrees of thanksgiving

I:65–66

Ibn Juzayy said: “The blessings for which thanksgiving (الشكر) is rendered are of three kinds: worldly, such as health, well-being, and lawful wealth; religious, such as knowledge, reverent fear, and gnosis (المعرفة); and otherworldly, such as abundant reward for little work.” He also said: “People occupy two stations in thanksgiving. Some give thanks for blessings that reach them personally and belong especially to them; others thank God on behalf of all His creatures for blessings reaching them all. Thanksgiving has three degrees: the common people give thanks for blessings; the elect give thanks for blessings and afflictions, in every state; the elect of the elect become absent from seeing the blessing through witnessing the Benefactor (مشاهدة المنعم).” A man said to Ibrāhīm ibn Adham, may God be pleased with him: “When the spiritual poor are given, they give thanks; when they are denied, they are patient.” Ibrāhīm replied: “Those are the manners of dogs! Our people give thanks when denied, and when given, prefer others to themselves.”

Exposition · Wrath and straying

I:66

He then guarded against the path that is not straight, saying, “not those upon whom wrath has fallen”: that is, not the path of those with whom You are angry. Do not guide us to it or lead us along its way, but keep us safe from the places to which it leads. The Jews are intended; thus the Prophet, God bless him and grant him peace, explained it. In its general application it holds of all those with whom God is angry. “Nor those who are astray”: that is, nor the path of those who are astray, who have lost the truth. These are the Christians, as he, God bless him and grant him peace, said. Both explanations are taken from the Book of God Most High. He says concerning the Jews, “They incurred wrath upon wrath” [2:90], and concerning the Christians, “They went astray before, led many astray, and strayed from the even way” [5:77].

Theology · The divine Names and the three classes of creation

Know that the Real, glorified is He, has divided His creatures into three classes. One class He prepared for generosity and beneficence, to manifest in them His Name the Generous or the Ever-Merciful. These are those favoured with faith and uprightness. Another class He prepared for retribution and wrath, to manifest in them His Name the Avenger or the Overpowering. These are those upon whom wrath has fallen and who stray from the path of truth in understanding or in action: the unbelievers. A third class God prepared for forbearance and pardon, to manifest in them His Names the Forbearing and the Pardoner. These are the disobedient among the believers.

Whoever wishes existence to be free of these three classes, and all people to be alike in guidance or in its opposite, is ignorant of God and His Names. For the effects of His Names must appear in the human being: generosity, overpowering might, forbearance, and the rest. And God Most High knows best.

Spiritual · The path of arrival and spiritual guidance

The straight path that the Real commands us to seek is the path of arrival (طريق الوصول) at the Presence (الحضرة): knowledge of God in the mode of witnessing and direct vision (العلم بالله على نعت الشهود والعيان). This is the station of the special tawḥīd (مقام التوحيد الخاص), the highest degree of the people of tawḥīd; above it is only the tawḥīd of the prophets and messengers. It necessarily requires spiritual formation at the hands of a perfected shaykh who knows the path of journeying (طريق السير), has traversed the stations through tasting (ذوقاً) and unveiling (كشفاً), has attained the stations of effacement and subsistence (الفناء والبقاء), and has combined attraction and wayfaring (الجذب والسلوك). The path is difficult, its travellers few and its highwaymen many; the devil of this path is learned in its stations and the occurrences that arise in them (نوازله). A guide is therefore indispensable, otherwise the traveller will stray from the even way. Ibn al-Bannāʾ alluded to this meaning:

The folk are travellers,
journeying toward the Presence of the Real.
Upon this way they therefore need a guide,
one with insight into travel and the resting-place,
who has travelled the path and then returned
to tell the people what he gained.

I:67

In Laṭāʾif al-minan it is said: “Whoever has no master to connect him with the chain of following (سلسلة الأتباع) and lift the covering from his heart is, in this affair, a foundling without a father and a claimant without lineage. If he possesses light, the usual case is that the spiritual state overwhelms him, and that he stops with whatever comes to him from God. The governance of discipline and refinement (سياسة التأديب والتهذيب) has not trained him, nor have the reins of formation and exercise (التربية والتدريب) led him.” This path is what the reciter of al-Fātiḥa should inwardly call to mind at “Guide us upon the straight path,” together with the ascent (الترقي) mentioned earlier by Shaykh Abū al-ʿAbbās al-Mursī. At “the path of those whom You have blessed,” he should inwardly apprehend: those whom You blessed with arrival (الوصول) and firm establishment (التمكين) in gnosis of You.

Spiritual · Guidance, deprivation and fulfilment

Al-Wartajbī said: “Guide us to what You desire of us; the straight path is what the Real desires from creation: truthfulness and sincerity in His servanthood (عبوديته) and service.” Then: “It has been said: ‘Guide us with the guidance of direct vision (هدى العيان) after clear exposition (البيان), so that we may stand straight before You according to Your will.’ It has also been said: ‘Guide us with the guidance of one whose beginning is from You, so that his end may be unto You.’” He then quotes someone who said: “‘Guide us’ means: make us firm upon the path in which there is no crookedness, namely Islam, the straight path and upright way. ‘The path of those whom You have blessed’: the stations of those whom You blessed with gnosis, love, and beautiful courtesy in service.” He then said: “‘Not those upon whom wrath has fallen’: those expelled from the gate of servanthood. ‘Nor those who are astray’: those bankrupt of the precious treasures of gnosis.”

I say: it is better to say that “not those upon whom wrath has fallen” are those whom following their egoic shares and appetites (الحظوظ والشهوات) has halted from journeying (السير), casting them into the abysses of disobedience and opposition. “Nor those who are astray” are those whom ignorance and imitation (الجهل والتقليد) have imprisoned, so that their inner sights (بصائرهم) do not penetrate to pure tawḥīd. They have recoiled from the tawḥīd of direct vision (توحيد العيان) to the tawḥīd of proof and demonstration (توحيد الدليل والبرهان). Among the people of witnessing and direct vision (أهل الشهود والعيان), this is a kind of error, even if a person reaches the utmost possible degree of righteousness.

Spiritual · Inward attentiveness when reciting al-Fātiḥa

In the Iḥyāʾ it is said: “When you say, ‘In the Name of God, the All-Merciful, the Ever-Merciful,’ understand that all affairs are through God, and that what is intended here is the One named. If all affairs are through God, it necessarily follows that all praise belongs to God.” Then he said: “When you say, ‘The All-Merciful, the Ever-Merciful,’ bring the forms of His gentleness into your heart, so that His mercy may open to you and your hope may be stirred by it. Then let your heart feel magnification and fear at your words, ‘the Day of Recompense.’” Then he said: “Renew sincerity with your saying, ‘You alone we worship,’ and renew helplessness, need, and the disclaiming of strength and power with your saying, ‘and You alone we ask for help.’ Then ask for your need by name, and say, ‘Guide us upon the straight path,’ which leads us to Your proximity and brings us to Your good pleasure. Add explanation, detail, and confirmation to it, and invoke the testimony of those upon whom He poured the blessings of guidance: the prophets, the utterly truthful, the martyrs, and the righteous, as distinct from those upon whom His wrath fell among the unbelievers, the deviating, the Jews, the Christians, and the Sabians.” Here ends the abridged quotation.

I:68

Al-Qushayrī said: “In ‘Guide us upon the straight path,’ the imperative is implicit (الأمر مضمر): that is, ‘Say: Guide us.’ The straight path is the path of the Real, the way of the people of tawḥīd. That is: ‘Direct us to the Real, lest we rely upon the intermediary means of our dealings (وسائط المعاملات) and the dust of suppositions and reckonings fall upon the face of tawḥīd; be Yourself our guide to You.’ ‘The path of those whom You have blessed’ means those who arrive through You unto You. ‘Not those upon whom wrath has fallen’: through forgetting divine enabling (التوفيق) and deliberately blinding themselves to divine support (التأييد). ‘Nor those who are astray’: from witnessing the antecedence of divine choice (سابق الاختيار) and the unfolding dispositions of the decrees (تصاريف الأقدار).”

Exposition · 1. Al-Fātiḥa as a summary of the Qurʾān

This sūrah gathers all the meanings of the Qurʾān, as though it were an abridged copy of it. For this reason it is called the Mother of the Qurʾān. Matters concerning divinity are contained in His saying, “Praise belongs to God, Lord of the worlds, the All-Merciful, the Ever-Merciful”; the abode of the hereafter in His saying, “King of the Day of Recompense”; all forms of worship, comprising beliefs and the outward ordinances entailed by commands and prohibitions, in His saying, “You alone we worship”; the spiritual stations and the mysteries of inward dealings, through purification and adornment [of the soul], in His saying, “Guide us upon the straight path”; the prophets and others in His saying, “those upon whom You have bestowed favour”; and the mention of the classes of unbelievers in His saying, “not those upon whom wrath has fallen, nor those who are astray.”

Shaykh Ibn Abī Jamra, may God be pleased with him, said, explaining how it comprises the Book of God: “The word ‘praise’ includes everything in the Book of God relating to praise and thanksgiving, for praise is more general than thanksgiving: the more general term was used to indicate both attributes. The word ‘God’ indicates what is in the Mighty Book of Names of exaltation and magnification, since it has been said to be God’s Greatest Name. The phrase ‘Lord of the worlds’ indicates what it contains of the Names of God, glorified is He, and of the worlds, their differences, their Creator, and the One who disposes their affairs. ‘The All-Merciful, the Ever-Merciful’ includes everything in the Book relating to forgiveness, mercy, the bestowal of blessings, pardon, and munificence. ‘King of the Day of Recompense’ indicates its mention of the hereafter and its terrors. ‘You alone we worship’ includes its acts of worship and the singling of Him out as God. ‘You alone we ask for help’ indicates its requests for assistance and its mention of utter need. ‘Guide us upon the straight path’ includes its requests for guidance upon the way of good. ‘The path of those upon whom You have bestowed favour’ includes its mention of the elect, those with whom God is pleased, those who are pardoned, and the people of felicity. ‘Not those upon whom wrath has fallen’ includes its kinds of unbelief and transgression, the evil qualities of those concerned, and their final end. It therefore deserves to be called a Mother.” Here ends the quotation.

I:69

It is related from ʿAlī, may God ennoble his countenance, that he said: “Moses, peace be upon him, expounded the Torah in seventy volumes. If the Messenger of God, God bless him and grant him peace, gave me leave, I would load seventy camels with an exposition of al-Fātiḥa.” I say: his mention of “seventy” is approximate, for it admits of more than that. The detail would be lengthy. We have mentioned the foundations of its sciences in our greater commentary upon it. And God Most High knows best.

Theology · 2. Praise, tawḥīd and entering Islam

Ibn Juzayy said: “According to the discerning scholars, our saying, ‘Praise belongs to God, Lord of the worlds,’ is superior to ‘There is no god but God,’ for two reasons. The first is what al-Nasāʾī related from the Messenger of God, God bless him and grant him peace: ‘Whoever says, “There is no god but God,” has twenty good deeds recorded for him, and whoever says, “Praise belongs to God, Lord of the worlds,” has thirty good deeds recorded for him.’ The second is that the affirmation of unity entailed by ‘There is no god but God’ is contained in your saying, ‘Lord of the worlds,’ while the phrase adds your words, ‘Praise belongs to God,’ with the meanings we have already explained. As for his saying, God bless him and grant him peace, ‘The best thing that I and the prophets before me have said is: There is no god but God,’ this is because of the affirmation of unity which it entails. ‘Praise belongs to God, Lord of the worlds’ shares that affirmation and adds something to it. This applies to a believer who has made his faith firm and seeks reward. For one entering Islam, however, ‘There is no god but God’ is specifically required.” Here ends the quotation.

I say: The sound determination is that any expression indicating the affirmation of unity suffices for entering Islam, as al-Bannānī said in his gloss.

Legal · 3. Reciting al-Fātiḥa in prayer

Reciting al-Fātiḥa in prayer is obligatory according to Mālik and al-Shāfiʿī, contrary to Abū Ḥanīfa. We have explained the origin of the disagreement in the greater commentary.

Exposition · 4. Saying Āmīn: reports and practice

Saying Āmīn upon completing al-Fātiḥa is called for because of the supplication it contains. The Messenger of God, God bless him and grant him peace, said: “The Jews have envied you for nothing as they have envied you for the greeting of peace and for saying Āmīn.” Ibn Mājah related it. He also said, God bless him and grant him peace: “God has granted me three distinctions: He granted me prayer in rows; He granted me the greeting, which is indeed the greeting of the people of Paradise; and He granted me the saying of Āmīn. He gave it to none of the prophets before me, unless God gave it to Aaron: Moses would supplicate and Aaron would say Āmīn.” Ibn Khuzayma related it.

He, blessings and peace be upon him, heard a man supplicating insistently and said: “He has made it assured, if he seals it.” Some of those present asked, “With what should he seal it?” He replied: “He should say Āmīn; if he seals it with Āmīn, he has made it assured.” Abū Zuhayr, the transmitter, said: “Āmīn is like the seal (الطابع) upon the sheet.” Ibn ʿAjība adds that this last statement may itself be traceable to the Prophet. Abū Dāwūd related the report.

Language · Āmīn: pronunciation, grammar and meaning

Āmīn may be pronounced with its initial vowel long or short (بالمد والقصر), with an ungeminated mīm; gemination of the mīm is also an attested linguistic form. It has been said to be one of the Names of God Most High. It has also been explained as “O God, answer,” “thus, do [it],” or “thus, let it be.” Al-Mundhirī mentions this in al-Targhīb. Al-Bayḍāwī said: “It is indeclinable with final fatḥa (مبني على الفتح), like ayna, because of the meeting of two quiescent consonants (لالتقاء الساكنين). Its alif is transmitted both long and short.” The poet said: “May God have mercy upon a servant who says Āmīn.”

Legal · Āmīn in congregational prayer

I:70

“By agreement, it is not part of the Qurʾān. It is, however, a sunna to conclude the sūrah with it, because of his saying, blessings and peace be upon him: ‘Gabriel, peace be upon him, taught me Āmīn when I finished reciting al-Fātiḥa.’ He said that it is like the seal upon a letter.

“The imam says it, and says it aloud in prayers recited aloud, because of the report from Wāʾil ibn Ḥujr that when he, blessings and peace be upon him, recited ‘nor those who are astray,’ he would say Āmīn, raising his voice with it. It is related from Abū Ḥanīfa, may God have mercy upon him, that the imam does not say it. The well-known position related from him is that the imam says it quietly, as ʿAbd Allāh ibn Mughaffal and Anas reported.” I say: the well-known position of Mālik’s school is that the imam does not say it in prayers recited aloud.

Then he said: “The person praying behind the imam says Āmīn with him, because of his saying, blessings and peace be upon him: ‘When the imam says, “nor those who are astray,” say Āmīn, for the angels say Āmīn. Whoever’s saying of Āmīn coincides with the angels’ saying of Āmīn will have his previous sins forgiven.’” Success is through God, and He guides to the even way. May God bless our master and patron Muḥammad, the very reality of truth and realisation, and his family and his Companions, purified after him, the standards of the path, and grant them abundant peace.

من تربية على يد شيخ كامل عارف بطريق السير، قد سلك المقامات ذوقاً وكشفاً، وحاز مقام الفناء والبقاء، وجمع بين الجذب والسلوك لأن الطريق عويص، قليلٌ خُطَّارُهُ، كثيرٌ قُطَّاعُه، وشيطان هذه الطريق فَقِيهٌ بمقاماته ونوازِله، فلا بد فيه من دليل، وإلا ضلّ سالكها عن سواء السبيل، وإلى هذا المعنى أشار ابن البنا، حيث قال: وَإِنَّمَا القَوْمُ مُسَافِرُونَ ... لِحَضْرَةِ الْحَقِّ وَظَاعِنُونَ فَافْتَقَرُوا فِيهِ إلَى دَلِيل ... ذِي بَصَرٍ بالسَّيْرِ وَالْمَقِيلِ قَدْ سَلَكَ الطَّرِيقَ ثُمَّ عَادَ ... لِيُخْبِرَ الْقَوْمَ بِمَا اسْتَفَادَ وقال في لطائف المنن: (مَن لم يكن له أستاذ يصله بسلسلة الأتباع، ويكشف له عن قلبه القناع، فهو في هذا الشأن لَقيطٌ لا أب له، دعي لا نسب له، فإن يكن له نور فالغالب غلبة الحال عليه، والغالب عليه وقوفه مع ما يرد من الله إليه، لم تَرْضْهُ سياسةُ التأديب والتهذيب، ولم يقده زمام التربية والتدريب) ، فهذا الطريق الذي ذكرنا هو الذي يستشعره القارئ للفاتحة عند قوله: اهْدِنَا الصِّراطَ الْمُسْتَقِيمَ مع الترقي الذي ذكره الشيخ أبو العباس المرسي رضى الله عنه المتقدم، وإذا قرأ صِراطَ الَّذِينَ أَنْعَمْتَ عَلَيْهِمْ استشعر، أَيْ: أنعمتَ عليهم بالوصول والتمكين في معرفتك. وقال الورتجبي: اهدنا مُرَادَك مِنَّا لأن الصراط المستقيم ما أراد الحق من الخلق، من الصدق والإخلاص في عبوديته وخدمته. ثم، قال: وقيل: اهدنا هُدَى العِيَانِ بعد البيان، لنستقيم لك حسب إرادتِك. وقيل: اهدنا هُدَى مَنْ يكون منك مبدؤه ليكون إليك منتهاه. ثم قال: وقال بعضهم: اهدنا، أي: ثبِّتْنا على الطريق الذي لا اعوجاج فيه، وهو الإسلام، وهو الطريق المستقيم والمنهاج القويم صِراطَ الَّذِينَ أَنْعَمْتَ عَلَيْهِمْ أي: منازل الذين أنعمت عليهم بالمعرفة والمحبة وحسن الأدب في الخدمة. ثم قال: غَيْرِ الْمَغْضُوبِ عَلَيْهِمْ يعني: المطرودين عن باب العبودية، وَلَا الضَّالِّينَ يعني المُفْلِسين عن نفائس المعرفة هـ. قلت: والأحسن أن يقال: غَيْرِ الْمَغْضُوبِ عَلَيْهِمْ هم الذين أَوْقَفَهُمْ عن السير اتباعُ الحظوظ والشهوات، فأوقعهم في مَهَاوِي العصيان والمخالفات، وَلَا الضَّالِّينَ هم الذين حبسهم الجهل والتقليد، فلم تنفُذْ بصائرهم إلى خالص التوحيد، فنكصوا عن توحيد العيان إلى توحيد الدليل والبرهان، وهو ضلال عند أهل الشهود والعِيان، ولو بلغ في الصلاح غايةَ الإمكان. وقال في الإحياء: إذا قلت بِسْمِ اللَّهِ الرَّحْمنِ الرَّحِيمِ فافْهَمْ أن الأمور كلها بالله، وأن المراد هاهنا المُسمَّى، وإذا كانت الأمورُ كلها بالله فلا جرَم أنَّ الحمد كله لله، ثم قال: وإذا قلت: الرَّحْمنِ الرَّحِيمِ فأحضرْ في قلبك أنواعَ لطفه لتتفتحَ لك رحمتُه فينبعث به رجاؤُك، ثم استشعر من قلبك التعظيم والخوف من قولك: يَوْمِ الدِّينِ. ثم قال: ثم جَدَّد الإخلاص بقولك: إِيَّاكَ نَعْبُدُ. وجدَّد العجز والاحتياج والتبرِّيَ من الحوْل والقوة بقولك: وَإِيَّاكَ نَسْتَعِينُ، ثم اطلب اسم حاجتك، وقل: اهْدِنَا الصِّراطَ الْمُسْتَقِيمَ الذي يسوقنا إلى جوارك ويُفضي بنا إلى مرضاتك، وزِدْهُ شرحاً وتفصيلاً وتأكيداً، واستشهد بالذين أفاض عليهم نعم الهداية من النبيين والصديقين والشهداء والصالحين، دون الذين غضب عليهم من الكفار والزائغين واليهود والنصارى والصابئين. هـ. ملخصاً. وقال القشيري: قوله تعالى: اهْدِنَا الصِّراطَ الْمُسْتَقِيمَ الأمر في هذه الآية مضمر، أي: قولوا: اهدنا. والصراط المستقيم: طريق الحق، وهو ما عليه أهل التوحيد، أي: أرشدْنا إلى الحق لئلا نتكل على وسائط المعاملات، فيقعَ على وجه التوحيد غُبَارُ الظنون والحسابات لتكون دليلنا عليك. ثم قال: صِراطَ الَّذِينَ أَنْعَمْتَ عَلَيْهِمْ أي: الواصلين بك إليك، ثم قال: غَيْرِ الْمَغْضُوبِ عَلَيْهِمْ بنسيان التوفيق والتَّعامِي عن رؤية التأييد، وَلَا الضَّالِّينَ عن شهودِ سابقِ الاختيار، وجريان تصاريف الأقدار. هـ. تتمات: الأولى: هذه السورة جمعت معانى القرآن كلّها، فكأنها نسخة مختصرة منه، ولذلك سميت أمّ القرآن، فالإلهيات حاصلة من قوله: الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ الْعالَمِينَ. الرَّحْمنِ الرَّحِيمِ، والدار الآخرة من قوله: مَلِكِ يَوْمِ الدِّينِ، والعبادات كلّها من الاعتقادات والأحكام الظاهرة التي تقتضيها الأوامر والنواهي، من قوله: إِيَّاكَ نَعْبُدُ والمقامات وأسرار المعاملات الباطنة- تخلية وتحلية- من قوله: اهْدِنَا الصِّراطَ الْمُسْتَقِيمَ والأنبياء وغيرهم من قوله: الَّذِينَ أَنْعَمْتَ عَلَيْهِمْ وذكر طوائف الكفار من قوله: غَيْرِ الْمَغْضُوبِ عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا الضَّالِّينَ. وقال الشيخ ابن أبي جمرة رضى الله عنه فى بيان تضمنها لكتاب الله: إن لفظ (الحمد) يتضمن كل ما في كتاب الله من الحمد والشكر لأن الحمد أعمّ من الشكر، وأتى بالعام ليدل على الصفتين. ولفظة (الله) تدل على ما فى الكتاب العزيز من أسماء الترفيع والتعظيم لأنه قيل: أنه اسم الله الأعظم، ولفظ: رَبِّ الْعالَمِينَ يدل على ما فيه من أسماء الله، سبحانه، وعلى العوالم وعلى اختلافها وخالقها والمتصرف فيها. ولفظ: الرَّحْمنِ الرَّحِيمِ يتضمن كل ما فى الكتاب من المغفرة والرحمة والإنعام والعفو والإفضال، ولفظ مَلِكِ يَوْمِ الدِّينِ يدل على ما فيه من ذكر الآخرة وما فيه من الأهوال، ولفظ إِيَّاكَ نَعْبُدُ يتضمن ما فيه من التعبّدات وإفراده بالألوهية، ولفظ إِيَّاكَ نَسْتَعِينُ يدل على ما فيه من طلب الاستعانة وذكر الاضطرار، ولفظ اهْدِنَا الصِّراطَ الْمُسْتَقِيمَ يتضمن ما فيه من طلب الهداية إلى سبيل الخير، ولفظ: صِراطَ الَّذِينَ أَنْعَمْتَ عَلَيْهِمْ يتضمن ما فيه من ذكر الخصوص والمرضىّ عنهم والمعفوّ عنهم وأهل السعادة، ولفظ غَيْرِ الْمَغْضُوبِ عَلَيْهِمْ يتضمن ما فيه من أنواع الكفر والمخالفات ومساوئهم ومآلهم فاستحقّت أن تسمى أمّا. هـ. وعن عليّ- كرّم الله وجهه- قال: (شرح موسى عليه السّلام التوراة فى سبعين سفرا، ولو أذن لى رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلّم لأوقرت على الفاتحة سبعين بعيرا) . قلت: قوله (سبعين) تقريبا، وإلا فهى قابلة لأكثر من ذلك، وتفصيل ذلك يطول، وقد ذكرنا أصول علومها فى شرحنا الكبير عليها. والله تعالى أعلم. الثانية: قال ابن جزىّ: قولنا: الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ الْعالَمِينَ أفضل عند المحققين من (لا إله إلا الله) لوجهين: أحدهما: ما أخرج النّسائيّ: عن رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلّم «أنه مَن قال: لا إله إلا الله، كتبت له عشرون حسنة، ومن قال: الحمد لله رب العالمين، كتبت له ثلاثون حسنة» . والثاني: أن التوحيد الذي تقتضيه (لا إله إلا الله) ، حاصل فى قولك: (رب العالمين) وزادت بقولك: الحمد لله، وفيه من المعاني ما قدّمنا. وأما قوله صلى الله عليه وسلّم: «أفضل ما قلته أنا والنبيون من قبلى: لا إله إلا الله» فإنما ذلك للتوحيد الذي تقتضيه، وقد شاركتها (الحمد لله رب العالمين) فى ذلك وزادت عليها. وهذا لمؤمن حقّق إيمانه وطلب الثواب، وأما لمن دخل فى الإسلام فيتعيّن «لا إله إلا الله» . هـ. قلت: والتحقيق أن كل ما يدل على التوحيد من الألفاظ يكفى فى الدخول فى الإسلام، كما قال البنانىّ فى حاشيته. الثالثة: قراءة الفاتحة فى الصلاة واجبة عند مالك والشافعي خلافاً لأبي حنيفة، وقد ذكرنا فى الشرح الكبير منشأ الخلاف. الرابعة: التأمين عند ختم الفاتحة مطلوب للدعاء الذي فيها، قال رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلّم: «ما حسدتكم اليهود على شىء ما حسدتكم على السّلام والتّأمين» . رواه ابن ماجة. وقال أيضا صلى الله عليه وسلّم: «إن الله أعطانى خصالا ثلاثة: أعطانى صلاة الصّفوف وأعطانى التحية، وإنها لتحية أهل الجنة، وأعطانى التأمين، ولم يعطه أحدا من النبيين قبلى، إلا أن يكون الله أعطاه هارون، يدعو موسى ويؤمن هارون» رواه ابن خزيمة. وسمع عليه الصلاة والسلام رجلا يدعو ويلح فقال: «أوجب إن ختم» فقال بعض القوم: بأيّ شىء يختم؟ فقال: «يؤمن فإنه إن ختم بآمين فقد أوجب» . قال أبو زهير- راوى الحديث- فإن آمين مثل الطّابع على الصحيفة. ولعلّه مرفوع إلى النبي صلى الله عليه وسلّم، رواه أبو داود. ولفظ آمِّينَ بالمدّ والقصر مخففا. وتشديد الميم لغة. قيل: هو اسم من أسماء الله تعالى.. وقيل معناه: اللهم استجب، أو كذلك فافعل، أو كذلك فليكن. قاله المنذرى فى الترغيب. قال البيضاوي: بنى على الفتح كأين لالتقاء الساكنين، وجاء مدّ ألفه وقصرها. قال: ويرحم الله عبدا قال آمينا(1) وليس من القرآن اتفاقا، ولكن يسنّ ختم السورة به لقوله عليه الصلاة والسلام: «علّمنى جبريل عليه السّلام آمين عند فراغى من قراءة الفاتحة» . وقال: إنّه كالختم على الكتاب. ويقوله الإمام ويجهر به فى الجهرية، لما روى عن وائل بن حجر «أنه عليه الصلاة والسلام كان إذا قرأ: وَلَا الضَّالِّينَ قال: آمين، رفع بها صوته» وعن أبى حنيفة- رحمه الله- أنه لا يقوله. والمشهور عنه أنه يخفيه كما رواه عبد الله بن مُغفل وأنس. قلت: ومشهور مذهب مالك أن الإمام لا يقوله فى الجهرية. ثم قال: والمأموم يؤمن معه لقوله- عليه الصلاة والسلام-: «إذا قال الإمام ولا الضالين، فقولوا: آمين، فإن الملائكة تقول: آمين، فمن وافق تأمينه تأمين الملائكة غُفِر لَه ما تقدَم من ذنبه» . وبالله التوفيق، وهو الهادي إلى سواء الطريق. وصلّى الله على سيدنا ومولانا محمد عين الحق والتحقيق، وعلى آله وصحبه المطهرين بعده، أعلام الطريق، وسلّم تسليما.
