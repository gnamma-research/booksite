## Correct scholarly standing — unchanged Arabic and no invented repair

- **SH-002:** A and H2014 contain `القطع ببطلان مسألة التوحيد`; earlier in the same argument stands `القطع بحقيّة هذه المسألة`, and the immediate preceding quotation repeats `إذ القطع ببطلان جميع الأحكام العقلية`. A scribal attraction of **ببطلان** may explain the paradox and a **conjectural** `بحقيّة` would restore coherence, **but no inspected historical witness contains that positive substitution at the disputed point**. The reading edition remains literal and the conjecture is confined to a scholarly note.
- **QT-059:** The available ancestor's paragraph is fully translated; the reported larger ancestral omission has **not** been repaired or its exact extent verified. The independently edited original by Majīd Hādīzādeh is bibliographically located in volume IV of *Nuṣūṣ wa-Rasāʾil* at pp. **37–72**, but only vols. I–III are publicly supplied by the inspected collection. Its actual pages were **not read**.
- **New promising historical printing:** the Tehran **1315 AH (1898)** lithographic *Tamhīd*, 234 pp, quoted treatise pp. 2–181, is independently indexed by Kyoto / NII under **BB28267336** and by CDSIA, and a matching 1898/234pp **free ebook is advertised** on Google Play. The relevant *pages have not been inspected*; the listing does not prove that its SH-002 or QT-059 readings differ. Its edition may derive from the same manuscript tradition as later prints.
- **Independent old manuscript lead:** British Library **Bijapur 128/2** (869–871 AH), catalogued but page photographs not acquired.

The current electronic-text translation and interface **are complete**. **Two historical textual-critical questions remain genuinely open**. A catalogued or advertised edition does not supply its unread Arabic; mathematical or doctrinal intuition cannot recover a lost folio. A definitive historical reading awaits actual early copy or independent original-text pages.
