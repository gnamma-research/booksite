## SH-002: precise textual logic and alternatives

### A. Attested wording and its voices

In the ancestor's **QT-002**, the opponents say that certainty about the doctrine indicates mental/physiological derangement, because, *as they see it*, it implies the overturning of ordinary rational and sensory distinctions. The ancestor quotes their objection; he does not endorse its old humoral medical premise.

In the grandson's **SH-002**, the argument is made more explicit. The passage earlier calls the disputed belief `القطع بحقيّة هذه المسألة` (certainty in the *truth* of this thesis). It immediately follows with:

> `إذ القطع ببطلان جميع الأحكام العقلية`

and then the disputed line:

> `و بيان ذلك ان القطع ببطلان مسألة التوحيد قطع ببطلان جميع اقسام اليقينيّات`

The exact `القطع ببطلان مسألة التوحيد` is **present both in the A electronic base and H2014's modern printed quotation** (H2014 I:221–224, relevant locus p. 224). This is evidence for the **transmitted text**, *not* for its logical correctness or historical archetypal priority.

The objection's explanation that distinguishing quiddities and determinations contradicts an absolutely unifying thesis supports the intended direction: the *affirmation* of that thesis is what the objector claims would undercut ordinary cognition. A claim that the thesis is *false* does not yield that consequence. Accordingly the strongest **internal, conjectural explanation** is an attraction from the immediately preceding `ببطلان جميع الأحكام العقلية`, reproducing `ببطلان` where `بحقيّة` (or an equivalent positive expression) would be coherent.

### B. Two translations, kept distinct

- **Witness-exact English (retained in reader):** “Certainty that the doctrine of unity is invalid entails certainty that every class of certain cognition is invalid.” **Transmitted but logically awkward.**
- **Critical *interpretative* paraphrase, unattested as Arabic:** “Certainty that the doctrine of unity is true would, according to these opponents, overturn every class of cognition they consider certain.” **Probable intended argument, not verified original wording.**

**Do not** insert `بحقّيّة` in the running Arabic, call it a witnessed variant, or add an intrusive “sic” in normal reading. The conjecture belongs in this note and the scholarly reference apparatus. A correct translation must not falsely claim the printed Arabic says what it does not.

### C. What would close the historical reading?

A photograph, genuinely distinct printed copy, or collated medieval manuscript containing the relevant line (with antecedent and succeeding Arabic clauses), with source identity and page/folio, is required. The existence of two modern editions sharing `ببطلان` cannot establish that the textual corruption **did not** originate in a shared ancestor.

**Editorial decision: finished. Independent historical proof: unavailable.**

## QT-059: extant text, argument and alleged additional words

### A. The *complete surviving* quotation

The original author's surviving `قال` begins:

> `وما قيل إنّ علوم النظّار مأخوذة من الحواسّ وعلوم أصحاب المجاهدة مأخوذة من المبدأ الفيّاض فليس بشيء`

and concludes:

> `وأمّا الثاني فلما بيّنّاه في كتبنا المعمولة في صناعة الحكمة`

The argument refutes an excessive contrast between (1) inferential inquiry supposedly originating in sense perception and (2) contemplative knowledge supposedly originating in the effusive Principle. Both paths require sense-related and embodied preparatory conditions, while what **bestows knowledge as such** is the effusive Principle. The argumentative force is *iʿdād* (preparation/disposition) versus *ifāḍa* (bestowal), not a refusal of either rational study or spiritual purification.

The final sentence points **elsewhere**, to the author's philosophical works for the demonstration of the second thesis. An authorial referral **is not itself a gap**, an editorial ellipsis, or permission to insert the grandson's explanation.

### B. The reported ancestral omission

The working edition's prior apparatus reports that an editor's Persian note, associated with Āshtiyānī printed pp. 262–263, speaks of **more than a page** of ancestral text omitted by a copyist. The **actual Persian note and a distinct ancestor folio have not been independently photographed and compared in this research run**. Thus “a reported loss in the separately transmitted original” is justified, but its precise locus, extent and words are not presently verified.

The modern H2014 printed commentary, II:571–572, quotes the same extant ancestral argument, without furnishing the alleged additional ancestral words. The grandson's `SH-059` discusses why premises prepare the recipient while the Principle bestows knowledge independently; this is an illuminating **commentarial explanation** of the argument, not an original quotation recoverable as the grandfather's missing words.

**Reader policy:** offer the full surviving QT-059 uninterrupted; link the source-critical statement through reference apparatus. No “restored” paragraph, artificial page of dots, false original, or false English completion.

### C. What would close the historical loss?

The separately edited original *Qawāʿid al-Tawḥīd* attributed to Majīd Hādīzādeh, *Nuṣūṣ wa-Rasāʾil min Turāth Iṣfahān al-ʿIlmī al-Khālid*, vol. IV, **pp. 37–73** (the photographed bibliography, *al-Khizāna*, 2018, printed p. 521, no. 301) is the highest-priority textual check. Its text pages have not been acquired and **a bibliographic mention is not a textual variant**. The older Bijapur 128/2 manuscript may embed the ancestor's lemma, but its actual relevant folios remain inaccessible here.

The editor's Persian note itself must also be inspected before stating definitively **where** the alleged lacuna lies. A possible *omission from the stand-alone ancestral work* and a *difference in the descendant's selection of quotation* are distinct phenomena.

**Editorial decision: finished. Claimed lost authorial words: never supplied without evidence.**
