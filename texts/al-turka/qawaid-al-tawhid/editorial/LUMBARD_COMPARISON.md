# Lumbard comparison memo — *Qawāʿid al-Tawḥīd* (ancestral matn)

**Gnamma Research · 2 November 2026**

## Identification

Joseph E. B. Lumbard, “Establishing the Principles,” in Seyyed Hossein Nasr and Mehdi Aminrazavi (eds.), *An Anthology of Philosophy in Persia*, vol. 4, pp. **464–479**. The 16-page [published PDF](https://traditionalhikma.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/08/Annotated-translation-from-Tamhi%CC%84d-al-Qawa%CC%84%CA%BEid-of-Ibn-Turkah-al-Is%CC%A3faha%CC%84ni%CC%84-by-Joseph-Lumbard.pdf) translates a **selection of the descendant's sharḥ**, including short quotations of the grandfather's *matn*. This is a **secondary English translator's comparison**, not a distinct Arabic witness or full translation of the ancestor's treatise. Lumbard's first note specifies the Āshtiyānī and Ḥasan-zādeh Āmulī editions he compared and identifies some differences in their readings.

The Gnamma English translation is independently composed from the original Arabic, checked **against** Lumbard, and not reproduced from his English wording. His interpretation and notes must not be silently substituted for readings of the Arabic.

## Critical separation of works in Lumbard's selection

**Important source-attribution trap:** Lumbard's pp. **474–476** discuss Suhrawardī and the reality of existence partly by reproducing material which Ibn Turka attributes to the ancestor's *al-Iʿtimād* and *al-Ḥikmah al-Rashīdiyyah* (and refers to *al-Ḥikmah al-Manīʿa*). These are **not quotations from the *Qawāʿid al-Tawḥīd* matn merely because the grandfather is their author.** They may become evidence in a separate parallel philosophical sourcebook but cannot be inserted between QT-005 and QT-006.

Similarly, Lumbard's text beginning with Ibn Turka's own doxology (pp. 464–468) belongs to the **sharḥ**, not the shorter ancestor's original. The matn begins in the selection only where the commentator explicitly introduces his grandfather's words around p. **468**.

## Interpretation versus textual variants

- **Lumbard's English preference** ≠ an Arabic textual variant.
- **Variant readings mentioned in his annotations** are leads for consultation of the printed editions, not independent manuscript witnesses.
- **Āshtiyānī's added subsection heading** is a feature of the modern edition, not an attested ancestor heading.
- **Ḥads** (intellectual intuition), **naẓar** (discursive reasoning), **ishtirāk maʿnawī** (commonality of meaning), and **wājib li-dhātihi** (necessary through itself) must retain their technical force in the translation. Glosses using Akbarian terminology should be clearly identified as supplied by the descendant.
