# Lumbard comparison standard — Tamhīd commentary

**3 November 2026.** The complete medieval commentary is the translation target. Joseph E. B. Lumbard is a substantive comparator, not merely a bibliographical mention; the Arabic remains decisive.

## Comparator and scope

**L:** Joseph E. B. Lumbard, “Establishing the Principles,” *An Anthology of Philosophy in Persia*, IV, pp.464–479. [Published selection](https://traditionalhikma.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/08/Annotated-translation-from-Tamhi%CC%84d-al-Qawa%CC%84%CA%BEid-of-Ibn-Turkah-al-Is%CC%A3faha%CC%84ni%CC%84-by-Joseph-Lumbard.pdf).

His selection covers the preface and selected early matn/commentary. It does not supply the intervening systematic prolegomenon translated here as SH-INTRO-008–040. His comparisons of printed editions are useful, but his English is not an Arabic manuscript witness.

The opening preface is compared at L pp.464–468. Where the Arabic states exclusive alternatives, a warning against a possible consequence, or an explicit family blessing, the new translation follows that syntax rather than importing a differing English construction. These are local translation decisions, not a blanket judgment on Lumbard’s work.

## Arabic controlling text and newly translated material

**N:** Ibn Turka’s medieval text as reproduced in Muḥammad Riḍā al-Nekounam’s edition, Tehran: Ṣubḥ-i Fardā, 1393 SH; [online authorial introduction](https://ayatollahnekounam.com/2018/07/16/التمهید-فی-شرح-قواعد-التوحید-الطلیعه-و/). The modern editor’s own introduction, inserted explanatory glosses, and footnotes are excluded.

The source expressly distinguishes **العلم الإنّي والكشف العِلّي**; the translation preserves the effects/causes contrast. It also distinguishes **الوجود المجرد المطلق** from **مطلق الوجود** without treating the addition of absoluteness as harmless. These decisions arise from the Arabic text, not from a commitment to uniform English labels regardless of context.

The present installment translates the continuous authorial opening into **40 bilingual units**, from the doxology to the explicit end of the prolegomenon. Its page references are **N’s**, not Āshtiyānī’s. N’s final online continuation follows the marker 42 without a following page-ending label; its locator is recorded accordingly. The new closing translation uses **A**, the Āshtiyānī digital transcription, pp.270–274.

## Publication policy

All English paragraphs are independently composed from the medieval Arabic. L is acknowledged wherever its terminology or interpretation is used as a comparator. Extensive modern translation prose is not reproduced. A term adopted from L is not silently represented as a newly discovered Arabic variant. A source that supplies only a modern summary does not license invented Arabic or a claimed source translation.

**Current result:** 40 introductory units plus 6 concluding commentary units. The intervening main commentary remains untranslated; this is not the completed whole work.
