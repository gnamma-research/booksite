## Initial close-reading dossier

1. **Ibn Turka:** SH-015, A pp.83–87 (tashkīk); SH-017, pp.90–93 (opposition versus comprehensive determination); SH-027–028, pp.118–130 (names, unity, first determination); SH-030–031, pp.133–141 (knowledge and relation); SH-035/035-B, pp.154–160 (possibility and causation under different considerations); SH-037, pp.164–167 (greater comprehensiveness of manifestation at a lower level).
2. **Qayṣarī:** opening corollary in the Muqaddima to the Fuṣūṣ. Mukhtar H. Ali, *The Horizons of Being*, bilingual pp.48–51. Distinguish the original argument from modern translator notes.
3. **Avicenna:** *Ilāhiyyāt* I.5.21 (Marmura p.27), VI.3.27–29 (pp.213–214), and logical treatments of mushakkik names. Do not substitute intensive gradation for every form of unequal predication.
4. **Aristotle:** *Metaphysics* IV.2 (1003a33–b19), V.7 (1017a7–b9), IX.6; *Nicomachean Ethics* I.6 (1096b26–29). Accidental being is not just being an accident; focal unity is not automatically proportionality.
5. **Aquinas:** *SCG* I.26; *ST* I q.13 aa.2–6, I q.44 a.1, and I q.4. Distinguish participated intrinsic actuality from merely extrinsic denomination and from numerical identity with God's act of being.
6. **Poinsot:** *Material Logic*, q.13 and q.14 a.3; English *Basic Treatises*, pp.170–172 and 204–207. Do not impose his technical restriction of attribution on every Thomist.
7. **Ṣadrā:** obtain direct passages from *al-Asfār* or *al-Mashāʿir*. Use Ṭabāṭabāʾī's *Bidāyat al-Ḥikma* I.5 as an expressly later exposition, not a substitute for examining Ṣadrā's own formulations.
8. **Henry J. Koren:** *An Introduction to the Science of Metaphysics*. The title was identified, but the relevant chapter has not been consulted. His co-translation with Edward A. Bushinski of Cajetan's *The Analogy of Names, and the Concept of Being* is another lead. Verify actual content before attributing a doctrine to him.

## Questions to settle

What is being determined or graded in each passage: a concept, predication, reality, name, relation, receptivity or manifestation? What does iʿtibārī exclude? How can something be more comprehensive in manifestation without being intrinsically nobler? Does the Thomist category of an intrinsically real but dependent accident provide a relevant counterexample, or is it answering a different question?

Give source-grounded distinctions, short original-language excerpts, qualified translations, and a matrix of the competing claims. Do not assume doctrinal identity from shared words, shared examples, or rejection of being as a genus. Treat the previous comparison as a research map to test, not a settled verdict.
