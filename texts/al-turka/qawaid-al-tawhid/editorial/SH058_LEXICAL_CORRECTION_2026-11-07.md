# SH-058 — second-pass translation correction

**7 November 2026 · A, printed pp.260–262 · Arabic witness status: retained electronic transcription, no inspected manuscript images.**

**Issue:** the previous working English rendered **النوم والسهو** as “sleep and wakefulness.” In Arabic, **السهو** denotes inadvertence, distraction, or a lapse of attention, **not** wakefulness. The latter would require a different reading such as **السهر** (staying awake), for which there is presently no established manuscript evidence. The full phrase in the preserved source is **النوم والسهو وما يوجبهما من الجوع والشبع**.

**Corrected English (local context):** “The same applies to sleep and lapses of attention, and to the hunger and satiety that can give rise to them. Excess in any of these dulls and weakens the discursive faculty…”

This follows the electronic Arabic as it stands rather than introducing an unacknowledged emendation. It also preserves the causal relationship of hunger and satiety to the disturbances. The Arabic text is **not changed**.

**Internal comparison:** the same commentary uses the distinct Arabic word **السهر** in SH-002 and SH-047, where the English appropriately renders excessive sleeplessness. The author's introductory SH-INTRO-008 uses **السهو** in the sense of oversight. This strengthens the lexical correction for SH-058, without resolving whether the printed A base itself contains an error.

## New clause-level parallel (8 November 2026)

**The ancestor's actual lemma QT-058 reads «وهكذا الكلام في النوم والسهر»**, explicitly invoking sleep and sleeplessness, whereas the descendant’s electronic commentary SH-058 reads «وهكذا الكلام في النوم والسهو وما يوجبهما من الجوع والشبع». This is stronger contextual evidence than the earlier cross-references from SH-002 and SH-047. It does **not** establish that the SH-058 spelling is an error: sleep and lapses of attention can be causally associated with hunger and satiety, while the ancestor's immediate wording suggests sleep and sleeplessness. A printed-image or independent witness check is required.

**Editorial disposition:** Do not silently harmonize the two texts. In the SH-058 translation, «السهو» is provisionally represented by “lapses of attention”; the ancestor's different «السهر» is translated as “sleeplessness”. The earlier “wakefulness” wording is supported as an *interpretation of the ancestor’s wording*, but not a literal translation of the recorded descendant's «السهو». Any assertion that the first English was definitively wrong in doctrinal context should be qualified accordingly.

**Remaining question:** inspect the printed page or independent Arabic witness to determine whether the electronic transcription has confused **السهو**, **السهر**, or another reading. Until that is done, report the issue as a possible source-level problem, not a definite scribal correction. The Arabic source fingerprint is preserved; this is an English translation correction only.
