Working casebook v0.1 · B02

Extent of wiping the head

adopted_provisionally Medium confidence B02

Provisional result: A genuine partial head-wipe suffices with no fixed fraction; the full sweep remains the strongly evidenced standard performance. Preserve forelock and turban together when interpreting Muslim 274h.

Articles: C02, C03, C06, C11. Source references: F_Q5_6 F_BID_WIPE F_UMM_WIPE F_B185 F_M274H F_B157.

Full original case discussion

B02 — How much of the head must be wiped?

Provisional conclusion — existing ruling, newly organized rationale; medium confidence. A real wet wiping of some scalp, or of hair situated within the head’s boundary, satisfies the minimum. No quarter, third, number of fingers, or number of hairs is imposed. Wiping across the whole head remains the strongly evidenced standard performance. Someone who wipes a substantial front portion of an uncovered head has not, on this provisional rule, omitted a necessary quantitative condition. This conclusion does not make an unrelated touch, dry contact, or water landing on the head automatically equivalent to wiping.

The dispute and its evidence. Ibn Rushd, Bidāyat al-mujtahid, Book of Purification, wudu, second chapter, sixth question of delimitation, records Mālik’s whole-head requirement, partial-wiping positions, Abū Ḥanīfa’s quarter, and al-Shāfiʿī’s absence of a specified fraction. His grammatical discussion identifies an interpretative dispute; it does not license treating one account of the Qurʾānic bāʾ as a conclusive calculation. Qurʾān 5:6 establishes head-wiping. The contested proposition is its minimum extent. [F_BID_WIPE; F_Q5_6]

The positive demonstration must receive substantial weight. In Bukhārī 185, ʿAbd Allāh b. Zayd demonstrates the Prophet’s ablution: hands move from the front of the head to the nape and return. This supports teaching a full sweep, rather than constructing ordinary practice around the smallest imaginable contact. Muslim 274h reports wiping the forelock and the turban, alongside the footwear. The turban is a stable component of that report. Removing it from the description and presenting the remainder as a demonstrated bareheaded minimum would misrepresent the evidence. [F_B185; F_M274H]

The charter’s reasoning. Three propositions need distinct warrants: head-wiping is obligatory; the full sweep is a demonstrated sufficient and commendable performance; every smaller wiping is invalid. The first two do not logically entail the third. Al-Shāfiʿī’s own al-Umm, “Bāb masḥ al-raʾs,” explicitly recognizes partial wiping while preferring the full movement. This supplies a historical legal reading of the command, not merely a later grammatical slogan. The present proposal accepts its distinction between minimum and preferred performance, while declining to make its abbreviated or mursal reports independent proof that a turban may be silently omitted from a composite concession. [F_UMM_WIPE]

The inference is deliberately defeasible. A demonstrated action can explain the constitutive content of a command. However, ablution demonstrations also include differing repetitions: Bukhārī 185 describes two and three washings, while Bukhārī 157 records washing once. Consequently, demonstration must be analyzed feature by feature. This comparison does not itself prove that whole-head coverage is optional; it defeats the blanket rule that every exhibited detail is a validity condition. For extent, the deciding consideration is the reasonable partial-wiping reading of the command combined with insufficient evidence, in this first pass, for a universal quantitative boundary. [F_B185; F_B157]

Strongest opponent. The whole-head position can argue that an ordinary command to wipe an identified body part includes that part, and that the consistent uncovered-head demonstration supplies the intended extent. The forelock-and-turban example may satisfy comprehensive coverage through an authorized substitute. On that account, requiring the whole head is explanation of the original duty, not addition of a new condition. This is a serious argument. Our provisional preference depends on judging the command intelligible without an exhaustive quantitative specification; it is not entailed merely by calling the opponent’s requirement “additional.”

Limits, tests, and reversal conditions. The decision should not introduce a disguised minimum such as “one palm” while claiming to reject fixed minima. Borderline contacts require ordinary linguistic and physical assessment. Turban-only wiping, displaced hair, disability adaptations, and whether washing can substitute for wiping remain separate questions. A well-established legislative explanation requiring comprehensive uncovered-head coverage, or stronger transmission and early-reception evidence showing that partial wiping was consistently treated as insufficient, would reopen this result. An alternative demonstration must be compared in its complete circumstances. The same discipline must preserve stable details that support stricter conclusions elsewhere.

Constitution amendment proposed: separate an obligatory act, a demonstrated sufficient form, and a minimum validity condition; treat explanatory Prophetic action as genuine evidence, and preserve every stable component when evaluating a composite concession.

Formation hypothesis (Edition II only): Careful performance without confusing a legal minimum with the fullest practice; assess whether instruction encourages attention or repetitive scruple.
Editorial hypothesis; not an operative legal ʿilla or a new validity condition.

Working casebook v0.1 · B03

Proposing over a prior proposal

adopted_provisionally Medium confidence B03

Provisional result: Knowing interference with an accepted pending proposal is prohibited; an independently valid ensuing marriage stands.

Articles: C03, C05, C09, C12. Source references: REL01 REL02 REL03 REL04.

Full original case discussion

B03 — Proposing over a prior proposal

Original dispute and operative evidence

Ibn Rushd reports an established prohibition on proposing over another's proposal, followed by disagreement about an ensuing marriage: Dāwūd would dissolve it; al-Shāfiʿī and Abū Ḥanīfa would uphold it; Mālik has both reports and a third position distinguishing consummation. Ibn al-Qāsim allows a righteous second suitor to intervene against an unrighteous first one. The prohibition's ordinary trigger is also disputed: Ibn Rushd presents the majority's restriction to a first proposal that has reached mutual inclination or acceptance. A merely unanswered approach does not necessarily exclude other candidates. [REL01]

Al-Bukhārī 5142 prohibits the second proposal until the first suitor abandons his proposal or permits another. This supplies express ways for the restriction to end. Muslim 1480a reports that Fāṭima bint Qays received proposals from Muʿāwiya and Abū Jahm and, after seeking advice, married Usāma on the Prophet's recommendation. That account supports distinguishing multiple unaccepted approaches from interference with an accepted proposal: it does not tell us that she had already accepted one of the two. Al-Bukhārī 5136 independently requires the woman's consultation or permission for marriage. [REL02–REL04]

Competing premise

The decisive disagreement is whether the prohibition makes the later marriage an unauthorized legal act, or regulates the suitor's conduct during an antecedent stage. This is not settled by calling the first engagement a “right.” A right against interference might carry a duty to desist without carrying ownership of the woman's future marriage, an entitlement to force her original choice, or a power to dissolve a later marriage. Each of those would be a distinct legal proposition. Similarly, the annulment position can be strong only if it connects the prohibited interference to the formation or legally necessary legitimacy of the second marriage.

Candidate verdict and reasons

Provisional verdict: knowingly interfering with an accepted, still-pending proposal is prohibited; a subsequent marriage satisfying its independent formation requirements remains valid. The first suitor's abandonment or permission ends the particular obstacle described in al-Bukhārī. A woman's genuine rejection ends the premise of a still-accepted first proposal; this is our inference from the restricted trigger and her independently established consent, rather than an express additional clause of hadith 5142.

The two normative relations are different: the second suitor owes a duty concerning interference; the spouses form a marriage through its requisite procedure and consent. The cited prohibition establishes the first duty. It does not expressly vest an annulment power in the earlier suitor or state that a marriage later concluded is void. The case record supplies no stronger bridge sufficient to defeat the otherwise established marriage. This preference agrees in outcome with established jurists. It does not turn an unlawful act into an approved one: desisting before marriage, responsibility for deliberate wrongdoing, and the marriage's legal existence are separate conclusions.

Consequences and counterexamples

After a valid marriage, ordinary marital effects follow; the first suitor cannot withhold their recognition as a bargaining device. No compensation for a “lost spouse” is created. If someone incurred a separately actionable loss through proven deception, restitution or damages would require that independent cause and its own measure; the frustrated expectation of marriage does not establish it. Nor does the classification itself authorize a particular disciplinary penalty. This distinction is the proposed rule's main practical contribution: a duty's existence does not identify every claimant or remedy.

The rule cannot validate a marriage lacking a separately established formation condition. If the later ceremony lacks legally required consent, witnesses, capacity, or another condition adopted elsewhere in the charter, the answer must follow that defect and its dependencies. Conversely, ignorance of the first proposal can change culpability without supplying a new reason to dissolve a marriage. The protection also cannot lock the woman into the first proposal: the prohibition addresses competitive interference, and her consent remains independently necessary.

We do not provisionally adopt Ibn al-Qāsim's righteousness exception as a general permission to undercut. Advising someone truthfully about a real concern and making a self-interested competing proposal are different acts. The Fāṭima report supplies an example of relevant advice; it does not establish that any suitor may certify himself morally superior and thereby cancel the prohibition. A properly bounded exception would need evidence about the legally relevant disqualification and the necessity of intervention.

Result and remaining research

Status: existing substantive view, newly systematized rationale; moderate confidence. Further work should collate the acceptance threshold in early school texts, identify precisely which Mālikī report turns on consummation and why, and test Dāwūd's own argument rather than rely solely on Ibn Rushd's attribution. The verdict would change if the act's express invalidation, a demonstrated shared early rule, or a convincing formation-based derivation were established. Merely redescribing the interference as harmful would not answer that missing question.

Formation hypothesis (Edition II only): Respect for another person’s deliberation and consent; no formation claim creates ownership of a prospective spouse.
Editorial hypothesis; not an operative legal ʿilla or a new validity condition.

Working casebook v0.1 · B05

Intention as a condition of wudu

adopted_provisionally Medium-low confidence B05

Provisional result: Verified completion of every independently required physical act and material condition can establish readiness for prayer without an intention of ritual purification or devotional obedience; intentional obedience and devotional credit remain intention-dependent.

Articles: C02, C03, C06, C09, C12. Source references: F_Q5_6 F_BID_INT F_MABSUT_INT F_B1 F_MASAIL118.

Full original case discussion

B05 — Is intention of ritual purification necessary for valid wudu?

Provisional conclusion — existing Ḥanafī/Thawrī result, revised rationale; medium-to-low confidence. If a person actually completes the independently required washing and wiping, using qualifying water and satisfying the other established conditions, the resulting readiness for prayer does not require an intention of ritual purification or devotional obedience. Intention remains necessary for claiming intentional obedience and its devotional credit. For a concrete test, someone who deliberately performs all the required acts solely to cool down may subsequently pray, provided no nullifier intervenes. Mere accidental wetting or an ordinary bath is not evidence that those acts and conditions were fulfilled.

The dispute. Bidāya, Book of Purification, wudu, first question of conditions, attributes the intention requirement to Mālik, al-Shāfiʿī, Aḥmad, Abū Thawr, and Dāwūd; it attributes the contrary result to Abū Ḥanīfa and Sufyān al-Thawrī. Ibn Rushd locates the dispute in ablution’s combination of worship and intelligible purification. That historical explanation is a starting point, not sufficient proof that physical cleaning and ritual readiness are identical. [F_BID_INT]

The strongest textual objection. Bukhārī 1 restricts actions to intentions and each person to what they intend, illustrating this through migration for different ends. Its Arabic does not contain an explicit noun meaning “reward”; the English translation’s insertion of that word cannot settle the legal dispute. The opposing interpretation takes the statement to condition an act’s religious legal standing. Since wudu is a prescribed preparation for prayer, completing its bodily motions without intending purification would then fail to constitute the required act. This objection relies on a general Prophetic statement, not merely analogy with prayer. [F_B1]

Why the proposed result nevertheless remains possible. The charter distinguishes the agent’s obedience from a legally specified result. A command may direct someone to produce a condition for another duty without making the mental purpose of producing it a constitutive part of that condition. Qurʾān 5:6 identifies bodily acts when prayer is intended; it does not expressly state that the readiness produced by those acts vanishes when their motive differs. The migration example distinguishes the significance of an action according to its aim. It does not expressly define the validity of every ancillary legal effect of every action. Reading these sources together, we provisionally find stronger evidence for intention’s effect on attribution and devotional value than for its negation of ablution’s preparatory effect. [F_Q5_6; F_B1]

This severability has a direct classical analogue. Al-Sarakhsī, al-Mabsūṭ 1:72–73, “Bāb al-wuḍūʾ wa-l-ghusl,” distinguishes worship obtained through intention from removal of the impediment to prayer. He also argues that adding intention to the Qurʾānic prescription cannot be established by an isolated report or analogy. We adopt the first distinction, subject to the present evidentiary audit, without adopting that blanket exclusion of solitary reports: the charter accepts critically established Sunna as legally productive. Nor do we adopt “water purifies by itself” as sufficient proof about a juridical state. The relevant issue is what the sources make constitutive of that state. [F_MABSUT_INT]

An additional opponent worth preserving. Al-Kawsaj’s Masāʾil, report 118, transmits Isḥāq’s explanation that undertaking ablution and prayer requires intention, connecting this to the Qurʾānic direction to rise for prayer. Here a jurist treats purpose as inherent in the legislated act, rather than an externally added condition. That interpretation may defeat ours if a broader corpus establishes it more convincingly. The printed apparatus also reports a variant affecting part of this wording; it should not be ignored when making exact verbal claims. [F_MASAIL118]

Consequences and reversal conditions. A spoken formula, uninterrupted introspection, and exact mental timing do not follow automatically even from the opposing view. The cooling example must satisfy B02’s actual wiping requirement and every other independently established physical condition; this dossier does not validate a head that merely became wet. Tayammum, prayer, fasting, and mixed intentions require their own analyses. An authenticated, properly scoped direction to repeat completed ablution specifically because devotional intention was absent would reverse this ruling. So would a sufficiently strong combined demonstration that “wudu” in the relevant legislative texts inherently denotes purification undertaken with that purpose. No repetition is demanded merely because the person cannot later remember a distinct mental formula.

Formation hypothesis (Edition II only): Deliberate awareness may deepen obedience even if readiness is legally severable; legal sufficiency is not proof that intention lacks value.
Editorial hypothesis; not an operative legal ʿilla or a new validity condition.

Working casebook v0.1 · B08

Remoter guardian with nearer guardian present

adopted_provisionally Medium confidence B08

Provisional result: For the stipulated ordinary case, an eligible remoter guardian's otherwise valid marriage stands without a proximity-only veto.

Articles: C06, C09, C12. Source references: REL04 REL05 REL06 REL07.

Full original case discussion

B08 — A remoter guardian contracts while the nearer guardian is present

Original dispute and scope

Ibn Rushd reports three Mālikī outcomes: the marriage stands; it is dissolved; or the nearer guardian can approve or reject it. He expressly excludes two cases from this variation: the father present in relation to his virgin daughter, and an executor present in relation to the ward entrusted to him; he reports a consistent dissolution position for those within Mālik's account. He also reports al-Shāfiʿī's stronger paternal priority. These exceptions must not disappear when extracting a general principle. [REL05]

This dossier therefore decides a narrower case: a consenting adult woman; a legally eligible remoter guardian; an otherwise properly formed marriage; no present father or specially empowered executor; no proved coercion, disqualification, or independent restriction on the match. Eligibility is a stipulated fact here and must be independently justified when applying the decision; it cannot be defined circularly as “whoever performed this marriage.” The full general-guardianship controversy and ranking of relatives remain separate research questions; B15 is unassessed.

Text and competing premise

Abū Dāwūd 2085 transmits the requirement of a guardian. Abū Dāwūd 2083 transmits ʿĀʾisha's report declaring a marriage without the guardians' permission void, while expressly awarding dower after consummation and assigning authority to the ruler where guardianship fails through dispute. The displayed catalogue grades both reports ṣaḥīḥ according to al-Albānī; that is a named modern grading, not a declaration that earlier jurists unanimously accepted every application. Neither wording expressly sets out the complete order of family proximity. [REL06–REL07]

Ibn Rushd isolates three premises. If priority has not been established as a revealed requirement, the remoter guardian's act stands. If priority is a private right of the nearer guardian, ratification becomes intelligible. If it is a nonwaivable divine requirement, the act is unformed. This is a diagnostic map of the dispute, not proof that any one of these classifications applies. In particular, the ratification result still needs evidence that the nearer person holds a power to cure this particular defect. [REL05]

Candidate verdict and reasons

Provisional verdict for the stated ordinary case: the marriage stands, and proximity alone does not give the nearer relative a veto or a power of retrospective ratification. This dossier provisionally accepts a guardian requirement for its stipulated class on REL06–REL07; the present case supplies such a guardian. This local premise does not settle the full B15 controversy. Moving from “a guardian is necessary” to “only this nearest guardian can form the marriage” adds an exclusion. The inspected evidence has not established that exclusion for this nonexceptional case. The woman’s actual consent is independently required; al-Bukhārī 5136 prevents a guardian's participation from substituting for it. [REL04, REL06–REL07]

This is an evidential preference, not the claim that family order can never be legally significant. A demonstrated assignment of exclusive authority would change the ordinary formation requirement itself. Equally, a demonstrated private priority could make a particular defect curable if its holder possesses a ratification power. We would then follow that evidence. The proposed procedure demands proof of the complete relation—holder, protected act, power, and legal effect—rather than letting the label “private right” supply missing incidents automatically.

Consequences and dependency tests

Because the stipulated ordinary case is valid, ordinary marital consequences follow without awaiting the nearer relative's signature. The mere hurt of being bypassed establishes neither damages nor annulment. Family consultation can be valuable without supplying a specific legal remedy. If the remoter person is actually disqualified, or if an independently established exclusive authority was bypassed, this verdict does not apply: the formation analysis must be redone instead of saving the outcome through sympathetic consent alone.

The ʿĀʾisha report provides a decisive check against an overly simple rule that “invalid means no effects.” It expressly combines invalidity with dower after consummation. The dower has its stated independent basis in the consummation; it need not imply that the marriage was valid all along. Thus our method can uphold one consequence and deny another when the evidence makes that division. By the same reasoning, dower alone would not prove inheritance, unrestricted continued cohabitation, or a power of private ratification. [REL07]

An eligible but obstructive nearer guardian presents another test. One cannot assume that any relative may simply replace him: the report's reference to the ruler supplies a reason to investigate an adjudicative route. Nor can a guardian exploit the charter's rejection of an unproved veto to erase the woman's consent. A guardian's authority and the woman's permission are distinct necessary questions, and neither should be silently inferred from the other.

Result and remaining research

Status: existing permissive outcome with a proposed rule about proving the incidents of authority; moderate confidence within the express scope. Remaining work is concentrated: compare early Mālikī texts on the three reports; investigate actual proofs for the order of guardians; examine al-Shāfiʿī's paternal argument and the executor exception; and audit the guardian reports' transmission and rival interpretation. The present decision does not suspend the ordinary case, but it does not pretend to have settled the excluded cases. A source establishing exclusive proximity in this very class would overturn it.

Formation hypothesis (Edition II only): Accountable use of entrusted authority; family cooperation does not itself create a veto.
Editorial hypothesis; not an operative legal ʿilla or a new validity condition.

Working casebook v0.1 · B09

Traveller fasting

adopted_provisionally Medium-high confidence B09

Provisional result: A qualifying traveller's otherwise valid completed Ramadan fast discharges that day, with no extra makeup due solely to travel; lawful nonfasting entails makeup.

Articles: C02, C03, C04, C05, C09. Source references: W_reader W_bidaya_fasting W_daruri_58 W_q_2_184 W_bukhari_1943 W_muslim_1121e W_bukhari_1947 W_bukhari_1946 W_muslim_1114a W_muslim_1114b.

Full original case discussion

B09 — A traveller’s Ramadan fast: concession, alternative time, and validity

1. Historical dispute and precise question

Ibn Rushd reports a majority view accepting a traveller’s completed fast and an opposing view attributed to the Ẓāhirīs requiring other days. The interpretive issue is whether Q 2:184’s makeup clause includes the understood condition that the traveller actually did not fast. Reports support the competing readings. See Bidāya, Book of Fasting, second division, discussion of travellers, first and second questions, opening “أما المسألة الأولى … هل يجزيه صومه”.

Darūrī §58 additionally distinguishes al-Karkhī’s reported position: the duty attaches to later days, yet present fasting can discharge it. Thus “the ordinary performance time is displaced” and “present performance is invalid” are separate claims. The underlying Arabic is inspected at Darūrī, p. 59, §58. B09’s association with other semantic sections remains an editorial parallel.

2. Evidence that controls the reading

Q 2:184 provides the other-days clause. Bukhārī 1943, Book 30, hadith 50, reports an express choice between fasting and not fasting during travel. Muslim 1121e, Book 13, hadith 135, names the concession and approves taking it, while permitting the capable traveller to fast. Bukhārī 1947, Book 30, hadith 54, records travelling Companions’ mutual acceptance of both practices.

The adverse evidence has actual context. In Bukhārī 1946, Book 30, hadith 53, the saying denying the righteousness of travel fasting concerns a man being shaded. In Muslim 1114a–b, Book 13, hadiths 116–117, the condemnation follows the Prophet’s public breaking of the fast; the accompanying version expressly reports the people’s difficulty and their watching his conduct. These contexts are transmitted evidence, not an invented explanation that every adverse report concerned extreme danger.

3. Methodological fork

A surface reading can attach makeup directly to traveller status. The second route understands makeup as the substitute for a fast not performed, using the permission reports to establish the omitted condition. The al-Karkhī alternative shows a third possible architecture: substituted obligation plus authorized early discharge. Our choice must explain why its reconstruction is needed and how little it adds.

Nor should the statement about righteousness be silently converted into a declaration of ritual invalidity. An assessment of the wisdom or virtue of fasting, an instruction to break it in particular circumstances, and the legal sufficiency of a completed fast are different propositions.

4. Candidate principle and provisional conclusion

Candidate principle: when reliable explanatory speech and acknowledged practice establish an authorized alternative, they can determine the scope of an apparently unconditional remedial clause. Supply only the condition that the evidence supports. Before treating another report as abolishing that authorization, establish that it governs the same legally relevant circumstances and supplies an adequate basis for supersession.

Working ruling: for an otherwise eligible person on a legally qualifying journey, a Ramadan fast completed with its required intention and other conditions is valid and discharges that day; travel alone creates no additional makeup obligation. The traveller may instead use the concession and make up the missed day. The completed fast’s validity does not depend on establishing that it was the more virtuous choice.

The reason for choosing validity is the positive authorization of fasting. It is not merely an absence of blame. The proposed reading consequently explains why makeup arises when the alternative of not fasting is used, without turning permission into an obligation to fast.

5. Scope, failure tests, and classification

A person cannot manufacture this permission simply by calling a short outing “travel”; the qualifying journey requires its own determination. Equally, an easier mode of transport does not automatically erase a concession attached to a legally established travel category. Hardship can affect how a concession should be used without being the only condition for its existence.

The case does not decide the validity of a fast undertaken in defiance of an independently established duty to avoid serious harm or obey a particular binding instruction. Darūrī §58 itself distinguishes dangerous illness as a further question. That separation must not become permission to ignore demonstrated danger.

Classification: the principal result is an existing juristic position. The candidate contribution is a mandatory ordering of scope, contextual reconciliation, and the distinction between merit and validity. The ellipsis and concession analysis are not new discoveries.

6. Residual evidence tasks

Collate the Ramadan-specific reports against reports of frequent or voluntary fasting; complete the chronology analysis advanced for abrogation; and separately specify travel thresholds, departure-day rules, and severe-harm cases. Compare al-Karkhī’s account in a direct Ḥanafī source before treating Ibn Rushd’s compressed report as its exhaustive statement.

Formation hypothesis (Edition II only): Receptivity to concessions and restraint from judging another traveller; the hypothesis is distinct from the fast’s validity.
Editorial hypothesis; not an operative legal ʿilla or a new validity condition.

Working casebook v0.1 · B13_B23

Fixed minimum dower

adopted_provisionally High, narrow confidence B13, B23

Provisional result: No universal numerical floor for an otherwise lawful specified dower; retain the woman's entitlement and independently required consent and material conditions.

Articles: C02, C06, C09, C10. Source references: F_BID_DOWER F_B5132 F_Q4_4 F_Q2_236 F_AWSAT_DOWER F_T1113.

Full original case discussion

B13/B23 — Is there a fixed minimum dower?

Provisional conclusion — existing ruling with a strengthened general rationale; high confidence on the narrow question. No universal numerical minimum is imposed on an otherwise lawful, specified dower. An agreed item of recognized value is not disqualified merely because it falls below a quarter dinar or ten dirhams. This rejects those fixed floors; it does not erase the woman’s entitlement, validate coercion, make every purported object lawful, or decide all questions about services as dower. Naming an amount, the existence of an entitlement, its enforceability, and any remedy for a defect are separate propositions.

The map in the primary source. Bidāya, Book of Marriage, chapter on the conditions of the contract, “al-faṣl al-thālith fī al-ṣadāq,” first subdivision, second question “fī qadrihi,” records a quarter dinar/three dirhams for Mālik and ten dirhams for Abū Ḥanīfa, against no fixed minimum for al-Shāfiʿī, Aḥmad, Isḥāq, Abū Thawr, and others. The title and question are better locators than volume numbering, which varies between editions; the inspected display has pp. 407–408. [F_BID_DOWER]

The decisive case. In the Sahl b. Saʿd report, the Prophet asks the prospective husband about something he can provide, including an iron ring, and eventually concludes the marriage with reference to his knowledge of the Qurʾān. The details concerning the final benefit admit further legal discussion, but the search for even an iron ring directly addresses the amount at a moment when a minimum would matter. A hidden universal floor requires evidence; its omission is evidentially significant here because this is an actual negotiation under Prophetic direction, not a narrator’s incidental silence about an unrelated legal matter. [F_B5132]

The stricter analogy test. The strongest minimum argument first classifies the dower as a religiously required institution rather than an entirely optional exchange. It then argues for a prescribed threshold and uses the theft threshold to identify the amount. Ibn Rushd distinguishes those stages. He challenges the move from “religious duty” to “numerically fixed duty,” and criticizes the shared description of access to a body part for conflating very different legal relations. A punishment and a marriage do not acquire the same operative ground merely because similar vocabulary describes them. The defect is not that analogy can never identify a quantity; it is that the proposed connecting reason has not been shown to explain both rulings. [F_BID_DOWER]

Under the charter, the opponent must show why the feature that fixes the theft threshold also fixes the dower. Neither general solemnity nor the importance of marriage supplies that warrant. Once the numerical inference fails, the ordinary command to give the woman her dower survives intact. Qurʾān 4:4 locates the entitlement with women and recognizes voluntary remission. Qurʾān 2:236 contemplates divorce before an amount has been settled, so failure to name an amount cannot simply be equated with the absence of a marriage. These distinctions support protecting the entitlement while rejecting an unsupported floor. [F_Q4_4; F_Q2_236]

Opponent, transmission control, and historical checks. A defender may regard the iron-ring episode as an exceptional concession, or introduce a report expressly requiring ten dirhams. Both would matter if securely established and correctly scoped; neither may be asserted to rescue the preferred analogy. Ibn Rushd judges the ten-dirham report weak. The sandals report, Tirmidhī 1113, displays al-Tirmidhī’s favorable assessment and a later weak grading; we therefore do not make it the necessary premise. Ibn al-Mundhir independently gives the no-fixed-minimum position and explicitly demands evidence before imposing limits on divine obligations. [F_BID_DOWER; F_T1113; F_AWSAT_DOWER]

Practical boundary and outstanding work. A low dower and no dower are not identical, and a low agreed dower cannot justify withholding what was actually promised. An unlawful or indeterminate item calls for a separate remedy analysis; the charter does not infer marriage invalidity automatically. Assessing free consent, rights of a person with limited legal capacity, valuation of a particular item, customary dower where no amount was named, and the precise status of promised teaching remain distinct questions. A sound legislative minimum would change this result. A preference for larger dowers, or evidence that they were usual, would not by itself establish a validity threshold. This dossier supplies a reusable rule against importing quantities through a merely verbal resemblance.

Formation hypothesis (Edition II only): Honouring a financial promise and the woman’s consent; no particular amount is inferred from this educational aim.
Editorial hypothesis; not an operative legal ʿilla or a new validity condition.

B16 — Hajj timing: the first genuinely feasible season

1. Historical dispute and exact issue

The dispute concerns immediate performance or permitted postponement. Ibn Rushd contrasts analogies with the beginning and end of prayer time, the gap between pilgrimage seasons, and the danger of losing a later opportunity. He expressly rejects reducing this dispute to the bare imperative. See Bidāya, Book of Hajj, first inquiry, “وأما متى تجب”. Darūrī §201 likewise treats timing as dependent on indications beyond the form: p. 122.

The present question is when a person who already owes a first hajj and actually can undertake it must perform. It is not whether hajj occurs annually as a new personal obligation.

2. Primary evidence and its limits

Q 3:97 establishes the pilgrimage duty with the ability qualification. Muslim 1337, Book 15, hadith 461, distinguishes the command to perform hajj from an annually repeated requirement. Its continuation also links commanded performance to ability. Neither text alone identifies the first available year as an inflexible deadline.

A more specific timing report matters: Abū Dāwūd 1732, Book 11, hadith 12, directs someone intending hajj to hasten; the displayed grading attributes “ḥasan” to al-Albānī. Ibn Mājah 2883, Book 25, hadith 2, gives a related version mentioning possible illness, loss of transport, and intervening need; its displayed Darussalam grade is also “ḥasan”. These are attributed assessments, not a claim that their chains and every addition are undisputed.

The counterargument from the Prophet’s later performance requires attention. Bukhārī 1622, Book 25, hadith 107, establishes Abū Bakr’s leadership of the preceding pilgrimage and the public announcements then made. It does not itself establish the date when a binding first-hajj duty attached to the Prophet or prove why he did not personally lead that earlier pilgrimage. Supplying an unrecorded excuse would be methodologically improper.

3. Methodological fork

The competing prayer analogies select different features of time. The proposed method therefore first records whether the duty is one-off, whether performance windows are continuous, and what direct timing evidence exists. An isolated analogy does not get to choose the answer before that description is completed.

In this case, the specific instruction to hasten supplies the principal proposed bridge to promptness. The annual interruption and risk of losing ability explain its practical significance. Ordinary uncertainty about death is not being made a freestanding source of an immediate obligation.

4. Candidate principle and provisional conclusion

Candidate principle: for a duty confined to separated execution windows, determine urgency from authenticated timing directions and the structure of legally feasible opportunities. A general imperative establishes neither a license for indefinite postponement nor, by itself, the first-window deadline.

Working ruling: a person owing a first hajj should, as a legal duty under this draft, perform it in the first season in which the relevant ability and access conditions are actually satisfied. This is a provisional selection of prompt performance, supported by the presently accepted hastening reports read as legislative guidance about fulfilling the already established duty.

The conditional wording concerning someone who intends hajj is a material interpretive issue. Our present reconstruction treats it as addressing the person undertaking an existing duty, rather than enabling avoidance simply through refusing to intend compliance. That reconstruction remains preponderant, not certain. It does not turn an intention to undertake an additional voluntary pilgrimage into a vow.

5. Scope and counterexamples

“Feasible” is substantive. A theoretical financial balance does not, without further analysis, establish an available route, required access, adequate health, or the ability to meet competing enforceable responsibilities. Conversely, assuming that later arrangements will probably be easier does not automatically defeat a presently established duty.

As a proposed application, where timely administrative preparation is a necessary and available means of the first feasible pilgrimage, the preparation belongs in the ability assessment and practical performance plan. The rule does not change the ritual calendar or invent a universal financial or risk threshold.

Voluntary repeat hajj is the decisive counterexample to an unlimited reading of “whoever intends”. If the timing report necessarily makes every voluntary intention immediately binding, the chosen scope needs revision. Prayer within a continuously open window is another test: the hajj conclusion cannot simply be exported to forbid its otherwise permitted postponement.

Classification: first-feasible-season performance follows an existing juristic result. The candidate architecture makes the choice of temporal features and the independent timing warrant explicit; it is not a newly discovered obligation.

6. Residual evidence tasks

Audit the hastening reports’ chains and the causal addition separately; investigate the force of their conditional wording; establish the chronology and circumstances relevant to the Prophetic-delay argument; and compare the immediate-performance schools’ actual definitions of ability. If the specific timing evidence fails or proves advisory, this dossier must reopen promptness rather than preserve it by precaution alone.

Editorial hypothesis; not an operative legal ʿilla or a new validity condition.

B17 — Initial ʿumra and mandatory completion: the stage rule survives an obligatory result

1. Historical dispute and editorial qualification

Ibn Rushd reports both obligation and nonobligation of initial ʿumra. One dispute concerns whether Q 2:196’s completion command establishes initiation; another concerns the reports. See Bidāya, Book of Hajj, first inquiry, “وقد بقي … القول في حكم النسك الذي هو العمرة”. The online attribution to Abū Thawr is internally inconsistent and is not used here.

B17’s link to Darūrī §204 is editorial and illustrative: that section concerns makeup of timed worship. The adjacent §205 separately distinguishes commanded completion from sufficient discharge, using defective pilgrimage as its example: Darūrī, p. 123. Neither section should be cited as Ibn Rushd directly deriving ʿumra’s initial status through our proposed stage rule.

2. Evidence beyond the completion verse

Q 2:196 commands completion and includes provisions for obstruction. Its stage and scope must be interpreted; translating its opening as “complete” does not by itself settle whether it also directs initiation.

Independent reports prevent an easy conclusion of nonobligation. Ibn Mājah 2901, Book 25, hadith 20, reports ʿĀʾisha asking whether jihad is upon women and an answer describing hajj and ʿumra as their noncombatant jihad; its displayed Darussalam grade is “ṣaḥīḥ”. Abū Dāwūd 1810, Book 11, hadith 90, records the instruction to perform hajj and ʿumra for an elderly father; the displayed al-Albānī grade is “ṣaḥīḥ”.

There is real adverse evidence. Tirmidhī 931, Book 9, hadith 124, directly denies initial obligation. Tirmidhī’s preserved Arabic assessment calls it “ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ”, while the modern Darussalam panel marks it weak. Bidāya reports Ibn ʿAbd al-Barr’s objection to relying on al-Ḥajjāj b. Arṭāʾ’s isolated transmission. The grading conflict cannot be concealed by quoting only the preferred panel.

The wording also needs comparison: Bukhārī 1520, Book 25, hadith 8, reports ʿĀʾisha’s question through Ḥabīb b. Abī ʿAmra and ʿĀʾisha bt. Ṭalḥa, but gives an answer about excellent hajj without the same ʿumra wording. This is a prompt for variant analysis, not proof that the addition is either automatically sound or automatically erroneous.

3. The methodological fork

The completion verse can be read as governing an undertaking already begun or as encompassing full performance. Either reading must answer the independent reports. Conversely, accepting an initial obligation does not entitle the jurist to say that completion language necessarily proved it.

The instruction concerning the elderly father may clarify authorized proxy performance rather than independently announce a universal initial duty. Likewise, the female-jihad report requires a judgment about obligation versus comparative virtue. Our decision therefore relies on an explicit cumulative reading, not on the existence of an imperative anywhere in the evidence.

4. Candidate principle and provisional conclusion

Candidate principle: identify whether a command governs initiation, continuation, completion, or repair. Do not transfer a duty to another stage without a stated semantic, textual, or analogical bridge. This is a presumption against unexplained transitions, not a presumption that worship is optional.

Working ruling: on the present evidence, initial ʿumra is provisionally obligatory once for an otherwise qualified, capable adult, with further performances not required merely by this general obligation. The principal support is the presently accepted obligation reading of the ʿĀʾisha report, corroborated by the father’s report; the latter also supports considering male obligation rather than silently generalizing a report addressed to women. The nonobligation report’s transmission and interpretive standing remain a significant unresolved challenge.

Once a validly constituted ʿumra has begun, completion is required, subject to the law’s established release and obstruction provisions. These conclusions have distinct warrants. The completion rule alone is not our proof of the first duty; the initial duty alone is not a rule that every subsequent ʿumra becomes a fresh lifetime obligation.

5. Counterexamples and classification

Muslim 1154a, Book 13, hadith 220, records the Prophet eating after having begun a voluntary fast. Whatever further details different schools derive, this is concrete adverse evidence against transporting pilgrimage’s completion rule wholesale into every voluntary observance. The proposed system must investigate each activity’s specific transition.

Darūrī §205 supplies the opposite warning: an act can require completion yet fail to discharge the underlying duty because of an independently established defect. “Must finish” therefore entails neither “had to begin” nor “completion certainly suffices”.

Classification: the chosen initial obligation is an existing juristic position. Mandatory stage analysis is the proposed methodological feature. This case deliberately rejects the tempting but unsupported inference that a new completion principle must produce a more permissive final verdict.

6. Residual evidence tasks

Prioritize classical criticism of the ʿumra addition in the ʿĀʾisha routes and of Tirmidhī 931’s chain; determine the legal force of the proxy instruction; collate the contradictory Bidāya attribution; and distinguish obstruction, invalidation, vows, and additional voluntary performances. A successful defense of the contrary report or a weaker legal reading of the affirmative evidence could change the initial-status ruling without invalidating the stage principle.

Formation hypothesis (Edition II only): Perseverance in an undertaken observance, with initiation and completion duties separately justified.
Editorial hypothesis; not an operative legal ʿilla or a new validity condition.

B18 — Divorce pronounced during menstruation

Original dispute and evidence

Ibn Rushd separates occurrence of the divorce, whether return is obligatory, the duration of compulsion, and the timetable for a later lawful divorce. He attributes counting to the majority, nonoccurrence to a dissenting group, compulsory return to Mālik, and recommendation to several other leading jurists. He also records different time limits and the dispute between waiting only until the next purity and waiting through an additional menstrual cycle. His analysis at one point presses a supposed contradiction between counting the divorce and compelling return. These are distinct questions, not a single majority package. [REL08]

Al-Bukhārī 5251 reports the Prophet's instruction that Ibn ʿUmar take his wife back, retain her until purity, then menstruation, then purity, and only then divorce before intercourse if he chooses. Reports 5252–5253 add positive evidence of counting: Ibn ʿUmar answers the question whether the pronouncement counted and reports that one divorce was charged against him. These are retrospective reports by the participant; one should not replace them with an invented verbatim prophetic sentence declaring “it counts.” [REL09–REL11]

Abū Dāwūd 2185 preserves Abū al-Zubayr's wording that the Prophet restored her and did not regard it as anything. Abū Dāwūd then notes that other transmitted accounts differ from this version. The webpage nevertheless gives al-Albānī's overall ṣaḥīḥ grading. The problem is comparative evaluation of a particular wording, not a licence to erase the report as simply fabricated or unanimously weak. [REL12]

Competing premise and actual verdict

Provisional verdict: one such divorce counts, and the husband has a binding corrective duty to take her back while lawful revocation remains available. The case here is an otherwise effective first or second, revocable divorce in a consummated marriage. Counting is preferred because of the affirmative counted reports, their connection to the narrated incident, and their convergence with the instruction to return. It is not inferred merely from the prohibition, nor exclusively from treating the verb of return as an immutable technical term.

The strongest contrary reading deserves its full argument. Al-Shawkānī defends the noncounting addition: Abū al-Zubayr expressly heard the report; “return” can have a broader linguistic meaning; and a clear denial should outweigh an implication. He also challenges whether Ibn ʿUmar's legal understanding alone is binding. [REL13] We accept the semantic objection against using “return” alone, but do not find that it eliminates the independent, affirmative counted reports. On practical preponderance, those reports and the collector's variant comparison justify counting. A full reconstruction of the routes could reverse that preference; it is not immunized by a consensus claim.

Corrective duty and its separate warrant

The imperative to take her back supplies the duty. Counting supplies a statement about what the earlier act accomplished. There is no inherent contradiction in an effective wrongful act generating a duty to correct its consequences through an available legal procedure. Our proposal therefore declines Ibn Rushd's suggestion here that validity must make return merely recommended. Conversely, the binding character of the corrective command does not prove that the original act never occurred. Revocation restores the continuing marriage in the prescribed way; it does not erase the used divorce count.

For the remedial sequence in this incident, the fuller timetable in al-Bukhārī 5251 is provisionally preferred: the added intervening cycle is positive detail, and an abbreviated account need not negate it. That preference does not automatically create the same waiting sequence for every different divorce case. Qurʾān 65:1 supplies the general regulation of divorce in relation to the waiting period; the incident supplies a concrete corrective sequence. [REL09, REL14]

Counterexamples and dependencies

A binding duty is not by itself a judicial instrument. The sources considered here justify requiring lawful return when it is available; they do not independently establish that a judge's order retrospectively reverses a completed irrevocable divorce, authorizes physical coercion, or recreates a marriage after the waiting period expires. Those powers require their own warrants. If revocation is no longer legally available, the duty cannot be executed by pretending otherwise. Nor does this dossier decide simultaneous triple divorce or a third divorce by importing the easier revocable example.

Before consummation the waiting-period premise changes, so this precise corrective sequence cannot simply be extended. The report also cannot be used to treat menstruation as a general incapacity of the wife or a basis to disregard her interests. Its regulated act is the husband's divorce. Where return itself would be deployed to harm, independently established limits on harmful retention must be included; the present directive is not permission for indefinite control. Maintenance, residence, succession during revocable waiting, and subsequent remarriage must follow the adopted status rules rather than whichever isolated consequence is attractive in a particular dispute.

Result and remaining research

Status: existing counted-divorce and obligatory-return outcome, with a new explicit separation of adjudicated fact, corrective duty, and enforcement power; moderate confidence. Priority research is an isnād-and-wording table for Nāfiʿ, Sālim, Anas ibn Sīrīn, Yūnus ibn Jubayr, and Abū al-Zubayr; the precise attribution of affirmative counting; and comparison of early commentary on the longer timetable. A superior reconstruction establishing prophetic noncounting would change the count and all dependent consequences. Mere general disapproval of irregular divorce would not suffice.

Editorial hypothesis; not an operative legal ʿilla or a new validity condition.

B21 — Irrevocable divorce in a fatal illness and inheritance

Original dispute and positive evidence

Ibn Rushd reports substantial disagreement when a man irrevocably divorces in illness and dies: no inheritance in al-Shāfiʿī's position; inheritance during the waiting period for Abū Ḥanīfa and al-Thawrī; until remarriage for Aḥmad and Ibn Abī Laylā; and broader survival of the claim for Mālik and al-Layth. Consent or the wife's request is another disputed condition. He rejects an alleged Companion consensus because Ibn al-Zubayr's dissent is reported. Recovery is a distinct case and must not be silently included in the fatal-illness rule. [REL15]

The pro-inheritance case has affirmative transmitted evidence. Mālik transmits through Ibn Shihāb from Ṭalḥa ibn ʿAbd Allāh ibn ʿAwf and Abū Salama that ʿUthmān awarded inheritance to ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn ʿAwf's irrevocably divorced wife after her waiting period. The same chapter contains another ʿUthmān award and a separate report through Rabīʿa, introduced as information that reached him, that the wife had requested divorce. That latter transmission must not acquire the first report's evidential standing merely because both appear together. [REL16]

Al-Shāfiʿī preserves Ibn Abī Mulayka's report of asking Ibn al-Zubayr, who acknowledges ʿUthmān's award and disagrees with it. The transmitted accounts also differ about whether the award fell within the waiting period. Al-Rabīʿ reports al-Shāfiʿī's subsequent choice of no inheritance. Ibn Ḥazm additionally preserves a report of ʿUmar threatening to restore wives' inheritance when divorces accompanied disposal of property near anticipated death. This is serious early anti-evasion evidence, although threat, adjudication, and general rule must be distinguished. [REL17–REL18]

The competing premise

The issue is whether terminal illness establishes a protected succession expectancy that this unilateral divorce cannot defeat. Qurʾān 4:12 positively assigns inheritance to spouses. The ordinary-divorce side reads the effective severance of marriage as ending that cause; the pro-inheritance side treats the early awards as evidence for a special protection against evasion. Neither proposition is established just by calling the result coherent. In particular, an inheritance exception need not maintain all marital relations or require reciprocal succession if its independent legal cause is protection of the wife's expectancy. [REL19]

Candidate verdict and its evidential cost

Version 0.1 provisionally follows no automatic spousal inheritance after an otherwise effective irrevocable divorce, including the stated fatal-illness case. Confidence is lower than in B03 or B18. This is an existing position, chosen by a disputable evidential preference: ordinary spousal succession has an express textual cause; effective irrevocable severance ends that cause; and the transmitted exception rests on contested Companion adjudication rather than a presently established prophetic rule or demonstrated common Companion proposition. Ibn al-Zubayr's informed dissent prevents treating the award as undisputed exposition of that cause.

This verdict does not say that a Companion judgment can never establish an exception, or that a predeath expectancy is legally impossible. It assigns less normative weight to this disputed adjudication than to the established cause plus the dissenting early interpretation. The remaining gap is a sufficiently persuasive basis for preferring the adjudication as a general legal qualification of succession, with its operative facts established. That is a source-authority choice open to reversal, not a neutral deduction compelled by the proposed consequences principle. If the charter generally adopts well-transmitted Companion judgments despite informed dissent when supported by anti-evasion analogies, this verdict would require reconsideration for consistency.

Strongest bounded alternative and counterexamples

A substantial alternative protects the wife where the husband unilaterally effects irrevocable divorce during an objectively dangerous illness from which he dies, she did not freely procure that severance, and the threatened succession expectancy existed at divorce. Protection ends on recovery or her remarriage. Death from the original illness supplies the causal boundary; protection is not a perpetual claim. Ibn Qudāma develops a related until-remarriage position and a waiting-period-only alternative. His claimed prior consensus cannot simply override the reported dissent. [REL20]

This alternative's foundation is the early adjudication, reinforced by the independently recognized concern with evading succession, not general sympathy. Its particular consent and endpoint conditions still need support. One cannot simultaneously invoke an after-waiting-period award and quietly say the original event proves an idda-only rule. Nor does a wife's performance of an unavoidable religious duty count automatically as freely choosing a divorce attached to it: al-Shāfiʿī's discussion of the pro-inheritance view itself distinguishes such conditions. [REL21]

Under the adopted verdict, unpaid dower and other independently established debts survive; ordinary revocable divorce during its waiting period remains a different status. We do not create a new damages claim equal to the excluded inheritance. Under the alternative, the succession share would rest on special protection rather than fictional cohabitation, and other heirs' shares must be recalculated accordingly. Neither recovery nor remarriage can be ignored merely because the earlier motive was wrongful.

Result and remaining research

Status: existing no-inheritance view, with a provisional and contested source-authority rationale. The immediate research priorities are the exact circumstances and routes of ʿUthmān's decisions, the dating and scope of Ibn al-Zubayr's dissent, the ʿUmar report, and comparisons with legally established anti-evasion cases. Corroboration establishing a general early terminal-illness protection, or a stronger account of Companion adjudication within the charter, could overturn this verdict. Abstract discomfort with asymmetric effects would not defeat that evidence.

B22 — Which operative grounds extend the ribā rules beyond six commodities?

Provisional conclusion — familiar core rulings and extensions, with a new selection protocol; medium confidence beyond the textual core. Preserve the expressly regulated exchanges of gold, silver, wheat, barley, dates, and salt. Admit a bounded extension to paradigmatic measured, storable provision foods, including rice and raisins: unequal same-kind exchanges fall within the prohibition under the proposed analogy. Do not stop at six merely because jurists dispute the cause. Equally, do not classify every weighable nonfood as ribā-bearing merely because scales can measure it. This is a historical analysis of commodity-exchange reasoning; it makes no determination about a modern financial product.

Textual core and differentiated rules. Muslim 1587c states equivalence and immediate exchange for each of the six against its own kind, and allows differential quantities where the relevant kinds differ, with its stated delivery condition. Determining which cross-kind transactions share that condition requires the rest of the corpus; the sentence must not be detached into a universal ban on deferred exchanges between every money and food item. Qurʾān 2:279 independently preserves the principal while prohibiting wrongdoing in the ribā context. Questions of debt and of a barter quantity rule must not be collapsed. [F_M1587C; F_Q2_279]

Competing accounts. Bidāya, Book of Sales, second part, second chapter, first section on the things subject to excess and delay, records the textual restriction associated with literalists and several extending analyses: food, storability and provision, weight or measure, and the monetary role of the precious metals. It also reports al-Bāqillānī’s especially narrow extension from dates to raisins. Ibn Rushd treats many proposed extensions as resemblance reasoning and nevertheless weighs their merits. His taxonomy cannot substitute for proving the operative ground in a particular new case. [F_BID_RIBA]

Why rice and raisins cross the proposed boundary. This is not a vote among schools. The case combines several evidentially supported connections: a textual food category, staple or stored provision, and quantity-based exchange of goods whose same-kind equivalence the law regulates. Muslim 1592 supplies the food wording; its transmitter also mentions barley as the food then in use. That contextual comment must be recorded without pretending it automatically cancels every broader reading of “food.” Rice belongs to the paradigmatic cereal application; raisins also have the particularly close preserved-fruit analogy that Ibn Rushd reports. The convergence makes these applications better supported than an inference to iron based only on weight. [F_M1592; F_BID_RIBA]

The required counterexample. Bukhārī 2302–2303 rejects exchanging larger quantities of lower-quality dates for smaller quantities of better dates and directs separate sale and purchase. It also contains a statement concerning weighing. This evidence defeats a proposal that equal market value or the absence of perceived exploitation automatically cancels the prescribed equality rule. Our purpose-based component must explain and extend the rule without overriding this explicit case. The weighing clause supports investigating measurement; it does not, without interpretation, name every conceivable weighable object as the legal domain. [F_B2302]

Strongest opponents. A Shāfiʿī argument can treat the food wording as broad enough to cover fresh foods without the added storability restriction. A Ḥanafī argument can give measurement a more general legal role, stressing the repeated quantitative commands. A literalist can deny that the proposed combination identifies an authorized extension at all. Our answer to the last is that practical analogy need not attain certainty: al-Shāfiʿī’s Risāla, discussion of qiyās, explicitly distinguishes closer and more contestable analogies while allowing warranted practical judgment. Our answer to the broader positions is provisional differentiation of evidential strength, not proof that every disputed extension is false. [F_BID_RIBA; F_RISALA_QIYAS]

Unresolved extensions and safeguards. Fresh fruit, medicinal ingestibles, metals serving as ordinary monetary media, and changes in how goods are measured require separate dossiers. The absence of an established commodity analogy never licenses a transaction independently prohibited as debt ribā, fraud, coercion, or another wrong. Monetary function is a serious candidate ground; defining that function and reconciling it with texts about the metals themselves remains outstanding. We should test a broader food rule and a broader measurement rule against the complete hadith families and early practice before finalizing the boundary. Each extension must identify its ground, the rival grounds, the relevant facts, and a counterexample capable of defeating it; disagreement alone defeats neither analogy nor its opponent.

Formation hypothesis (Edition II only): Fair dealing and attention to the substance of exchanges; perceived fair market value does not displace an explicit quantity rule.

B25 — Mistaken qibla: correct inquiry, objective error, and subsequent remedies

1. Historical dispute and the three separate findings

Ibn Rushd reports al-Shāfiʿī’s requirement to repeat when directional error becomes known and the contrasting treatment of adequate inquiry associated with Mālik and Abū Ḥanīfa; Mālik recommends repetition within time. Deliberate disregard and failure to inquire are excluded from the latter protection. The dispute includes report criticism and analogy with praying before time: Bidāya, Prayer, conditions, qibla chapter, second issue, opening “هل فرض المجتهد في القبلة الإصابة أو الاجتهاد”.

The proposed analysis records three findings independently: objective directional accuracy; adequacy of inquiry and personal fault; and validity plus any remedy. Darūrī §243, p. 141 explicitly separates excused error from correctness. Its discussion of juristic truth is not itself a ruling that every mistaken prayer is valid.

2. What the inspected sources do and do not establish

Q 2:144 supplies the orientation command. Q 64:16 and Bukhārī 7288, Book 96, hadith 19, connect obedience to ability. Neither general capacity text alone settles retrospective validity.

The direct dark-night report appears in Tirmidhī 345, Book 2, hadith 197. Tirmidhī’s own commentary criticizes its chain, identifying weakness associated with Ashʿath al-Sammān; the displayed Darussalam grade is weak. It cannot be presented as a securely authenticated direct declaration settling this case. Tirmidhī nevertheless reports substantial juristic reception of the no-repeat result. Reception does not turn this particular chain into a sound one or establish consensus.

Bukhārī 403, Book 8, hadith 55, describes worshippers at Qubāʾ turning during prayer upon receiving news of the changed qibla. Its chapter heading connects the report to nonrepetition, but that heading is Bukhārī’s legal framing, not Prophetic speech. The narration concerns newly received legal information; extending it to a factual miscalculation is an analogy that requires defense.

3. The competing methodological paths

One path makes correct orientation the relevant condition and concludes that discovered failure defeats discharge. Another interprets the unseen-qibla obligation as satisfied by a legally authorized, competent inquiry in the circumstances.

The draft selects the second path on a cumulative, preponderant basis: capacity-relative compliance, the Qubāʾ model of responding to newly accessible decisive information, and the inherited reception of the particular qibla rule. The weak report is corroborative historical material, not an independent foundation. The proposed shared ground is reasonable access to the information needed for orientation. Whether the legal-change and factual-error cases share that ground adequately is this dossier’s weakest bridge.

The strongest repeat objection is that an unchanged objective condition failed, as when prayer precedes its time: Qubāʾ involved reliance on a previously correct legal direction, whereas this case involves an inaccurate factual conclusion. Capacity can excuse the person without fulfilling the condition. The proposed qibla-specific account must defeat that objection; neither a weak report nor sympathy for diligent error is sufficient.

4. Candidate principle and provisional conclusion

Candidate principle: where the evidence establishes a fallible procedure as a legally sufficient performance standard, judge performance by competent use of that procedure and the information reasonably available then. Later discovery of error does not itself establish invalidity or a remedial duty. Mere permission to consult an indicator is insufficient: its status as a performance standard must be demonstrated.

Working ruling: a person unable to ascertain the unseen qibla directly, who used adequately reliable available indications without neglect and completed the prayer, is provisionally treated as having discharged it despite subsequently demonstrated directional error. No obligatory iʿāda within time or qaḍāʾ after time follows solely from that discovery. The direction remains objectively wrong; the ruling concerns legal discharge under the selected interpretation.

This does not derive validity from pardon. It adopts, with moderate-to-low confidence, a specific capacity-sensitive account of the orientation condition. If that positive account fails, absence of blame cannot rescue the conclusion.

5. Scope and concrete failure tests

When the correct direction becomes reliably known during prayer, the worshipper must turn toward it as soon as practicable; the selected protection does not authorize continuing knowingly in error. Better accessible means may raise what counts as adequate inquiry; hindsight alone cannot prove earlier neglect. Knowingly disregarding a clear direction, guessing without inquiry when inquiry is available, and emergency prayer concessions require different rules.

The timing distinction matters. Darūrī §§204–205, p. 123 discusses makeup and complete compliance. Section 204 cannot by itself remove iʿāda while the original time remains. Our within-time conclusion instead depends on the prior judgment of valid discharge.

A counterexample is Bukhārī 135, Book 4, hadith 1, explicitly conditioning acceptance after ritual impurity on ablution. The qibla ruling does not erase independently established purity conditions or imply that every sincere mistaken belief produces a valid act. Third-party restitution is likewise outside this worship-specific conclusion.

Classification: no obligatory repetition after adequate inquiry is an existing position. The distinct candidate contribution is the mandatory three-part assessment and explicit evidential threshold for authorizing a fallible standard. This is the least secure selection among these four dossiers.

6. Residual evidence tasks

Collate all dark-night routes and early rulings, examine classical arguments using the Qubāʾ report, distinguish changed opinion from conclusive discovery, and specify material directional deviation and adequate inquiry. Test prayer-time mistakes and undiscovered impurity independently. A successful objection to the positive performance-standard bridge would require revisiting validity, then separately addressing within-time repetition and post-time makeup.

Formation hypothesis (Edition II only): Reasoned confidence under uncertainty, coupled with correction when knowledge changes; excuse is distinct from truth.
Editorial hypothesis; not an operative legal ʿilla or a new validity condition.

Countertests

Four further test cases for the proposed interpretive articles. These are not four additional authoritative legal determinations.

Countertest · T01

An evidential sign is not always a prescribed procedure

Provisional finding: Other adequate evidence of the event can replace sound or smell; this does not automatically replace the lunar sighting/completion procedure.

Source references: H04, M10, H05
Full discussion

T01. An evidential sign is not always a prescribed procedure

Evidence and question

Al-Bukhārī 137 tells a person troubled by doubt about ritual purity not to leave prayer without sound or smell. Al-ʿAynī explains that actual hearing and smelling are not exclusive conditions: the point concerns knowing that the relevant event occurred, including for a person unable to use those senses. Al-Bukhārī 1909, however, gives both a sighting instruction for the fasting month and an express thirty-day fallback when the crescent is obscured. These texts present different interpretive structures. [H04; M10; H05]

Provisional rule and conclusion

C07 requires identifying whether a named item is evidence of a fact or part of the legally authorized procedure. For the ablution report, certainty of the relevant invalidating event can arise through adequate evidence other than those two senses; mere anxiety remains insufficient. This result is already classical. The more general proposed discipline is to forbid transfer of this result to a second subject until its evidential structure has been established.

The crescent report therefore defeats the proposed universal principle that a technically improved detector may always replace a named method. The explicit fallback needs an account. Version 0.1 retains the report's sighting-or-completion framework in this limited test; it does not establish astronomical calculation as a universal substitute simply by analogy with the sound-and-smell case. A complete calendar position would require the broader report family, local and global sighting questions, the role of calculation in rejecting impossible testimony, and the legal meaning of visibility. Those questions are not decided here.

Strongest objection and revision trigger

An opponent may argue that sighting and the fallback were practical means of identifying a naturally occurring month for a community without easy computation. That is a substantive interpretation to investigate. It must explain the wording and its procedural alternative, not simply assert that a newer method is more accurate. Conversely, this test does not prove that instruments, observational assistance, or calculation can never have a role. Its conclusion is narrower: substitution depends on the legal function of the method, and the same epistemic threshold must be preserved.

Result: accept reliable evidence beyond sound and smell; reject automatic transfer to replacing the lunar procedure. Confidence: high for the first narrow result; medium for the procedural restraint. Amendment: C07 must include explicit fallbacks as evidence against a pure-proxy reading.

Countertest · T02

Office can be the relevant relationship even when a payment is called a gift

Provisional finding: An office-derived gift does not become private entitlement through a label or proved coordinated intermediary; disposition requires separate warrant.

Source references: H06
Full discussion

T02. Office can be the relevant relationship even when a payment is called a gift

Evidence and question

In al-Bukhārī 7174, the collector separates official receipts from what he calls personal gifts. The Prophetic response tests whether he would have received those benefits while remaining at his parents' home. The report supplies an explicit comparison that connects the benefit to the entrusted position. [H06]

Provisional rule and conclusion

Under C10, legal analysis should preserve an evidenced relationship across changes in the arrangement's superficial form. A gift obtained because a person exercises the entrusted collection function cannot become an unrestricted private entitlement merely by being labelled a gift. If a donor routes the same benefit through an intermediary, the extension rests on the same demonstrated relationship, provided the intermediary arrangement is actually proved. That application is proposed reasoning, not a direct report about modern intermediaries.

The inquiry would record the donor's relation to the officer's decisions, timing, established prior pattern of gifts, comparable gifts in the absence of the appointment, conditions attached, and any coordinated routing. These are possible evidence of a causal relationship, not a fixed statutory checklist claimed to come from the hadith. The officeholder's personal virtue is not a substitute for establishing the facts.

Strongest objection and revision trigger

A counterfactual can be underdetermined: colleagues can become actual friends, and a pre-existing family gift can continue during public service. The report does not establish that every benefit received by every employed person is identical to an official collector's benefit. The provisional prohibition is bounded by entrusted authority and the demonstrated connection. It also does not alone determine whether a particular benefit must be returned to the donor or paid to a public fund; disposition requires its own evidence under C09.

Result: the relevant legal unit can be the evidenced arrangement, including coordinated intermediaries; a change of label does not by itself alter the ruling. Confidence: high for the collector's office-derived benefit; medium for the extension. Novelty: established anti-evasion reasoning made subject to an explicit causal-evidence and remedy requirement.

Countertest · T03

Financial maturity should be tested as an operative capacity

Provisional finding: Demonstrated maturity and financial competence control the specified substantive assessment; arbitrary gendered temporal proxies do not establish incapacity.

Source references: Q01, B07-P
Full discussion

T03 / B07. Financial maturity should be tested as an operative capacity

Evidence and question

Qurʾān 4:6 connects handing over an orphan's property to maturity and demonstrated sound judgment. In Bidāya, Book of Interdiction, chapter 2, Ibn Rushd distinguishes actual competence from a formal declaration ending supervision. He also criticizes fixed waiting periods in accounts of women's financial maturity where competence can exist earlier. His attribution to Ibn al-Qāsim concerns a specified ward case; it must not be turned into a claim that every jurist already applied one rule to every category. [Q01; B07-P]

Provisional rule and conclusion

The constitution selects demonstrated maturity and sound financial judgment as the operative conditions in the adult ward problem. An otherwise competent adult's transactions do not become substantively void solely because an executor has failed to announce release. This follows the branch that treats recognition as evidential in the specified setting. C07 then supports a further, explicitly proposed application: assess a woman's competence by adequate evidence of competence itself, rather than make marriage or an arbitrary period after marriage its indispensable proof.

The case also forces separation of competence from moral admiration. In this property setting, v0.1 favors capacity to manage the relevant property responsibly as the controlling meaning of rushd; it does not treat every unrelated moral failing as sufficient financial incapacity. This is a choice between inherited readings, not a neutral dictionary fact. Its support is the passage's property-management setting and the command to test the person.

Strongest objection and revision trigger

Private competence assessments can conflict; third parties need public knowledge of authority; an exploitative guardian may falsely attest competence. The conclusion therefore leaves room for an independently justified public process, records, accessible review, and protection against fraud. It rejects an unsupported inference from absent paperwork to actual incompetence. It does not settle how modern positive law assigns the effects of registration, nor does it claim that an adult's assertion of competence is enough.

Result: demonstrated financial competence controls the substantive assessment; gendered temporal proxies require evidence and cannot replace it by assumption. Confidence: medium-high within the stated classical problem. Novelty: an inherited criterion applied consistently to the empirical assumptions used to establish it. Reversal condition: proof that a particular declaration is independently constitutive for the disputed effect, rather than merely a means of recognizing competence.

Countertest · T04

An excused treatment error can still leave a compensation claim

Provisional finding: Moral excuse is not an automatic bar to compensation for a proved compensable accidental treatment injury; payer and unavoidable-risk cases remain separate.

Source references: B26-P, H07
Full discussion

T04 / B26. An excused treatment error can still leave a compensation claim

Evidence and question

Bidāya's treatment of accidental injury records compensation for a physician's mistake, a Mālikī exemption for a qualified practitioner, and agreement on the liability of an unqualified intruder. It also reports disagreement about the fund that pays. The passage's broad agreement language must be read alongside its stated exception. Abū Dāwūd 4586 concerns someone practising medicine without established competence; Abū Dāwūd's own comment expresses uncertainty about its route, while the displayed later grading is ḥasan. That report is not direct proof of every liability rule for qualified physicians. [B26-P; H07]

Provisional rule and conclusion

Version 0.1 adopts the bounded accidental-injury branch: a proved unintended injury caused by an erroneous intervention can generate compensation despite the absence of deliberate wrongdoing. The example is an unintended cutting injury, not every unsuccessful treatment or every unavoidable complication of a properly authorized procedure. The basis is the analogy to accidental injury and the recorded juristic treatment; the novice-practitioner report corroborates a different, more culpable category.

C08 therefore cannot turn competent inquiry into immunity from all downstream effects. An injured patient's entitlement concerns a distinct person and a different legal effect from the clinician's blameworthiness. The same constitution can hold a prayer sufficient after authorized directional inquiry and hold compensation due for an unintended bodily injury. That is coherence through different operative facts and interests, rather than two contradictory conclusions about “mistakes.”

Strongest objection and revision trigger

The strongest opposing position treats competent, authorized treatment as an activity whose unavoidable risks are not attributed to the practitioner in this way. The dispute cannot be resolved by compassion for either party. One must establish the nature of the error, causation, authorization, the accepted risk, and the proof that makes this particular injury compensable. Whether payment comes from the practitioner, a collective arrangement, or another fund is a further question. Version 0.1 deliberately decides the survival of a possible claim in the specified error case without inventing a modern payment system.

Result: moral excuse is not an automatic bar to compensation for a proved compensable accidental injury. Confidence: medium for the selected branch; high for the logical distinction between culpability and liability. Amendment: C08 must require a separate inquiry into third-party rights and cannot be formulated as a universal “reasonable process makes everything valid” rule.

Interpretive constitution: C01–C15

A working set of rules to test across legal disputes, not an established legal school.

Interpretive article · v0.1

C01 · State the authority of the evidence before combining doctrines

The Qurʾān and critically established Prophetic Sunna are binding sources within this prototype. Distinguish certainty of transmission from certainty of interpretation. A claim of ijmāʿ must identify its exact proposition, relevant period and participants, evidence of agreement, and surviving dissent. Agreement among the four later Sunni schools is not automatically identical with universal early agreement. Reported silence is weaker than positively attested agreement unless a defensible account of expected objection supports the inference. When two selected rules meet within one act or transaction, assess the complete combination, including an alleged shared judgment of invalidity despite different juristic reasons. Individual precedents and the label of independent derivation do not settle that assessment. [M02; M11]

A Companion's established judgment has substantial interpretive and historical weight. A disputed Companion judgment is not independently conclusive here; its legal relevance, reason, rival evidence, and reception must be evaluated. It can nevertheless supply sufficient defeasible warrant on a balance of reasons, including for an exception. A Companion's report about what occurred in a Prophetic incident must also be distinguished from that Companion's own subsequent legal judgment; ambiguous attribution must not silently become direct Prophetic speech. These choices have real costs in the divorce cases. They cannot be covertly reversed whenever a Companion supports a preferred answer. The first prototype works within these common Sunni source assumptions. Jaʿfarī, Zaydī, Ibāḍī, and Ismāʿīlī materials can expose alternatives, but their different authority premises must be argued before importing conclusions that depend on them. [M02; B18; B21]

Interpretive article · v0.1

C02 · Let the precision of the argument match the precision of transmission

Before deriving a rule from one word, assemble the relevant report family: routes, companion witnesses, event identity, wording differences, additions, omissions, and early criticism. Several later chains through one source do not automatically provide several independent witnesses. A grading label on an entire report does not by itself decide which competing wording is original.

Where rival reliable wordings describe one event, an argument uniquely dependent on one wording needs a reason to prefer it. Agreement on the event's legal core can remain stronger than confidence in a particular formula. Compatible additions remain usable after ordinary transmission criticism; this article does not reduce every report to the intersection of all its versions. Nor does it permit treating a composite concession as if every component were independently optional. The proposed novelty is to make these limits a mandatory step in legal derivation, rather than an occasional observation in commentary. The marriage-formula variants and Ibn Daqīq al-ʿĪd's discussion, preserved in Fatḥ al-bārī, provide a direct classical anchor. [M03; B02; B18]

Interpretive article · v0.1

C03 · Identify the speaker's capacity, then assign the force of the demand

Ask whether the utterance conveys general religious instruction, adjudicates particular evidence, exercises public authority, advises on a worldly matter, intercedes, or reports a personal practice. Context must establish a departure from general religious instruction; merely disliking a consequence is not evidence of a different capacity. Al-Qarāfī treats religious communication as the predominant category in doubtful cases while distinguishing judicial and executive actions. [M04]

Once a command is established as legislative, obligation is the rebuttable default of this constitution. Context or stronger evidence may establish recommendation or permission instead. Remedial purpose is a separate classification: a direction to repair an earlier act can itself be obligatory, as the proposed menstrual-divorce decision illustrates. An obligation to do something does not, without more, establish immediate performance, indefinite repetition, exclusive means, or nullity on breach. Barīra's explicit question about whether the Prophet was commanding or interceding gives unusually clear primary evidence that speech capacity matters. It does not license classifying every unwelcome command as advice. [H01; B18]

Interpretive article · v0.1

C04 · Prefer evidenced context to invented explanations

Collect reports before declaring conflict. Give weight to contextual indications that actually shape the utterance's meaning. A specific occasion alone does not automatically narrow otherwise general language. An authenticated explanation of a temporary restriction is stronger evidence of its operative context than a modern conjecture about its purpose. Ibn Daqīq al-ʿĪd makes the distinction between meaningful contextual indications and merely occurring on a particular occasion especially clear in the travel-fasting discussion. [M05]

Reconciliation must preserve a plausible meaning of both proofs. It may not be purchased by inventing two occasions, an unreported chronology, or a hidden exception. Abrogation requires adequate chronology and a genuine conflict that cannot be plausibly reconciled. Where a superior transmission or interpretation emerges, prefer it with reasons. If a material conflict remains, state the residual uncertainty and use the appropriate legal default; do not announce that the reports “cancel out” as if their authority and scope had never mattered. The sacrificial-meat report provides a useful positive example of an expressed circumstance affecting a restriction. [H02]

Interpretive article · v0.1

C05 · Keep initiation, continuation, completion, timing, and remedy distinct

A command addressed to somebody already engaged in an act ordinarily governs that stage. Moving from “complete X” to “everyone must begin X,” or from “perform X” to “repeat X after every error,” requires an additional inference. That inference can be sound, but must be shown. Likewise, the bare imperative does not settle whether an obligation must be performed at the first conceivable moment.

Time is part of the legally described situation. A recurring annual opportunity separated by months of impossibility differs from one continuous prayer interval; foreseeable loss of capacity can matter where the duty is already established. This article therefore rejects both automatic immediacy from grammar alone and automatic permission to postpone a serious established duty indefinitely. In this casebook, B17 provisionally selects initial ʿumra obligation on independent evidence while limiting what the completion verse itself proves. B16 separately favors the first realistically available hajj season through specific timing reports and a stated restriction of their binding force to an already owed first pilgrimage. Continuing to owe a duty does not itself prove that its postponement is forbidden. [B16; B17]

Interpretive article · v0.1

C06 · Distinguish a demonstrated form from the minimum valid performance

Establish what an instruction positively requires using language, context, explanatory practice, and other proofs together. A complete Prophetic performance demonstrates a sound form; it does not automatically prove that every detail is a condition of validity. Conversely, calling something “only a demonstration” cannot remove a detail that the surrounding evidence makes obligatory.

An added numerical minimum, exclusive formula, or invalidating condition requires a defensible basis. A context in which a person urgently needs complete instruction gives omitted indispensable conditions evidential significance: the proposed presumption of sufficient instruction applies only where completeness was actually expected, the relevant report is adequately preserved, and another reliable proof does not supply the condition. Narrator abbreviation and selective questions can defeat the presumption. The dower discussion is a particularly direct classical test. Head-wiping adds the corrective rule that a demonstrated composite concession must remain composite unless severability is independently established. [B02; B13/B23]

Interpretive article · v0.1

C07 · Separate the operative fact, its evidence, and a constitutive procedure

Every rule dependent on a sign should identify three things: the fact that matters, the standard by which it is established, and whether the named procedure merely proves the fact or legally constitutes a status. New information can replace an evidential proxy where the proof shows that reliable knowledge is what matters. It cannot automatically replace a prescribed constitutive act or a specifically mandated fallback.

Facts used in legal reasoning must remain open to appropriate investigation. Financial competence cannot be reduced to an assumed period of marriage when competence can be demonstrated independently. An official declaration may nevertheless have separately justified effects for publicity, third-party reliance, and dispute resolution. The article thus favors evidence-sensitive recognition of maturity without pretending that every registration requirement is meaningless. Sound and smell in the ablution-doubt report illustrate evidence of an event; moon-sighting together with the thirty-day fallback supplies a counterexample to universal substitution. [T01; T03]

Interpretive article · v0.1

C08 · Authorized inquiry can discharge a duty only where its legal role is proved

Separate factual correctness, reasonable inquiry, moral excuse, validity, and remaining remedy. If the law authorizes a fallible procedure as the relevant standard of performance in a setting, its competent use can discharge the duty despite a later discovery of error. But being excused does not itself establish discharge: a requirement to repeat may remain, and a third party's loss may still require compensation.

Record what inquiry was realistically available, why it was adequate, when contrary knowledge arose, and whether the remedy would be repetition within time or performance after time. Correct ongoing conduct once reliable contrary information becomes available. The qibla decision adopts a bounded no-obligatory-repetition position after competent inquiry, with a weaker evidential bridge explicitly recorded. The physician countertest prevents generalizing that conclusion to all consequences of reasonable error. Judicial procedure likewise cannot turn another person's known property into one's own rightful possession. [B25; T04; H03]

Interpretive article · v0.1

C09 · Establish effects and claimants separately, then preserve proved dependencies

For each transaction or status, record the basis of formation, conditions, prohibitions, persons holding rights, power to waive, authorized remedy, and downstream consequences. After formation has been established, a prohibition affecting a surrounding act does not by itself annihilate the completed institution. This is a rebuttable rule of inference, not a universal claim that prohibited contracts are valid. If the prohibition addresses an indispensable constituent, or a source specifies nullity, that evidence controls.

Do not invent a veto merely because an order of precedence exists. Do not invent an inheritance entitlement merely because a motive is reprehensible. Equally, do not reject a positively evidenced special inheritance entitlement because it leaves other effects of divorce intact. Selective effects can be legally real. The constitution must keep all dependencies it actually accepts: a counted revocable divorce, for example, carries the consequences of that status as well as any separately established corrective duty. The strongest objections to this article come from concrete transmitted exceptions, not an abstract preference for conceptual symmetry. [B03; B08; B18; B21]

Interpretive article · v0.1

C10 · Extend rules through relevant meanings and real arrangements

Qiyās is admissible on preponderant evidence. State the original rule, proposed operative feature, evidence of its relevance, its occurrence in the new case, and the strongest rival explanation. Superficial verbal resemblance is insufficient. Several independent considerations can strengthen an extension even when no single universally accepted cause exhausts the field. The same analysis must identify counterexamples and limits; disagreement does not by itself suspend analogy.

Analyse a coordinated arrangement as a whole when evidence establishes that its parts implement one legally relevant relationship or purpose. The official-gift report gives an unusually strong reason to examine whether a benefit arose because of entrusted office; routing it through an intermediary need not change that relationship. An ordinary independent gift does not become illicit merely because one party happens to hold an office. Similarly, blocking evasion may justify a properly grounded response, but does not determine its recipient, amount, or duration without further law. In the ribā dossier, supported extension reaches clear food analogues while broader contested boundaries remain research questions. [T02; B21; B22]

Interpretive article · v0.1

C11 · Treat reception and legal defaults as reasoned evidence

Early public practice can bear on scope, observance, and expected transmission. Its weight depends on who witnessed what, how widely the rule should have been known, what contrary evidence survives, and whether a local practice claims a privileged status. The prototype gives reception defeasible evidential weight, not an automatic veto over a reliable individual report. Al-Layth's criticism of an undifferentiated appeal to Medinan authority and the Ḥanafī discussion of widespread need belong in this inquiry; neither can be compressed into “practice beats hadith.” [M06; M07]

State the default that actually fits the case. A new devotional obligation needs warrant; an established obligation is not discharged by mere doubt; an established person's property is not reassigned by conjecture; a formed transaction does not disappear from uncertainty about an additional restriction. These defaults can conflict, so first decide which positive conditions and effects have been established. The burden may change as proof accumulates. No universal “choose ease,” “always be cautious,” or “always preserve validity” principle governs this constitution.

Interpretive article · v0.1

C12 · Keep formation accountable to law and keep the constitution revisable

Formation concerns what living and applying the law does to attention, appetite, fairness, trust, courage, compassion, and institutions. These effects should be investigated, especially where a rule claims a benefit that may or may not occur. They do not automatically create a legal cause, invalidate an act, or authorize a private revelation to bind other people. The Qurʾān's links between fasting and taqwā, prayer and restraint from wrongdoing, and charity and purification supply a scriptural basis for studying formation as an effect. [Q02; Q03; Q04]

When consequences legitimately enter legal reasoning, distinguish an established textual purpose, a plausible explanatory wisdom, a measurable causal claim, and a particular adjudicative remedy. Al-Shāṭibī's attention to consequences and his warning that intelligible transactions can retain irreducible prescribed conditions are both necessary. A formation-based interpretation that simply removes the condition it finds inconvenient fails this constitution. [M08; M09]

Every amendment must be reapplied to earlier cases. Record what result would count against it. Maintain the rival view and the evidence that could reverse the provisional conclusion. A principle that survives only by acquiring a new unmotivated exception at each difficult case should be revised or abandoned. This discipline, rather than the number of unfamiliar verdicts, determines whether the project becomes a coherent new orientation.

Additional interpretive article · v0.2

C13 · Coercion and legally operative volition

Separately assess intentional utterance, practical freedom, authorized legal disposition, and proof of coercion. A legally consequential act performed under established coercion requires distinct justification of effectiveness. Tests: B06, B08, B24.

Additional interpretive article · v0.2

C14 · Attach liability to the proper legal subject

Determine whether a duty attaches to competent agency, ownership, wealth, a relationship, or public office; distinguish obligation, representation, collection, and blame. Tests: B04, B19, B20.

Additional interpretive article · v0.2

C15 · Resolve semantic alternatives by convergent evidence

For an ambiguous word, compare Qurʾānic distribution, authenticated Prophetic use, relevant practice, competing historical readings, and coherence with other legal instructions without treating a preferred paraphrase as certain. Tests: B12, B10, B27.

Scope and method · complete working text

Scope and method

Status and result

This dossier executes a first experiment in constructing a coherent candidate madhhab: twelve substantial decision studies, four additional countertests, a twelve-article interpretive constitution, and a recoverable source programme. Its distinctive commitment is to make the passage from transmitted evidence to each particular legal effect explicit. An act's prohibition, legal validity, required correction, liability, and spiritual benefit are related questions, but their relationship must be established rather than assumed.

The proposed conclusions are actual working positions within this experiment. They are neither a claim to have founded a socially recognized school nor a claim that the twelve articles are unprecedented in Islamic intellectual history. Most case outcomes have classical antecedents. The proposed innovation lies chiefly in their combination, priority rules, explicit burdens of inference, and systematic application across subjects. A school would also require a substantially larger body of worked law, teaching and adjudicative traditions, and sustained criticism. The work here supplies a concrete constitution to criticize and revise. Individually selected arguments do not automatically authorize every combined practice; the joint application of B02 and B05 remains an expressly unfinished test.

The retrieved project witness is Daruri_Interactive_Reader_Updated.html, version 1. Its twenty-eight Bidāya applications provide the B01-B28 sequence used here; their linked Darūrī section numbers are preserved separately. B13 and B23 concern two arguments about the same dower issue and count as one decision. Twelve detailed decisions therefore cover thirteen application addresses. B07 and B26 receive shorter countertests. Thirteen further application addresses remain explicitly unassessed. The canonical Revision16 master was not retrieved in this pass; no mapping to its K-position register or replacement of its content is claimed.

The central design choice

The proposed method combines textual discipline with justified inference. It accepts analogy on a well-supported balance of reasons; it does not require mathematical certainty or unanimous agreement on a cause. It also refuses to let a broad purpose silently generate a numerical threshold, a ritual condition, or somebody else's legal entitlement. Conversely, a reliably transmitted special legal effect can defeat our preferred conceptual model. The law is not obliged to fit an artificially tidy picture of marriage, worship, or property.

Ibn Rushd's Darūrī offers a suitable point of departure: the opening methodological discussion proposes examining how alternative principles entail other positions and correspond to branches of law, including different paths for textualists and advocates of analogy. That is a programme of comparative dependence, not a warrant for selecting attractive answers independently. Al-Āmidī's discussion of a third opinion supplies another useful constraint: on his selected account, a new distinction must not destroy a proposition genuinely shared by the antecedent positions. His account is itself contested, and he does not authorize unrestricted combination. [M01; M02]

Definitions used throughout

Thubūt concerns whether a report is established; dalāla concerns what it indicates. A reliable transmission may contain an ambiguous expression. ʿIlla means an operative legal ground supported for purposes of extension; ḥikma names a wisdom or benefit that may help explain an institution without defining its legally sufficient conditions. Taḥqīq al-manāṭ investigates whether the relevant condition occurs in a particular case. Ṣiḥḥa concerns legal validity; imtithāl concerns compliance; ijzāʾ concerns discharge of an obligation; ḍamān concerns liability. The exact relation between these concepts can itself be disputed, so the register records the substantive proposition instead of treating terminology as an answer.

“Independent warrant” below does not mean that every consequence needs its own separate verse. Linguistic entailment, a proved dependency, supported analogy, and an applicable general rule can supply warrant. It means that the inference must be stated and defended. This distinction prevents the constitution from becoming a disguised rejection of reasoning beyond literal wording.

Valid incorporation under the shared authority rules

Incorporate propositions with their warrants, not packages inferred from names. An early jurist’s view is historical evidence and an argument to assess. The charter’s binding sources and accepted inference rules still decide the result. A useful proposal extracted from a disputed attribution can be tested as a present hypothesis, provided the text does not falsely present it as that jurist’s established doctrine.

Preserve the level of the witness. Mark a proposition as an authorial statement, transmitted answer, opponent’s quotation, bibliographical title, or modern reconstruction. An attributed position does not carry every surrounding explanation with it. For example, Ibn al-Mundhir’s rationale following his list of jurists cannot be distributed retrospectively to every name in that list.

Apply imported principles consistently. The charter can use Dāwūdian criticism to expose unsupported analogies while admitting other analogies on independently stated grounds. It cannot simultaneously declare all qiyās intrinsically invalid and treat qiyās as binding when convenient. Likewise, al-Layth’s challenge can motivate investigation of geographically dispersed reception without turning every regional practice into an overriding source.

Retain genuine conflicts. Al-Thawrī’s reported position on intention and Isḥāq’s explanation supporting intention cannot both decide the same proposition under unchanged facts. Record both, evaluate their arguments by the same rules, and state why one wins. This permits synthesis without concealing contradictory premises.

Use loss of documentation to limit claims. It weakens the inference that a doctrine was absent; it does not convert a proposed doctrine into an authentic recovered rule. Search Ibn al-Mundhir’s al-Awsaṭ and al-Ishrāf, al-Marwazī’s Ikhtilāf al-ʿulamāʾ, early Muṣannaf collections, and surviving school polemics as additional witnesses. These are further investigation targets, not assertions that every relevant passage has now been audited.

For each extracted rule, retain: exact locus; named speaker; witness and transmission path; legal proposition; explicit reason, if present; counterargument; uncertainty; and whether adoption changes an existing ruling or supplies a new rationale. This makes the discontinued traditions usable without inventing complete lost constitutions.

An unfinished test: B02 together with B05 in one wuḍūʾ

B02 and B05 require a separate test of one complete wuḍūʾ: wiping less than the applicable Ḥanafī minimum while lacking the intention required by the other three standard school packages. Classical antecedents for each individual ruling do not establish that their combination is permissible. The potentially shared proposition concerns the complete act, even where the reasons for rejecting it differ.

Al-Āmidī's selected position permits a new distinction only when it does not overturn a proposition actually shared by the earlier positions. An established agreement that this composite act is invalid would therefore constrain C01. Conversely, rejection under four later school packages alone does not demonstrate the requisite agreement of all relevant earlier jurists. The historical and propositional question remains to be investigated. [M02]

There is a substantial early objection to the easy consensus claim. Ibn al-Mundhir attributes to al-Thawrī sufficiency of wiping one hair in the explicit case of a woman. Ibn ʿAbd al-Barr attributes to him water purification without ritual intention and names three transmitters of a teaching-demonstration example. These are separately transmitted propositions, not a directly attested judgment on the exact composite act; neither inspected passage supplies a complete transmission audit. Their conjunction is a plausible reconstruction requiring examination of scope and possible joint qualifications. The woman's example must not silently become a reported universal rule. [M12; M13]

IIFA Resolution 70, sections 5-6, addresses talfīq in following schools and prohibits, among other forms, consensus-breaking combinations and a composite accepted by no mujtahid. Independent derivation requires its own justification; calling this project “ijtihād” does not exempt it from authority, competence, or consensus constraints. [M11]

Recorded result: B02 and B05 remain individual provisional arguments, but their combined application is not yet defended as a complete practice. Before endorsing it, evaluate both requirements together, identify the exact allegedly shared proposition, collate the identified early dissent, and answer the strongest objection. No artificial compromise quantity is inserted simply to conceal the unresolved issue.

What the first cases forced us to change

1. A report's legal effect cannot be read from a grading panel alone

B17 retains an initial ʿumra obligation only as a working choice conditional on two substantive premises: acceptance of the ʿumra addition in the ʿĀʾisha report family, and its obligation rather than virtue reading. The proxy report independently corroborates ʿumra's presence and a male beneficiary; its scope has not been established as a second independent universal-obligation proof. Version 0.1 has compared important versions and recorded the conflict; it has not completed a critical reconstruction. A final adoption under C02 must resolve that task. The same discipline requires preserving the contrary wording and the collector's comparison in B18.

2. A stage rule does not determine the final verdict by itself

The completion principle does useful work even when initial ʿumra remains obligatory. It locates which evidence proves which duty. It cannot be judged a failure merely because it does not yield an optional practice. Likewise, completion of an invalidated pilgrimage and discharge of the original duty can come apart; the method must track both.

3. A continuing obligation does not prove that all postponement is forbidden

B16's first-feasible-season conclusion depends on its timing reports and its restriction of their binding scope to an already owed first pilgrimage. A person defending lawful postponement is not claiming to have discharged hajj. C11's preservation of an established obligation cannot settle that dispute. Nor can vague precaution concerning death replace a failed timing argument.

4. Remedial purpose and obligatory force are different questions

The initial constitution's wording put a remedial direction beside recommendation and permission. That was wrong: B18 selects an obligatory remedial command. C03 has been amended accordingly. A counted wrongful act, a binding direction to correct its consequences, and a public power to enforce that direction require separate assessment.

5. Selective consequences can be positively required

B08's guardian report is a powerful counterexample to an overly tidy legal-effect theory: the report states invalidity while awarding dower after consummation. The effect has its own warrant. B21's early inheritance awards likewise cannot be rejected merely because other marital effects end. The low-confidence no-automatic-inheritance selection remains an explicit source-authority judgment; it is not the only result C09 can coherently support.

6. Dissent defeats an easy consensus claim, not every positive argument

C01 now expressly allows a disputed Companion judgment to provide sufficient defeasible warrant. It also distinguishes a Companion's testimony about a Prophetic incident from a later Companion adjudication. B18 and B21 therefore cannot be treated identically merely because both contain a Companion's speech. Their individual evidential roles must be examined.

7. No universal immunity follows from reasonable inquiry

B25's no-repeat ruling requires an affirmative, qibla-specific interpretation of the performance standard. Its analogy with receiving a changed direction remains a weak point. T04 shows why even justified activity can leave a distinct compensation question. The relevant legal condition and protected relation matter more than the shared word “error.”

8. A better factual detector does not automatically replace a legal procedure

T01 permits other adequate evidence of the invalidating event in the ablution case, but preserves the need to account for the lunar report's express fallback. T03 similarly distinguishes competence from its formal recognition while allowing independently grounded institutional effects. C07 now explicitly requires inquiry into constitutive procedures and stated alternatives.

9. Analogy need not find one undisputed cause before it can work

B22 rejects both confinement to six commodities on the sole basis of juristic disagreement and expansion to every measurable item on the sole basis of measurability. Rice and raisins receive actual provisional extensions. Marginal cases remain open because their arguments need study, not because uncertainty automatically means permission. The quality-date example also prevents “equal market value” from displacing an explicit equality rule.

10. Demonstrated sufficiency is not identical to a minimum, but demonstration remains evidence

B02 must answer the whole-head account on its merits. Describing that account as an “added condition” cannot settle whether the demonstration already explains the command's content. The forelock-and-turban report remains composite. This qualification prevents C06 from becoming a routine device for reducing every ritual to the smallest conceivable movement.

The strongest general objections

“This is eclecticism with a procedural vocabulary.” That objection succeeds if every case is chosen first and the articles are adjusted afterward. The answer is to record general commitments that can lose cases: productive individual reports, probabilistic qiyās, obligatory legislative commands, separately warranted effects, and nonconclusive but potentially sufficient Companion evidence. These commitments rule out attractive shortcuts. A broader corpus may still expose selection bias; the present twelve cases cannot prove that it has disappeared.

“Your default against extra conditions favors the result you want.” A strong objection. C06 therefore requires deciding whether a disputed feature explains the original act or adds a further restriction. B02 and B05 do not mechanically follow from that default. Their serious opponents are retained, and B05 is among the lower-confidence cases. A valid derivation from general speech can establish a condition; “no separate verse” is never a sufficient refutation.

“Most outcomes are already found in existing schools.” Correct. The first pass does not establish a set of historically unprecedented rulings. A novel school would be identifiable by how it consistently ranks evidence, extends law, and revises decisions across a large corpus. A new rationale for an inherited result can be more consequential than a novel isolated answer, because it changes the next cases that the system can decide.

“The system should choose the widest protection of human interests.” The constitution admits legally supported protection and consequences, but interests can conflict and do not measure their own remedies. In B21, a protective exception affects other heirs; in T04, excusing a clinician does not remove the patient's loss; in B03, safeguarding an earlier suitor cannot erase the woman's choice. The method must establish whose interest has what legal form, rather than allowing a general value to settle every relation.

“A complete school must answer the unresolved cases.” Eventually it needs a much larger, usable corpus. A first research version should instead expose exactly where an inference is pending. The register distinguishes an adopted provisional verdict, a countertest, and an unassessed project address. It does not fill unknown parts with silently borrowed school positions. Conditional case assumptions are listed so that later research can either justify or revise them.

Scope and method

Status and result

This dossier executes a first experiment in constructing a coherent candidate madhhab: twelve substantial decision studies, four additional countertests, a twelve-article interpretive constitution, and a recoverable source programme. Its distinctive commitment is to make the passage from transmitted evidence to each particular legal effect explicit. An act's prohibition, legal validity, required correction, liability, and spiritual benefit are related questions, but their relationship must be established rather than assumed.

The proposed conclusions are actual working positions within this experiment. They are neither a claim to have founded a socially recognized school nor a claim that the twelve articles are unprecedented in Islamic intellectual history. Most case outcomes have classical antecedents. The proposed innovation lies chiefly in their combination, priority rules, explicit burdens of inference, and systematic application across subjects. A school would also require a substantially larger body of worked law, teaching and adjudicative traditions, and sustained criticism. The work here supplies a concrete constitution to criticize and revise. Individually selected arguments do not automatically authorize every combined practice; the joint application of B02 and B05 remains an expressly unfinished test.

The retrieved project witness is Daruri_Interactive_Reader_Updated.html, version 1. Its twenty-eight Bidāya applications provide the B01-B28 sequence used here; their linked Darūrī section numbers are preserved separately. B13 and B23 concern two arguments about the same dower issue and count as one decision. Twelve detailed decisions therefore cover thirteen application addresses. B07 and B26 receive shorter countertests. Thirteen further application addresses remain explicitly unassessed. The canonical Revision16 master was not retrieved in this pass; no mapping to its K-position register or replacement of its content is claimed.

The central design choice

The proposed method combines textual discipline with justified inference. It accepts analogy on a well-supported balance of reasons; it does not require mathematical certainty or unanimous agreement on a cause. It also refuses to let a broad purpose silently generate a numerical threshold, a ritual condition, or somebody else's legal entitlement. Conversely, a reliably transmitted special legal effect can defeat our preferred conceptual model. The law is not obliged to fit an artificially tidy picture of marriage, worship, or property.

Ibn Rushd's Darūrī offers a suitable point of departure: the opening methodological discussion proposes examining how alternative principles entail other positions and correspond to branches of law, including different paths for textualists and advocates of analogy. That is a programme of comparative dependence, not a warrant for selecting attractive answers independently. Al-Āmidī's discussion of a third opinion supplies another useful constraint: on his selected account, a new distinction must not destroy a proposition genuinely shared by the antecedent positions. His account is itself contested, and he does not authorize unrestricted combination. [M01; M02]

Definitions used throughout

Thubūt concerns whether a report is established; dalāla concerns what it indicates. A reliable transmission may contain an ambiguous expression. ʿIlla means an operative legal ground supported for purposes of extension; ḥikma names a wisdom or benefit that may help explain an institution without defining its legally sufficient conditions. Taḥqīq al-manāṭ investigates whether the relevant condition occurs in a particular case. Ṣiḥḥa concerns legal validity; imtithāl concerns compliance; ijzāʾ concerns discharge of an obligation; ḍamān concerns liability. The exact relation between these concepts can itself be disputed, so the register records the substantive proposition instead of treating terminology as an answer.

“Independent warrant” below does not mean that every consequence needs its own separate verse. Linguistic entailment, a proved dependency, supported analogy, and an applicable general rule can supply warrant. It means that the inference must be stated and defended. This distinction prevents the constitution from becoming a disguised rejection of reasoning beyond literal wording.

The interpretive constitution

C01. State the authority of the evidence before combining doctrines

The Qurʾān and critically established Prophetic Sunna are binding sources within this prototype. Distinguish certainty of transmission from certainty of interpretation. A claim of ijmāʿ must identify its exact proposition, relevant period and participants, evidence of agreement, and surviving dissent. Agreement among the four later Sunni schools is not automatically identical with universal early agreement. Reported silence is weaker than positively attested agreement unless a defensible account of expected objection supports the inference. When two selected rules meet within one act or transaction, assess the complete combination, including an alleged shared judgment of invalidity despite different juristic reasons. Individual precedents and the label of independent derivation do not settle that assessment. [M02; M11]

A Companion's established judgment has substantial interpretive and historical weight. A disputed Companion judgment is not independently conclusive here; its legal relevance, reason, rival evidence, and reception must be evaluated. It can nevertheless supply sufficient defeasible warrant on a balance of reasons, including for an exception. A Companion's report about what occurred in a Prophetic incident must also be distinguished from that Companion's own subsequent legal judgment; ambiguous attribution must not silently become direct Prophetic speech. These choices have real costs in the divorce cases. They cannot be covertly reversed whenever a Companion supports a preferred answer. The first prototype works within these common Sunni source assumptions. Jaʿfarī, Zaydī, Ibāḍī, and Ismāʿīlī materials can expose alternatives, but their different authority premises must be argued before importing conclusions that depend on them. [M02; B18; B21]

C02. Let the precision of the argument match the precision of transmission

Before deriving a rule from one word, assemble the relevant report family: routes, companion witnesses, event identity, wording differences, additions, omissions, and early criticism. Several later chains through one source do not automatically provide several independent witnesses. A grading label on an entire report does not by itself decide which competing wording is original.

Where rival reliable wordings describe one event, an argument uniquely dependent on one wording needs a reason to prefer it. Agreement on the event's legal core can remain stronger than confidence in a particular formula. Compatible additions remain usable after ordinary transmission criticism; this article does not reduce every report to the intersection of all its versions. Nor does it permit treating a composite concession as if every component were independently optional. The proposed novelty is to make these limits a mandatory step in legal derivation, rather than an occasional observation in commentary. The marriage-formula variants and Ibn Daqīq al-ʿĪd's discussion, preserved in Fatḥ al-bārī, provide a direct classical anchor. [M03; B02; B18]

C03. Identify the speaker's capacity, then assign the force of the demand

Ask whether the utterance conveys general religious instruction, adjudicates particular evidence, exercises public authority, advises on a worldly matter, intercedes, or reports a personal practice. Context must establish a departure from general religious instruction; merely disliking a consequence is not evidence of a different capacity. Al-Qarāfī treats religious communication as the predominant category in doubtful cases while distinguishing judicial and executive actions. [M04]

Once a command is established as legislative, obligation is the rebuttable default of this constitution. Context or stronger evidence may establish recommendation or permission instead. Remedial purpose is a separate classification: a direction to repair an earlier act can itself be obligatory, as the proposed menstrual-divorce decision illustrates. An obligation to do something does not, without more, establish immediate performance, indefinite repetition, exclusive means, or nullity on breach. Barīra's explicit question about whether the Prophet was commanding or interceding gives unusually clear primary evidence that speech capacity matters. It does not license classifying every unwelcome command as advice. [H01; B18]

C04. Prefer evidenced context to invented explanations

Collect reports before declaring conflict. Give weight to contextual indications that actually shape the utterance's meaning. A specific occasion alone does not automatically narrow otherwise general language. An authenticated explanation of a temporary restriction is stronger evidence of its operative context than a modern conjecture about its purpose. Ibn Daqīq al-ʿĪd makes the distinction between meaningful contextual indications and merely occurring on a particular occasion especially clear in the travel-fasting discussion. [M05]

Reconciliation must preserve a plausible meaning of both proofs. It may not be purchased by inventing two occasions, an unreported chronology, or a hidden exception. Abrogation requires adequate chronology and a genuine conflict that cannot be plausibly reconciled. Where a superior transmission or interpretation emerges, prefer it with reasons. If a material conflict remains, state the residual uncertainty and use the appropriate legal default; do not announce that the reports “cancel out” as if their authority and scope had never mattered. The sacrificial-meat report provides a useful positive example of an expressed circumstance affecting a restriction. [H02]

C05. Keep initiation, continuation, completion, timing, and remedy distinct

A command addressed to somebody already engaged in an act ordinarily governs that stage. Moving from “complete X” to “everyone must begin X,” or from “perform X” to “repeat X after every error,” requires an additional inference. That inference can be sound, but must be shown. Likewise, the bare imperative does not settle whether an obligation must be performed at the first conceivable moment.

Time is part of the legally described situation. A recurring annual opportunity separated by months of impossibility differs from one continuous prayer interval; foreseeable loss of capacity can matter where the duty is already established. This article therefore rejects both automatic immediacy from grammar alone and automatic permission to postpone a serious established duty indefinitely. In this casebook, B17 provisionally selects initial ʿumra obligation on independent evidence while limiting what the completion verse itself proves. B16 separately favors the first realistically available hajj season through specific timing reports and a stated restriction of their binding force to an already owed first pilgrimage. Continuing to owe a duty does not itself prove that its postponement is forbidden. [B16; B17]

C06. Distinguish a demonstrated form from the minimum valid performance

Establish what an instruction positively requires using language, context, explanatory practice, and other proofs together. A complete Prophetic performance demonstrates a sound form; it does not automatically prove that every detail is a condition of validity. Conversely, calling something “only a demonstration” cannot remove a detail that the surrounding evidence makes obligatory.

An added numerical minimum, exclusive formula, or invalidating condition requires a defensible basis. A context in which a person urgently needs complete instruction gives omitted indispensable conditions evidential significance: the proposed presumption of sufficient instruction applies only where completeness was actually expected, the relevant report is adequately preserved, and another reliable proof does not supply the condition. Narrator abbreviation and selective questions can defeat the presumption. The dower discussion is a particularly direct classical test. Head-wiping adds the corrective rule that a demonstrated composite concession must remain composite unless severability is independently established. [B02; B13/B23]

C07. Separate the operative fact, its evidence, and a constitutive procedure

Every rule dependent on a sign should identify three things: the fact that matters, the standard by which it is established, and whether the named procedure merely proves the fact or legally constitutes a status. New information can replace an evidential proxy where the proof shows that reliable knowledge is what matters. It cannot automatically replace a prescribed constitutive act or a specifically mandated fallback.

Facts used in legal reasoning must remain open to appropriate investigation. Financial competence cannot be reduced to an assumed period of marriage when competence can be demonstrated independently. An official declaration may nevertheless have separately justified effects for publicity, third-party reliance, and dispute resolution. The article thus favors evidence-sensitive recognition of maturity without pretending that every registration requirement is meaningless. Sound and smell in the ablution-doubt report illustrate evidence of an event; moon-sighting together with the thirty-day fallback supplies a counterexample to universal substitution. [T01; T03]

C08. Authorized inquiry can discharge a duty only where its legal role is proved

Separate factual correctness, reasonable inquiry, moral excuse, validity, and remaining remedy. If the law authorizes a fallible procedure as the relevant standard of performance in a setting, its competent use can discharge the duty despite a later discovery of error. But being excused does not itself establish discharge: a requirement to repeat may remain, and a third party's loss may still require compensation.

Record what inquiry was realistically available, why it was adequate, when contrary knowledge arose, and whether the remedy would be repetition within time or performance after time. Correct ongoing conduct once reliable contrary information becomes available. The qibla decision adopts a bounded no-obligatory-repetition position after competent inquiry, with a weaker evidential bridge explicitly recorded. The physician countertest prevents generalizing that conclusion to all consequences of reasonable error. Judicial procedure likewise cannot turn another person's known property into one's own rightful possession. [B25; T04; H03]

C09. Establish effects and claimants separately, then preserve proved dependencies

For each transaction or status, record the basis of formation, conditions, prohibitions, persons holding rights, power to waive, authorized remedy, and downstream consequences. After formation has been established, a prohibition affecting a surrounding act does not by itself annihilate the completed institution. This is a rebuttable rule of inference, not a universal claim that prohibited contracts are valid. If the prohibition addresses an indispensable constituent, or a source specifies nullity, that evidence controls.

Do not invent a veto merely because an order of precedence exists. Do not invent an inheritance entitlement merely because a motive is reprehensible. Equally, do not reject a positively evidenced special inheritance entitlement because it leaves other effects of divorce intact. Selective effects can be legally real. The constitution must keep all dependencies it actually accepts: a counted revocable divorce, for example, carries the consequences of that status as well as any separately established corrective duty. The strongest objections to this article come from concrete transmitted exceptions, not an abstract preference for conceptual symmetry. [B03; B08; B18; B21]

C10. Extend rules through relevant meanings and real arrangements

Qiyās is admissible on preponderant evidence. State the original rule, proposed operative feature, evidence of its relevance, its occurrence in the new case, and the strongest rival explanation. Superficial verbal resemblance is insufficient. Several independent considerations can strengthen an extension even when no single universally accepted cause exhausts the field. The same analysis must identify counterexamples and limits; disagreement does not by itself suspend analogy.

Analyse a coordinated arrangement as a whole when evidence establishes that its parts implement one legally relevant relationship or purpose. The official-gift report gives an unusually strong reason to examine whether a benefit arose because of entrusted office; routing it through an intermediary need not change that relationship. An ordinary independent gift does not become illicit merely because one party happens to hold an office. Similarly, blocking evasion may justify a properly grounded response, but does not determine its recipient, amount, or duration without further law. In the ribā dossier, supported extension reaches clear food analogues while broader contested boundaries remain research questions. [T02; B21; B22]

C11. Treat reception and legal defaults as reasoned evidence

Early public practice can bear on scope, observance, and expected transmission. Its weight depends on who witnessed what, how widely the rule should have been known, what contrary evidence survives, and whether a local practice claims a privileged status. The prototype gives reception defeasible evidential weight, not an automatic veto over a reliable individual report. Al-Layth's criticism of an undifferentiated appeal to Medinan authority and the Ḥanafī discussion of widespread need belong in this inquiry; neither can be compressed into “practice beats hadith.” [M06; M07]

State the default that actually fits the case. A new devotional obligation needs warrant; an established obligation is not discharged by mere doubt; an established person's property is not reassigned by conjecture; a formed transaction does not disappear from uncertainty about an additional restriction. These defaults can conflict, so first decide which positive conditions and effects have been established. The burden may change as proof accumulates. No universal “choose ease,” “always be cautious,” or “always preserve validity” principle governs this constitution.

C12. Keep formation accountable to law and keep the constitution revisable

Formation concerns what living and applying the law does to attention, appetite, fairness, trust, courage, compassion, and institutions. These effects should be investigated, especially where a rule claims a benefit that may or may not occur. They do not automatically create a legal cause, invalidate an act, or authorize a private revelation to bind other people. The Qurʾān's links between fasting and taqwā, prayer and restraint from wrongdoing, and charity and purification supply a scriptural basis for studying formation as an effect. [Q02; Q03; Q04]

When consequences legitimately enter legal reasoning, distinguish an established textual purpose, a plausible explanatory wisdom, a measurable causal claim, and a particular adjudicative remedy. Al-Shāṭibī's attention to consequences and his warning that intelligible transactions can retain irreducible prescribed conditions are both necessary. A formation-based interpretation that simply removes the condition it finds inconvenient fails this constitution. [M08; M09]

Every amendment must be reapplied to earlier cases. Record what result would count against it. Maintain the rival view and the evidence that could reverse the provisional conclusion. A principle that survives only by acquiring a new unmotivated exception at each difficult case should be revised or abandoned. This discipline, rather than the number of unfamiliar verdicts, determines whether the project becomes a coherent new orientation.

How these articles decide a case

First specify the question and factual assumptions. Next assemble the report family and relevant passages, identify their authority and scope, and state the exact contested effect. Write at least two serious derivations and identify the earliest point where their premises diverge. Apply the articles, including their exceptions; do not count article names as independent evidence. Adopt a provisional conclusion with its confidence and the strongest reason against it. Finally, test a nearby case where the same principle becomes inconvenient and record what would reverse the conclusion.

Confidence here is an editorial assessment of the strength and completeness of the first-pass argument, not a numerical probability that a divine judgment has been recovered. “High” means the narrow conclusion has strong direct support and survives the principal objection examined. “Medium” marks a consequential interpretive or analogy choice. “Low” marks a fragile source-authority or evidential bridge. Even high-confidence entries remain bounded by their stated facts and the limits of textual collation.

What is proposed as new

Five features merit sustained development: making legal-effect accounting compulsory in every dossier; adjusting the precision of legal inference to the stability of report wording; distinguishing facts, evidential signs, and constitutive procedures before modern substitutions; admitting cumulative but bounded analogy without demanding one undisputed universal cause; and requiring every principle to survive a documented countercase and a revision across previous decisions. Each has recognizable antecedents. The research claim is that their integrated use might define a distinctive school of reasoning. Historical priority has not been established.

B02 - How much of the head must be wiped?

Provisional conclusion - existing ruling, newly organized rationale; medium confidence. A real wet wiping of some scalp, or of hair situated within the head’s boundary, satisfies the minimum. No quarter, third, number of fingers, or number of hairs is imposed. Wiping across the whole head remains the strongly evidenced standard performance. Someone who wipes a substantial front portion of an uncovered head has not, on this provisional rule, omitted a necessary quantitative condition. This conclusion does not make an unrelated touch, dry contact, or water landing on the head automatically equivalent to wiping.

The dispute and its evidence. Ibn Rushd, Bidāyat al-mujtahid, Book of Purification, wudu, second chapter, sixth question of delimitation, records Mālik’s whole-head requirement, partial-wiping positions, Abū Ḥanīfa’s quarter, and al-Shāfiʿī’s absence of a specified fraction. His grammatical discussion identifies an interpretative dispute; it does not license treating one account of the Qurʾānic bāʾ as a conclusive calculation. Qurʾān 5:6 establishes head-wiping. The contested proposition is its minimum extent. [F_BID_WIPE; F_Q5_6]

The positive demonstration must receive substantial weight. In Bukhārī 185, ʿAbd Allāh b. Zayd demonstrates the Prophet’s ablution: hands move from the front of the head to the nape and return. This supports teaching a full sweep, rather than constructing ordinary practice around the smallest imaginable contact. Muslim 274h reports wiping the forelock and the turban, alongside the footwear. The turban is a stable component of that report. Removing it from the description and presenting the remainder as a demonstrated bareheaded minimum would misrepresent the evidence. [F_B185; F_M274H]

The charter’s reasoning. Three propositions need distinct warrants: head-wiping is obligatory; the full sweep is a demonstrated sufficient and commendable performance; every smaller wiping is invalid. The first two do not logically entail the third. Al-Shāfiʿī’s own al-Umm, “Bāb masḥ al-raʾs,” explicitly recognizes partial wiping while preferring the full movement. This supplies a historical legal reading of the command, not merely a later grammatical slogan. The present proposal accepts its distinction between minimum and preferred performance, while declining to make its abbreviated or mursal reports independent proof that a turban may be silently omitted from a composite concession. [F_UMM_WIPE]

The inference is deliberately defeasible. A demonstrated action can explain the constitutive content of a command. However, ablution demonstrations also include differing repetitions: Bukhārī 185 describes two and three washings, while Bukhārī 157 records washing once. Consequently, demonstration must be analyzed feature by feature. This comparison does not itself prove that whole-head coverage is optional; it defeats the blanket rule that every exhibited detail is a validity condition. For extent, the deciding consideration is the reasonable partial-wiping reading of the command combined with insufficient evidence, in this first pass, for a universal quantitative boundary. [F_B185; F_B157]

Strongest opponent. The whole-head position can argue that an ordinary command to wipe an identified body part includes that part, and that the consistent uncovered-head demonstration supplies the intended extent. The forelock-and-turban example may satisfy comprehensive coverage through an authorized substitute. On that account, requiring the whole head is explanation of the original duty, not addition of a new condition. This is a serious argument. Our provisional preference depends on judging the command intelligible without an exhaustive quantitative specification; it is not entailed merely by calling the opponent’s requirement “additional.”

Limits, tests, and reversal conditions. The decision should not introduce a disguised minimum such as “one palm” while claiming to reject fixed minima. Borderline contacts require ordinary linguistic and physical assessment. Turban-only wiping, displaced hair, disability adaptations, and whether washing can substitute for wiping remain separate questions. A well-established legislative explanation requiring comprehensive uncovered-head coverage, or stronger transmission and early-reception evidence showing that partial wiping was consistently treated as insufficient, would reopen this result. An alternative demonstration must be compared in its complete circumstances. The same discipline must preserve stable details that support stricter conclusions elsewhere.

Constitution amendment proposed: separate an obligatory act, a demonstrated sufficient form, and a minimum validity condition; treat explanatory Prophetic action as genuine evidence, and preserve every stable component when evaluating a composite concession.

B03 - Proposing over a prior proposal

Original dispute and operative evidence

Ibn Rushd reports an established prohibition on proposing over another's proposal, followed by disagreement about an ensuing marriage: Dāwūd would dissolve it; al-Shāfiʿī and Abū Ḥanīfa would uphold it; Mālik has both reports and a third position distinguishing consummation. Ibn al-Qāsim allows a righteous second suitor to intervene against an unrighteous first one. The prohibition's ordinary trigger is also disputed: Ibn Rushd presents the majority's restriction to a first proposal that has reached mutual inclination or acceptance. A merely unanswered approach does not necessarily exclude other candidates. [REL01]

Al-Bukhārī 5142 prohibits the second proposal until the first suitor abandons his proposal or permits another. This supplies express ways for the restriction to end. Muslim 1480a reports that Fāṭima bint Qays received proposals from Muʿāwiya and Abū Jahm and, after seeking advice, married Usāma on the Prophet's recommendation. That account supports distinguishing multiple unaccepted approaches from interference with an accepted proposal: it does not tell us that she had already accepted one of the two. Al-Bukhārī 5136 independently requires the woman's consultation or permission for marriage. [REL02-REL04]

Competing premise

The decisive disagreement is whether the prohibition makes the later marriage an unauthorized legal act, or regulates the suitor's conduct during an antecedent stage. This is not settled by calling the first engagement a “right.” A right against interference might carry a duty to desist without carrying ownership of the woman's future marriage, an entitlement to force her original choice, or a power to dissolve a later marriage. Each of those would be a distinct legal proposition. Similarly, the annulment position can be strong only if it connects the prohibited interference to the formation or legally necessary legitimacy of the second marriage.

Candidate verdict and reasons

Provisional verdict: knowingly interfering with an accepted, still-pending proposal is prohibited; a subsequent marriage satisfying its independent formation requirements remains valid. The first suitor's abandonment or permission ends the particular obstacle described in al-Bukhārī. A woman's genuine rejection ends the premise of a still-accepted first proposal; this is our inference from the restricted trigger and her independently established consent, rather than an express additional clause of hadith 5142.

The two normative relations are different: the second suitor owes a duty concerning interference; the spouses form a marriage through its requisite procedure and consent. The cited prohibition establishes the first duty. It does not expressly vest an annulment power in the earlier suitor or state that a marriage later concluded is void. The case record supplies no stronger bridge sufficient to defeat the otherwise established marriage. This preference agrees in outcome with established jurists. It does not turn an unlawful act into an approved one: desisting before marriage, responsibility for deliberate wrongdoing, and the marriage's legal existence are separate conclusions.

Consequences and counterexamples

After a valid marriage, ordinary marital effects follow; the first suitor cannot withhold their recognition as a bargaining device. No compensation for a “lost spouse” is created. If someone incurred a separately actionable loss through proven deception, restitution or damages would require that independent cause and its own measure; the frustrated expectation of marriage does not establish it. Nor does the classification itself authorize a particular disciplinary penalty. This distinction is the proposed rule's main practical contribution: a duty's existence does not identify every claimant or remedy.

The rule cannot validate a marriage lacking a separately established formation condition. If the later ceremony lacks legally required consent, witnesses, capacity, or another condition adopted elsewhere in the charter, the answer must follow that defect and its dependencies. Conversely, ignorance of the first proposal can change culpability without supplying a new reason to dissolve a marriage. The protection also cannot lock the woman into the first proposal: the prohibition addresses competitive interference, and her consent remains independently necessary.

We do not provisionally adopt Ibn al-Qāsim's righteousness exception as a general permission to undercut. Advising someone truthfully about a real concern and making a self-interested competing proposal are different acts. The Fāṭima report supplies an example of relevant advice; it does not establish that any suitor may certify himself morally superior and thereby cancel the prohibition. A properly bounded exception would need evidence about the legally relevant disqualification and the necessity of intervention.

Result and remaining research

Status: existing substantive view, newly systematized rationale; moderate confidence. Further work should collate the acceptance threshold in early school texts, identify precisely which Mālikī report turns on consummation and why, and test Dāwūd's own argument rather than rely solely on Ibn Rushd's attribution. The verdict would change if the act's express invalidation, a demonstrated shared early rule, or a convincing formation-based derivation were established. Merely redescribing the interference as harmful would not answer that missing question.

B05 - Is intention of ritual purification necessary for valid wudu?

Provisional conclusion - existing Ḥanafī/Thawrī result, revised rationale; medium-to-low confidence. If a person actually completes the independently required washing and wiping, using qualifying water and satisfying the other established conditions, the resulting readiness for prayer does not require an intention of ritual purification or devotional obedience. Intention remains necessary for claiming intentional obedience and its devotional credit. For a concrete test, someone who deliberately performs all the required acts solely to cool down may subsequently pray, provided no nullifier intervenes. Mere accidental wetting or an ordinary bath is not evidence that those acts and conditions were fulfilled.

The dispute. Bidāya, Book of Purification, wudu, first question of conditions, attributes the intention requirement to Mālik, al-Shāfiʿī, Aḥmad, Abū Thawr, and Dāwūd; it attributes the contrary result to Abū Ḥanīfa and Sufyān al-Thawrī. Ibn Rushd locates the dispute in ablution’s combination of worship and intelligible purification. That historical explanation is a starting point, not sufficient proof that physical cleaning and ritual readiness are identical. [F_BID_INT]

The strongest textual objection. Bukhārī 1 restricts actions to intentions and each person to what they intend, illustrating this through migration for different ends. Its Arabic does not contain an explicit noun meaning “reward”; the English translation’s insertion of that word cannot settle the legal dispute. The opposing interpretation takes the statement to condition an act’s religious legal standing. Since wudu is a prescribed preparation for prayer, completing its bodily motions without intending purification would then fail to constitute the required act. This objection relies on a general Prophetic statement, not merely analogy with prayer. [F_B1]

Why the proposed result nevertheless remains possible. The charter distinguishes the agent’s obedience from a legally specified result. A command may direct someone to produce a condition for another duty without making the mental purpose of producing it a constitutive part of that condition. Qurʾān 5:6 identifies bodily acts when prayer is intended; it does not expressly state that the readiness produced by those acts vanishes when their motive differs. The migration example distinguishes the significance of an action according to its aim. It does not expressly define the validity of every ancillary legal effect of every action. Reading these sources together, we provisionally find stronger evidence for intention’s effect on attribution and devotional value than for its negation of ablution’s preparatory effect. [F_Q5_6; F_B1]

This severability has a direct classical analogue. Al-Sarakhsī, al-Mabsūṭ 1:72-73, “Bāb al-wuḍūʾ wa-l-ghusl,” distinguishes worship obtained through intention from removal of the impediment to prayer. He also argues that adding intention to the Qurʾānic prescription cannot be established by an isolated report or analogy. We adopt the first distinction, subject to the present evidentiary audit, without adopting that blanket exclusion of solitary reports: the charter accepts critically established Sunna as legally productive. Nor do we adopt “water purifies by itself” as sufficient proof about a juridical state. The relevant issue is what the sources make constitutive of that state. [F_MABSUT_INT]

An additional opponent worth preserving. Al-Kawsaj’s Masāʾil, report 118, transmits Isḥāq’s explanation that undertaking ablution and prayer requires intention, connecting this to the Qurʾānic direction to rise for prayer. Here a jurist treats purpose as inherent in the legislated act, rather than an externally added condition. That interpretation may defeat ours if a broader corpus establishes it more convincingly. The printed apparatus also reports a variant affecting part of this wording; it should not be ignored when making exact verbal claims. [F_MASAIL118]

Consequences and reversal conditions. A spoken formula, uninterrupted introspection, and exact mental timing do not follow automatically even from the opposing view. The cooling example must satisfy B02’s actual wiping requirement and every other independently established physical condition; this dossier does not validate a head that merely became wet. Tayammum, prayer, fasting, and mixed intentions require their own analyses. An authenticated, properly scoped direction to repeat completed ablution specifically because devotional intention was absent would reverse this ruling. So would a sufficiently strong combined demonstration that “wudu” in the relevant legislative texts inherently denotes purification undertaken with that purpose. No repetition is demanded merely because the person cannot later remember a distinct mental formula.

B08 - A remoter guardian contracts while the nearer guardian is present

Original dispute and scope

Ibn Rushd reports three Mālikī outcomes: the marriage stands; it is dissolved; or the nearer guardian can approve or reject it. He expressly excludes two cases from this variation: the father present in relation to his virgin daughter, and an executor present in relation to the ward entrusted to him; he reports a consistent dissolution position for those within Mālik's account. He also reports al-Shāfiʿī's stronger paternal priority. These exceptions must not disappear when extracting a general principle. [REL05]

This dossier therefore decides a narrower case: a consenting adult woman; a legally eligible remoter guardian; an otherwise properly formed marriage; no present father or specially empowered executor; no proved coercion, disqualification, or independent restriction on the match. Eligibility is a stipulated fact here and must be independently justified when applying the decision; it cannot be defined circularly as “whoever performed this marriage.” The full general-guardianship controversy and ranking of relatives remain separate research questions; B15 is unassessed.

Text and competing premise

Abū Dāwūd 2085 transmits the requirement of a guardian. Abū Dāwūd 2083 transmits ʿĀʾisha's report declaring a marriage without the guardians' permission void, while expressly awarding dower after consummation and assigning authority to the ruler where guardianship fails through dispute. The displayed catalogue grades both reports ṣaḥīḥ according to al-Albānī; that is a named modern grading, not a declaration that earlier jurists unanimously accepted every application. Neither wording expressly sets out the complete order of family proximity. [REL06-REL07]

Ibn Rushd isolates three premises. If priority has not been established as a revealed requirement, the remoter guardian's act stands. If priority is a private right of the nearer guardian, ratification becomes intelligible. If it is a nonwaivable divine requirement, the act is unformed. This is a diagnostic map of the dispute, not proof that any one of these classifications applies. In particular, the ratification result still needs evidence that the nearer person holds a power to cure this particular defect. [REL05]

Candidate verdict and reasons

Provisional verdict for the stated ordinary case: the marriage stands, and proximity alone does not give the nearer relative a veto or a power of retrospective ratification. This dossier provisionally accepts a guardian requirement for its stipulated class on REL06-REL07; the present case supplies such a guardian. This local premise does not settle the full B15 controversy. Moving from “a guardian is necessary” to “only this nearest guardian can form the marriage” adds an exclusion. The inspected evidence has not established that exclusion for this nonexceptional case. The woman’s actual consent is independently required; al-Bukhārī 5136 prevents a guardian's participation from substituting for it. [REL04, REL06-REL07]

This is an evidential preference, not the claim that family order can never be legally significant. A demonstrated assignment of exclusive authority would change the ordinary formation requirement itself. Equally, a demonstrated private priority could make a particular defect curable if its holder possesses a ratification power. We would then follow that evidence. The proposed procedure demands proof of the complete relation - holder, protected act, power, and legal effect - rather than letting the label “private right” supply missing incidents automatically.

Consequences and dependency tests

Because the stipulated ordinary case is valid, ordinary marital consequences follow without awaiting the nearer relative's signature. The mere hurt of being bypassed establishes neither damages nor annulment. Family consultation can be valuable without supplying a specific legal remedy. If the remoter person is actually disqualified, or if an independently established exclusive authority was bypassed, this verdict does not apply: the formation analysis must be redone instead of saving the outcome through sympathetic consent alone.

The ʿĀʾisha report provides a decisive check against an overly simple rule that “invalid means no effects.” It expressly combines invalidity with dower after consummation. The dower has its stated independent basis in the consummation; it need not imply that the marriage was valid all along. Thus our method can uphold one consequence and deny another when the evidence makes that division. By the same reasoning, dower alone would not prove inheritance, unrestricted continued cohabitation, or a power of private ratification. [REL07]

An eligible but obstructive nearer guardian presents another test. One cannot assume that any relative may simply replace him: the report's reference to the ruler supplies a reason to investigate an adjudicative route. Nor can a guardian exploit the charter's rejection of an unproved veto to erase the woman's consent. A guardian's authority and the woman's permission are distinct necessary questions, and neither should be silently inferred from the other.

Result and remaining research

Status: existing permissive outcome with a proposed rule about proving the incidents of authority; moderate confidence within the express scope. Remaining work is concentrated: compare early Mālikī texts on the three reports; investigate actual proofs for the order of guardians; examine al-Shāfiʿī's paternal argument and the executor exception; and audit the guardian reports' transmission and rival interpretation. The present decision does not suspend the ordinary case, but it does not pretend to have settled the excluded cases. A source establishing exclusive proximity in this very class would overturn it.

B09 - A traveller’s Ramadan fast: concession, alternative time, and validity

1. Historical dispute and precise question

Ibn Rushd reports a majority view accepting a traveller’s completed fast and an opposing view attributed to the Ẓāhirīs requiring other days. The interpretive issue is whether Q 2:184’s makeup clause includes the understood condition that the traveller actually did not fast. Reports support the competing readings. See Bidāya, Book of Fasting, second division, discussion of travellers, first and second questions, opening “أما المسألة الأولى … هل يجزيه صومه”.

Darūrī §58 additionally distinguishes al-Karkhī’s reported position: the duty attaches to later days, yet present fasting can discharge it. Thus “the ordinary performance time is displaced” and “present performance is invalid” are separate claims. The underlying Arabic is inspected at Darūrī, p. 59, §58. B09’s association with other semantic sections remains an editorial parallel.

2. Evidence that controls the reading

Q 2:184 provides the other-days clause. Bukhārī 1943, Book 30, hadith 50, reports an express choice between fasting and not fasting during travel. Muslim 1121e, Book 13, hadith 135, names the concession and approves taking it, while permitting the capable traveller to fast. Bukhārī 1947, Book 30, hadith 54, records travelling Companions’ mutual acceptance of both practices.

The adverse evidence has actual context. In Bukhārī 1946, Book 30, hadith 53, the saying denying the righteousness of travel fasting concerns a man being shaded. In Muslim 1114a-b, Book 13, hadiths 116-117, the condemnation follows the Prophet’s public breaking of the fast; the accompanying version expressly reports the people’s difficulty and their watching his conduct. These contexts are transmitted evidence, not an invented explanation that every adverse report concerned extreme danger.

3. Methodological fork

A surface reading can attach makeup directly to traveller status. The second route understands makeup as the substitute for a fast not performed, using the permission reports to establish the omitted condition. The al-Karkhī alternative shows a third possible architecture: substituted obligation plus authorized early discharge. Our choice must explain why its reconstruction is needed and how little it adds.

Nor should the statement about righteousness be silently converted into a declaration of ritual invalidity. An assessment of the wisdom or virtue of fasting, an instruction to break it in particular circumstances, and the legal sufficiency of a completed fast are different propositions.

4. Candidate principle and provisional conclusion

Candidate principle: when reliable explanatory speech and acknowledged practice establish an authorized alternative, they can determine the scope of an apparently unconditional remedial clause. Supply only the condition that the evidence supports. Before treating another report as abolishing that authorization, establish that it governs the same legally relevant circumstances and supplies an adequate basis for supersession.

Working ruling: for an otherwise eligible person on a legally qualifying journey, a Ramadan fast completed with its required intention and other conditions is valid and discharges that day; travel alone creates no additional makeup obligation. The traveller may instead use the concession and make up the missed day. The completed fast’s validity does not depend on establishing that it was the more virtuous choice.

The reason for choosing validity is the positive authorization of fasting. It is not merely an absence of blame. The proposed reading consequently explains why makeup arises when the alternative of not fasting is used, without turning permission into an obligation to fast.

5. Scope, failure tests, and classification

A person cannot manufacture this permission simply by calling a short outing “travel”; the qualifying journey requires its own determination. Equally, an easier mode of transport does not automatically erase a concession attached to a legally established travel category. Hardship can affect how a concession should be used without being the only condition for its existence.

The case does not decide the validity of a fast undertaken in defiance of an independently established duty to avoid serious harm or obey a particular binding instruction. Darūrī §58 itself distinguishes dangerous illness as a further question. That separation must not become permission to ignore demonstrated danger.

Classification: the principal result is an existing juristic position. The candidate contribution is a mandatory ordering of scope, contextual reconciliation, and the distinction between merit and validity. The ellipsis and concession analysis are not new discoveries.

6. Residual evidence tasks

Collate the Ramadan-specific reports against reports of frequent or voluntary fasting; complete the chronology analysis advanced for abrogation; and separately specify travel thresholds, departure-day rules, and severe-harm cases. Compare al-Karkhī’s account in a direct Ḥanafī source before treating Ibn Rushd’s compressed report as its exhaustive statement.

B13/B23 - Is there a fixed minimum dower?

Provisional conclusion - existing ruling with a strengthened general rationale; high confidence on the narrow question. No universal numerical minimum is imposed on an otherwise lawful, specified dower. An agreed item of recognized value is not disqualified merely because it falls below a quarter dinar or ten dirhams. This rejects those fixed floors; it does not erase the woman’s entitlement, validate coercion, make every purported object lawful, or decide all questions about services as dower. Naming an amount, the existence of an entitlement, its enforceability, and any remedy for a defect are separate propositions.

The map in the primary source. Bidāya, Book of Marriage, chapter on the conditions of the contract, “al-faṣl al-thālith fī al-ṣadāq,” first subdivision, second question “fī qadrihi,” records a quarter dinar/three dirhams for Mālik and ten dirhams for Abū Ḥanīfa, against no fixed minimum for al-Shāfiʿī, Aḥmad, Isḥāq, Abū Thawr, and others. The title and question are better locators than volume numbering, which varies between editions; the inspected display has pp. 407-408. [F_BID_DOWER]

The decisive case. In the Sahl b. Saʿd report, the Prophet asks the prospective husband about something he can provide, including an iron ring, and eventually concludes the marriage with reference to his knowledge of the Qurʾān. The details concerning the final benefit admit further legal discussion, but the search for even an iron ring directly addresses the amount at a moment when a minimum would matter. A hidden universal floor requires evidence; its omission is evidentially significant here because this is an actual negotiation under Prophetic direction, not a narrator’s incidental silence about an unrelated legal matter. [F_B5132]

The stricter analogy test. The strongest minimum argument first classifies the dower as a religiously required institution rather than an entirely optional exchange. It then argues for a prescribed threshold and uses the theft threshold to identify the amount. Ibn Rushd distinguishes those stages. He challenges the move from “religious duty” to “numerically fixed duty,” and criticizes the shared description of access to a body part for conflating very different legal relations. A punishment and a marriage do not acquire the same operative ground merely because similar vocabulary describes them. The defect is not that analogy can never identify a quantity; it is that the proposed connecting reason has not been shown to explain both rulings. [F_BID_DOWER]

Under the charter, the opponent must show why the feature that fixes the theft threshold also fixes the dower. Neither general solemnity nor the importance of marriage supplies that warrant. Once the numerical inference fails, the ordinary command to give the woman her dower survives intact. Qurʾān 4:4 locates the entitlement with women and recognizes voluntary remission. Qurʾān 2:236 contemplates divorce before an amount has been settled, so failure to name an amount cannot simply be equated with the absence of a marriage. These distinctions support protecting the entitlement while rejecting an unsupported floor. [F_Q4_4; F_Q2_236]

Opponent, transmission control, and historical checks. A defender may regard the iron-ring episode as an exceptional concession, or introduce a report expressly requiring ten dirhams. Both would matter if securely established and correctly scoped; neither may be asserted to rescue the preferred analogy. Ibn Rushd judges the ten-dirham report weak. The sandals report, Tirmidhī 1113, displays al-Tirmidhī’s favorable assessment and a later weak grading; we therefore do not make it the necessary premise. Ibn al-Mundhir independently gives the no-fixed-minimum position and explicitly demands evidence before imposing limits on divine obligations. [F_BID_DOWER; F_T1113; F_AWSAT_DOWER]

Practical boundary and outstanding work. A low dower and no dower are not identical, and a low agreed dower cannot justify withholding what was actually promised. An unlawful or indeterminate item calls for a separate remedy analysis; the charter does not infer marriage invalidity automatically. Assessing free consent, rights of a person with limited legal capacity, valuation of a particular item, customary dower where no amount was named, and the precise status of promised teaching remain distinct questions. A sound legislative minimum would change this result. A preference for larger dowers, or evidence that they were usual, would not by itself establish a validity threshold. This dossier supplies a reusable rule against importing quantities through a merely verbal resemblance.

B16 - Hajj timing: the first genuinely feasible season

1. Historical dispute and exact issue

The dispute concerns immediate performance or permitted postponement. Ibn Rushd contrasts analogies with the beginning and end of prayer time, the gap between pilgrimage seasons, and the danger of losing a later opportunity. He expressly rejects reducing this dispute to the bare imperative. See Bidāya, Book of Hajj, first inquiry, “وأما متى تجب”. Darūrī §201 likewise treats timing as dependent on indications beyond the form: p. 122.

The present question is when a person who already owes a first hajj and actually can undertake it must perform. It is not whether hajj occurs annually as a new personal obligation.

2. Primary evidence and its limits

Q 3:97 establishes the pilgrimage duty with the ability qualification. Muslim 1337, Book 15, hadith 461, distinguishes the command to perform hajj from an annually repeated requirement. Its continuation also links commanded performance to ability. Neither text alone identifies the first available year as an inflexible deadline.

A more specific timing report matters: Abū Dāwūd 1732, Book 11, hadith 12, directs someone intending hajj to hasten; the displayed grading attributes “ḥasan” to al-Albānī. Ibn Mājah 2883, Book 25, hadith 2, gives a related version mentioning possible illness, loss of transport, and intervening need; its displayed Darussalam grade is also “ḥasan”. These are attributed assessments, not a claim that their chains and every addition are undisputed.

The counterargument from the Prophet’s later performance requires attention. Bukhārī 1622, Book 25, hadith 107, establishes Abū Bakr’s leadership of the preceding pilgrimage and the public announcements then made. It does not itself establish the date when a binding first-hajj duty attached to the Prophet or prove why he did not personally lead that earlier pilgrimage. Supplying an unrecorded excuse would be methodologically improper.

3. Methodological fork

The competing prayer analogies select different features of time. The proposed method therefore first records whether the duty is one-off, whether performance windows are continuous, and what direct timing evidence exists. An isolated analogy does not get to choose the answer before that description is completed.

In this case, the specific instruction to hasten supplies the principal proposed bridge to promptness. The annual interruption and risk of losing ability explain its practical significance. Ordinary uncertainty about death is not being made a freestanding source of an immediate obligation.

4. Candidate principle and provisional conclusion

Candidate principle: for a duty confined to separated execution windows, determine urgency from authenticated timing directions and the structure of legally feasible opportunities. A general imperative establishes neither a license for indefinite postponement nor, by itself, the first-window deadline.

Working ruling: a person owing a first hajj should, as a legal duty under this draft, perform it in the first season in which the relevant ability and access conditions are actually satisfied. This is a provisional selection of prompt performance, supported by the presently accepted hastening reports read as legislative guidance about fulfilling the already established duty.

The conditional wording concerning someone who intends hajj is a material interpretive issue. Our present reconstruction treats it as addressing the person undertaking an existing duty, rather than enabling avoidance simply through refusing to intend compliance. That reconstruction remains preponderant, not certain. It does not turn an intention to undertake an additional voluntary pilgrimage into a vow.

5. Scope and counterexamples

“Feasible” is substantive. A theoretical financial balance does not, without further analysis, establish an available route, required access, adequate health, or the ability to meet competing enforceable responsibilities. Conversely, assuming that later arrangements will probably be easier does not automatically defeat a presently established duty.

As a proposed application, where timely administrative preparation is a necessary and available means of the first feasible pilgrimage, the preparation belongs in the ability assessment and practical performance plan. The rule does not change the ritual calendar or invent a universal financial or risk threshold.

Voluntary repeat hajj is the decisive counterexample to an unlimited reading of “whoever intends”. If the timing report necessarily makes every voluntary intention immediately binding, the chosen scope needs revision. Prayer within a continuously open window is another test: the hajj conclusion cannot simply be exported to forbid its otherwise permitted postponement.

Classification: first-feasible-season performance follows an existing juristic result. The candidate architecture makes the choice of temporal features and the independent timing warrant explicit; it is not a newly discovered obligation.

6. Residual evidence tasks

Audit the hastening reports’ chains and the causal addition separately; investigate the force of their conditional wording; establish the chronology and circumstances relevant to the Prophetic-delay argument; and compare the immediate-performance schools’ actual definitions of ability. If the specific timing evidence fails or proves advisory, this dossier must reopen promptness rather than preserve it by precaution alone.

B17 - Initial ʿumra and mandatory completion: the stage rule survives an obligatory result

1. Historical dispute and editorial qualification

Ibn Rushd reports both obligation and nonobligation of initial ʿumra. One dispute concerns whether Q 2:196’s completion command establishes initiation; another concerns the reports. See Bidāya, Book of Hajj, first inquiry, “وقد بقي … القول في حكم النسك الذي هو العمرة”. The online attribution to Abū Thawr is internally inconsistent and is not used here.

B17’s link to Darūrī §204 is editorial and illustrative: that section concerns makeup of timed worship. The adjacent §205 separately distinguishes commanded completion from sufficient discharge, using defective pilgrimage as its example: Darūrī, p. 123. Neither section should be cited as Ibn Rushd directly deriving ʿumra’s initial status through our proposed stage rule.

2. Evidence beyond the completion verse

Q 2:196 commands completion and includes provisions for obstruction. Its stage and scope must be interpreted; translating its opening as “complete” does not by itself settle whether it also directs initiation.

Independent reports prevent an easy conclusion of nonobligation. Ibn Mājah 2901, Book 25, hadith 20, reports ʿĀʾisha asking whether jihad is upon women and an answer describing hajj and ʿumra as their noncombatant jihad; its displayed Darussalam grade is “ṣaḥīḥ”. Abū Dāwūd 1810, Book 11, hadith 90, records the instruction to perform hajj and ʿumra for an elderly father; the displayed al-Albānī grade is “ṣaḥīḥ”.

There is real adverse evidence. Tirmidhī 931, Book 9, hadith 124, directly denies initial obligation. Tirmidhī’s preserved Arabic assessment calls it “ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ”, while the modern Darussalam panel marks it weak. Bidāya reports Ibn ʿAbd al-Barr’s objection to relying on al-Ḥajjāj b. Arṭāʾ’s isolated transmission. The grading conflict cannot be concealed by quoting only the preferred panel.

The wording also needs comparison: Bukhārī 1520, Book 25, hadith 8, reports ʿĀʾisha’s question through Ḥabīb b. Abī ʿAmra and ʿĀʾisha bt. Ṭalḥa, but gives an answer about excellent hajj without the same ʿumra wording. This is a prompt for variant analysis, not proof that the addition is either automatically sound or automatically erroneous.

3. The methodological fork

The completion verse can be read as governing an undertaking already begun or as encompassing full performance. Either reading must answer the independent reports. Conversely, accepting an initial obligation does not entitle the jurist to say that completion language necessarily proved it.

The instruction concerning the elderly father may clarify authorized proxy performance rather than independently announce a universal initial duty. Likewise, the female-jihad report requires a judgment about obligation versus comparative virtue. Our decision therefore relies on an explicit cumulative reading, not on the existence of an imperative anywhere in the evidence.

4. Candidate principle and provisional conclusion

Candidate principle: identify whether a command governs initiation, continuation, completion, or repair. Do not transfer a duty to another stage without a stated semantic, textual, or analogical bridge. This is a presumption against unexplained transitions, not a presumption that worship is optional.

Working ruling: on the present evidence, initial ʿumra is provisionally obligatory once for an otherwise qualified, capable adult, with further performances not required merely by this general obligation. The principal support is the presently accepted obligation reading of the ʿĀʾisha report, corroborated by the father’s report; the latter also supports considering male obligation rather than silently generalizing a report addressed to women. The nonobligation report’s transmission and interpretive standing remain a significant unresolved challenge.

Once a validly constituted ʿumra has begun, completion is required, subject to the law’s established release and obstruction provisions. These conclusions have distinct warrants. The completion rule alone is not our proof of the first duty; the initial duty alone is not a rule that every subsequent ʿumra becomes a fresh lifetime obligation.

5. Counterexamples and classification

Muslim 1154a, Book 13, hadith 220, records the Prophet eating after having begun a voluntary fast. Whatever further details different schools derive, this is concrete adverse evidence against transporting pilgrimage’s completion rule wholesale into every voluntary observance. The proposed system must investigate each activity’s specific transition.

Darūrī §205 supplies the opposite warning: an act can require completion yet fail to discharge the underlying duty because of an independently established defect. “Must finish” therefore entails neither “had to begin” nor “completion certainly suffices”.

Classification: the chosen initial obligation is an existing juristic position. Mandatory stage analysis is the proposed methodological feature. This case deliberately rejects the tempting but unsupported inference that a new completion principle must produce a more permissive final verdict.

6. Residual evidence tasks

Prioritize classical criticism of the ʿumra addition in the ʿĀʾisha routes and of Tirmidhī 931’s chain; determine the legal force of the proxy instruction; collate the contradictory Bidāya attribution; and distinguish obstruction, invalidation, vows, and additional voluntary performances. A successful defense of the contrary report or a weaker legal reading of the affirmative evidence could change the initial-status ruling without invalidating the stage principle.

B18 - Divorce pronounced during menstruation

Original dispute and evidence

Ibn Rushd separates occurrence of the divorce, whether return is obligatory, the duration of compulsion, and the timetable for a later lawful divorce. He attributes counting to the majority, nonoccurrence to a dissenting group, compulsory return to Mālik, and recommendation to several other leading jurists. He also records different time limits and the dispute between waiting only until the next purity and waiting through an additional menstrual cycle. His analysis at one point presses a supposed contradiction between counting the divorce and compelling return. These are distinct questions, not a single majority package. [REL08]

Al-Bukhārī 5251 reports the Prophet's instruction that Ibn ʿUmar take his wife back, retain her until purity, then menstruation, then purity, and only then divorce before intercourse if he chooses. Reports 5252-5253 add positive evidence of counting: Ibn ʿUmar answers the question whether the pronouncement counted and reports that one divorce was charged against him. These are retrospective reports by the participant; one should not replace them with an invented verbatim prophetic sentence declaring “it counts.” [REL09-REL11]

Abū Dāwūd 2185 preserves Abū al-Zubayr's wording that the Prophet restored her and did not regard it as anything. Abū Dāwūd then notes that other transmitted accounts differ from this version. The webpage nevertheless gives al-Albānī's overall ṣaḥīḥ grading. The problem is comparative evaluation of a particular wording, not a licence to erase the report as simply fabricated or unanimously weak. [REL12]

Competing premise and actual verdict

Provisional verdict: one such divorce counts, and the husband has a binding corrective duty to take her back while lawful revocation remains available. The case here is an otherwise effective first or second, revocable divorce in a consummated marriage. Counting is preferred because of the affirmative counted reports, their connection to the narrated incident, and their convergence with the instruction to return. It is not inferred merely from the prohibition, nor exclusively from treating the verb of return as an immutable technical term.

The strongest contrary reading deserves its full argument. Al-Shawkānī defends the noncounting addition: Abū al-Zubayr expressly heard the report; “return” can have a broader linguistic meaning; and a clear denial should outweigh an implication. He also challenges whether Ibn ʿUmar's legal understanding alone is binding. [REL13] We accept the semantic objection against using “return” alone, but do not find that it eliminates the independent, affirmative counted reports. On practical preponderance, those reports and the collector's variant comparison justify counting. A full reconstruction of the routes could reverse that preference; it is not immunized by a consensus claim.

Corrective duty and its separate warrant

The imperative to take her back supplies the duty. Counting supplies a statement about what the earlier act accomplished. There is no inherent contradiction in an effective wrongful act generating a duty to correct its consequences through an available legal procedure. Our proposal therefore declines Ibn Rushd's suggestion here that validity must make return merely recommended. Conversely, the binding character of the corrective command does not prove that the original act never occurred. Revocation restores the continuing marriage in the prescribed way; it does not erase the used divorce count.

For the remedial sequence in this incident, the fuller timetable in al-Bukhārī 5251 is provisionally preferred: the added intervening cycle is positive detail, and an abbreviated account need not negate it. That preference does not automatically create the same waiting sequence for every different divorce case. Qurʾān 65:1 supplies the general regulation of divorce in relation to the waiting period; the incident supplies a concrete corrective sequence. [REL09, REL14]

Counterexamples and dependencies

A binding duty is not by itself a judicial instrument. The sources considered here justify requiring lawful return when it is available; they do not independently establish that a judge's order retrospectively reverses a completed irrevocable divorce, authorizes physical coercion, or recreates a marriage after the waiting period expires. Those powers require their own warrants. If revocation is no longer legally available, the duty cannot be executed by pretending otherwise. Nor does this dossier decide simultaneous triple divorce or a third divorce by importing the easier revocable example.

Before consummation the waiting-period premise changes, so this precise corrective sequence cannot simply be extended. The report also cannot be used to treat menstruation as a general incapacity of the wife or a basis to disregard her interests. Its regulated act is the husband's divorce. Where return itself would be deployed to harm, independently established limits on harmful retention must be included; the present directive is not permission for indefinite control. Maintenance, residence, succession during revocable waiting, and subsequent remarriage must follow the adopted status rules rather than whichever isolated consequence is attractive in a particular dispute.

Result and remaining research

Status: existing counted-divorce and obligatory-return outcome, with a new explicit separation of adjudicated fact, corrective duty, and enforcement power; moderate confidence. Priority research is an isnād-and-wording table for Nāfiʿ, Sālim, Anas ibn Sīrīn, Yūnus ibn Jubayr, and Abū al-Zubayr; the precise attribution of affirmative counting; and comparison of early commentary on the longer timetable. A superior reconstruction establishing prophetic noncounting would change the count and all dependent consequences. Mere general disapproval of irregular divorce would not suffice.

B21 - Irrevocable divorce in a fatal illness and inheritance

Original dispute and positive evidence

Ibn Rushd reports substantial disagreement when a man irrevocably divorces in illness and dies: no inheritance in al-Shāfiʿī's position; inheritance during the waiting period for Abū Ḥanīfa and al-Thawrī; until remarriage for Aḥmad and Ibn Abī Laylā; and broader survival of the claim for Mālik and al-Layth. Consent or the wife's request is another disputed condition. He rejects an alleged Companion consensus because Ibn al-Zubayr's dissent is reported. Recovery is a distinct case and must not be silently included in the fatal-illness rule. [REL15]

The pro-inheritance case has affirmative transmitted evidence. Mālik transmits through Ibn Shihāb from Ṭalḥa ibn ʿAbd Allāh ibn ʿAwf and Abū Salama that ʿUthmān awarded inheritance to ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn ʿAwf's irrevocably divorced wife after her waiting period. The same chapter contains another ʿUthmān award and a separate report through Rabīʿa, introduced as information that reached him, that the wife had requested divorce. That latter transmission must not acquire the first report's evidential standing merely because both appear together. [REL16]

Al-Shāfiʿī preserves Ibn Abī Mulayka's report of asking Ibn al-Zubayr, who acknowledges ʿUthmān's award and disagrees with it. The transmitted accounts also differ about whether the award fell within the waiting period. Al-Rabīʿ reports al-Shāfiʿī's subsequent choice of no inheritance. Ibn Ḥazm additionally preserves a report of ʿUmar threatening to restore wives' inheritance when divorces accompanied disposal of property near anticipated death. This is serious early anti-evasion evidence, although threat, adjudication, and general rule must be distinguished. [REL17-REL18]

The competing premise

The issue is whether terminal illness establishes a protected succession expectancy that this unilateral divorce cannot defeat. Qurʾān 4:12 positively assigns inheritance to spouses. The ordinary-divorce side reads the effective severance of marriage as ending that cause; the pro-inheritance side treats the early awards as evidence for a special protection against evasion. Neither proposition is established just by calling the result coherent. In particular, an inheritance exception need not maintain all marital relations or require reciprocal succession if its independent legal cause is protection of the wife's expectancy. [REL19]

Candidate verdict and its evidential cost

Version 0.1 provisionally follows no automatic spousal inheritance after an otherwise effective irrevocable divorce, including the stated fatal-illness case. Confidence is lower than in B03 or B18. This is an existing position, chosen by a disputable evidential preference: ordinary spousal succession has an express textual cause; effective irrevocable severance ends that cause; and the transmitted exception rests on contested Companion adjudication rather than a presently established prophetic rule or demonstrated common Companion proposition. Ibn al-Zubayr's informed dissent prevents treating the award as undisputed exposition of that cause.

This verdict does not say that a Companion judgment can never establish an exception, or that a predeath expectancy is legally impossible. It assigns less normative weight to this disputed adjudication than to the established cause plus the dissenting early interpretation. The remaining gap is a sufficiently persuasive basis for preferring the adjudication as a general legal qualification of succession, with its operative facts established. That is a source-authority choice open to reversal, not a neutral deduction compelled by the proposed consequences principle. If the charter generally adopts well-transmitted Companion judgments despite informed dissent when supported by anti-evasion analogies, this verdict would require reconsideration for consistency.

Strongest bounded alternative and counterexamples

A substantial alternative protects the wife where the husband unilaterally effects irrevocable divorce during an objectively dangerous illness from which he dies, she did not freely procure that severance, and the threatened succession expectancy existed at divorce. Protection ends on recovery or her remarriage. Death from the original illness supplies the causal boundary; protection is not a perpetual claim. Ibn Qudāma develops a related until-remarriage position and a waiting-period-only alternative. His claimed prior consensus cannot simply override the reported dissent. [REL20]

This alternative's foundation is the early adjudication, reinforced by the independently recognized concern with evading succession, not general sympathy. Its particular consent and endpoint conditions still need support. One cannot simultaneously invoke an after-waiting-period award and quietly say the original event proves an idda-only rule. Nor does a wife's performance of an unavoidable religious duty count automatically as freely choosing a divorce attached to it: al-Shāfiʿī's discussion of the pro-inheritance view itself distinguishes such conditions. [REL21]

Under the adopted verdict, unpaid dower and other independently established debts survive; ordinary revocable divorce during its waiting period remains a different status. We do not create a new damages claim equal to the excluded inheritance. Under the alternative, the succession share would rest on special protection rather than fictional cohabitation, and other heirs' shares must be recalculated accordingly. Neither recovery nor remarriage can be ignored merely because the earlier motive was wrongful.

Result and remaining research

Status: existing no-inheritance view, with a provisional and contested source-authority rationale. The immediate research priorities are the exact circumstances and routes of ʿUthmān's decisions, the dating and scope of Ibn al-Zubayr's dissent, the ʿUmar report, and comparisons with legally established anti-evasion cases. Corroboration establishing a general early terminal-illness protection, or a stronger account of Companion adjudication within the charter, could overturn this verdict. Abstract discomfort with asymmetric effects would not defeat that evidence.

B22 - Which operative grounds extend the ribā rules beyond six commodities?

Provisional conclusion - familiar core rulings and extensions, with a new selection protocol; medium confidence beyond the textual core. Preserve the expressly regulated exchanges of gold, silver, wheat, barley, dates, and salt. Admit a bounded extension to paradigmatic measured, storable provision foods, including rice and raisins: unequal same-kind exchanges fall within the prohibition under the proposed analogy. Do not stop at six merely because jurists dispute the cause. Equally, do not classify every weighable nonfood as ribā-bearing merely because scales can measure it. This is a historical analysis of commodity-exchange reasoning; it makes no determination about a modern financial product.

Textual core and differentiated rules. Muslim 1587c states equivalence and immediate exchange for each of the six against its own kind, and allows differential quantities where the relevant kinds differ, with its stated delivery condition. Determining which cross-kind transactions share that condition requires the rest of the corpus; the sentence must not be detached into a universal ban on deferred exchanges between every money and food item. Qurʾān 2:279 independently preserves the principal while prohibiting wrongdoing in the ribā context. Questions of debt and of a barter quantity rule must not be collapsed. [F_M1587C; F_Q2_279]

Competing accounts. Bidāya, Book of Sales, second part, second chapter, first section on the things subject to excess and delay, records the textual restriction associated with literalists and several extending analyses: food, storability and provision, weight or measure, and the monetary role of the precious metals. It also reports al-Bāqillānī’s especially narrow extension from dates to raisins. Ibn Rushd treats many proposed extensions as resemblance reasoning and nevertheless weighs their merits. His taxonomy cannot substitute for proving the operative ground in a particular new case. [F_BID_RIBA]

Why rice and raisins cross the proposed boundary. This is not a vote among schools. The case combines several evidentially supported connections: a textual food category, staple or stored provision, and quantity-based exchange of goods whose same-kind equivalence the law regulates. Muslim 1592 supplies the food wording; its transmitter also mentions barley as the food then in use. That contextual comment must be recorded without pretending it automatically cancels every broader reading of “food.” Rice belongs to the paradigmatic cereal application; raisins also have the particularly close preserved-fruit analogy that Ibn Rushd reports. The convergence makes these applications better supported than an inference to iron based only on weight. [F_M1592; F_BID_RIBA]

The required counterexample. Bukhārī 2302-2303 rejects exchanging larger quantities of lower-quality dates for smaller quantities of better dates and directs separate sale and purchase. It also contains a statement concerning weighing. This evidence defeats a proposal that equal market value or the absence of perceived exploitation automatically cancels the prescribed equality rule. Our purpose-based component must explain and extend the rule without overriding this explicit case. The weighing clause supports investigating measurement; it does not, without interpretation, name every conceivable weighable object as the legal domain. [F_B2302]

Strongest opponents. A Shāfiʿī argument can treat the food wording as broad enough to cover fresh foods without the added storability restriction. A Ḥanafī argument can give measurement a more general legal role, stressing the repeated quantitative commands. A literalist can deny that the proposed combination identifies an authorized extension at all. Our answer to the last is that practical analogy need not attain certainty: al-Shāfiʿī’s Risāla, discussion of qiyās, explicitly distinguishes closer and more contestable analogies while allowing warranted practical judgment. Our answer to the broader positions is provisional differentiation of evidential strength, not proof that every disputed extension is false. [F_BID_RIBA; F_RISALA_QIYAS]

Unresolved extensions and safeguards. Fresh fruit, medicinal ingestibles, metals serving as ordinary monetary media, and changes in how goods are measured require separate dossiers. The absence of an established commodity analogy never licenses a transaction independently prohibited as debt ribā, fraud, coercion, or another wrong. Monetary function is a serious candidate ground; defining that function and reconciling it with texts about the metals themselves remains outstanding. We should test a broader food rule and a broader measurement rule against the complete hadith families and early practice before finalizing the boundary. Each extension must identify its ground, the rival grounds, the relevant facts, and a counterexample capable of defeating it; disagreement alone defeats neither analogy nor its opponent.

B25 - Mistaken qibla: correct inquiry, objective error, and subsequent remedies

1. Historical dispute and the three separate findings

Ibn Rushd reports al-Shāfiʿī’s requirement to repeat when directional error becomes known and the contrasting treatment of adequate inquiry associated with Mālik and Abū Ḥanīfa; Mālik recommends repetition within time. Deliberate disregard and failure to inquire are excluded from the latter protection. The dispute includes report criticism and analogy with praying before time: Bidāya, Prayer, conditions, qibla chapter, second issue, opening “هل فرض المجتهد في القبلة الإصابة أو الاجتهاد”.

The proposed analysis records three findings independently: objective directional accuracy; adequacy of inquiry and personal fault; and validity plus any remedy. Darūrī §243, p. 141 explicitly separates excused error from correctness. Its discussion of juristic truth is not itself a ruling that every mistaken prayer is valid.

2. What the inspected sources do and do not establish

Q 2:144 supplies the orientation command. Q 64:16 and Bukhārī 7288, Book 96, hadith 19, connect obedience to ability. Neither general capacity text alone settles retrospective validity.

The direct dark-night report appears in Tirmidhī 345, Book 2, hadith 197. Tirmidhī’s own commentary criticizes its chain, identifying weakness associated with Ashʿath al-Sammān; the displayed Darussalam grade is weak. It cannot be presented as a securely authenticated direct declaration settling this case. Tirmidhī nevertheless reports substantial juristic reception of the no-repeat result. Reception does not turn this particular chain into a sound one or establish consensus.

Bukhārī 403, Book 8, hadith 55, describes worshippers at Qubāʾ turning during prayer upon receiving news of the changed qibla. Its chapter heading connects the report to nonrepetition, but that heading is Bukhārī’s legal framing, not Prophetic speech. The narration concerns newly received legal information; extending it to a factual miscalculation is an analogy that requires defense.

3. The competing methodological paths

One path makes correct orientation the relevant condition and concludes that discovered failure defeats discharge. Another interprets the unseen-qibla obligation as satisfied by a legally authorized, competent inquiry in the circumstances.

The draft selects the second path on a cumulative, preponderant basis: capacity-relative compliance, the Qubāʾ model of responding to newly accessible decisive information, and the inherited reception of the particular qibla rule. The weak report is corroborative historical material, not an independent foundation. The proposed shared ground is reasonable access to the information needed for orientation. Whether the legal-change and factual-error cases share that ground adequately is this dossier’s weakest bridge.

The strongest repeat objection is that an unchanged objective condition failed, as when prayer precedes its time: Qubāʾ involved reliance on a previously correct legal direction, whereas this case involves an inaccurate factual conclusion. Capacity can excuse the person without fulfilling the condition. The proposed qibla-specific account must defeat that objection; neither a weak report nor sympathy for diligent error is sufficient.

4. Candidate principle and provisional conclusion

Candidate principle: where the evidence establishes a fallible procedure as a legally sufficient performance standard, judge performance by competent use of that procedure and the information reasonably available then. Later discovery of error does not itself establish invalidity or a remedial duty. Mere permission to consult an indicator is insufficient: its status as a performance standard must be demonstrated.

Working ruling: a person unable to ascertain the unseen qibla directly, who used adequately reliable available indications without neglect and completed the prayer, is provisionally treated as having discharged it despite subsequently demonstrated directional error. No obligatory iʿāda within time or qaḍāʾ after time follows solely from that discovery. The direction remains objectively wrong; the ruling concerns legal discharge under the selected interpretation.

This does not derive validity from pardon. It adopts, with moderate-to-low confidence, a specific capacity-sensitive account of the orientation condition. If that positive account fails, absence of blame cannot rescue the conclusion.

5. Scope and concrete failure tests

When the correct direction becomes reliably known during prayer, the worshipper must turn toward it as soon as practicable; the selected protection does not authorize continuing knowingly in error. Better accessible means may raise what counts as adequate inquiry; hindsight alone cannot prove earlier neglect. Knowingly disregarding a clear direction, guessing without inquiry when inquiry is available, and emergency prayer concessions require different rules.

The timing distinction matters. Darūrī §§204-205, p. 123 discusses makeup and complete compliance. Section 204 cannot by itself remove iʿāda while the original time remains. Our within-time conclusion instead depends on the prior judgment of valid discharge.

A counterexample is Bukhārī 135, Book 4, hadith 1, explicitly conditioning acceptance after ritual impurity on ablution. The qibla ruling does not erase independently established purity conditions or imply that every sincere mistaken belief produces a valid act. Third-party restitution is likewise outside this worship-specific conclusion.

Classification: no obligatory repetition after adequate inquiry is an existing position. The distinct candidate contribution is the mandatory three-part assessment and explicit evidential threshold for authorizing a fallible standard. This is the least secure selection among these four dossiers.

6. Residual evidence tasks

Collate all dark-night routes and early rulings, examine classical arguments using the Qubāʾ report, distinguish changed opinion from conclusive discovery, and specify material directional deviation and adequate inquiry. Test prayer-time mistakes and undiscovered impurity independently. A successful objection to the positive performance-standard bridge would require revisiting validity, then separately addressing within-time repetition and post-time makeup.

Four additional countertests

These shorter studies test the constitution outside the twelve principal decisions. T03 and T04 correspond to existing reader applications B07 and B26. T01 and T02 are supplementary hadith-based studies. They prevent a predominantly worship and family-law sample from determining the constitution without exposure to evidence, administration, property, and liability.

T01. An evidential sign is not always a prescribed procedure

Evidence and question

Al-Bukhārī 137 tells a person troubled by doubt about ritual purity not to leave prayer without sound or smell. Al-ʿAynī explains that actual hearing and smelling are not exclusive conditions: the point concerns knowing that the relevant event occurred, including for a person unable to use those senses. Al-Bukhārī 1909, however, gives both a sighting instruction for the fasting month and an express thirty-day fallback when the crescent is obscured. These texts present different interpretive structures. [H04; M10; H05]

Provisional rule and conclusion

C07 requires identifying whether a named item is evidence of a fact or part of the legally authorized procedure. For the ablution report, certainty of the relevant invalidating event can arise through adequate evidence other than those two senses; mere anxiety remains insufficient. This result is already classical. The more general proposed discipline is to forbid transfer of this result to a second subject until its evidential structure has been established.

The crescent report therefore defeats the proposed universal principle that a technically improved detector may always replace a named method. The explicit fallback needs an account. Version 0.1 retains the report's sighting-or-completion framework in this limited test; it does not establish astronomical calculation as a universal substitute simply by analogy with the sound-and-smell case. A complete calendar position would require the broader report family, local and global sighting questions, the role of calculation in rejecting impossible testimony, and the legal meaning of visibility. Those questions are not decided here.

Strongest objection and revision trigger

An opponent may argue that sighting and the fallback were practical means of identifying a naturally occurring month for a community without easy computation. That is a substantive interpretation to investigate. It must explain the wording and its procedural alternative, not simply assert that a newer method is more accurate. Conversely, this test does not prove that instruments, observational assistance, or calculation can never have a role. Its conclusion is narrower: substitution depends on the legal function of the method, and the same epistemic threshold must be preserved.

Result: accept reliable evidence beyond sound and smell; reject automatic transfer to replacing the lunar procedure. Confidence: high for the first narrow result; medium for the procedural restraint. Amendment: C07 must include explicit fallbacks as evidence against a pure-proxy reading.

T02. Office can be the relevant relationship even when a payment is called a gift

Evidence and question

In al-Bukhārī 7174, the collector separates official receipts from what he calls personal gifts. The Prophetic response tests whether he would have received those benefits while remaining at his parents' home. The report supplies an explicit comparison that connects the benefit to the entrusted position. [H06]

Provisional rule and conclusion

Under C10, legal analysis should preserve an evidenced relationship across changes in the arrangement's superficial form. A gift obtained because a person exercises the entrusted collection function cannot become an unrestricted private entitlement merely by being labelled a gift. If a donor routes the same benefit through an intermediary, the extension rests on the same demonstrated relationship, provided the intermediary arrangement is actually proved. That application is proposed reasoning, not a direct report about modern intermediaries.

The inquiry would record the donor's relation to the officer's decisions, timing, established prior pattern of gifts, comparable gifts in the absence of the appointment, conditions attached, and any coordinated routing. These are possible evidence of a causal relationship, not a fixed statutory checklist claimed to come from the hadith. The officeholder's personal virtue is not a substitute for establishing the facts.

Strongest objection and revision trigger

A counterfactual can be underdetermined: colleagues can become actual friends, and a pre-existing family gift can continue during public service. The report does not establish that every benefit received by every employed person is identical to an official collector's benefit. The provisional prohibition is bounded by entrusted authority and the demonstrated connection. It also does not alone determine whether a particular benefit must be returned to the donor or paid to a public fund; disposition requires its own evidence under C09.

Result: the relevant legal unit can be the evidenced arrangement, including coordinated intermediaries; a change of label does not by itself alter the ruling. Confidence: high for the collector's office-derived benefit; medium for the extension. Novelty: established anti-evasion reasoning made subject to an explicit causal-evidence and remedy requirement.

T03 / B07. Financial maturity should be tested as an operative capacity

Evidence and question

Qurʾān 4:6 connects handing over an orphan's property to maturity and demonstrated sound judgment. In Bidāya, Book of Interdiction, chapter 2, Ibn Rushd distinguishes actual competence from a formal declaration ending supervision. He also criticizes fixed waiting periods in accounts of women's financial maturity where competence can exist earlier. His attribution to Ibn al-Qāsim concerns a specified ward case; it must not be turned into a claim that every jurist already applied one rule to every category. [Q01; B07-P]

Provisional rule and conclusion

The constitution selects demonstrated maturity and sound financial judgment as the operative conditions in the adult ward problem. An otherwise competent adult's transactions do not become substantively void solely because an executor has failed to announce release. This follows the branch that treats recognition as evidential in the specified setting. C07 then supports a further, explicitly proposed application: assess a woman's competence by adequate evidence of competence itself, rather than make marriage or an arbitrary period after marriage its indispensable proof.

The case also forces separation of competence from moral admiration. In this property setting, v0.1 favors capacity to manage the relevant property responsibly as the controlling meaning of rushd; it does not treat every unrelated moral failing as sufficient financial incapacity. This is a choice between inherited readings, not a neutral dictionary fact. Its support is the passage's property-management setting and the command to test the person.

Strongest objection and revision trigger

Private competence assessments can conflict; third parties need public knowledge of authority; an exploitative guardian may falsely attest competence. The conclusion therefore leaves room for an independently justified public process, records, accessible review, and protection against fraud. It rejects an unsupported inference from absent paperwork to actual incompetence. It does not settle how modern positive law assigns the effects of registration, nor does it claim that an adult's assertion of competence is enough.

Result: demonstrated financial competence controls the substantive assessment; gendered temporal proxies require evidence and cannot replace it by assumption. Confidence: medium-high within the stated classical problem. Novelty: an inherited criterion applied consistently to the empirical assumptions used to establish it. Reversal condition: proof that a particular declaration is independently constitutive for the disputed effect, rather than merely a means of recognizing competence.

T04 / B26. An excused treatment error can still leave a compensation claim

Evidence and question

Bidāya's treatment of accidental injury records compensation for a physician's mistake, a Mālikī exemption for a qualified practitioner, and agreement on the liability of an unqualified intruder. It also reports disagreement about the fund that pays. The passage's broad agreement language must be read alongside its stated exception. Abū Dāwūd 4586 concerns someone practising medicine without established competence; Abū Dāwūd's own comment expresses uncertainty about its route, while the displayed later grading is ḥasan. That report is not direct proof of every liability rule for qualified physicians. [B26-P; H07]

Provisional rule and conclusion

Version 0.1 adopts the bounded accidental-injury branch: a proved unintended injury caused by an erroneous intervention can generate compensation despite the absence of deliberate wrongdoing. The example is an unintended cutting injury, not every unsuccessful treatment or every unavoidable complication of a properly authorized procedure. The basis is the analogy to accidental injury and the recorded juristic treatment; the novice-practitioner report corroborates a different, more culpable category.

C08 therefore cannot turn competent inquiry into immunity from all downstream effects. An injured patient's entitlement concerns a distinct person and a different legal effect from the clinician's blameworthiness. The same constitution can hold a prayer sufficient after authorized directional inquiry and hold compensation due for an unintended bodily injury. That is coherence through different operative facts and interests, rather than two contradictory conclusions about “mistakes.”

Strongest objection and revision trigger

The strongest opposing position treats competent, authorized treatment as an activity whose unavoidable risks are not attributed to the practitioner in this way. The dispute cannot be resolved by compassion for either party. One must establish the nature of the error, causation, authorization, the accepted risk, and the proof that makes this particular injury compensable. Whether payment comes from the practitioner, a collective arrangement, or another fund is a further question. Version 0.1 deliberately decides the survival of a possible claim in the specified error case without inventing a modern payment system.

Result: moral excuse is not an automatic bar to compensation for a proved compensable accidental injury. Confidence: medium for the selected branch; high for the logical distinction between culpability and liability. Amendment: C08 must require a separate inquiry into third-party rights and cannot be formulated as a universal “reasonable process makes everything valid” rule.

Valid incorporation under the shared authority rules

Incorporate propositions with their warrants, not packages inferred from names. An early jurist’s view is historical evidence and an argument to assess. The charter’s binding sources and accepted inference rules still decide the result. A useful proposal extracted from a disputed attribution can be tested as a present hypothesis, provided the text does not falsely present it as that jurist’s established doctrine.

Preserve the level of the witness. Mark a proposition as an authorial statement, transmitted answer, opponent’s quotation, bibliographical title, or modern reconstruction. An attributed position does not carry every surrounding explanation with it. For example, Ibn al-Mundhir’s rationale following his list of jurists cannot be distributed retrospectively to every name in that list.

Apply imported principles consistently. The charter can use Dāwūdian criticism to expose unsupported analogies while admitting other analogies on independently stated grounds. It cannot simultaneously declare all qiyās intrinsically invalid and treat qiyās as binding when convenient. Likewise, al-Layth’s challenge can motivate investigation of geographically dispersed reception without turning every regional practice into an overriding source.

Retain genuine conflicts. Al-Thawrī’s reported position on intention and Isḥāq’s explanation supporting intention cannot both decide the same proposition under unchanged facts. Record both, evaluate their arguments by the same rules, and state why one wins. This permits synthesis without concealing contradictory premises.

Use loss of documentation to limit claims. It weakens the inference that a doctrine was absent; it does not convert a proposed doctrine into an authentic recovered rule. Search Ibn al-Mundhir’s al-Awsaṭ and al-Ishrāf, al-Marwazī’s Ikhtilāf al-ʿulamāʾ, early Muṣannaf collections, and surviving school polemics as additional witnesses. These are further investigation targets, not assertions that every relevant passage has now been audited.

For each extracted rule, retain: exact locus; named speaker; witness and transmission path; legal proposition; explicit reason, if present; counterargument; uncertainty; and whether adoption changes an existing ruling or supplies a new rationale. This makes the discontinued traditions usable without inventing complete lost constitutions.

Compatibility, objections, and the next research pass

Which combinations survive this first pass?

The constitution is a method for selecting and combining warranted propositions. It does not declare every borrowed doctrine true in its original, unrestricted form. The following combinations are coherent only because their points of conflict are stated and adjudicated.

| Proposed combination | Reason it can cohere | Commitment that cannot be imported unchanged | |---|---|---| | Al-Sarakhsī's distinction between devotional attribution and readiness for prayer, together with productive use of established individual hadith reports | The distinction can be defended as an interpretation of the act's effects; it need not depend on a general ban on additions through individual reports. | The separate source theory excluding such reports from particular additions to the Qurʾānic prescription is not adopted wholesale. B05 must win on its own interpretive evidence. | | Al-Shāfiʿī's distinction between partial wiping and the fuller preferred form, together with a strong presumption that legislative commands bind | The dispute concerns the content and minimum of the commanded act, not whether a demonstrated legislative demand may simply be ignored. | A full demonstration cannot be reduced to insignificance; its explanatory role must be assessed for each feature. | | Al-Qarāfī's distinction among Prophetic capacities, together with a default favoring religious instruction | Differentiating capacities avoids converting a court judgment or an intercession into an unrestricted personal power; the stated default prevents arbitrary contextual dismissal. | The jurist cannot select a nonlegislative category merely because the ordinary reading is difficult. | | Al-Layth's challenge to geographical exclusivity, together with evidential respect for early public practice | Dispersed witnesses can strengthen or weaken a reconstruction without every local practice becoming binding. | Neither undifferentiated Medinan supremacy nor equal automatic authority for every region is adopted. | | Textualist objections to unsupported extensions, together with warranted qiyās | The objection supplies a test of relevant similarity; the charter independently accepts analogy where the test is met. | The categorical invalidity of all qiyās cannot coexist with C10. The compatibility judgments are the present study's reasoning. They are not assertions that the cited authors endorsed this combined constitution.

An unfinished test: B02 together with B05 in one wuḍūʾ

B02 and B05 require a separate test of one complete wuḍūʾ: wiping less than the applicable Ḥanafī minimum while lacking the intention required by the other three standard school packages. Classical antecedents for each individual ruling do not establish that their combination is permissible. The potentially shared proposition concerns the complete act, even where the reasons for rejecting it differ.

Al-Āmidī's selected position permits a new distinction only when it does not overturn a proposition actually shared by the earlier positions. An established agreement that this composite act is invalid would therefore constrain C01. Conversely, rejection under four later school packages alone does not demonstrate the requisite agreement of all relevant earlier jurists. The historical and propositional question remains to be investigated. [M02]

There is a substantial early objection to the easy consensus claim. Ibn al-Mundhir attributes to al-Thawrī sufficiency of wiping one hair in the explicit case of a woman. Ibn ʿAbd al-Barr attributes to him water purification without ritual intention and names three transmitters of a teaching-demonstration example. These are separately transmitted propositions, not a directly attested judgment on the exact composite act; neither inspected passage supplies a complete transmission audit. Their conjunction is a plausible reconstruction requiring examination of scope and possible joint qualifications. The woman's example must not silently become a reported universal rule. [M12; M13]

IIFA Resolution 70, sections 5-6, addresses talfīq in following schools and prohibits, among other forms, consensus-breaking combinations and a composite accepted by no mujtahid. Independent derivation requires its own justification; calling this project “ijtihād” does not exempt it from authority, competence, or consensus constraints. [M11]

Recorded result: B02 and B05 remain individual provisional arguments, but their combined application is not yet defended as a complete practice. Before endorsing it, evaluate both requirements together, identify the exact allegedly shared proposition, collate the identified early dissent, and answer the strongest objection. No artificial compromise quantity is inserted simply to conceal the unresolved issue.

What the first cases forced us to change

1. A report's legal effect cannot be read from a grading panel alone

B17 retains an initial ʿumra obligation only as a working choice conditional on two substantive premises: acceptance of the ʿumra addition in the ʿĀʾisha report family, and its obligation rather than virtue reading. The proxy report independently corroborates ʿumra's presence and a male beneficiary; its scope has not been established as a second independent universal-obligation proof. Version 0.1 has compared important versions and recorded the conflict; it has not completed a critical reconstruction. A final adoption under C02 must resolve that task. The same discipline requires preserving the contrary wording and the collector's comparison in B18.

2. A stage rule does not determine the final verdict by itself

The completion principle does useful work even when initial ʿumra remains obligatory. It locates which evidence proves which duty. It cannot be judged a failure merely because it does not yield an optional practice. Likewise, completion of an invalidated pilgrimage and discharge of the original duty can come apart; the method must track both.

3. A continuing obligation does not prove that all postponement is forbidden

B16's first-feasible-season conclusion depends on its timing reports and its restriction of their binding scope to an already owed first pilgrimage. A person defending lawful postponement is not claiming to have discharged hajj. C11's preservation of an established obligation cannot settle that dispute. Nor can vague precaution concerning death replace a failed timing argument.

4. Remedial purpose and obligatory force are different questions

The initial constitution's wording put a remedial direction beside recommendation and permission. That was wrong: B18 selects an obligatory remedial command. C03 has been amended accordingly. A counted wrongful act, a binding direction to correct its consequences, and a public power to enforce that direction require separate assessment.

5. Selective consequences can be positively required

B08's guardian report is a powerful counterexample to an overly tidy legal-effect theory: the report states invalidity while awarding dower after consummation. The effect has its own warrant. B21's early inheritance awards likewise cannot be rejected merely because other marital effects end. The low-confidence no-automatic-inheritance selection remains an explicit source-authority judgment; it is not the only result C09 can coherently support.

6. Dissent defeats an easy consensus claim, not every positive argument

C01 now expressly allows a disputed Companion judgment to provide sufficient defeasible warrant. It also distinguishes a Companion's testimony about a Prophetic incident from a later Companion adjudication. B18 and B21 therefore cannot be treated identically merely because both contain a Companion's speech. Their individual evidential roles must be examined.

7. No universal immunity follows from reasonable inquiry

B25's no-repeat ruling requires an affirmative, qibla-specific interpretation of the performance standard. Its analogy with receiving a changed direction remains a weak point. T04 shows why even justified activity can leave a distinct compensation question. The relevant legal condition and protected relation matter more than the shared word “error.”

8. A better factual detector does not automatically replace a legal procedure

T01 permits other adequate evidence of the invalidating event in the ablution case, but preserves the need to account for the lunar report's express fallback. T03 similarly distinguishes competence from its formal recognition while allowing independently grounded institutional effects. C07 now explicitly requires inquiry into constitutive procedures and stated alternatives.

9. Analogy need not find one undisputed cause before it can work

B22 rejects both confinement to six commodities on the sole basis of juristic disagreement and expansion to every measurable item on the sole basis of measurability. Rice and raisins receive actual provisional extensions. Marginal cases remain open because their arguments need study, not because uncertainty automatically means permission. The quality-date example also prevents “equal market value” from displacing an explicit equality rule.

10. Demonstrated sufficiency is not identical to a minimum, but demonstration remains evidence

B02 must answer the whole-head account on its merits. Describing that account as an “added condition” cannot settle whether the demonstration already explains the command's content. The forelock-and-turban report remains composite. This qualification prevents C06 from becoming a routine device for reducing every ritual to the smallest conceivable movement.

The strongest general objections

“This is eclecticism with a procedural vocabulary.” That objection succeeds if every case is chosen first and the articles are adjusted afterward. The answer is to record general commitments that can lose cases: productive individual reports, probabilistic qiyās, obligatory legislative commands, separately warranted effects, and nonconclusive but potentially sufficient Companion evidence. These commitments rule out attractive shortcuts. A broader corpus may still expose selection bias; the present twelve cases cannot prove that it has disappeared.

“Your default against extra conditions favors the result you want.” A strong objection. C06 therefore requires deciding whether a disputed feature explains the original act or adds a further restriction. B02 and B05 do not mechanically follow from that default. Their serious opponents are retained, and B05 is among the lower-confidence cases. A valid derivation from general speech can establish a condition; “no separate verse” is never a sufficient refutation.

“Most outcomes are already found in existing schools.” Correct. The first pass does not establish a set of historically unprecedented rulings. A novel school would be identifiable by how it consistently ranks evidence, extends law, and revises decisions across a large corpus. A new rationale for an inherited result can be more consequential than a novel isolated answer, because it changes the next cases that the system can decide.

“The system should choose the widest protection of human interests.” The constitution admits legally supported protection and consequences, but interests can conflict and do not measure their own remedies. In B21, a protective exception affects other heirs; in T04, excusing a clinician does not remove the patient's loss; in B03, safeguarding an earlier suitor cannot erase the woman's choice. The method must establish whose interest has what legal form, rather than allowing a general value to settle every relation.

“A complete school must answer the unresolved cases.” Eventually it needs a much larger, usable corpus. A first research version should instead expose exactly where an inference is pending. The register distinguishes an adopted provisional verdict, a countertest, and an unassessed project address. It does not fill unknown parts with silently borrowed school positions. Conditional case assumptions are listed so that later research can either justify or revise them.

Next use B04 (zakāt on a child's property), B14 (the sales-session option), B15 (the general guardian condition), B19 (foetal compensation's claimant), B20 (jewellery), B24 (tacit approval after liʿān), B27 (cupping while fasting), and B28 (deferred animal barter). For each issue, produce the same seven-part record: precise question and facts; source family; competing derivations; earliest differing premise; actual provisional ruling; strongest countercase; and evidence that could reverse it. Integrate with the canonical Revision16 register only after retrieving its real K-position identifiers. The reader's B addresses are useful links, not substitutes for those identifiers.

Priority 3: compare the surviving traditions at the level of arguments

For Ḥanafī theory, begin with al-Jaṣṣāṣ's al-Fuṣūl fī al-uṣūl and al-Sarakhsī's Uṣūl, using the al-Mabsūṭ intention discussion as a concrete branch-level check. Study general and particular wording, the relation of additions to an established prescription, widespread need, and istiḥsān. Do not assume that these authors state one invariant school algorithm. [R01; M07; F_MABSUT_INT]

For Mālikī approaches, compare the Muwaṭṭaʾ's reports and transmitted judgments, al-Qarāfī's distinction among capacities and rights, and both halves of al-Shāṭibī's programme: consequences and prescribed constraints. This sequence tests what “practice” or “purpose” actually proves in a case. [REL16; M04; M08; M09]

For Shāfiʿī argument, read al-Risāla's report, explanation, and analogy discussions alongside al-Umm's wiping and deathbed-divorce chapters. The branch texts prevent assigning a modern slogan to the school that the author's own reasoning does not sustain. [F_RISALA_QIYAS; F_UMM_WIPE; REL17; REL21]

For Ḥanbalī and adjacent early hadith jurists, compare al-Kawsaj's named answers of Aḥmad and Isḥāq with Ibn Qudāma's Rawḍat al-nāẓir and the relevant al-Mughnī passages. Distinguish the two respondents and later synthesis. The intention case already demonstrates why retaining only convenient early opinions would bias the reconstruction. [F_MASAIL118; R02; REL20]

For Ẓāhirī approaches, use Ibn Ḥazm's al-Iḥkām and al-Muḥallā as his own substantial corpus, and the early founder evidence in the discontinued-school matrix as a different historical problem. Dāwūd's and Muḥammad b. Dāwūd's lost works cannot simply be filled with Ibn Ḥazm's later positions. [REL18; discontinued-school matrix]

Priority 4: widen the comparison while keeping authority premises explicit

The first prototype has not audited non-Sunni systems. A genuinely wider comparison should examine Muḥammad Bāqir al-Ṣadr's Lessons in Islamic Jurisprudence for a Jaʿfarī account of legal inference and practical principles; Ibn al-Murtaḍā's Miʿyār al-ʿuqūl for Zaydī legal theory; al-Sālimī's Sharḥ Ṭalʿat al-shams for Ibāḍī theory; and al-Qāḍī al-Nuʿmān's Disagreements of the Jurists for Ismāʿīlī criticism of rival legal foundations. These are reading targets, not sources claimed to support the present twelve verdicts. The project must specify whether it is accepting a tradition's account of authoritative teaching, translating one of its arguments into the prototype's premises, or using it as a critical comparison. [R03-R06]

For the formation programme, investigate the project's Ihyāʾ material alongside the Qurʾānic passages and the legal dossiers. Ask what disposition a practice is meant to cultivate, how the institution may help or obstruct it, and what evidence connects the two. A formation note should neither masquerade as a new operative cause nor become a compulsory moral biography of the person whose legal act is under review.

Conditions for advancing to version 0.2

Version 0.2 should include completed report-family tables for B17, B18, and B25; a defended general policy on Companion judgments applied beyond B21; at least eight additional decisions from the identified queue; a checked map to the actual Revision16 propositions; and a record of every ruling changed by an amended article. The standard is a more stable, usable method. It is entirely possible that this work will yield a coherent independent research orientation without warranting a separate madhhab, or that its strongest contributions will be corrections and alternatives within existing schools.

Amendments

A01 · C01, C09 — Disputed Companion judgment can provide sufficient defeasible warrant; dissent defeats an easy consensus claim rather than every substantive argument. Distinguish testimony about a Prophetic incident from later judgment.

A02 · C03 — Remedial purpose is independent of whether a command is obligatory or recommended.

A03 · C02, C06 — Distinguish obligatory act, demonstrated sufficiency and minimum condition; preserve stable composite concession components.

A04 · C02, C05 — Stage separation does not imply nonobligation; initial umra result retains unresolved transmission and force premises.

A05 · C05, C11 — Continuing to owe a duty does not prove forbidden postponement; timing needs its own warrant.

A06 · C07 — A prescribed fallback is evidence against automatic substitution of a named method as a pure proxy.

A07 · C08, C09 — A proved fallible performance standard can support discharge only in its own scope; third-party liability requires separate analysis.

A08 · C10 — Preponderant supported analogy can extend beyond named commodities despite disputed causes; preserve explicit quantity rules and identify marginal cases.

A09 · C01, C06 — Two individually adopted provisional arguments do not automatically justify their joint application within one complete practice; preserve express nonendorsement of the B02+B05 combination pending its common-proposition and early-dissent audit.
