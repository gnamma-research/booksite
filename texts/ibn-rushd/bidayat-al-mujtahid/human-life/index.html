Source analysis to verify
Follow the juristic inquiry
  1. Establish operative facts and precise question.
  2. Compare attributed positions, distinguishing Ibn Rushd's report from each jurist's own texts.
  3. Check Qurʾānic wording, transmitted reports and relevant legal principles.
  4. Locate the earliest premise at which interpretations diverge.
  5. Distinguish legal validity, enforceability, duty, and moral praise.
  6. Record the strongest countercase and what evidence could change the analysis.

Bidāya / Law and Human Life
The expanded edition's distinctive view

From legal form to lived life

Not a proof that law automatically produces virtue. A map of **possible and contested relationships**, each requiring its own mechanism, sources and counterexamples.

Current illustration: Voluntary relinquishment of dower. Each node is a different analytical question—not a rung in a necessary causal ladder.
Each link must declare: describes · enables · tends-to · qualifies · contests · analogy · unknown
Selected relationship

Legal status is not moral praise

A legally effective action and a praiseworthy disposition are different descriptions. The second cannot be assumed from the first; intention, freedom and social pressure may matter.

Vertical horizon

Tawfīq, sincerity, divine guidance

Related sources and theological accounts may interpret action from above. Such interpretations are neither a legal ʿilla by default nor automatically the same as Thomistic grace.

Source discipline: a Khaldūnian account of crafts or social solidarity, a Ghazālian account of inward formation and a Thomistic account of prudence are different source traditions and **editorial comparisons** unless evidence shows direct reception or borrowing.

Method and research notes

These are existing editorial research discussions, reproduced from the earlier research workspace and Bidāya orientation. They are not quotations from Ibn Rushd unless a separate, attributed source excerpt is displayed. Legal method remains distinct from human-formation comparisons.

Research provenance: 5 preserved workspace chapters and 2 earlier orientation notes. Source quotations here concern al-Ḍarūrī, not a newly imported Bidāya text. Original material remains accessible under Gnamma Research.
Existing research chapter 1

Why jurists disagree

The explanation of disagreement is not complete when two legal conclusions are merely placed side by side. We also need to understand the evidentiary and interpretive operations through which each conclusion becomes intelligible.

The project investigates how Ibn Rushd’s method in al-Ḍarūrī illuminates the comparative legal discussions in Bidāyat al-Mujtahid, before asking a further question: how might forms of legal reasoning shape the capacities, practices and relationships of those who live under law?

Reading lenses
Method · Explanatory method

Distinguish reported legal positions, the stated evidence for each position, and the methodological principles applied to that evidence.

Evidence · Primary-source control

No legal opinion is assigned to Ibn Rushd or to a named school in this preview unless the relevant Bidāya passage has been checked.

Formation · Normative and human formation

The question of formation is an additional inquiry into what applying law does to persons and communities; it does not itself create legal status.

Source and citation notes

Ibn Rushd, al-Ḍarūrī, §1 — Ibn Rushd, al-Ḍarūrī fī uṣūl al-fiqh, §1, ed. Jamāl al-Dīn al-ʿAlawī (1994), p. 34; English in the supplied working edition.

Bidāya study framework — Uṣūl–Law–Rushd representative research project. The individual comparative legal dossiers and edition concordances remain pending import here.

Existing research chapter 2

A discipline with its own instruments

The opening of al-Ḍarūrī presents the work as a concise recollection of what is necessary in the craft of legal theory, drawing on al-Ghazālī’s al-Mustaṣfā. But an abridgement need not reproduce its source’s architecture or disciplinary assumptions without alteration.

The distinction between a claim about the proper method of inquiry and a claim about a determinate ruling matters throughout the research: legal theory is not a substitute for working through the particulars.

Attributed parallel text: Ibn Rushd, al-Ḍarūrī §1
فإن غرضي في هذا الكتاب أن أثبت لنفسي، على جهة التذكرة، من كتاب أبي حامد رحمه الله في أصول الفقه الملقب بالمستصفى، جملة كافية بحسب الأمر الضروري في هذه الصناعة

My purpose in this book is to set down for myself, as a reminder, a sufficient account of what is necessary in this craft, drawn from Abū Ḥāmid’s book on the principles of jurisprudence entitled al-Mustaṣfā.

Reading lenses
Philology · Ṣināʿa

The immediate context suggests a rule-governed craft or discipline. This interpretation is distinguished from a bare dictionary equivalent.

Method · Al-Ghazālī and Ibn Rushd

One must separate the work Ibn Rushd abridges from the changes of arrangement, compression and criticism that he makes.

Comparative · A wider philosophical comparison

An Aristotelian or Thomistic comparison is a conceptual reconstruction unless historical dependence is separately established.

Source and citation notes

Ibn Rushd, al-Ḍarūrī §1, p. 34 — Ibn Rushd, al-Ḍarūrī fī uṣūl al-fiqh, §1, ed. Jamāl al-Dīn al-ʿAlawī (1994), p. 34. Working English translation.

Existing research chapter 3

Knowledge ordered to belief and action

In the subsequent methodological discussion Ibn Rushd distinguishes kinds of knowledge in part according to their ends: knowledge directed towards belief and knowledge directed towards action. This is not simply an opposition between abstract theory and unreasoned practice.

The legal question is how knowledge of universal principles and knowledge of particular rulings can remain appropriately related without turning one into a mechanical replacement for the other.

Attributed parallel text: Ibn Rushd, al-Ḍarūrī §2 (abridged excerpt)
إن المعارف والعلوم ثلاثة أصناف: إما معرفة غايتها الاعتقاد الحاصل عنها في النفس فقط ... وإما معرفة غايتها العمل

Forms of knowledge and the sciences are of three kinds. There is knowledge whose end is simply the belief it produces in the soul ... There is knowledge whose end is action.

Reading lenses
Philology · Kullī and juzʾī

Universal and particular describe differing levels of inquiry; the terms do not justify suppressing the particulars of a dispute.

Method · Rules for sound inquiry

The longer passage explicitly describes rules and conditions that guide the intellect towards correctness.

Formation · The agent of practical judgement

Further research asks whether competence is only procedural skill or contributes to the habits and dispositions of a skilled judge.

Source and citation notes

Ibn Rushd, al-Ḍarūrī §2, pp. 34–35 — Ibn Rushd, al-Ḍarūrī, §2, ed. al-ʿAlawī (1994), pp. 34–35. Ellipses indicate omissions from the translated passage.

Existing research chapter 4

Juristic competence as disciplined exertion

Ijtihād is described as the jurist’s exertion of capacity through instruments required of the qualified practitioner. The formulation does not license unfettered personal interpretation.

This account opens a question about the education of judgement: which forms of training make a legal reasoner capable of discerning a ruling when several forms of evidence are available?

Attributed parallel text: Ibn Rushd, al-Ḍarūrī §231
أَمَّا الِاجْتِهَادُ فَهُوَ بَذْلُ الْمُجْتَهِدِ وُسْعَهُ فِي الطَّلَبِ بِالْآلَاتِ الَّتِي تُشْتَرَطُ فِيهِ.

Independent inquiry is the jurist’s exertion of his utmost capacity in seeking an answer by means of the instruments required of him.

Reading lenses
Method · Instruments and conditions

The definition builds competence into the practice of ijtihād; it cannot be reduced to choosing between established positions.

Philology · Meaning of ijtihād

The technical sense here requires the text's definition rather than inference from the root alone.

Comparative · Prudentia and phronēsis

Comparisons with Aristotelian phronēsis or Thomistic prudentia must preserve differences in disciplinary object and evidentiary method.

Source and citation notes

Ibn Rushd, al-Ḍarūrī §231, p. 137 — Ibn Rushd, al-Ḍarūrī fī uṣūl al-fiqh, §231, ed. al-ʿAlawī (1994), p. 137. Supplied working translation.

Existing research chapter 5

From valid inference to human formation

A juridical ʿilla is not identical to the wisdom, benefit or spiritual disposition that one may associate with a practice. The difference is crucial if a study of law is to connect determination of rulings with their effects on human and communal life.

This proposed extension should therefore be treated as a separate, source-controlled inquiry. It can seek goods and dispositions that practices foster, without claiming that they are already the operative causes of those rulings.

Reading lenses
Formation · Practising a rule

Study formation through living and applying law rather than treating ethical effects as a mechanism of legal status creation.

Method · Distinction of ʿilla and ḥikma

The legally operative ground and the wider wisdom of a rule need distinct treatments.

Comparative · Cross-tradition comparison

Connections to Aristotelian–Thomistic accounts of law are philosophical proposals subject to independent source verification.

Earlier orientation · 1

From disagreements to reasoning

Juristic disagreement becomes philosophically interesting when the competing positions are examined together with the textual evidence, assumptions about language, and principles of interpretation that make each position intelligible. The project compares Ibn Rushd’s legal-method analysis in al-Ḍarūrī with the concrete disagreements catalogued in Bidāyat al-Mujtahid.

Editorial project summary, not a quotation from Ibn Rushd. Complete Bidāya reference dossiers pending import.
Positions

Preserve the historical positions and exact attribution of each school or individual jurist.

Evidence

Link Qurʾān, ḥadīth, editions and commentary with a controlled citation record.

Method

Show the legal principles used in arriving at divergent rulings.

Formation

Ask about goods, virtues and dispositions without confusing ḥikma with a juridically operative ʿilla.

Earlier orientation · 2

General and particular

An adequate methodological account distinguishes the general disciplines that furnish rules of inquiry from their application to particular legal cases. It must also avoid identifying each moral good with a technical legal cause, or assuming that later philosophical reconstructions are themselves Ibn Rushd’s conclusions.

Editorial synthesis for the Uṣūl–Law–Rushd project; not authorial Ibn Rushd text.
