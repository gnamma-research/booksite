Lecture notes on Bidāyat al-Ḥikma

Section-by-section source concordance and selective explanatory annotations drawn from notes by Aanand Krishnan based on lectures of Shaykh Mohammad Ali Shomali.

Attribution and method

The user-supplied 268-page document identifies Aanand Krishnan as the note-taker and Shaykh Mohammad Ali Shomali as the lecturer. It is not a verified verbatim transcript. All additional explanations below are editorial paraphrases and judgments, never wording silently imputed to the lecturer.

The PDF’s printed page numbering begins after the cover and contents; PDF page numbers are one higher than the printed folio. The uploaded notes are used as a source and are not being publicly republished wholesale. The table records all 110 authorial chapters; 109 have a direct lecture locator, while one is not separately listed in the lecture contents. 21 passages have a substantive original editorial annotation. This concordance is not a complete transcription or detailed critical commentary on every line.

Where the lecture notes explain elementary logic (e.g. conception, genus/differentia, two kinds of predication), they can be placed as short footnotes. Where they offer disputed metaphysical claims or compress different thinkers’ positions, they require checking against the Arabic primary text and academic scholarship before being added as a note.

1.1THE SELF-EVIDENT CHARACTER OF THE MEANING OF EXISTENCEPrinted p. 9; PDF p. 10A self-evident concept (badīhī) does not need to be inferred from a prior definition. The notes distinguish our clear conception of existence from the philosophical difficulty of explaining the reality of existence.
1.2THE CONCEPT OF EXISTENCE IS UNIVOCALPrinted p. 10; PDF p. 11The notes distinguish a shared meaning (ishtirāk maʿnawī) from accidental homonymy (ishtirāk lafẓī): saying God and a human exist need not make their modes of existence identical.
1.3EXISTENCE IS ADDITIONAL TO QUIDDITYPrinted p. 12; PDF p. 13The notes distinguish the concept of a quiddity from an existential assertion. The ability to deny that a quiddity is instantiated helps show that existence is not a constituent of its essential definition.
1.4THE FUNDAMENTAL REALITY OF EXISTENCEPrinted p. 13; PDF p. 14The lecture explains the primacy of existence (aṣālat al-wujūd) through concrete effects, unity of predication, and external versus mental existence. Use the author’s own four arguments as controlling evidence.

Corrections required before use

The student notes at printed p. 27 momentarily confuse the five kinds of substance with genus and differentia; the list is matter, form, body, soul, intellect. At printed p. 61, the example involving God appears to invert imkān ʿāmm and imkān khāṣṣ. Both are flagged rather than reproduced as authoritative explanations.

The most valuable material for a reader is on printed pp. 9–10 (self-evidence and definition), 24–25 (simple and composite existential propositions), 36–49 (mental existence), 50–52 (linking and independent existence), 64–69 (causal dependence), 71–75 (conditioned quiddity), 139–141 (predication), and 194–220 (knowledge and intellectual consideration).
