Three distinct claims

The question is not whether every definition must contain existence. First, an essential definition (ḥadd) seeks a thing’s quiddity (māhiyya) through its intrinsic constituents, conventionally genus and differentia. Second, what a contingent thing is does not entail that it exists. Third, existence itself does not have genus and differentia; it therefore cannot receive the type of essential definition appropriate to a quiddity.

Logic supplies the account of what a definition is. First philosophy supplies the argument that existence does not fall under such a definition. The latter conclusion is not simply an axiom of introductory logic.

Source concordance: Bidāya I.1 discusses why existence is self-evident and cannot be defined by ḥadd or rasm; I.3 establishes the conceptual distinction of existence from quiddity; I.4 argues for the primacy of existence. The new two-book reader allows these passages to be read in their original order alongside Nihāya I–II.

Which logic books belong to the seminary setting?

Actual programmes vary by school, period, and specialisation. The following separates the ordinary modern textbook from older and advanced sources: a curriculum survey notes al-Muẓaffar’s replacement of the older Mullā ʿAbd Allāh text in many Shiʿi seminaries, while one instructor’s teaching record documents both al-Muẓaffar and later Nihāya courses.

Critical point. The complete essential definition uses the proximate genus and specific differentia. To know the quiddity of a thing is not yet to prove that any instance exists. Conversely, a proposition asserting existence is not itself a complete essential definition. This is why Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the intelligibility of the notion of existence is prior to the attempt to define it.

Read al-Muẓaffar’s Arabic text and contents. A later printed commentary by Kamāl al-Ḥaydarī discusses the requirement that a complete ḥadd involve the essential constituents, and observes how difficult it is to attain the genuine differentiæ of natural things. See the commentary.

From Avicenna to Ṭabāṭabāʾī

Avicenna, al-Shifāʾ, al-Madkhal: the quiddity considered merely as itself is distinct from the conditions under which it is mental or extramental. This prepares the logical analysis of universal and particular and the ways of considering a nature.

Avicenna, al-Shifāʾ, al-Ilāhiyyāt I.5: being, thing and necessity are among the primary intelligible meanings. Being is not defined by anything more evident. The argument that being lacks a quiddity answerable by genus and differentia belongs to first philosophy, although it uses logic’s account of definition. Bertolacci, SEP.

Avicenna, al-Shifāʾ, al-Burhān: the demanding framework for scientific explanation, the role of causal middle terms, and the differences between defining a subject and demonstrating its attributes. A reconstruction of Bidāya and Nihāya should not rely solely on the brief introductory definition sections of a logic primer.

Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī: the critic of Avicenna in the Ishārāt commentary. His questions are philosophical and argumentative rather than the historical source of the basic five-universals doctrine.

Quṭb al-Dīn al-Rāzī: a different scholar, the major commentator on al-Shamsiyya; do not confuse him with Fakhr al-Dīn. The commentarial tradition adds difficult discussions of identity, conception, predication and the structure of definition. Both names matter, but in different textual lines.

Ṭabāṭabāʾī: inherits these Avicennian logical analyses, then uses them within the metaphysical doctrines of the primacy and gradation of existence. The logical structure of a real definition is traditional; the full Ṣadrian metaphysical synthesis is not thereby established by logic alone.

Best selections for a future critical translation

I recommend a small parallel reader of the relevant arguments rather than immediately translating every logic textbook. Its core would consist of Avicenna’s Shifāʾ, Madkhal on quiddity and its considerations; Burhān on real definitions and the demonstration of existence; and Ilāhiyyāt I.5 on primary meanings. Beside these, link the concise definitions in al-Muẓaffar and the relevant chapters of the older Shamsiyya and Tahdhīb traditions.

The distinction between material/textual layers is essential. Original historical Arabic passages, fresh English translation, excerpts from modern commentators and modern explanation must be labelled separately. The published contemporary translation of al-Manṭiq should be used as a reference only unless republication rights are confirmed. For modern Arabic sources, brief quotations with bibliographic citation can assist comparison without reproducing a complete copyrighted edition.

The existing Tahdhīb al-Manṭiq working edition supplies a natural starting point for linked terminology. In the Ḥikma reader, the top-level cross-reference should point back to the arguments on the primacy of existence and the question of what belongs to a quiddity.
