Source method

The early printed witness is Qum 1404 AH, supplied by Princeton through NYU’s Arabic Collections Online. The individual item identifies its materials as public domain; ACO describes its selection of out-of-copyright works. This source basis concerns the authorial Arabic work, not separately authored modern translations or later editorial additions.

The Arabic transcription and page locators follow Rafed’s transcription of the fourth edition, Qum 1417 AH. The original authorial text was separated from the later publisher’s and editor’s introductions, footnotes, bibliography, and indexes. Those additions have not been translated or reproduced.

The two editions have different pagination. The per-unit source references follow the 1417 transcription. The table below records separately the 1404 printed pages and physical PDF pages. A numerical offset between the two editions is not assumed.

Quoted passages from earlier philosophers are part of Ṭabāṭabāʾī’s argument and are translated where they occur. They have not all been independently checked against the works quoted. The reader preserves the author’s attribution without treating every historical attribution as separately verified.

Translation checks and remaining review

A targeted comparison of III.1 and XI.1–2 with the Arabic checked the distinctions between primary and common predication, acquired knowledge and knowledge by presence, and the union of knower and known. In XI.1, wahm is rendered “misapprehension” in the passage about mistakenly identifying the cognitive form with the material form, to keep it distinct from takhayyul, “imagination.” The choice is recorded in a translation note.

Structural checks verify that all 136 source identifiers occur in order, that the numbered chapters total 130, that every reading section has English, that the Arabic is preserved apart from the recorded XII.9 correction, and that the text download matches the reader. Such checks do not establish the accuracy of every translated sentence.

The English is a complete working rendering wherever the coverage table records a translation, not a claim of completed specialist certification. Exhaustive line-by-line collation of the Arabic with the scan, independent specialist Arabic–English review, systematic verification of quotations, and manuscript work remain open. No complete collation against an existing English translation is claimed.

The glossary supplies editorial explanations and consistent working terminology. It is not copied from Qarāʾī’s glossary. Its source links point to relevant discussions in the new reader.

Bidāyat al-Ḥikma

The author's introduction and first four numbered chapters have a fresh working English translation. All 110 numbered chapters are present in original Arabic; the other 106 are marked pending. Its dedicated website entry contains 110 original Arabic chapters, a newly translated introduction and four chapters, plus thirteen editorial guide sections. The Arabic in those guide sections is editorial guidance, not the author’s text.

The existing published English translation is The Elements of Islamic Metaphysics, translated by Sayyid ʿAlī Qulī Qarāʾī, ICAS Press. It is linked bibliographically; its English text is not reproduced. The user-supplied 325-page Arabic–English PDF is now the transcription source for all authorial Arabic chapters. Its embedded published English remains a comparison source, not published as a fresh translation. Arabic OCR/PDF glyph extraction requires character-level proofing.
