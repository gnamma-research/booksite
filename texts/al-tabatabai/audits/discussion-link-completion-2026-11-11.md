## Corrections after the second editorial review

- Bidāya X.10 affirmatively makes where a kind of position. Both directions now quote that assertion and preserve its difference from Nihāya VI.17 and the later qualification in IX.7.
- Nihāya IV.8 has precise counterparts in Muẓaffar’s implicative/coincidental conditional distinction and Bidāya’s predication about absolute nonexistence.
- Nihāya IX.2 develops Bidāya’s argument from degrees of preparedness.
- Nihāya XII.6 shares the derivation of sole lordship from the dependence of substances, effects and relations; its further unity-of-order argument remains qualified.
- Nihāya XII.17 develops Bidāya’s correspondence and graded perfection of the intellectual, imaginal and material realms into an explicit optimality conclusion.
- Nihāya XII.18 shares both the sword/privation example and the fivefold good/evil classification. Its additional pain, presential-knowledge and divine-decree arguments are stated in the cautions and the new Good and evil entry.

## Distinctions preserved

- A mental form is distinguished from an external instance of everything it represents.
- A concept’s universality does not make a second Necessary Existent possible.
- Logical graded predication is distinguished from the metaphysical gradation of existence.
- An accidental predicate is distinguished from an accident inhering in a subject.
- Intellectual consideration retains its different logical, philosophical and practical uses.
- Inquiry-acquired knowledge (kasbī) is distinguished from representational knowledge (ḥuṣūlī).
- Logical entailment, a reason for assent and a cause of being retain their distinct roles.
- Divine will, speech and providence preserve the author’s qualifications of reported accounts.
