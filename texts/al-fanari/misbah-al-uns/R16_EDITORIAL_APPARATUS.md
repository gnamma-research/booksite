# R16 editorial apparatus

First-pass electronic-source translation; no independent print or manuscript collation. Editorial notes are separated from the reading text. Earlier registers and the original R15 passage notes retain their authority.

## Main distinctions

- §§4.1036–1054: varieties of response to supplication; preparedness, means, and perfect compliance. The first-person visionary account in §4.1051 belongs to the quoted Qūnawī.
- §§4.1055–1074: apprehension, veiling and manifestation. Preserve the difference between effects appearing through the Real and changes occurring in the Essence. §4.1113 uses a differently qualified wording and remains flagged.
- §§4.1075–1090: knowledge always entails action, but action is not always its end; distinguish acquired perfection from intrinsic superabundant perfection.
- §§4.1091–1141: intelligible, spiritual, spoken and written speech; reciprocal mirrors; divine address; comprehensive Muḥammadan revelation.
- §§5.1–19: sustained al-Farghānī material on the first and second isthmuses, Aḥmadan light, prophetic mediation and Muḥammadan manifestation. Historical astronomical, embryological and reported material is translated as source claims.
- §§5.20–60: questions of self-knowledge, correspondence of human and cosmos, three ranks of perfection, Names and essential self-disclosure. Technical التمحض والتشكيك remains provisional.

## Passage notes

### §4.1041 · source labels 567–568

The distinction between response and delivery of the requested object is essential. Quotations from Ibn ʿArabī’s Sethian Fuṣūṣ and al-Jandī’s commentary remain pending independent textual collation.

### §4.1048 · source labels 569–569

The reports are given in the wording and abbreviated form of the source; no independent hadith collation is claimed.

### §4.1049 · source labels 569–570

The source calls the list ‘four’ while distinguishing العمر and الأجل and pairing السعادة والشقاوة; the count is preserved rather than silently harmonised.

### §4.1051 · source labels 570–570

The first-person speaker remains Qūnawī in the quotation begun in §4.1050; ‘our Shaykh’ refers within that quotation. The Abū Ṭālib attribution is retained but requires independent verification; it must not be silently replaced by a different familiar report.

### §4.1055 · source labels 572–572

The comparison of restricted and unrestricted and its asymmetric identity claims are retained as stated. The repeated ‘beyond’ marks the argument’s conceptual limit, not a spatial location beyond God.

### §4.1058 · source labels 573–573

The conclusion بل محتاجا إليه is retained as passive ‘needed’, rather than silently converted to ‘in need’. The compressed regress argument and its pronouns require printed-text review.

### §4.1060 · source labels 574–574

The maxim is translated as used in the argument, without assigning a hadith grade or asserting prophetic authorship.

### §4.1063 · source labels 575–575

The Arabic’s disjunctive syntax is highly compressed. The English makes its argumentative branches explicit; no additional metaphysical premise is supplied.

### §4.1070 · source labels 576–577

The source has several difficult phrases, notably والصيقل بعدمه وتهيؤ المقبول and فانطباع … وذلك لانطباعها … هو اختلافهما. The translation follows the mirror analogy provisionally; the full Arabic is retained for collation.

### §4.1071 · source labels 577–578

الأرواح العبقة is interpreted as fragrant breezes in context, alongside النسائم الطيبة; vocalisation and printed collation remain open. The optical physiology is the source’s historical account.

### §4.1074 · source labels 579–580

The hours/opening/madness/death claim is an attributed historical saying, not a verified outcome or a practice recommendation. The Arabic المدرك is unvowelled and may be read as apprehended or apprehender; the rendering is provisional.

### §4.1082 · source labels 582–582

The closing wording فإنما يتعين ذلك بالنسبة والغرض وغاية للمراتب is compressed and possibly defective; the English gives its relational sense provisionally.

### §4.1083 · source labels 582–583

أكملية / كمالية are distinguished here as superabundant or greater perfection versus perfection acquired as an end. The passage does not deny wisdoms or benefits in divine acts.

### §4.1093 · source labels 585–585

The witness reads آليا, apparently a defective form of إلهيا (‘divine’), as indicated by the divine/cosmic division in §4.1096. The reading is explicit here and Arabic is unchanged.

### §4.1094 · source labels 585–586

The final sentence is repunctuated provisionally: يجمعه وتلك الحقيقة موطن is read as ‘an abode unites the perceiver and that reality’.

### §4.1103 · source labels 587–588

The witness has آلة يقع and الالى; the first is interpreted as a defective relative clause (‘in which’), the second as ‘divine’, supported by the surrounding division. Arabic is preserved without conjectural correction.

### §4.1104 · source labels 588–588

The repeated clause وبين الأرواح الباقية is syntactically awkward; it is included in ‘the other spirits among which mutual address occurs’, not treated as an additional unseen category.

### §4.1105 · source labels 588–588

الالى is again read as ‘divine’ in context; the witness is unchanged.

### §4.1110 · source labels 589–589

The antecedent of وان تعين بالتعين الأول is compressed and retained provisionally. The distinction between relative and absolute unseen is explicitly perspectival.

### §4.1113 · source labels 590–590

The source here says بالحق وفيه (‘through and in the Real’), whereas §§4.1061–64 expressly distinguish ‘through Him, not in Him’. Both wordings are preserved, with this passage’s qualification ‘insofar as He is light and Being’; harmonisation requires further collation and analysis.

### §4.1117 · source labels 591–591

The source distinguishes a real cause from a customary cause and rejects independent philosophical causality for the Pen. The human/divine correspondence remains an analogy with distinctions specified in the next unit.

### §4.1122 · source labels 592–593

The source’s نعم قد is irregular; ‘yes’ renders its answer without inventing a missing continuation. The three degrees follow the initial simple conception, as §4.1123 explicitly explains.

### §4.1123 · source labels 593–594

The witness’s تعين الحق، إلى النفس الرحماني is unstable; the explanatory ‘that is’ is provisional, not a silent emendation of the Arabic.

### §4.1124 · source labels 594–594

The witness has الثانية عند الحق, interpreted provisionally as الثابتة (‘established’), since the second reported matter is introduced in §4.1131. Its Qurʾān 50:16 quotation contains إليكم إليه; the English gives the coherent cited wording while retaining the full Arabic witness. The fourfold count is preserved as in §4.1049.

### §4.1125 · source labels 594–594

العيَدانية appears to be a defective reading for عبْدانية (‘servanthood’); the contextual interpretation is explicit and Arabic unchanged. المجازاة here includes reciprocal exchange, before its application to recompense.

### §4.1128 · source labels 595–596

The source’s strong claims about reduced obligation and special permissions are retained as part of the quoted metaphysical account. They are not converted into practical exemptions for readers; earlier §§4.952–56, 4.974–80 expressly preserve obligation and distinctions of rank.

### §4.1129 · source labels 596–596

المرأة is read contextually as المرآة (‘mirror’); the witness is unchanged. The pronouns in the final clause are interpreted as referring to the thing reflected.

### §4.1133 · source labels 596–597

The closing ولكله عموما is uncertain; ‘the whole more generally’ preserves its scope without specifying an absent noun. The repeated reference to the Muḥammadan manifestation’s states is retained in substance.

### §4.1139 · source labels 599–599

The first-person vision belongs to Qūnawī’s quoted Nafaḥāt, not Fanārī or the translator. The figures and visionary claims are preserved as reported, without independent historical or hadith verification.

### §5.2 · source labels 600–600

الأحدية and الواحدية are distinguished as Oneness (aḥadiyya) and inclusive Unity (wāḥidiyya), rather than treated as synonyms.

### §5.3 · source labels 600–601

The source’s section 5.4 marker is broken across a line after this verse; it is restored as a separate numbered unit. The two light reports are translated as cited, without independent authentication.

### §5.4 · source labels 601–601

Recovered from the explicit source marker ‘4’ followed on the next continuation line by ‘- 5’; the section is not an invented subdivision.

### §5.6 · source labels 601–601

The relative clause الذي هو الخاتم أيضا is syntactically difficult; it is provisionally related to the inheritor. The source distinguishes inheritance of walāya from inheritance of prophethood. وترية means oddness, not singularity simpliciter.

### §5.9 · source labels 602–602

The celestial and stellar causal vocabulary is translated as historical cosmology; its empirical validity is not asserted.

### §5.11 · source labels 603–603

The witness has أو كان اثر where the context appears to continue with ‘the effect was strongest’; this conjunction remains a textual uncertainty. The astronomical identifications are retained as the source’s cosmology.

### §5.13 · source labels 604–604

The stray character ژ between الكامل and ووجوده is retained in the Arabic witness and treated as a transcription artefact, not a word with translated content.

### §5.14 · source labels 604–605

The sevenfold hierarchy and the broader use of ‘possessors of resolve’ are the source’s metaphysical scheme, not a silently substituted conventional enumeration.

### §5.15 · source labels 605–605

The witness has الانصياع twice, provisionally interpreted as الانصباغ (‘being coloured’), consistent with the surrounding argument. Arabic remains unchanged.

### §5.18 · source labels 605–606

This is al-Farghānī’s metaphysical account of prophetic generation, including historical embryological and astrological concepts; it is not presented as modern biological evidence.

### §5.19 · source labels 606–606

Ends the sustained al-Farghānī account introduced in §5.1. ‘His being sought’ (مطلوبية) and ‘his being beloved’ (محبوبية) are kept distinct from his own seeking and loving.

### §5.20 · source labels 606–606

The electronic marker reads 520 - 5. Sequential context (19, 520, 521, 22) establishes the intended reader label §5.20; the raw marker is retained in provenance.

### §5.21 · source labels 606–607

The witness marker 521 - 5 is normalised to §5.21 with the raw form preserved. التمحض والتشكيك are technical and provisionally rendered ‘exclusive identification and graded differentiation’, not purity and scepticism; later usage should be collated before finalising the glossary.

### §5.35 · source labels 608–608

معنى يقوم بنفسه is provisionally read as a meaning subsisting in the person himself, contrasted with a form standing in its own right outside him. The pronoun’s reference should be checked against print.

### §5.40 · source labels 608–608

قرآنية / فرقانية invoke gathering and differentiation respectively. The final حقيقية may represent حقيقتِه (‘his reality’); the translation follows the human–world correspondence provisionally.

### §5.41 · source labels 608–609

The descending logical sequence is preserved as the source calls it ‘highest genera’; it is not silently corrected to a standard Porphyrian classification.

### §5.44 · source labels 609–609

Identity is asserted within the two explicitly qualified perspectives of §5.43. The translation does not turn it into an unqualified identity between contingent limitations and the Essence as such.

### §5.47 · source labels 610–611

The source explicitly distinguishes intellectual accord with the divine Will from natural or legal disapproval; neither is omitted. الانتفاء is interpreted as الانتفاع (‘benefit’), and the technical pair التمحض والتشكيك remains provisional as in §5.21.

### §5.58 · source labels 612–613

The phrase يبحر ساحة القلب وشرع جداوله and the following علوا are textually difficult; the sea-and-stream rendering is provisional. The source’s Qurʾānic reference lacks the verse number, supplied here as 40:16. The poem remains unattributed in this passage.

### §5.60 · source labels 614–615

The source itself qualifies continuous witnessing through the requirement of comprehensive manifestation. This entire unit is translated, although it reaches the following source-page label; that following page is not counted complete because §5.61 also occupies it.
