## Main analytical results

### A. Four considerations of ontic determination (§§4.577–4.581)

The four ways of considering a reality include its being (1) an essential Name insofar as it is a determination in knowledge, (2) a locus of divine manifestation insofar as it is an image of self-disclosure, (3) a manifestation of the Essence **only insofar as** the comprehensive Names or loci are considered, not of the Essence *qua* absolute, and (4) a ruling/state of the first determination concealed in the undifferentiated depth of the Essence. The fourth does not posit a multiplicity of self-subsistent divine constituents. In §4.580, the contrast between **manifestation to the Real within knowledge** and **manifestation of determinate realities to themselves or to one another in concrete existence** is vital to the project's study of the fixed entities (al-aʿyān al-thābita). The text explicitly excludes `ḥulūl`, `ittiḥād`, and `inqisām`.

**Terms to preserve:** `taʿayyun ʿilmī` (epistemic / knowledge-determination), `taʿayyun ʿaynī` (concrete determination), `aḥadiyya` (unicity), `iṭlāq` (absoluteness / unrestrictedness), `buṭūn` (inwardness), `ẓuhūr` (manifestation), `majlā` (locus of disclosure).

**Open critical issue:** §4.580's compressed construction `ففي كل وجود عيني خلق تعينه العيني الوجودي صورة تعينه العلمي النسبي ... وحق بلا حلول` must be checked against Khvājavī's printed punctuation, visual layout, and independent witnesses; the “creaturely / Real” translation remains a reasoned first-pass reading rather than a resolved textual emendation.

### B. Throne, Footstool, spheres and the celestial symbolism (§§4.582–4.606)

- Qūnawī's distinction as transmitted in §4.583: the Throne corresponds to `al-qalam`, the all-encompassing Name and `al-Raḥmān`; the Footstool to `al-lawḥ al-maḥfūẓ`, the detailing of determinate existences and `al-Raḥīm`.
- Ibn ʿArabī's reported celestial schema (§§4.584–4.606) associates the two relational spheres with two further spheres, then twelve angelic zodiacal divisions and elemental triplicities, lunar mansions and articulated sounds. Its calendrical/astrological correspondences are preserved as *historical metaphysical cosmology*, not verified scientific descriptions.
- In §4.594 the Pisces division is said to be `بارد يابس` (cold/dry), though the familiar elemental system often attributes cold/moist to Pisces. No silent correction has been made.
- In §4.602, the electronic witness reads `الف مرتبة واحدى وعشرين مرتبة`, translated *one thousand and twenty-one degrees*. This number requires print/witness checking.
- In §4.603, `حروف ... خارجة عن حد الاستقامة` and references to between `bāʾ`/`fāʾ` and `jīm`/`shīn` appear to refer to variant or intermediary articulation; exact technical force requires checking the parallel source.
- The embedded attribution `ابن مسرة الجيلي` (§4.586) needs independent identification and collation. The Qurʾānic phrase from 33:43 is marked with a parenthetic correction `عليك (عليكم)` in the source transcription; do not normalise the quotation's witness silently.

### C. A precise reconciliation (§§4.607–4.609)

Fanārī poses a **real textual tension**: Qūnawī's accounts imply two enduring spheres, while Ibn ʿArabī and, apparently, al-Jandī speak of four layered bodies. His answer draws on al-Farghānī's preface: the Throne and Footstool can each be contemplated under an imaginal, rank-specific aspect, or under a bodily aspect made up of length, breadth and depth. Thus there need not be four distinct *things* merely because four names and descriptions occur. Fanārī adds historical support by reporting that Qūnawī reviewed and approved al-Farghānī's preface.

This is not a demonstration that all four accounts are literally identical in every statement; it is a **proposed harmonisation** grounded in the distinction of considerations and in a report of Qūnawī's reception of al-Farghānī. It closely mirrors the preceding distinction between a single reality and its multiple determinations.
