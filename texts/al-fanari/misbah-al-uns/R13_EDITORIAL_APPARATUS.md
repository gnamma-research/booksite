## 2. Key doctrinal architecture and analytical relevance

### Al-Jandī: one cosmological source and articulated spheres (611–617)

Al-Jandī develops an account in which Nature is the external expression of Divinity, while its inner identity lies in divine, unitive activity, and the **divine Word** is one at the level of the Throne. At the Footstool the Word bears the distinction of **ruling (ḥukm)** and **communication (khabar)**, symbolized by the Two Feet; this differentiation is then correlated with legal categories, zodiacal divisions and the differentiated spheres. His account preserves **one real agency** without denying the relational articulation of the manifestations. Translators must not turn `ظاهرية الإلهية` into a pantheistic identity of one physical substance with God or set up a second independent ultimate cause.

### Al-Farghānī: imaginal/bodily considerations of two great forms (618–632)

The Throne and Footstool each bear **imaginal** and **bodily** considerations. The Throne's three dimensions correspond to descent, differentiation, and the governance preserving forms. The Footstool's three faces are (1) toward **necessity/spirits**, (2) toward **possibility and sense/witnessing**, and (3) their comprehensive **Cloud-isthmus**. §4.628 distinguishes a measured **daily amount of time** from time as such; do not conflate them. The three gardens (§§4.630–631) and the interpretation of the Two Feet as **command and prohibition** (§4.632) articulate descent to multiplicity and return to unity. The reader is explicitly historical/descriptive, not a model of modern celestial mechanics or hadith authentication.

### Fanārī and Qūnawī: attributed causal role of manifestation (633–636)

§4.635 sets out three different senses of efficacy attributed to created/spiritual loci: (i) outward causal/conditional preparation, (ii) specifying or determining the effect without bringing its manifestation about, (iii) the unitive self-disclosure manifest within the locus **itself** bringing about appearance. The third is presented as the correct deepest explanation. This is important for cross-comparison with Avicenna, post-Avicennan causality, Ashʿarī kalām, and Akbarī ontological pluralism.

### Illuminationist framework: vertical/horizontal orders and correspondences (637–648)

Fanārī invokes a hierarchy of **dominant lights** and **governing lights**, vertical (al-ṭūliyya) and horizontal (al-ʿarḍiyya) orders, with images of imaginal forms and sensory forms. He uses these to explain why the Throne can be associated both with the First Intellect/Pen and with the Tablet through different considerations. §4.648 asserts the premodern notion of correspondence between intelligible and bodily worlds. Do not rephrase this claim as an empirically established account of stellar causation.

## 3. Explicitly unresolved readings

1. **§4.611:** The quotation `أول ما خلق الله الدرة` (“The first thing God created was the Pearl”) must be identified/authenticated; do not imply a rigorously authenticated hadith. Clarify the fifth unitive nature in relation to four underlying realities.
2. **§4.612:** `وبعد الرب قصد توجه الاسم الغنى` is syntactically problematic as transcribed. English is provisional; inspect Khvājavī printed layout and parallel al-Jandī source.
3. **§4.614:** Three hundred and sixty “faces” of the First Intellect are translated exactly; symbolic number not modern astronomical evidence.
4. **§4.615:** Text gives five legal qualifications, then a multiplication of two and six to reach twelve without explicit enumeration of the sixth factor. Preserve the stated factors and flag the intermediate link instead of silently supplying it.
5. **§4.617:** `نور التجلي النفسي الرحماني` may be a scribal/OCR confusion with `النفَسي` (Breath-related). Check source instead of silently changing its vocalization.
6. **§4.623:** `استواء` is expounded by multiple lexical considerations—stability, predominance, completeness/maturity, directedness and equilibrium. Do not reduce to physical sitting, and distinguish quotation from the translator's interpretation.
7. **§4.630:** **Critical possible corruption:** digital reading `أصل لخبان`. Context strongly suggests `أصل لجنان`, so English cautiously renders “origin of the gardens”, with explicit conjectural note. **Do not alter the Arabic** until print and independent witness verification. The saying of the hundred degrees, Firdaws and four rivers appears assembled; collate its component hadith clauses separately before calling the entire phrase a single authenticated tradition.
8. **§4.631:** `جنة الأعمال`, `جنة الميراث`, `جنة الامتنان` are analytically distinct; retain the explanation of the vacant paradisal dwelling as al-Farghānī's reported account.
9. **§4.634:** `لا حكم للجعل في امتياز تلك التعينات والأعيان` concerns the intrinsic distinction of determinations in knowledge, not a denial of their ontological dependence. Compare al-Nafaḥāt before definitive wording.
10. **§4.641:** `صاحب صنم` and use of `barzakh` in Illuminationist epistemology requires checking against Suhrawardī's lexicon; provisional rendered “possessor of an image”.
11. **§4.643:** The light hierarchy's “most noble produces most noble” should not be conflated with Aristotelian substance categories or material element generation.
12. **§4.648:** The reported “al-Shīrāzī” commentary on al-Ishrāq requires identification, potentially difficult given more than one later commentary; not yet verified.
