## Stage XIV findings, pages 102–145

### The fourth foundation I, the Muhammadan synthesis (pp102–118)
Farghānī distinguishes the essential perfection of the first determination, the actual manifestation of name-related perfections, and the return to the comprehensive Muhammadan locus. The differentiation of `أحدية` and `واحدية` is by regard/rank, not literal chronology. Adamic comprehensiveness is linked to the Law (`شريعة`) as the measure of the vicegerent; the Qurʾān is associated with a twofold cosmological 'book' of creation and of speech. A point of equilibrium is `ولاية`; relatedly, prophethood and messengership are distinguished according to authorization versus command to deliver the message. Sainthood, in Farghānī's account, persists spiritually after finality of legislative prophethood; this must not be misconstrued as an opening for new prophets. The Qurʾān includes multiple hermeneutical modalities `عبارات`, `إشارات`, `دلالات`, `مفهومات`; do not collapse into mere literal expression.

### Fourth foundation II, pathology and formative path (pp119–134)
P119 is an especially explicit direct conceptual definition: `ووصول هذا المدد دائما مع الآنات عبارة عن الخلق الجديد` — 'the continuous arrival of existential assistance at each instant is what is meant by renewed creation.' This should be set beside pp66 and 73 and critically compared with Ṣadrā's doctrine without treating them as equivalent. P120 distinguishes `تسويل` from `تسويف` (misrepresentation of temptation versus delay). P121–122 present revealed Law as a general therapeutic rule and the guiding master as a skilled physician of spiritual particularities; `عرفان كسبي` can be mistaken for `كشفي شهودي`, an epistemic caution against equating scholarship with witnessed realization.

The journey's three major dimensions are Islam (outward soul), īmān (spirit), and iḥsān (inner secret). Stage XIV-B pp126–134 reviews structured tasks and stations: repentance, discipline, renunciation, entrustment, patience, gratitude, contentment, firm intention, resolve, and certainty. A true heart `قلب حقيقي` is poetically *born* from harmonious soul/spirit; the `مشيمة` imagery is metaphorical, not anatomically literal.

### Fourth foundation III, realization, stability and love (pp135–143)
Progression of states includes protective jealousy (`غيرة`), longing, anxiety, spiritual thirst, lightning and tasting; the realized path integrates `قبض` (contraction), `بسط` (expansion) and `تمكين` (stabilization). The giver/bestowed distinction `مفيض/مفاض` persists in the spiritual epistemology. P140 technical `تلبيس` means the station of appearances/assuming forms rather than 'deception'. P142 has a five-part taxonomy of love based on essence, act, state, rank and quality, taking distinct aspects of the lover-beloved relation. P143 closes the four foundations, explaining that Ibn al-Fāriḍ initially experiences vision *through action* rather than direct vision of Essence.

### Concluding supplemental preface (pp144–145)
This is **not** part of the Fourth Foundation proper nor the following verse commentary: `تتمة الديباجة` is its own textual unit. P144 distinguishes four ranks of proximity **المحبة، التوحيد، المعرفة، التحقيق** (love, unification, knowledge, realization). P145 analyzes a connection through **act, attribute and Essence**; by returning from beginning to end, their judgments are gathered in comprehensive perfection. These ranks offer the most compact pedagogical summary of the authorial entire introductory architecture.
