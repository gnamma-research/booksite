## Particularly important philological and philosophical findings

### 1. Knowledge and existence under distinct considerations (pp79–80)
The first determination permits relational detail without real otherness, whereas the second exhibits real detail in the domain of named realities. As earlier, `مفيض` (bestowing) and `مفاض` (bestowed) are distinguished within the All-merciful Breath. A single invariant 'existence' category erases the argument's subject/object polarity.

### 2. Matter and levels of causality (pp81–90)
The account of `الهباء`, qualities (heat, cold, moisture, dryness), indivisible parts, and four elements belongs to *historical natural philosophy*, not empirical contemporary atomic theory. Twelve Throne divisions are explicitly **مفروضة لا محسوسة** (conceptual, not sensed). Farghānī's cosmological account of the Footstool and time must be indexed as part of its medieval explanatory setting. At p87 the secondary transcription reproduces a modern editorial note on **محضة**: 'كذا بالأصل وهي غير ظاهرة المعنى'. This is a modern print/editorial crux, not independently verified against I1876/MS.

At p90 the effect of divine-name realities is mediated through created causes described as **معدّات لقبول أثر** (preparatory to receiving effects), while `الفعل والتأثير` are attributed to names themselves. The author allows extraordinary cases without ordinary mediators. This is not simply a denial of sensory causal regularity: rather an account of true efficacy and ordinary preparation. In a separate p90 phrase he treats earth as **كروية من حيث الإدراك العقلي ومسطحة بحسب ظاهر الإدراك**, spherical for rational apprehension and flat as it appears to the senses.

### 3. The seven-name textual crux (p89 against p38)
The Moon's correspondence explicitly uses **الاسم القائل** (p89), as do pp52,55,56 and p72 under varied contexts. This reinforces the hypothesis that p38 digital **القابل** is a scribal/print/digital error for **القائل**, but each parallel is **internal authorial context from the same modern edited textual tradition**, not independent I1876 or codex collation. Never publish an emendation as witnessed until physical images confirm it.

### 4. Natural kingdoms, human constitution and the Adamic whole (pp93–99)
The author's minerals, plants and animals correspond to graded degrees of composition, powers and equilibrium. Human constitution integrates these and is described as a comprehensive locus of the names. At pp96–97, perfection may refer to the whole's inclusive form or to the actual perfection of each part. He interprets the angels' exchange in Q2:30 as a pedagogical unveiling of their limits; his list of **eighteen** 'blameworthy' states ascribed to angels is a striking **exegetical hypothesis**, *not* an agreed doctrine about the sinlessness or fallibility of angels.

At p98 Farghānī explicitly says **فإن علم كنه الذات ممتنع ، وعلم الأسماء كلّها ممكن**: knowing the Divine Essence as such is impossible, but the names are knowable. At p99 he distinguishes `أسماء الأسماء` (verbal and graphic signs) from the existential determinations that are the names proper. The phrase **ذات آدم** disambiguates Adam's knowledge of his own created reality, rather than the Essence of God.

### 5. Anti-Avicennan conclusion (p101)
The foundation ends: **وهذا دليل واضح على أن الملائكة والأرواح والعقول لهم الترقّي والزيادة على خلاف ما زعمت الفلاسفة**. Farghānī positively argues that angels, spirits and intellects undergo progress and increase, contrary to what he attributes to philosophers. Do not claim that every philosopher in every period denied intellectual increase; identifying the specific target requires separate Avicennan comparisons.
