# Farghānī · Further direct collation of the 1876 printing — Stage VI

**Date:** 9 November 2026 · *Muntahā al-madārik*, Arabic introductory uṣūl and *tatimmat al-dībāja*. This stage **adds one independent historical-print correction and two carefully bounded agreements** to the five previously established comparisons. The 1876 facsimile is a historical **printing**, not a medieval manuscript.

## 1. Textual result: B2007 p.29, **المذكورة** → I1876 **المذكور**

The B2007 digital Beirut text reads:

> والتعيّن الأول المذكورة وقلبه المطهّر التقي النقي صورة هذا التعين والبرزخية

The historical printing, **I1876 printed p.13 / PDF scan17**, visibly reads:

> والتعيّن الأول المذكور وقلبه المطهّر التقي النقي صورة هذا التعين والبرزخية

The adjective **المذكور** (masculine singular) agrees with **التعيّن**. The feminine **المذكورة** in B2007 is difficult to explain as agreeing with that noun. **Editorial decision:** adopt I1876 `المذكور` in the provisional edited Arabic, retain B2007 `المذكورة` in the textual apparatus, and preserve the remainder of Farghānī's construction without silently resolving other pronouns or coordinations. The existing translation need not invent an object to make the adjective agree. The reading remains subject to collation against C1107, R670, K273, F3966, and Y706.

## 2. A difficult semantic passage preserved, not corrected: B2007 p23

The statement `فلم تعلم ولم يحكم بشيء منها إلا بعد تعينها` is directly present across **I1876 printed p8–9 / scans12–13**, with the beginning of the clause at the end of p8 and its completion at the start of p9. Its difficult implications for divine knowledge **cannot be dismissed as a new Beirut typo**, but the printed agreement does not authorize assimilating the text to a later theological system or claiming how Farghānī's manuscript archetype reads.

Photographs: [printed p8, scan12](https://github.com/gnamma-research/booksite/blob/master/texts/al-farghani/muntaha-al-madarik/facsimile-I1876/images/scan_0012.jpg) and [printed p9, scan13](https://github.com/gnamma-research/booksite/blob/master/texts/al-farghani/muntaha-al-madarik/facsimile-I1876/images/scan_0013.jpg). **Textual grade:** bounded historical-print agreement; **interpretive grade:** unresolved.

## 3. Mercy, nonmanifestation, and direction: B2007 p25

I1876 **printed p10 / scan14** carries the narrow wording `حكم الغضب عارجا لا نازلا`, also present in B2007 p25. This agreement establishes only the concluding directional comparison (ascending, not descending). The wider construction **`على أنها غايات`** and its attachment to what precedes remain difficult; do not revise the English on a conjectural textual basis or pretend that a printed agreement solves the syntax.

Photograph: [printed p10, scan14](https://github.com/gnamma-research/booksite/blob/master/texts/al-farghani/muntaha-al-madarik/facsimile-I1876/images/scan_0014.jpg). **Textual grade:** bounded agreement; **translation grade:** pending close grammatical adjudication.

## 5. Unresolved B2007 p33 witness target

Beirut B2007 p33's compressed `في عون هذا` falls near I1876 printed p16 (PDF scan20). The low-resolution photograph is legible enough to place the passage in its context but **not used here to certify a variant**. A higher-resolution crop/photograph and a second textual witness are required before a definitive editorial decision. In particular, do not replace `عون` with an imagined `عين` merely to remove syntactic difficulty.

## 6. What makes the next stage genuinely manuscript-critical?

First, inspect full photographic text for the unresolved p23, p25, p29, p33 loci in **at least two medieval witnesses** with dated folios and copying hands. Second, map the **entire 128-page B2007 introduction** to actual printed or manuscript foliation, recording line-by-line source decisions instead of selected isolated words. Third, choose a copy-text after distinguishing witness families and shared errors. The full working English introduction is already preserved; this edition is an *evidence-corrected working text*, **not yet a definitive Arabic critical edition**.
