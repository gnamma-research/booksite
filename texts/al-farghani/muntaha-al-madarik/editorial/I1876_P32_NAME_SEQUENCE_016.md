## The critical difference and its implications

**1876 photographic I1876 p32:** `تفرّع ( اسم الحكم ) منه` (name **al-Ḥakam** — the Judge).

**Modern digital Beirut p52:** `تفرّع ( سم الحكيم ) منه` (name **al-Ḥakīm** — the Wise; with `سم` lacking the initial `ا`).

The historical reading is now legible in the enlarged original photo rather than merely conjectured from a 760px derivative. We provisionally translate “From it branches the name **the Judge**.” But whether the modern **physical B2007 printed paper** really reads `الحكيم`, rather than its online retyping/OCR being at fault, is **not yet established**. Neither form has been checked in actual medieval manuscripts.

A further authorial comparison makes this particularly interesting: at B2007 p59 (distinct section under **al-Malik**), Farghānī also presents **al-Ḥakam** as a derivative of Sovereignty in the judgment of litigants. The p52 attribution, however, concerns the manifestation and intelligibility of what is known. Two *relations* of the same divine name—one with knowing, the other with sovereignty—are consistent with Farghānī's explicit doctrine that name affiliations can vary under **iʿtibār**. This is a proposed interpretive account, **not** an inference that both manuscript readings were necessarily al-Ḥakam.
