## M1 · Leiden University Libraries, MS Or. 5675 — authentic historical manuscript

- **Work:** `مشرع الخصوص إلى معاني النصوص` (*Mashraʿ al-khuṣūṣ ilā maʿānī al-nuṣūṣ*) by ʿAlāʾ al-Dīn ʿAlī al-Mahāʾimī, commentary on Ṣadr al-Dīn al-Qūnawī's `النصوص`.
- **Shelfmark:** **Leiden University Libraries, Or. 5675**; NOT Or. 5673 (a different manuscript).
- **Description:** Arabic, **362 folios**, dated **1142 AH / 1729–30 CE**. Jan Just Witkam's catalogue describes a fine calligraphic copy belonging formerly to Sultan Muḥammad Shifāʾ of Banten (r. 1733–1748).
- **Scribal identification in later scholarship:** A.C.S. Peacock identifies **Muḥyī al-Dīn ibn Shaykh ʿAbd al-Shakūr ibn ʿAbd al-Karīm ibn al-Sulṭān Abū al-Mafākhir** as copyist (figure 7.10 caption). His figure 3.3 refers to folios **1v–2r** of this same manuscript and identifies Sultan Abū al-Fatḥ Muḥammad Shifāʾ Zayn al-ʿĀrifīn as its owner. The book's figure 7.10 caption contains a mistaken Gregorian conversion ("1142/1749"): its own figure 3.3 has 1142/1729, as expected for that hijrī year.
- **Bibliography:** Jan Just Witkam, *Inventory of the Oriental Manuscripts of Leiden University Library*, vol. 6 (Leiden: Ter Lugt, 2007), printed p. 183, under Or. 5675 (item Ar. 2485): https://bibliography.mk.iq/files/0e8ddc804348667f.pdf
- **Further study:** A.C.S. Peacock, *Arabic Literary Culture in Southeast Asia in the Seventeenth and Eighteenth Centuries*, esp. figure captions 3.3 and 7.10, https://urn.ub.unibe.ch/urn%3Ach%3Aslsp%3Azbz%3A9004548785%3Aihv%3Apdf
- **Status:** bibliographically confirmed; **no facsimile or manuscript pages acquired/collated by this project**. A reproduction of the opening folios is *referenced* by Peacock, not yet used for textual collation. Exact ending folio and completeness at the end are unknown.
- **Acquisition priority:** high. Request a legal complete digital reproduction, or at minimum the text's ending and colophon, from Leiden University Libraries using precisely shelfmark **Or. 5675**. First inspect the final words and compare with 2008 p. 232's truncated electronic transcription `فإذا ظهرت آثار الوجود بشرط`; then investigate variants `الظهور في الوجود`/`الظهور للوجود` and `توجب`/`يوجب`/`بموجب`.

### Manuscript M1: interlinear transmission evidence (2025)

A recent independent study **actually analyzes this same manuscript**, strengthening the identification and showing a bilingual pedagogical layer: Muhammad Dluha Luthfillah, “Not the Utawi You Think You Know: Some Preliminary Notes on Early Javanese Interlinear Translations,” *Journal of Arabic and Islamic Studies* **25.3 (2025): 175–195**, DOI **10.5617/jais.12698**. The study explicitly describes Leiden Or. 5675 (1142 AH / 1729–30) as a **Banten copy with interlinear Javanese translation**. In its examples, *utawi* functions primarily as a marker of the nominal-sentence subject (*mubtadaʾ*), unlike some earlier usages connected to the conjunction `wāw al-istiʾnāf`. This bears on the history of reading and teaching the commentary, but the published linguistic observations are **not a manuscript collation performed by GNAMMA**, nor proof of the entire textual content or ending.

- Peer-reviewed published item: https://doi.org/10.5617/jais.12698
- Open study text (volume PDF): https://tawfeeq-shekh-ahmad.huji.ac.il/sites/default/files/textual_microcosms/files/001-222_intlintr_complete.pdf

The register should ultimately distinguish (a) the copyist's Arabic hand, (b) interlinear Javanese glosses and their writer/date if establishable, (c) marginalia, and (d) later owner inscriptions.

## Collation protocol once actual M1 or E1 text is available

1. Record shelfmark / edition and page or folio (recto/verso); distinguish image verification, transcription, and editorial reconstruction.
2. Compare incipit, selected central lemmata, the final text and colophon; preserve Qūnawī's cited text separately from al-Mahāʾimī's gloss.
3. Transcribe Arabic exactly at each locus, classify differences (orthography, skipped text, interpolation, substance, editor annotation).
4. Revise translation only where the Arabic evidence supports it; retain uncertainty and never turn a conjectural completion into an established original.
5. Assign real manuscript sigla only after actual witness inspection and stemmatic analysis.

**Counts at this stage: identified actual manuscripts 1; manuscript images directly inspected 0; manuscript pages collated 0.** English draft completion does *not* imply a critically established Arabic text.
