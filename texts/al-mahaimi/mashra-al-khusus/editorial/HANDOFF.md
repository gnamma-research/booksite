## New passages most crucial to the metaphysical and phenomenological project

- **pp.89–90:** why name-conditioned, restricted spiritual reports may be correct while insufficient for the encompassing station; the criterion is comprehensive validity, not mere personal authority.
- **pp.101–103 and 121–123:** predicating unity, necessity and names is already a determinate *iʿtibār*. Negative essential unrestrictedness must not be conflated with the first principial determination or with the logical application of predicates.
- **pp.108–110:** the special *tajallī dhātī barqī* is fleeting, and al-Mahāʾimī explicitly denies that withdrawal from attention implies the soul, body or faculties have ceased to exist. *Jamʿiyya* accounts for both possibility and transience of the disclosure.
- **pp.112–116:** neither the mirrors of fixed entities nor the Real under a new relation alters the intrinsic reality of the other. Yet plurality appears through relational determinations. Qūnawī calls alternative attributions of causation *ṣawāb nisbī* (relatively correct).
- **pp.117–130:** causal analysis requires *min ḥaythu huwa huwa*, and condition (*sharṭ*) must be kept distinct from cause (*sabab*); a single first intelligible effusion becomes determinate by receptivity without borrowing a separate contingency property from each medium.
- **pp.131–138:** cognition and invocation are station-qualified. Correct perception under a divine name differs from addressing a mental fantasy, but even correct intermediate cognition does not equal the perfect station. **p.137 expressly teaches that the brief essential disclosure gives rise to knowledge that abides after the immediate disclosure ceases.** The final pages distinguish intrinsically impossible things from events excluded given divine foreknowledge, and conditional from irrevocable decrees.

## Necessary editorial controls

1. **Do not count any manuscript collation**: only the page-indexed transcription was consulted.
2. **Do not silently import 2008 editor notes**: especially the long notes on pp.128, 133, 134 and the attribution note on p138. The source text of the notes was deliberately omitted from medieval authorial content.
3. **Do not call p.137 an inference**: it explicitly says the brief *barqī* witnessing yields a complete knowledge that persists in the knower's spirit after disclosure ceases.
4. **Do not conflate causal and perspectival terms**: *iʿtibār*, *nisba*, *ḥaythiyya*, *taʿayyun*, subjective attentional withdrawal and manifest effects refer to different operations.
5. **No imposed Guénonian taxonomy**: comparisons with Absolute, Universal Possibility, Non-Being or Schuon's perfections await independent exposition of the Arabophone source text.
6. **Source-check doctrinal claims** in original editions, especially the end of punishment attributed to Ibn ʿArabī on p125 and the distinctions in divine knowledge on p138.
7. **Finer paragraph alignment needed** for printed pp. 116, 118, 120, 122, 123, 124, 125, 126, 128, 129, 130, 131, 134, 135, 137, 138. Arabic and English remain fully present on those pages, but are aligned by printed page rather than paragraph.
8. **Continue with exactly printed p.139**, remaining within Text Eleven, targeting next substantial batch **pp.139–188** for 50 new printed pages, preserving the full cumulative corpus.
