# GNAMMA | Al-Mahāʾimī Stage 04 — Comparative Findings from printed pp.89–138

**Evidence:** The original Arabic and first-pass translation are in the cumulative file. Page references cite the al-Mazīdī 2008 page-indexed transcription; independent manuscripts, Āshtiyānī’s edition, and parallel critical readings are not yet collated. The comparison below is consequently a structured working interpretation, not a definitive assessment.

## A. Station-relative truth is not “anything goes”

At p.89, Qūnawī and al-Mahāʾimī distinguish a taste primarily related to a particular divine name from the distinctive taste of the most comprehensive station. The latter aspires to correspondence with the truth from a more inclusive horizon. P.116 then explicitly says a contrary formulation can nevertheless be *ṣawāb nisbī*—right in a relative sense. The relationship is not an easy verbal relativism. A restricted statement must be tested for (i) what reality is under consideration, (ii) what names or determinations are operative, (iii) the knower’s station, and (iv) what is suppressed as irrelevant under that restricted aspect. A more comprehensive statement explains rather than simply negates the earlier one. On this criterion, “God alone acts” and “effects are referred to creatures” can be ordered as different attributions, not both accepted as equally exhaustive. The causal model used to support the stronger claim remains philosophically debatable.

## B. Discriminate four operations called “difference”

**First, a conceptual consideration (*iʿtibār*).** Pp.102 and 121–122 mark names and predicates as determinate points of view on the Real; the negative unrestrictedness of the Essence cannot itself be predicated through the ordinary logical forms. Even *absolute existence* predicated as one and necessary occurs under a determinate relation. This is not the same as creating a world or acquiring a new attribute.

**Second, principial determination (*taʿayyun*).** The first determinate principle is the source of further names, and the levels of essential and act-related attributes are ordered by dependence without temporal succession (pp.121–123). The consequence is stronger than saying that humans happen to use different words, but weaker than treating each name as a separately subsisting entity.

**Third, relation (*nisba*) and conditioning.** Mirrors of fixed entities are said not to alter an intrinsically undetermined source. They furnish relations in which concealed plurality becomes manifest (pp.112–116). The particular relation is not the source’s essential constitution, and the reported absence of intrinsic causal efficacy is qualified by the account of conditions.

**Fourth, manifested effect.** Pp.117–130 distinguish *sharṭ*, on which something depends without that condition operating as an efficient producer, from *sabab*, which supplies causal efficacy within the ordered manifestation. The first intelligible effusion does not acquire a separate contingency-form as it passes through loci; its *taʿayyun* becomes intelligible under their restrictions. This is an explicit response to the problem of intermediate causes, not a proof that all ordinary causal mechanisms are illusory.

## C. The brevity of essential self-disclosure: an important source-based answer

On pp.108–109, the special essential disclosure is characterized as *barqī*, shorter than the span of two breaths. Al-Mahāʾimī expressly states that the human faculties do **not cease to exist in reality** when they are withdrawn from attention. Human comprehensiveness (*jamʿiyya*) makes a general closure of other functions possible for a moment but also demands their return; this is why the experience cannot persist in the same exclusive mode. **P.137 supplies independent interpretive confirmation:** the initial flash itself ends, but complete cognition of the divine names, levels and aspects remains in the spirit of the realized knower. The briefness is therefore first of all a description of a *mode of disclosure and human apprehension*, not a measurement of divine reality changing. It remains open whether the word “breath” is entirely phenomenological, a temporal expression for the recipient, or has a further technical meaning in Qūnawī’s broader corpus. One should not settle that question by speculation.

## D. Comprehensiveness differs from absolute union

Pp.132 and 136–137 explicitly allow the Real to be apprehended through names while excluding confinement, *ḥulūl* and *ittiḥād*. An intermediate knower can possess correct yet restricted cognition, while the Perfect Human—having experienced essential disclosure—can grasp the names without letting the names occlude the Essence or vice versa. Thus higher realization does not entail that discursive theology or name-conditioned spirituality is wholly false. It reorders them. The register must retain the distinction between *absence of a separate image in witnessing*, *absence of independent ontological reality*, and *identity in the unqualified divine Essence*.

## E. The level of divine knowledge changes the explanation of possibility

The final pages (pp.137–138) differentiate events impossible in themselves from events rendered non-actual under the contents of prior divine knowledge. The accomplished knower does not seek something absolutely excluded by knowledge of the decree; some decrees are conditional upon prayer, volition or another means, while others are not. This introduces a genuine philosophical challenge: how can condition-dependent events be eternally known without collapsing their contingency, and how can the knower’s will be a real condition rather than merely an appearance? These are not resolved by asserting either Avicennian foreknowledge or Akbarian unity. They demand direct comparison with Avicenna, Ṭūsī, later kalām and Qūnawī's full account of knowledge.

## F. Comparative matrix: provisional tests, not doctrinal equivalences

| Problem | Textual finding in al-Mahāʾimī | Comparative question |
|---|---|---|
| One Being and many determinations | Pp.102, 121–130: predicates, names and recipient-rules belong to several orders | Does Turka’s *taʿayyun* offer the same first-principle relation, or a later explicit reconstruction? |
| Relations of effect | Pp.115–119, 128–129: real difference of conditional relations without change in the Real | Does a Thomistic doctrine of participated created *esse* require an extra ontological principle absent from this model? |
| Unity and degree | Pp.121–123: first determination and nontemporal dependence | How does this differ from Ṣadrā’s intrinsic *tashkīk* of existence? |
| Human realization and temporality | Pp.108–110, 137: lightning disclosure ends; understanding continues | Which rank of cognition is being narrated, and does *nafas* refer to actual time or the knower’s experiential horizon? |
| Unmanifested possibilities | Pp.112–115: fixed entities and their effects distinguished | This does **not** yet demonstrate entities whose manifestation is intrinsically impossible. What would count as such an entity? |
| Scriptural response and causality | Pp.131–138: name-conditioned response and conditional decree | Can the metaphysics make sense of prayer changing the contingent outcome while divine knowledge remains infallible? |

## G. Questions to carry forward

1. Are the categories of *iʿtibār*, *ḥaythiyya*, *nisba*, *taʿayyun*, *shuʾūn* and the *aʿyān* related by an explicit deductive chain, or are some shifts of register only clarified by taste?
2. Does a first effusion have reality distinguishable in thought or in being from the Real's absolute existence?
3. Does p.137's durable knowledge function as a *malaka* of the soul, a nonformal form of knowledge, or something sui generis?
4. What exact principles restrict a spiritual report to one station, and by what criterion can a knower recognize that another is more comprehensive?
5. How can the apparent correctness of competing causal attributions be tested without rendering the differences vacuous?
6. Do the apparently nonproduced fixed entities have a mode of ontic or noetic actuality, or are they only principially distinct from manifested effects?
7. Does the comparative inquiry gain precision by keeping Guénon’s Absolute / Infinite / Universal Possibility separate until each Arabic term is established rather than conflating them with “nothingness”?

**Research standard:** Any future cross-author agreement or contradiction must be established by an original-language, clause-level citation to *both* authors, not by resemblance of technical vocabulary alone.
