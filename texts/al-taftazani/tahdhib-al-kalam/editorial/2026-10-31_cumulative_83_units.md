# Tahdhīb al-Kalām — cumulative Arabic–English locus edition

**Editable textual working source (83 editorial units).** This compilation is supplementary to the complete [1912 page-by-page English working reader](complete/2026-10-31_cumulative_113_page_reader.md) and scanned original. Some Arabic units remain paraphrastic, recomposed or abbreviated; their status is declared below. Do not treat all entries as continuous diplomatic transcription.

Copy-text: Cairo 1330/1912. Commentary guidance: al-Sanandajī, *Taqrīb al-Marām*. The editorial apparatus belongs outside the public reading paragraphs.

## Chapter I

### I.01 — The definition and subject of kalām

Cairo printed page(s): 15. Textual status: working unit, requires witness validation where not independently checked.

**Arabic:**
الكلام هو العلم بالعقائد الدينية عن الأدلة اليقينية، وموضوعه المعلوم من حيث يتعلق بذلك.

**English:**
Kalām is knowledge of religious doctrines acquired through conclusive proofs. Its subject is the knowable insofar as it bears upon that purpose.

- *Explanation:* As Sanandajī explains, the intended end of kalām is assent to doctrine; its subject is the knowable, not solely the existent, because inquiry into nonexistence and possibility also serves theological demonstration (Taqrīb I:8–12).
- *Sanandajī I:8–9:* The criterion separating doctrinal and practical rulings is the intended object: assent of the heart in the first case, performance in the second. That practical rulings must be believed true does not by itself make their detailed study kalām.
- *Sanandajī I:11–13:* He explains why the subject is the knowable (al-maʿlūm), not merely the existent (al-mawjūd): kalām treats existence and nonexistence, among other disputed categories, insofar as their study serves the establishment of religious doctrines.

### I.02 — Why knowledge is explained rather than defined

Cairo printed page(s): 15–16. Textual status: working unit, requires witness validation where not independently checked.

**Arabic:**
والعلم لا يحد، والتفسير له بمثل حصول صورة الشيء في العقل، أو الاعتقاد الجازم المطابق الثابت، أو صفة يتجلى بها المذكور لمن قامت به، أو إدراك المركب أو الكلي، تنبيه على اختلاف الاصطلاحات.

**English:**
Knowledge admits of no strict definition. It may be explained as the obtaining of the form of a thing in the intellect; as firm, reality-conforming, settled belief; as a quality through which the object in question becomes manifest to its possessor; or as the apprehension of something composite or universal. The alternatives alert us to differences in technical usage.

- *Explanation:* A definition restricted to firm assent does not capture conception, and a definition based on representation may apply more widely than the acceptance of a true proposition. The alternatives are not cumulative conditions.
- *Philosophical note:* The formula ‘settled, true assent’ is narrower than conception: one can conceive an object without judging it to exist or making an affirmation about it. This distinction is important for the later discussion of propositions, reality-conformity, and certainty.
- *Sanandajī I:14–17:* The commentary discusses differences between cognition by representation, assent to a proposition, and a wider capacity for apprehension. A definition restricted to firm assent cannot straightforwardly include conception (taṣawwur). The full argument, not simply its three slogans, must govern the final translation.

### I.02a — Rational inquiry and the possibility of knowledge

Cairo printed page(s): 16. Textual status: working unit, requires witness validation where not independently checked.

**Arabic:**
وحقيقة النظر حركة النفس في المعقولات عودًا على بدء لتحصيل المجهول، وكونه مفيدًا للعلم في الجملة ولو في الإلهيات، وبدونه العلم ضروري، والمنكر معاند كالسفسطائي المنكر للحسيات أو الأوليات أو كليهما.

**English:**
Rational inquiry properly so called is the soul’s movement among intelligibles, advancing and returning in order to attain what is unknown. It yields knowledge in at least some cases, even in divine matters. Knowledge reached without inquiry is immediate. One who denies the possibility of such knowledge obstinately resists evidence, like a sophist who rejects sense-perception, first principles, or both.

- *Explanation:* Sanandajī explains the ‘outward’ movement as seeking an appropriate mediating principle and the ‘returning’ movement as reasoning back to the sought conclusion (Taqrīb I:17–18).
- *Sanandajī’s explanation:* The two movements are an initial search among what is intelligible for an appropriate mediating principle, followed by arranging that principle and returning from it to the result sought. The image at Taqrīb I:18, PDF p. 31, explicitly distinguishes the two movements.

### I.03 — Why knowledge follows inquiry

Cairo printed page(s): 16. Textual status: working unit, requires witness validation where not independently checked.

**Arabic:**
وهل هو بطريق العادة أو التوليد أو الوجوب فيه خلاف.

**English:**
It is disputed whether knowledge follows rational inquiry by customary conjunction, generated production, or necessity.

- *Explanation:* These are different accounts of the causal or epistemic relation between sound inquiry and resultant knowledge, not three ways of expressing the same doctrine (Taqrīb I:17–25).
- *Sanandajī I:17–22:* The alternatives concern the manner in which knowledge follows sound inferential inquiry: customary concurrence (ʿāda), causation by generation (tawlīd), or necessary connection (wujūb/luzūm). They are reported competing explanations. The theological significance changes if one silently renders them all as ‘reasoning produces knowledge’.

### I.04 — Why inquiry directed to God is obligatory

Cairo printed page(s): 16. Textual status: working unit, requires witness validation where not independently checked.

**Arabic:**
والنظر في معرفة الله تعالى واجب بالنص والإجماع، ولكونه مقدمة للمعرفة الواجبة عندنا بذلك، وعند المعتزلة لكونها دافعة لضرر خوف العقاب.

**English:**
Inquiry directed toward knowledge of God, exalted is He, is obligatory by revealed text and consensus. On our account, it is obligatory as a prerequisite for knowledge made obligatory by those authorities; according to the Muʿtazila, the knowledge sought averts the harm of feared punishment.

- *Explanation:* The first account grounds obligation in text and consensus and treats inquiry as an instrument; the second invokes fear of harm as the reason for its obligatoriness.

### I.05 — The objection: inquiry before revealed authority

Cairo printed page(s): 16. Textual status: working unit, requires witness validation where not independently checked.

**Arabic:**
قالوا: لو لم يجب إلا شرعًا لما صح إلزام النظر في المعجزة لعدم الوجوب قبل ثبوت الشرع.

**English:**
They object: if inquiry were obligatory only by revealed law, it would not be legitimate to require someone to examine a miracle, for the obligation would not yet obtain before the authority of revelation had been established.

- *Explanation:* The challenge is circularity: one is asked to examine the miracle in order to establish the authority of the revelation that supposedly first makes the examination obligatory.

### I.06 — The distinction between obligation and knowing it

Cairo printed page(s): 17. Textual status: working unit, requires witness validation where not independently checked.

**Arabic:**
ورد بأن المتوقف على الشرع هو العلم بالوجوب لا نفس الوجوب.

**English:**
The reply is that what depends on revelation is knowledge of the obligation, not the obligation itself.

- *Explanation:* An obligation and the agent’s prior recognition of that obligation are distinct. This is the hinge of the reply to the objection (Taqrīb I:31–32).
- *Sanandajī's explanation:* A duty can obtain as a matter of the revealed command even before its addressee discovers or recognizes that duty. The potential circularity arises only if knowing the obligation is mistakenly taken to be a condition of the obligation's very existence.

### I.06a — Knowledge as an end; inquiry as its means

Cairo printed page(s): 17. Textual status: working unit, requires witness validation where not independently checked.

**Arabic:**
ثم إنها أول الواجبات المقصودة لتوقف البواقي عليها، والنظر فيها وسيلة إليها فيجب لذلك.

**English:**
Moreover, knowledge of God is the first obligation sought as an end in itself, since the others depend upon it. Inquiry into it is a means toward that knowledge and is obligatory on that account.

- *Explanation:* The antecedent of the feminine «إنها» is the required knowledge; naẓar is instrumental rather than another ultimate obligation.
- *Sanandajī:* The point is not that the preparatory inquiry is dispensable, but that knowledge of God is obligatory as an intended end, while inquiry is obligatory as a necessary means.

### I.07 — Proof, indication, reason and transmission

Cairo printed page(s): 17. Textual status: working unit, requires witness validation where not independently checked.

**Arabic:**
والدليل ما يمكن أن يتوصل بالنظر فيه إلى حكم، وقد يختص بالجازم فيقابله الأمارة، ثم إن توقف على نقل فنقلي وإلا فعقلي، وقد يستفاد منه بمونة القرائن القطع، ولا يثبت ما استوى طرفاه عند العقل إلا بالنقل، وما يتوقف النقل عليه لا يثبت إلا بالعقل.

**English:**
A proof is that through consideration of which one can arrive at a judgment. Sometimes the term is restricted to what yields a firm judgment, in contrast to an indicative sign. If it depends on transmission it is a transmitted proof; otherwise it is rational. Certainty may be obtained from transmitted evidence with the aid of corroborating indications. What presents alternatives equally possible to reason is established by transmission, whereas what the authority of transmission itself depends upon can be established only by reason.

- *Explanation:* The broader and narrower uses of dalīl should not be conflated. Rational evidence has epistemic priority in establishing the trustworthiness of transmitted evidence, but this does not make each transmitted proposition independently demonstrable by reason (Taqrīb I:32–33).

## Chapter II

### II.01 — The concept of existence

Cairo printed page(s): unfixed. Textual status: working unit, requires witness validation where not independently checked.

**Arabic:**
تصور الوجود ضروري، والتعريف بالكون والتحقق والشيئية لفظي.

**English:**
Our conception of existence is immediate. To define it using ‘being’, ‘actuality’ or ‘thinghood’ is only to clarify the expression by other familiar expressions.

- *Sanandajī:* Taqrīb I:33–34 (PDF pp. 46–47) explains that a person's understanding of ‘existent or nonexistent’ presupposes some conception of existence, rather than the result of a further essential definition.
- *Explanation:* A self-evident concept is not a self-evident proof of the necessary existence of a particular being. The definitions offered are verbal elucidations of an immediately apprehended meaning.

### II.02 — Existence and the necessary being

Cairo printed page(s): unfixed. Textual status: incomplete/elliptical.

**Arabic:**
[والوجود ...] ينقسم إلى الواجب وغيره ... والحكماء على أن حقيقة الواجب وجود خاص قائم بنفسه، مغاير للوجود الممكن في حقيقته.

**English:**
Existence is divided into the Necessary Existent and what is other than it. The philosophers maintain that the Necessary Existent’s reality is a special existence subsisting of itself, essentially unlike the existence of what is contingent. Thus one must distinguish what the mind means by ‘existence’ in common from the manner in which existence belongs to the Necessary and to possible beings.

- *Explanation:* The claimed addition of existence to quiddity is primarily conceptual: the mind can deny existence of a quiddity and learn something by affirming it. This does not posit two independently existing external objects.
- *Explanation:* Al-Taftāzānī attributes to philosophers the claim that the Necessary Existent is a specific existence subsisting through itself, distinct in reality from the existence of contingent things.

### II.03 — Degrees and conceptual universality of being

Cairo printed page(s): unfixed. Textual status: incomplete/elliptical.

**Arabic:**
[والوجود] المقول على الموجودات بالتشكيك كالنور على الأنوار، وما يقال [من أنه] في الكل نفس الماهية ... وإنما هو في العقل.

**English:**
Existence, as a concept predicable of different beings, is applied with degrees of priority or entitlement—rather as one speaks of light being present in different lights. Yet the shared intelligible meaning should not be mistaken for a single numerically identical existence existing separately from the things to which it is attributed.

- *Explanation:* Tashkīk means graded predication or analogy: existence is spoken of across beings according to differences such as priority, not simply as an equivocal sound.

### II.04 — Four uses of “existence”

Cairo printed page(s): unfixed. Textual status: working unit, requires witness validation where not independently checked.

**Arabic:**
ثم الوجود ينقسم إلى العيني والذهني حقيقة وإلى اللفظي والخطي مجازًا.

**English:**
Existence is then divided, properly speaking, into extramental and mental existence, and figuratively into verbal and written existence. For neither a spoken word nor a written inscription contains the human individual or the human essence; it contains only the name and its graphic or verbal form.

- *Explanation:* External and mental modes are called existence properly; verbal and written 'existence' are so called figuratively, because a sound or sign signifies its object rather than being that object.

### II.05 — Argument for mental existence

Cairo printed page(s): unfixed. Textual status: incomplete/elliptical.

**Arabic:**
والدليل على الذهني أنا نتعقل ما لا ثبوت له في الخارج، إذ نحكم على الممتنعات إيجابًا ... وعلى القضايا حقيقية؛ فالتعقل إن كان الحصول في الذهن فذاك، وإلا [فلا غاية ...].

**English:**
An argument for mental existence is that we conceive things that have no establishment outside the mind: we make affirmative judgments concerning impossibilities and formulate genuinely general propositions. If intellection is a form of presence in the mind, the point is established; if not, [the remaining argument must be restored from the witness].

- *Explanation:* The reasoning draws on universal and impossible objects of thought and on affirmative judgments concerning things absent externally: they require an account of what is apprehended, not an external phoenix or an externally existent contradiction.
- *Explanation:* A thing can be represented in the intellect without the mind possessing externally the attribute represented. Al-Taftāzānī’s example is a believer’s conceiving unbelief without thereby becoming an unbeliever (Cairo p. 19).

### II.06 — Mental presentation without real inherence

Cairo printed page(s): unfixed. Textual status: working unit, requires witness validation where not independently checked.

**Arabic:**
وهو وجود غير متأصل لا يقتضي الاتصاف، كمن صوّر الكفر فلا يوجب اتصاف الذهن بالأعراض حتى المتضادات.

**English:**
This is a non-fundamental mode of existence that does not entail being qualified by what is represented. Someone who conceives unbelief, for example, does not thereby acquire unbelief as a real qualification of the mind; nor must a mind become qualified by every accident, even mutually opposed ones, that it conceives.

- *Explanation:* Intentional reception of a form does not entail that the intellect becomes externally qualified by all the properties it conceives; one can understand unbelief without thereby being an unbeliever.

### II.07 — Thinghood, establishment, and nonexistence

Cairo printed page(s): unfixed. Textual status: working unit, requires witness validation where not independently checked.

**Arabic:**
ثم المعقول من الوجود والشيئية ليس إلا الثبوت، ومن العدم إلا النفي؛ فالمعدوم ليس بشيء ولا ثابت، ولا واسطة بينه وبين الموجود.

**English:**
What is understood by existence and thinghood is nothing other than establishment; and by nonexistence, nothing other than negation. Thus a non-existent is neither a thing nor established, and there is no intermediary between it and the existent.

- *Explanation:* The controversy is over the independently established status of the nonexistent, not the possibility of discussing it meaningfully. Sanandajī gives the opposing arguments from distinctness and possibility before disputing their inference.
- *Explanation:* The denial of independent thinghood and establishment to what does not exist is an ontological thesis. It does not entail that the nonexistent cannot be conceived or enter true judgments (Cairo p. 19).

### II.08 — Existence as predicate and copula

Cairo printed page(s): 20. Textual status: incomplete/elliptical.

**Arabic:**
ثم كل من الوجود والعدم قد يقع محمولاً وقد يقع رابطة، ويفتقر الحمل الإيجابي إلى اتحاد الطرفين هوية ليصح، وتغايرهما مفهوماً ليفيد، وصدقه يكون بمطابقته لما في نفس الأمر، ومعناه ما يفهم من قولنا هذا الأمر كذا في نفسه مع قطع النظر عن حكم الحاكم وإدراك المدرك.

**English:**
Both existence and nonexistence can occur as a predicate or as the nexus of a proposition. Affirmative predication requires the two terms to be identical in referent if it is to be true, and different in conception if it is to convey information. Its truth consists in correspondence to what obtains in the matter itself (nafs al-amr): what we understand when we say that this thing is thus in itself, independently of the judgment of the one judging or the apprehension of the one apprehending.

- *Explanation:* ‘Identity in referent’ does not imply sameness in conceptual meaning. Nafs al-amr describes the way the matter stands independent of a judgment; it is not simply another name for physical existence.
- *Explanation:* Informative affirmative predication joins identity of what the subject and predicate designate with a difference of concepts. Truth is correspondence to how the matter stands in itself (nafs al-amr), irrespective of the act of judging or conceiving it.
- *Explanation:* Affirmative judgment requires identity of referent while allowing conceptual diversity of subject and predicate. The truth of the predication is conformity to nafs al-amr, what obtains in itself regardless of the individual judger (Cairo p. 20).

### II.09 — Three considerations of quiddity

Cairo printed page(s): 20–21. Textual status: working unit, requires witness validation where not independently checked.

**Arabic:**
فصل. ماهية الشيء ما به يجاب عن السؤال ما هو، وقد تؤخذ بشرط شيء فتسمى المخلوطة ولا خفاء في وجودها، وبشرط لا شيء فتسمى المجردة ولا توجد في الأذهان فضلا عن الأعيان، ولا بشرط شيء وهي أعم من المخلوطة.

**English:**
The quiddity of a thing is what answers the question, ‘What is it?’ It may be considered on condition of something further and is then called mixed; that it exists is beyond dispute. Or it may be considered on condition of nothing further, and is then called abstracted: as so restricted, it does not exist in minds, much less externally. Or it may be considered without stipulating either condition; this unrestricted nature is more general than the mixed quiddity.

- *Explanation:* Sanandajī distinguishes the restrictive ‘with something’, ‘without anything’, and unrestricted ‘without condition’ considerations. The nonexistence claim attaches to the restrictive abstracted nature, not to any act of thinking about it (Taqrīb I:63–68).
- *Sanandajī:* The three considerations of quiddity differ by their conditions of intellectual attention: with accompanying features, with their exclusion, and without either stipulation. The distinctions are used to explain predicability and instantiation, not three independent externally existing natures (Taqrīb I:63–83).

### II.10 — Quiddity and the natural universal

Cairo printed page(s): 21. Textual status: working unit, requires witness validation where not independently checked.

**Arabic:**
ثم إذا اعتبرت معروضة للكل فهو الكلي الطبيعي، وإنما يوجد منه المعروض دون العوارض مجردا عن العوارض وهي الأفراد.

**English:**
When [the quiddity] is considered as the subject to which universality is attributed, it is the natural universal. What is found externally is the subject of those attributes, not the attributes considered as additional entities; the individuals exist with their concomitants.

- *Explanation:* The natural universal denotes the nature instantiated; logical universality is a feature of the intellect’s mode of predication, not a freestanding external accident (Taqrīb I:69–70).

### II.11 — Composite and simple essences

Cairo printed page(s): unfixed. Textual status: working unit, requires witness validation where not independently checked.

**Arabic:**
ولا خفاء في وجود الماهية المركبة، ولا بد من انتهائها إلى البسيطة، واحتياج بعض الأجزاء إلى بعض في المركب الحقيقي ضروري، بخلاف الاعتباري.

**English:**
There is no doubt about composite quiddity; and a compositional analysis must ultimately reach what is simple. Within a real composite, the dependence of certain components on others is necessary. The same cannot be said of a merely conceptual or notional composite.

- *Sanandajī:* Al-Sanandajī distinguishes a real compound with dependent constituents from a merely considered compound such as an army. In the latter case, individuals exist externally without a further collective substance existing over and above them (Taqrīb I:74–76).

### II.12 — The issue of whether quiddities are made

Cairo printed page(s): unfixed. Textual status: incomplete/elliptical.

**Arabic:**
ومن الخلاف في مجعولية الماهية ... احتياج الممكن إلى العلة ضروري.

**English:**
The dispute concerning whether quiddities themselves are produced must not obscure the necessary dependence of the contingent upon a cause.

- *Sanandajī:* The dispute over jaʿl concerns whether the quiddity as such is made, the actual existent is made, or ‘made’ means dependence upon an agent. It must be separated from the agreed necessity that a contingent existent depends upon a cause (Taqrīb I:76–77).

### II.13 — Determination: its meanings and the real–considerational distinction

Cairo printed page(s): 22. Textual status: incomplete/elliptical.

**Arabic:**
فصل في التعين. أفراد النوع إنما تتميز بعوارض ربما تنتهي إلى ما يفيد الماهية، فبعد تلخيص أن التعين والتشخص هو تلك الماهية أو ما يفيدها أو كون الفرد بحيث لا يقبل الشركة أو عدم قبوله لها، وأن العدمي هو المعدوم أو العدم المضاف أو ما يدخل في مفهومه العدم، والوجودي بخلافه، وأن الحقيقي ما له ثبوت في نفس الأمر من غير شائبة فرض وتقدير، والاعتباري بخلافه، لا شبهة أن التعين وجودي أو عدمي…

**English:**
Chapter on determination. Individuals of a species are distinguished by accidents that may ultimately terminate in what specifies the quiddity. Once the suggested meanings of determination and individuation have been clarified—the quiddity itself, what gives rise to it, the individual’s being such that it cannot be shared, or its not admitting sharing—and once the negative has been explained as the nonexistent, a qualified nonexistence, or what includes nonexistence in its conception, with the positive as its contrary; and once the real has been explained as what is established in the matter itself (nafs al-amr) without supposition or estimation, with the considerational as its contrary, then it is beyond doubt that determination is positive or negative… [the sentence continues on printed p. 23].

- *Explanation:* These are alternative definitions of individual determination and individuation, not cumulative conditions. The real–considerational opposition depends upon what obtains in nafs al-amr, independently of mere stipulation.
- *Sanandajī:* The fact that determination is a universal concept does not imply that an individual’s particular determination is shared numerically with other individuals (Taqrīb I:78–80).

### II.14 — Necessity, impossibility and possibility

Cairo printed page(s): 23–24. Textual status: working unit, requires witness validation where not independently checked.

**Arabic:**
فصل. الوجوب والامتناع والإمكان معقولات تحصل من نسبة المفهوم إلى هيئة بسيطة أو مركبة، وتصورها ضروري، والتعريف بمثل ضرورة الوجود أو ضرورة العدم أو لا ضرورتهما لفظي.

**English:**
Necessity, impossibility and possibility are intelligible notions apprehended by regarding a concept under a simple or composite modal qualification. Their conception is immediate. Explaining them as necessity of existence, necessity of nonexistence, or the absence of either necessity is a verbal elucidation rather than a strict demonstration.

- *Explanation:* The immediate grasp of a modal concept is distinct from proving that a particular subject possesses that modal status.

### II.15 — Necessary and impossible through themselves

Cairo printed page(s): 24. Textual status: working unit, requires witness validation where not independently checked.

**Arabic:**
وينقسم كل من الأولين إلى الذاتي والغيري؛ فالموصوف بالذاتي من الواجب واجب الوجود لذاته وهو الله تعالى، أو لشيء آخر كزوجية الأربعة، ومن الممتنع ممتنع الوجود لذاته كشريك الباري أو لشيء آخر كفردية الأربعة. والموصوف بالغيري كممكن هو واجب الوجود حين وجوده وممتنع الوجود حين عدمه.

**English:**
Necessity and impossibility admit distinctions between what belongs through the subject itself and what belongs in respect of something else. Existence through itself belongs necessarily to God, exalted is He; evenness belongs necessarily to four. A partner of the Creator is intrinsically impossible, while oddness is impossible for the number four. A possible being becomes necessary in the circumstance of its existence and impossible in the circumstance of its nonexistence when the determining conditions are fixed.

- *Explanation:* Necessity of existence through oneself differs from a property necessarily belonging to a subject and from the consequent necessity of an event given complete conditions.

### II.16 — Possibility and preparedness

Cairo printed page(s): 24. Textual status: working unit, requires witness validation where not independently checked.

**Arabic:**
وقد يؤخذ بمعنى سلب ضرورة الوجود أو العدم فيسمى بالإمكان العام لعمومه الخاص، وضرورة الطرف الآخر. وقد يعتبر بالنظر إلى الاستقبال ويسمى بالاستقبالي، ومعنى تهيؤ المادة لحصول الشيء باعتبار تحقق الشرائط شيئاً فشيئاً فيسمى بالاستعدادي.

**English:**
Possibility may mean the denial of necessity on one side, that is, of existence or nonexistence. This is general possibility and is wider than special possibility, since it can coexist with necessity on the other side. Possibility may instead be considered with reference to what lies in the future, and is then called future-directed possibility. Preparation of matter for the occurrence of something as its conditions come to be fulfilled successively is called dispositional possibility (imkān istiʿdādī).

- *Explanation:* Essential possibility, general possibility, future-directed possibility and material preparedness are analytically distinct.

### II.17 — Why the contingent needs a cause

Cairo printed page(s): 25. Textual status: working unit, requires witness validation where not independently checked.

**Arabic:**
ثم احتياج الممكن إلى المؤثر لامتناع ترجيح أحد طرفيه بلا مرجح ضروري، وهذا غير ترجيح اختيار أحد المتساويين على الآخر بالاختصاص بمحض الإرادة كالهارب يسلك أحد الطريقين والجائع يأكل أحد الرغيفين.

**English:**
The possible being's need of an efficient cause is necessary, since one of its two alternatives cannot prevail without a determining ground. This differs from a choosing agent's selection of one from two equal alternatives simply by the exercise of will, as when a fugitive takes one of two roads or a hungry person eats one of two loaves.

- *Explanation:* The demand for an explanation of the actuality of a contingent being is distinguished from a choice between otherwise equal available objects.

### II.18 — Objection: producing what is already present

Cairo printed page(s): 25. Textual status: working unit, requires witness validation where not independently checked.

**Arabic:**
فإن قيل التأثير حال الوجود تحصيل الحاصل وحال العدم جمع بين النقيضين، قلنا الممتنع تحصيل الحاصل بتحصيل آخر، والموجب هو الإمكان أو الحدوث ولكل وجه.

**English:**
It may be objected: if production occurs while the effect exists, it merely produces what is already present; if it occurs while the effect does not exist, it joins contradictories. The reply is that what is impossible is to produce what is already present by a second production. The ground claimed for the need of causation is possibility or origination, each position having its argument.

- *Explanation:* The objection distinguishes production prior to existence from production of an already existing effect. The reply rejects only a redundant second production.

### II.19 — The two senses of dependence

Cairo printed page(s): 25. Textual status: working unit, requires witness validation where not independently checked.

**Arabic:**
ومعنى الاحتياج إما توقف الوجود أو العدم أو استمرارهما على أمر ما، ولا تعقل أولوية بالذات لأحد الطرفين.

**English:**
Dependence can mean the dependence of a thing's existence or nonexistence, or the persistence of either, upon some further condition. Neither alternative possesses, through the contingent nature itself, any intrinsic priority over the other.

- *Explanation:* The analysis differentiates original causal dependence from continued dependence; a contingent nature as such does not privilege one alternative.

### II.20 — Eternity and origination

Cairo printed page(s): 27. Textual status: working unit, requires witness validation where not independently checked.

**Arabic:**
القدم بمعنى عدم المسبوقية بالغير وهو الذاتي، أو بالعدم وهو الزماني، والحدوث بخلافه. ولا قديم بالذات سوى الله تعالى، وبالزمان سوى صفاته. ولا يستند القدم إلى مختار، لأن القصد إلى الإيجاد يستلزم العدم.

**English:**
Pre-eternity means not being preceded by something else, which is essential pre-eternity, or not being preceded by nonexistence, which is temporal pre-eternity; origination is the opposite of each. Nothing is essentially pre-eternal except God Most High, and nothing else is temporally pre-eternal except His attributes. [The objection is raised that] a pre-eternal thing cannot depend upon a choosing agent, since the intention to bring something into being appears to presuppose its nonexistence.

- *Explanation:* This last argument is introduced as an objection concerning a choosing cause. An author's report of the objection must not be converted into his unconditional endorsement.

### II.21 — Different orders of priority

Cairo printed page(s): 28. Textual status: working unit, requires witness validation where not independently checked.

**Arabic:**
التقدم والتأخر بالعلية أو بالطبع أو بالذات أو بالشرف أو بالرتبة أو بالزمان. وسبق العدم على الحادث لا يلزم أن يكون بالزمان فيلزم قدم الزمان، كما لا يلزم أن يكون له إمكان استعدادي يلزم قدم مادة له.

**English:**
Priority and posteriority may be understood through causality, through nature, through essence, through dignity, through rank, or through time. The nonexistence preceding an originated thing need not be prior in time—for that would entail the pre-eternity of time itself. Nor need an originated thing have a dispositional possibility in some pre-existing matter, which would make that matter pre-eternal.

- *Explanation:* The precedence of nonexistence over origination need not be another temporal interval. Essential, causal, natural and temporal priority must remain distinct.

### II.22 — Unity and multiplicity

Cairo printed page(s): 28. Textual status: working unit, requires witness validation where not independently checked.

**Arabic:**
الوحدة والكثرة من المعاني الواضحة ومقوليتهما بالتشكيك، فتكون جهة الوحدة مقومة أو عارضة أو منسوبة. وتسمى الوحدة في الجنس مجانسة، وفي النوع مماثلة، وفي الكم مساواة، وفي الكيف مشابهة، وفي الخاصة مشاكلة، وفي الأطراف مطابقة، وفي الوضع موازاة، وفي النسبة مناسبة.

**English:**
Unity and multiplicity belong to evident concepts, and are predicated with gradation. The respect in which there is unity can be constitutive, accidental, or relational. Unity in genus is called commonality of genus (mujānasa); in species, sameness of species (mumāthala); in quantity, equality (musāwāt); in quality, resemblance (mushābaha); in a proper attribute, likeness (mushākala); in corresponding extremities, correspondence (muṭābaqa); in position, parallelism (muwāzāt); and in relation, proportion (munāsaba).

- *Explanation:* These are different respects in which a plurality may be called ‘one’ or ‘the same’. None of them, considered alone, establishes numerical identity of two substances.

### II.23 — Identity and opposition

**Arabic:**

**English:**
Otherness is opposed to identity. Contrariety holds when two determinations cannot, through what they are, be together in one subject and in one respect. The broader theory of opposition differentiates correlatives, contraries, possession-and-privation, and affirmation-and-negation.


### II.24 — Definition and divisions of cause

Cairo printed page(s): 31. Textual status: working unit, requires witness validation where not independently checked.

**Arabic:**
فصل في العلة والمعلول. العلة هي ما يحتاج إليه الشيء، فإن كانت داخلة فوجوب الشيء معها إما بالفعل فهي صورية وإما بالقوة فهي مادية، وإن كانت خارجة فالشيء إما بها فهي فاعلية أو لأجلها فهي غائية، ومرجع جمع الشروط والآلات إلى الفاعل. وجميع ما يتوقف عليه الشيء يسمى علة تامة، وعند تمام الفاعل يجب وجود المعلول لامتناع الترجيح بلا مرجح.

**English:**
A cause is that upon which something depends. If internal, the thing depends upon it either in actuality, and it is formal, or in potency, and it is material. If external, the thing is produced through it, and it is efficient, or exists for its sake, and it is final. Conditions and instruments are referred to the agent. Everything upon which the thing depends, taken together, is called its complete cause. When the agent is complete [with the conditions of its action], the effect must exist: otherwise one alternative would be preferred without a determinant.

- *Explanation:* The four causes are explicitly distinguished. The necessity of the effect is stated conditionally upon the complete causal nexus; it should not be turned into an assertion of independent natural creative efficacy (Cairo p. 31; compare Taqrīb I:141–142).
- *Sanandajī:* See Taqrīb I:141–142 for the relation between a complete cause, its conditions, and the necessary presence of the effect when that cause is complete.

### II.25 — Circularity and regress

**English:**
Circular dependence occurs when something depends upon what in turn depends upon it. The subsequent argument considers regress and the impossibility of a collection of dependent causes being sufficient through its dependent members alone.


### II.26 — Form, matter, and finality by extension

Cairo printed page(s): 35. Textual status: working unit, requires witness validation where not independently checked.

**Arabic:**
قد يقال الصورة لكل هيئة في قابل وحداني بالذات أو بالاعتبار، والمادة لما يقبل، كالبياض والجسم. والغاية ما ينتهي إليه الفعل وإن لم يكن له جهة علية واحتياج من الفعل إليه، بل وإن لم يكن للفاعل قصد.

**English:**
The term ‘form’ is sometimes also applied to any configuration inhering in a receptive subject whose unity is real or is established by consideration, and ‘matter’ to that which receives it, as in the case of whiteness and body. Likewise, ‘end’ can be used for what an action terminates in, even if it is not genuinely a final cause on which the action depends and even if the agent did not intend that terminus.

- *Explanation:* An extended usage of ṣūra does not imply every configuration is a substantial form. Likewise the terminus of an action need not be the intention-guiding final cause.

### II.27 — Divine production and customary causation
