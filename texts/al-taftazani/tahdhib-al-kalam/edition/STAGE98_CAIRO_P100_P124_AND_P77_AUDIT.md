# Tahdhīb Stage 98 — direct Cairo print review of pp.100, 124; p.77 remains a crux

**2 November 2026.** Saʿd al-Dīn al-Taftāzānī, *Tahdhīb al-Kalām*. Governing printed source: Cairo, Maṭbaʿat al-Saʿāda, 1330/1912. Source pages are original numbered **100, 124 and 77**, identically numbered PDF pages in the full 128-page first-edition scan. This is a **single printed-witness photographic collation**. It is NOT an independently established reading from al-Azhar 995 AH, Princeton Garrett 4139Y, or the 2017 critical edition.

## The result

The source-facing edition already has **1,044 stable Arabic–English clause IDs** spanning **113 printed kalām pages 15–127**. Stage98 re-read every printed main-text line on pp.100 and 124 against all existing units on those pages. The two previously bracketed passages are actually legible as short unexpanded Cairo print expressions. The single-print tally can therefore move from **110/113 to 112/113**, provided no unseen independent textual question is counted as a printed omission; p.77 is still a real printed-reading crux. **No independent manuscript comparison is hereby certified.**

### Cairo p.100 — KAL-100.02, KAL-100.07

The upper image (straight Cairo print) shows, continuously:

> وغاية متشبث المنكرين الطعن في النسخ مطلقا وقد بين ذلك في موضعه **ولدين موسى تمسكوا بالسبت أبدا وهذه شريعة مؤبدة ما دامت السماوات والأرض** والجواب أن هذا افتراء أو عبارة عن طول الزمان

The previously bracketed `قراءة مقدمة الاستشهاد غير محررة` can be replaced by the printed **«ولدين موسى»**. This is a compressed phrase and should not be normalised to a smoother conjecture. Punctuation and the insertion of quotation marks around the two reported formulae are editorial, not signs in the Cairo compositor. These statements are *attributions reported in a dispute over abrogation* and not automatically verified Torah quotations. Cf. Taftāzānī, *Sharḥ al-Maqāṣid*, V, p.44, as an explanatory authorial comparison **not an independent witness**.

**Literal-proximity English:** “The objectors' ultimate recourse is to challenge abrogation in general; this has been explained elsewhere. Regarding the religion of Moses, [they invoke] ‘Keep the Sabbath forever’ and ‘This is an enduring law for as long as the heavens and earth remain.’ The reply is that this is either a fabrication or an expression for a very long period.” This is a reported polemical argument, not the editor's endorsement of its attribution.

At the bottom of the same image the printed **KAL-100.07** reads:

> ... والسلامة عما تنفر عنه الطباع السليمة أو يخل بالمروءة **أو بحكمة البعثة**

Restore the preposition **بـ** before «حكمة», missed in the older clause stream. The English need not otherwise change. The remaining main lines of p.100 have been compared against KAL-100.01–.08; no missing authorial line was found in this page review.

### Cairo p.124 — KAL-124.06

The middle/lower image of original Cairo p.124 clearly gives the compact line:

> وأجيب بأن سوق الآية لولاية المحبة والنصرة وأما وصف المؤمنين فللمدح ولزيادة الشرف **وهم راكعون للعطف لا كصلاة اليهود أو خاضعون** على أن الحصر لنفي التنازع

The editorial placeholder `[موضع يحتاج مقابلة: «لا كصلاة اليهود أو خاضعون»]` can therefore be removed **from the Cairo transcription**: those very words are legible in the printed source. The grammar is condensed but the source characters are recoverable. **DO NOT** silently import the longer phrase *خالية عن الركوع* from Taftāzānī's **different** work *Sharḥ al-Maqāṣid*, V, p.271. The author's fuller passage supports interpreting `للعطف` as coordinating the description of bowing in prayer (versus the Jewish-prayer contrast), or understanding `راكعون` as humility (`خاضعون`). It does not alter the printed Tahdhīb. The bracket must move to editorial *commentary*, not continue falsely to label legible Cairo text as absent.

**Close English:** “The response is that the verse's context concerns affection and mutual help. The description of the believers praises them and increases their honour. ‘While bowing’ is taken as coordinated [with their prayer]: [they bow], unlike Jewish prayer; alternatively, [‘bowing’ means] ‘humble.’ In addition, the restrictive formula is intended to exclude competing claims.” The square-bracket elucidations are the translator's; the source itself does not supply the expanded verbs.

The p.124 printed main lines are represented across KAL-124.01–.09, and the source/photo-to-clause review is recorded as complete *for this printed witness*. The discussion reports historically contested interpretations of Q5:55; an edition records both sides without deciding the controversy by editorial fiat.

### Cairo p.77 — KAL-077.03

The full-size Cairo image was inspected again at 3× and in a 7× crop. The text after the crux is clear:

> ... على كل كلام في كل باب أعرضنا عنه مخافة الإطناب والله الهادي إلى طريق الصواب

The short expression immediately before «على كل كلام» remains visually and syntactically doubtful. The inherited **«وفاء»** has not been confirmed against an independent source. The unusual printed masculine **«ذات الواجب مخالف»** at KAL-077.10 remains deliberately unchanged: it is an actually observed Cairo reading, as already recorded in Stage84. We therefore leave **p.77 on the unverified single-print page list**.

### The newly accessible independent manuscript

The digitized **al-Azhar 995 AH manuscript**, al-Azhar private 2468 / general 96067, has been retrieved visually (41-page photographic PDF including cover): <https://www.alukah.net/Manu/Files/Manuscript_6154/Makhtotah.pdf>. Several actual photographed spreads were inspected, including beginning, middle and closing sections. However, **none of the three disputed Cairo phrases has yet been securely assigned to a folio side and read letter-for-letter**. It would be untrue to claim that merely viewing images is an actual variant collation. There is no adopted or rejected 995 AH reading in this release.
