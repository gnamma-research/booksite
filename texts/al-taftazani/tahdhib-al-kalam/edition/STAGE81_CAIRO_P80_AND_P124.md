## A. Restored Cairo p. 80, KAL-080.11

**Previously available:** the working corpus stopped the response at `قلنا ممنوع` and inserted an editorial bracket stating that the subsequent proof required a separate witness.

**Cairo p. 80, bottom two printed lines, direct photographic reinspection:**

> قالوا تماثل قدرته قدرة الشاهد فلا تختلف آثارهما، قلنا ممنوع. فمنها القدرة لاستناد الحوادث إليه تعالى وفاقا.

Punctuation is **editorial**. The phrases `فمنها القدرة`, `لاستناد الحوادث إليه تعالى` and `وفاقا` are **source** wording rather than invented connective English.

**Revised literal-proximity English**

> They object: His power is of the same kind as the power of an observed agent, and therefore their effects should not differ. We reply: this is denied. Among the positive attributes is power, since originated things are attributed to God—on this there is agreement.

**Logical articulation:** the opponent alleges that if the divine power and the humanly observed power are *mutamāthil* (alike in kind), their `āthār` (effects) should not differ. `قلنا ممنوع` denies the alleged equivalence/inference; it does not concede a shared power that must have the same effects. The text then identifies *power* as a positive attribute under the heading `فصل في الصفات الوجودية`. In English these are **two different argumentative movements**, and neither should be erased in translation. `وفاقا` reports agreement regarding attribution of originated things to God at the cited stage of the dialectic; it is not editorial proof that every later school uses a single causal account.

The next printed p. 81 starts `ولا استلزام ارتفاع ما ثبت بالإيجاب ارتفاع الموجب` and proceeds to the argument about an agent acting through necessitation, the positions of celestial bodies, and the objection to free choice. Do **not** move that page-81 continuation into p. 80. This correction fills previously missing printed text at p. 80 without changing the original stable clause count.

**Certification update:** p. 80's original printed main lines were rechecked photographically with KAL-080.01–.11. **Status upgraded to a complete single-print check of p. 80 only.** The independent manuscript proof requirement remains open.

## B. Cairo p. 124, KAL-124.06: partial restoration, NOT a clean certificate

The working reading had an unlocalized placeholder after the description of believers:

> وأما وصف المؤمنين فللمدح ولزيادة الشرف، و[قراءة العبارة التالية غير محررة] ...

**Textual material plainly visible in the original p. 124 photograph includes**:

> وأما وصف المؤمنين فللمدح ولزيادة الشرف، وهم راكعون للعطف ... على أن الحصر لنفي التنازع

The short intervening material appears in the Cairo print as `لا كصلاة اليهود أو خاضعون`, but its precise syntax (and perhaps some letterforms) is **not now established to manuscript-collation standard**. The working reader accordingly uses:

> وأجيب بأن سوق الآية لولاية المحبة والنصرة، وأما وصف المؤمنين فللمدح ولزيادة الشرف، و«وهم راكعون» للعطف، [موضع يحتاج مقابلة: «لا كصلاة اليهود أو خاضعون»]، على أن الحصر لنفي التنازع.

**Doctrinal/polemical context.** The matn reports a reply to a Shiʿi inference from Qurʾān 5:55 concerning the *walāya* of ʿAlī. The reported Sunni counterreading distinguishes `walī` in the sense of affection and mutual assistance from authority over affairs; treats the description of believers as praise; and engages whether `وهم راكعون` is coordinated or circumstantial, with bowing in ritual prayer versus humility also invoked. This paragraph is a record of a historical argument, not a verdict on the correctness of the rival tradition.

**Why no page-124 certificate?** Reconstructing a plausible later author's explanatory argument from *Sharḥ al-Maqāṣid* would not prove the abbreviated *Tahdhīb* wording. Manuscript/independent printed comparison is still required for exactly this grammatical turn. The reader now shows the recoverable printed material rather than deleting it or presenting editorial paraphrase as primary Arabic.
