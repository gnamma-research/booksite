**Date:** 2 November 2026. **Work:** Taftāzānī's *Tahdhīb al-Kalām*, Cairo 1330/1912, original pp. **77**, **100**, **124**.  
**Textual authority:** Cairo printed scan. **Interpretative aids:** a quoted passage in al-Sanandajī and Taftāzānī's own much fuller *Sharḥ al-Maqāṣid*. ## A. Cairo p.77: the apparent gender incongruity is *real printed wording*

**Locus:** `KAL-077.10`

The Cairo photograph plainly gives:

> ثم الحق أن **ذات الواجب مخالف لسائر الذوات**، لئلا يلزم وجوب الممكن أو إمكان الواجب ...

It is tempting to amend `مخالف` to the grammatically feminine `مخالفة`, given the apparent subject `ذات`. **Do not emend.** The masculine is what this printed witness actually displays; a later printed quotation by al-Sanandajī also reproduces precisely this masculine expression.

Corroborating **later commentary**, cited by Arzāq Fatḥī Abū Ṭāhā, “Mukhālafat dhāt Allāh taʿālā li-sāʾir al-dhawāt bayna al-Muʿtazila wa-l-Ashāʿira,” journal article, printed p.1090, quoting `Taqrīb al-Marām`, vol. I, part 2, p. 106:  
[Research article PDF](https://jsad.journals.ekb.eg/article_29277_e975f19de651409ac89c49389977bd1a.pdf). The cited commentary supplies the distinction of **concept** (`mafhum al-dhāt`) from **referent/instantiation** (`mā ṣadaqa ʿalayh`). A property held in common as a *predicable meaning* does not make divine and created quiddities numerically identical. Treating a supposedly common essence as necessary could force necessary existence upon the possible, or vice versa.

This **explains** the inference; it does **not** validate the claim that a particular al-Azhar folio reads `مخالف`. The main Taftāzānī source and the separately developing Sanandajī–Wasīm translation remain independent projects. The Sanandajī text is used here only to note a securely quoted lemma, not to translate the accompanying commentary again.

**Current status:** `KAL-077.10` has been marked `VISUAL_CHECKED` against Cairo, with the commentary parallel explicitly cited in an editorial note. **Original printed p.77 remains UNRESOLVED** because the opening closure in `KAL-077.03` still needs an independent reading; **do not raise the page count** based on one repaired clause.

## B. Cairo p.100: distinguish a polemical *report* from biblical authentication

**Locus:** `KAL-100.02`.

The Arabic securely reports objectors challenging the possibility of superseding Mosaic law and invoking formulae such as:

> تمسكوا بالسبت أبدا  
> هذه شريعة مؤبدة ما دامت السماوات والأرض

These are historical theological **allegations attributed to Moses by the opposing disputants**, not quotations whose literal existence in Torah is established by repeating them. Different medieval sources report similar formulations:

- [Taftāzānī, *Sharḥ al-Maqāṣid*, V, p.44](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/9293/44?lang=ar): reports the two claims.
- [Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī, *al-Maḥṣūl* (edition page 301)](https://scribetools.com/ar/library/usul-al-fiqh/%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%AD%D8%B5%D9%88%D9%84-%D9%84%D9%84%D9%81%D8%AE%D8%B1-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%B2%D9%8A/meof3-3b930269/text/0016): gives two claims about the Sabbath under perpetuity and the distinction between an obligation and its temporal scope.
- [al-Ḥillī, *Kashf al-Murād*, I, p.386](https://najafdesertlibrary.com/book/%D9%83%D8%B4%D9%81-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%AF_%D9%81%D9%8A_%D8%B4%D8%B1%D8%AD_%D8%AA%D8%AC%D8%B1%D9%8A%D8%AF_%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%B9%D8%AA%D9%82%D8%A7%D8%AF_%D8%AA%D8%AD%D9%82%D9%8A%D9%82_%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B2%D9%86%D8%AC%D8%A7%D9%86%D9%8A/v/1/p/386): reports the transmitted Sabbath argument and challenges its authentication.

**Logical issue** (Taftāzānī's authorial *Sharḥ al-Maqāṣid* V, 44): “the action is commanded forever” and “the *legal obligation* is eternally unalterable” are not equivalent. An indefinitely or even duratively described act could be obligatory only during a bounded interval; the claim that **the obligation itself** must obtain at all future times rules out later termination. The *Tahdhīb* excerpt compresses this debate and must not be expanded into the longer *Sharḥ* within the authorial Arabic.

**Unresolved printed wording:** the exact formula introducing the alleged Mosaic quotations at Cairo p.100 remains indistinct. The reader retains a localized bracket, with the conceptual history in a separate `editorial_note`.

## C. Cairo p.124: precise explanation of «وهم راكعون»

**Locus:** `KAL-124.06`. The Cairo print preserves the short phrase `وهم راكعون للعطف لا كصلاة اليهود أو خاضعون`, but the precise elliptical syntax is not fully resolved without another textual witness. The reader therefore retains a marked local uncertainty rather than interpolating the full wording of other works.

**Authorial parallel (not a Tahdhīb witness):** [*Sharḥ al-Maqāṣid* V, p.271](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/9293/271?lang=ar) explicitly presents the grammatical alternatives:

> وقوله: «وهم راكعون» كما يحتمل الحال يحتمل العطف، بمعنى أنهم يركعون في صلاتهم لا كصلاة اليهود خالية عن الركوع أو بمعنى أنهم خاضعون.

This supplies two distinct readings. On the circumstantial (`ḥāl`) reading, the phrase could qualify the act of giving alms. On the coordinated (`ʿaṭf`) reading, it may complete the description of communal prayer, emphasizing bowing in contrast to prayer characterized as lacking it; or `rākiʿūn` could describe humility. These interpretations bear on the Sunni–Shiʿi polemic over whether Q5:55 restricts appointment of leadership to a single recipient.

[The immediately preceding *Sharḥ al-Maqāṣid* p.270](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/9293/270?lang=ar) presents the inference in favour of ʿAlī, and p.271 presents Taftāzānī's reply, including arguments from contextual `walāya`, praise, and temporal scope. Both perspectives should remain legible to a reader; editing is not a license to declare either historical position doctrinally proven.

**Translation updated:** the expanded English now explicitly identifies the coordinated prayer reading and the humility reading, while the Cairo Arabic remains qualified and the manuscript question remains open.
