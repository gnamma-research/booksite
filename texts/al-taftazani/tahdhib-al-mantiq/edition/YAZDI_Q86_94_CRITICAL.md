## Critical apparatus and philosophical analysis

**CR-32 · The syllogism is structurally ordered.** Q86 distinguishes `قول` from `مؤلف`, the latter indicating appropriate composition of different premises rather than a random collection. The comment identifies an explicitly `صوري` constituent: not just premises taken as a set, but their arrangement as an inference.

**CR-33 · Intrinsic implication versus another premise.** Q87 defines `يلزمه لذاته` (conclusion follows from the discourse through itself). This excludes simple conversion of a single proposition, and also induction/analogy in their probable form. The equality example—A=B and B=C therefore A=C—requires a further premise `مساوي المساوي مساو` in the **given historical logic**. Formally treating equality as transitive in modern logic may license the inference, but that is precisely an **extra semantic/inferential assumption** relative to the displayed premises. Record this difference rather than ‘correcting’ al-Yazdī.

**CR-34 · Matter and form of the result.** Q87 separates the terms (matter) of the conclusion from the affirmative/negative arrangement (form), and distinguishes an **exceptive** syllogism containing a reference to the conclusion or its contradictory from a **conjunctive** syllogism whose material is present without the concluded arrangement. The comment that ‘in its matter’ is redundant is al-Yazdī’s own analysis, not an imported editorial gloss.

**CR-35 · Distinct orders of the four figures.** Q88–89 defines minor, major and middle relative to the intended conclusion. The first figure is held to be immediately evident; the remaining figures are reduced to it. The labels first/second/third/fourth reflect supposed epistemic priority, not an empirical chronological sequence. The language `في الغالب` acknowledges that the major often, not invariably, has wider extension.

**CR-36 · First-figure actual minor and universal major.** Q89–90 requires the middle term to hold of the minor **actually** (the same Avicennan convention discussed in Q77–78). Its major must be universal: otherwise a predicate of one subset of the middle cannot pass to a minor included in another subset. The four major productive forms yield two affirmatives or two negatives, distinguished by universal or particular minor. Formal reconstructors must not assume an empty subject can license a particular conclusion without existential import.

**CR-37 · Second-figure opposition and quantified scope.** Q92–93 uses change-of-matter counterexamples to show that the second figure requires premises of opposite affirmative/negative quality; it also demands universal major. Modal cases impose an additional condition, **either** a permanent/necessary minor **or** a convertible negative major, plus restrictions on possible premises. The source explicitly distinguishes six convertible and nine nonconvertible negative modal kinds. Its explicit four productive patterns have negative conclusions.

**CR-38 · Three reductions, not a single diagram.** Q93–94 lists: (1) reductio via the contradictory of the desired result, available for all four second-figure moods; (2) conversion of the major, available for the first and third moods; (3) conversion of the minor followed by premise reordering and conversion of the output, available for the second mood. Do not claim that every proof method applies to every mood.

**CR-39 · Third figure requires shared actual witness.** Q94 begins third-figure production requirements: affirmative and actually instantiated minor, and at least one universal premise. If both premises are particular their witnesses may be distinct: ‘Some animals are human’ and ‘Some animals are horses’ do not entail ‘Some humans are horses’. Source method and existence conditions must be maintained.

**CR-40 · Exact editorial certification limit.** All Q86–94 Arabic paragraphs are normalized transcriptions of the digitally searchable **main** printed Qum شرح. Printed footnote call numbers, marginal comment by other annotators, and questionable printed additions have been separated. **No photographic Qum, Gujarat, Tehran or manuscript collation has been completed for Q86–94.** No conjectural emendation or manuscript siglum has been fabricated. Q95–97 remain an explicit translation gap; Q98's two existing selections remain under their original source locator.
