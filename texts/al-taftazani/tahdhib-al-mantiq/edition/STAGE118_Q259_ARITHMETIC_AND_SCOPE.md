## A certain contradiction and several outstanding interpretation questions

1. The **certain internal arithmetical conflict** is (450\times120=54,000), whereas the text says `خمسة آلاف وأربع مائة` (5,400). One can fix the total, or the base count, or the order multiplier, but **no uniquely warranted emendation** follows merely from elementary arithmetic.
2. The text's combinatorial argument for the 450 five-element bases appeals to sets of three overlapping elements, a division into `112` preliminary forms, and substitutions leading to `450`. Whether any part of that counts the same unordered selection multiple times depends on how it treats the two overlapping groups and their substitutions.
3. For 3 and 4 elements, ordinary `C(7,k)` gives only 35 base sets each; the text’s 56 and 140 count *something else*, or the derivation is defective. Without a proof of equivalence between the historical `tarākīb` and mathematical subsets, declaring these numeral differences direct typographic errors would be speculative.
4. For 6 and 7 terms the printed numbers happen to agree exactly with ordinary arrangements: `7×720=5040`, `1×5040=5040`. This is evidence of an intended enumeration rule but is **not sufficient** to infer its correctness for 3–5.
