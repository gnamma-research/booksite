## I. Four term configurations

Use **S** = subject of the sought conclusion (minor term), **P** = predicate of the conclusion (major term), **M** = middle term. In “M–P” the first term is the subject of that premise.

| Figure | Major premise | Minor premise | Cairo source characterization |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | M–P | S–M | الوسط محمول الصغرى وموضوع الكبرى |
| II | P–M | S–M | الوسط محمولهما |
| III | M–P | M–S | الوسط موضوعهما |
| IV | P–M | M–S | عكس الأول |

A first-figure inference can directly transmit the predicate from M to S where S actually falls under M and M is comprehensively governed in the major. The other figures require further demonstrations, and modal qualification can affect **whether** the middle's actual extension supports that transfer.

## II. Figure I: why all three requirements matter

**C (p. 12):** «ويشترط في الأول إيجاب الصغرى وفعليتها وكلية الكبرى».

**Y (pp. 135–137):** explicitly distinguishes *affirmative quality*, *actuality of minor*, and *universality of major* as three conditions, corresponding to quality/jiha/quantity. The gloss signed **«سل»** contrasts a rule based on what **actually** has the middle (associated there with Ibn Sīnā, *al-Shaykh*) and an alternative attributed to al-Fārābī in which the major concerns what can have the middle, not solely what already actually has it. This is a **commentary attribution**, not an independently established passage from al-Fārābī.

**Why affirm minor?** If S is merely disjoint from M, knowing a predicate of M tells nothing determinate about S. **Why actual?** If S can be M but is not M in the actual subject-state to which the major applies, the judgment about actual M need not carry over. **Why universal major?** The major may concern a portion of M *different from* the portion under which S falls. Y's example: “Every human is an animal; some animals are horses” does not entail “some humans are horses” (pp. 136–137). This is not merely a rule about the word “some”; it concerns failure to identify the very portion of M to which the major ascribes P.

**B skeleton:** `S⊆M` and `M⊆P` entail `S⊆P`. If the first inclusion is only possible, replace it by a statement about a different domain/occasion and no actual-time conclusion follows without an appropriate modal bridge. If the major is merely `∃(M∧P)`, even actual `S⊆M` is insufficient to establish `∃(S∧P)`.

**Remaining:** The commentator's historical account of *subject-description persistence* and possibility must govern any further modal figure-I conclusions; this table itself is not a reconstruction of modal `mashrūṭa`, `ʿurfiyya` or `ḥīniyya`.

## III. Figure II: the compressed conjunction/disjunction of restrictions

**C (p. 12), anchor:** «وفي الثاني اختلافهما في الكيف وكلية الكبرى، أما مع دوام الصغرى أو انعكاس سالبة الكبرى وكون الممكنة مع ضرورة أو مع كبرى مشروطة».

**Y (pp. 138–139) disambiguates the syntax**:

**Nonmodal constraints:** the premises differ in quality, and the major is universal. If both affirm or both deny their common predicate M, no determinate opposition between S and P follows; if the major is only particular, it need not govern the portion of M relevant to the minor.

**Modal condition 1 — alternative:** either the minor premise has permanence (a permanent or necessary minor), **or** the negative of the major belongs to an appropriate class of convertible negatives. The Y spine counts six convertible negative types (against nine non-convertible ones) in the system it is expounding. This is not a choice between “always include permanence” and “always include a possible premise”.

**Modal condition 2 — restriction only if possibility occurs:** a possible minor is paired with a necessary or appropriate conditioned major; a possible major is paired with a necessary minor. A separately signed gloss («عب») insists that the clause does NOT require including a possible premise in every second-figure inference; in the absence of a possible premise, the extra disjunction is vacuous. Distinguish *absence of possible premise* from *one occurs and satisfies a stated partnership*.

**Source-facing English correction in L.16:** separates these conditions rather than rendering «وكون الممكنة» as one more optional positive premise. The parenthetic orientation is identified as commentary explanation.

**B skeleton:** in figure II, major `P–M`, minor `S–M`. Standard nonmodal E/A or A/E and universal-major constraints produce negative conclusions for the familiar four moods. Y's longer argument includes modalities under which even this geometry can fail if actuality, permanence, or the opposite's convertibility is omitted. Testing material implications alone cannot certify the sixteen modal combinations.

## IV. Figure III: necessity of actuality and why conclusions are particular

**C (p. 12):** «وفي الثالث إيجاب الصغرى وفعليتها مع كلية إحداهما».

**Y (pp. 140–141):** keeps positive/actual minor and **at least one** universal premise. Enumerates six eligible *formal patterns*: three affirmative and three negative, all yielding particular rather than universal conclusions.

- If the minor is merely possible and need never be actual, a major assertion about M need not yield a conclusion about an actually characterized S.
- If **both** premises are particular, they may concern different individuals under M. For instance, take `M={a,b}`, `S={a}`, `P={b}`. Then `I(M,S)` and `I(M,P)` are both true, but `I(S,P)` is false.
- The particular conclusion results because S (the predicate of the minor premise in figure III) may be much wider than M; a universal assertion about all S cannot be inferred from governing only M.
- In a pure Boolean model, some traditional III moods require nonemptiness of M, as the existing 512-model verifier records. This *is not* a substitute for the historical actualization condition `fiʿliyya`.

**Y (pp. 140–141) proof methods:** reductio using the contradictory of the proposed particular result as a **universal** premise to contradict the original major; or suitable conversion to figure I. These are commentary explanations, not new words to insert in C.

## V. Figure IV: eight *commentary configurations* ≠ eight unrestricted Boolean moods

**C (p.12), conditions:** the two premises are affirmative with a universal minor, **or** differ in quality with at least one universal. The subsequent concluding phrase is exceptionally compressed.

**Y (pp. 145–147):** lists **eight** configurations satisfying the stated form-pattern, but expressly remarks that the author's short matn **omits fully specifying modality restrictions for the fourth figure**, because of its remoteness from the natural order of reasoning. The marginal note signed **«سل»** on p. 145 lists further qualified constraints, including no unrestricted possible premise, convertibility of the negative premise, and case-specific permanence/conditioning requirements. The commentary does **not** establish that all eight are simply valid unrestricted Boolean moods.

For clarity, use `A` = universal affirmative, `E` = universal negative, `I` = particular affirmative, `O` = particular negative. Premises are always **major** `Q(P,M)`, **minor** `Q(M,S)`; conclusion `Q(S,P)`. The ordinal numbers are Y's, not Cairo's.

| Y ordinal | Minor | Major | Intended result | Modern Boolean check across all 512 models |
|---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|---|
| 1 | A | A | I | Requires **P nonempty** (27 countermodels otherwise) |
| 2 | A | I | I | Valid as stated |
| 3 | E | A | **E** | Valid as stated, a **universal negative** |
| 4 | A | E | O | Requires **M nonempty** (27 countermodels otherwise) |
| 5 | I | E | O | Valid as stated |
| 6 | O | A | O | **56 countermodels**, including 30 with S,P,M all nonempty |
| 7 | A | O | O | **56 countermodels**, including 30 with S,P,M all nonempty |
| 8 | E | I | O | **61 countermodels**, including 24 with S,P,M all nonempty |

**What the test establishes:** Under *a specifically stated modern, nonmodal* interpretation, the first five coincide with the five familiar figure-IV moods once existential import is supplied for 1 and 4. Patterns 6–8 are *not* rescued by assuming all three term extensions nonempty.

**What it does NOT establish:** that Y or Taftāzānī's historically defined modal configurations 6–8 are logically invalid. The attributed gloss attaches additional modal and convertibility restrictions (Y p. 145); an adequate reconstruction must first define what those premises assert through time, subject description and negative qualification. The eight-pattern list is **a source-based candidate enumeration, not a Boolean validity certificate**.

**Reproducible file:** `tools/tahdhib/verify_fourth_figure_scope.py`; generated result `tools/tahdhib/verified_fourth_figure_scope.json`; all 512 assignments across three terms are examined. A separate CI regression runs the new verifier and retains its JSON alongside the old 19-mood, 512-model nonmodal report.

### Three explicit all-nonempty countermodels (for the Boolean skeleton only)

Let the universe contain `0,1,2`. The three cases use only nonempty term extensions, so **existential import alone cannot save them**:

- **Pattern 6**, `O(M,S)`, `A(P,M)`, proposed `O(S,P)`: take `M={0,1}`, `S=P={0}`. Some M is not S, and all P are M, but no S fails to be P.
- **Pattern 7**, `A(M,S)`, `O(P,M)`, proposed `O(S,P)`: take `M=S={0}`, `P={0,1}`. Some P is not M, yet every S is P.
- **Pattern 8**, `E(M,S)`, `I(P,M)`, proposed `O(S,P)`: take `M={1}`, `S={0}`, `P={0,1}`. The minor excludes S from M and the major meets M, yet every S is P.

These are carefully limited **countermodels to an unqualified Boolean reading**, not to the richer traditional propositions under conditions that have not yet been modeled.

### The compressed negative result and an English correction

The sequence **«جزئية موجبة إن لم تكن بسلب وإلا فسالبة»** should *not* be translated as though **every negative conclusion must be particular**: Y p. 146 explicitly identifies pattern **3**, a universal-negative minor with universal-affirmative major, as yielding a **universal negative**. Y notes that the author's compression invites a misleading narrower construal. The revised English L16 keeps the short list but states in brackets that a negative conclusion may be universal or particular according to the pattern.

This distinction is an **interpretative correction**, not a revision of Cairo's photographed Arabic.
