Critical points already identified

  • Authorship: the work is a ḥāshiya keyed to short lemmata. Distinguish Taftāzānī’s words, al-Yazdī’s discussion, and later writers’ marginal notes before assigning textual authority.
  • Q p.114: al-Yazdī criticises بالبرهان as an addition found in some copies, but also explains how it could be construed defensibly. Both sides belong in the apparatus; independent readings remain unverified.
  • Q pp.105–107: a definition of induction or analogy must distinguish the method/operation from the complete inferential ḥujja. The causal premise of analogy points to jurisprudential argumentation.
  • Q p.110: the five arts are classified by the epistemic or imaginative force of their matter, not merely by modern logical validity.

Continued main-ḥāshiya: Qum pages 30–37

Ten consecutive, nonoverlapping editorial segments cover eight printed source pages. Page 31 bridges L.04–L.05 and page 35 bridges L.05–L.06; the splitting preserves the textual order without pretending that the source has three authorial chapter breaks. As with L.03, the separately printed هامش is excluded from al-Yazdī’s principal text. The physical page photographs have not been compared.

Editorial issue: al-Yazdī divides all pairs of universals into four relations, while the preceding page permits universals with no extant instances. The resulting extensional classification requires special care with empty classes. This question remains in the analytical apparatus; no philosophical doctrine is silently changed in the translation. Qum page 37 also needs an image check at the شرح / هامش boundary.

Logical universals and definition: Qum pp.48–51

Four consecutive printed pages explain the logical, natural and intellectual universal, the controversy over common natures existing in individuals, and the conditions for a proper defining account. Al-Yazdī expressly distinguishes the majority philosophical position, al-Taftāzānī’s preference, and his own reservation وفيه تأمل. He also distinguishes complete/incomplete ḥadd and rasm from mere lexical explanation.

Qum page 52 is blank in the searchable source; page 53 has only the authorial heading introducing assent. Neither is counted as translated al-Yazdī prose.

Propositions and existential import: Qum pp.54–58

Five consecutive main-text page positions cover mental and spoken propositions, truth as correspondence, subject/predicate/copula, al-Fārābī’s account of borrowing huwa as a nontemporal copula, conditionals, categorical quantification, and the distinct ways in which the subject of an affirmative proposition is required to exist.

Authorship qualification: Qum page 55 contains only a short continuation of al-Yazdī’s page-54 text. The substantial lower marginal note citing al-Sharīf al-Jurjānī is separately attributed to Mīrzā Muḥammad ʿAlī and is not included as al-Yazdī. The main commentary continues beyond p.58 into p.59; no conclusion is invented.

Simple and composite modal logic: Qum pp.59–64

Six further consecutive main-ḥāshiya page positions cover the distinction between **modal matter and stated modality**, four modalities of necessity, necessity versus permanence, actual and possible predication, and the seven named composite modes. Qum p.63 explicitly examines 16 combinations of base propositions and modal qualifications: three invalid, four acceptable and conventionally recognized, and nine acceptable without belonging to that conventional list.

These are translations of the Qum searchable main شرح, not an image-collated Arabic edition. The large printed marginal annotations are not attributed to al-Yazdī.

Connected, disjunctive and quantified conditionals: Qum pp.65–69

Five further source-page positions distinguish intrinsic, relation-grounded conditionals from accidental concurrence; truth-excluding, falsity-excluding and fully exclusive disjunction; and conditional quantity over suppositions of the antecedent. Qum p.65 begins by completing the prior modal discussion; p.69 ends the conditional section immediately before the next chapter on contradiction at p.70.

Source attribution: the extensive separately marked هامش discussions about triple disjunctions and imperatival consequents belong to later commentators, not to al-Yazdī's principal text. Read the source and critical apparatus register for the exact eleven page references and unresolved readings.

Contradiction and modal negation: Qum pp.70–73

Four complete searchable main-commentary page positions explain the double condition for contradiction, the eight identities that must be preserved when comparing two propositions, and the difference between the direct negation of a modal assertion and a proposition merely implied by its negation. For compound propositions, contradiction requires the inclusive alternative of negating a component. In a particular compound assertion, the universal quantifier must apply to each individual before disjoining the component-negations.

Simple conversion and modal rules: Qum pp.74–80

Seven consecutive printed pages distinguish the conversion operation from the converted assertion and distinguish truth-preservation from retention of quantity. Al-Yazdī offers reductio proofs for negative conversion, contrasts al-Fārābī’s possible-subject treatment with Ibn Sīnā’s actual-subject interpretation, and questions al-Taftāzānī’s assumption that a compound conversion must be reduced to separate conversions of its parts.

The long discussion of why ‘quality preservation’ may be redundant on Qum p.75 is a separately signed هامش by Mīrzā Muḥammad ʿAlī. It is not translated as al-Yazdī’s prose.

Contraposition: earlier and later methods: Qum pp.81–84

Four consecutive page positions distinguish earlier contraposition (both term-contradictories with quality retained) from the later arrangement (one contradictory term with quality reversed). The closing two pages contain a rigorous witness-based proof by iftirāḍ under Ibn Sīnā’s actual-subject convention. Qum page 85 is a heading introducing syllogism and is not counted as al-Yazdī commentary.

From the structure of the syllogism to the third figure: Qum pp.86–94

Al-Yazdī’s main ḥāshiya resumes on page 86 after the heading-only page 85. Nine consecutive translated page positions cover syllogism as formally ordered discourse, a conclusion following from premises without an external principle, exceptive and conjunctive syllogisms, the minor/middle/major terms, the first figure's necessary premise conditions, second-figure productivity and three different reduction proofs. Qum page 94 also begins the conditions of the third figure.

Translation gap: Qum printed pp.95–97 are not yet transcribed into this continuous text. The two earlier translated selections on p.98 remain accessible in the reader as selected excerpts, not a fabricated continuation of pp.86–94.

Attribution: on page 91 the principal al-Yazdī text is only a very short clause, followed by a much longer, separately marked هامش. It is not presented as if al-Yazdī wrote the latter. Read the Q86–94 scholarly dossier for source boundaries and editorial problems.

Completion of the categorical syllogism: Qum pp.95–101

Seven further source positions complete the third and fourth figures and explain the general requirements for syllogistic productivity. Al-Yazdī identifies eight fourth-figure moods, corrects the matn’s description of the universal-negative result, and at p.101 explicitly claims to have developed an original general explanation of the second figure’s modal incompatibility conditions.

Induction and causal analogy: Qum pp.105–108

Four pages distinguish complete from incomplete induction and explain the three premises needed for analogy. The central contested step is establishing the ʿilla, the causal property, through dawarān (covariation) or sabr wa-taqsīm (elimination of alternatives). The Qum page-108 wording has a suspected textual corruption, retained in the Arabic pending image comparison.

The five logical arts: Qum pp.110–113

Four further pages cover how demonstration, dialectic, rhetoric, poetry and fallacy differ by their epistemic material; the six immediate bases of certainty; and the difference between a cause of knowing and a cause in reality. The concluding passage criticizes the later manuals for neglecting the five arts in favour of elaborate conditional syllogisms. Qum p.109 contains a heading only, not a translated commentary paragraph.

Scientific subject and principles: Qum pp.114–118

This opening of the conclusion explains the subject, investigated propositions and conceptual/propositional principles of a science, including al-Yazdī’s critique of an excessively narrow definition of proper accidents. The eight heads and educational methods: Qum pp.119–123

These final five source pages distinguish purpose from benefit, explain the name and classification of logic, enumerate the nine or ten traditional sections, and discuss methods of constructing a proof, analyzing an existing inference, defining an object and attaining certainty without imitation.

Correct printed textual boundary: the principal al-Yazdī شرح continues through Qum p.123. The concluding prayer belongs to his discussion. The final printed completion notice is a **separately attributed copy/printing colophon**. Qum p.124 has no main text; p.125 begins the distinct حواشي الحاشية, additional glosses on the gloss. Release standard for a definitive edition

The main ḥāshiya must be transcribed continuously, each paragraph translated and lemma-aligned, with photographic proofing, a documented independent manuscript comparison, recorded variants, an explicit emendation policy, consistent technical terminology, and checked source-linked navigation. None of the unfinished divisions is marked certified.
