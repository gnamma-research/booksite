# Tahdhīb Stage 95 — The 15/13 modal-taxonomy crosswalk and six convertible-negative classes

**Date:** 2 November 2026. **Authorial text:** al-Taftāzānī, *Tahdhīb al-Manṭiq*, Cairo 1330/1912, divisions L11 (modal forms), L14 (simple conversion), L15 (contraposition), L16 (modal syllogism). **Nature of this task:** comparison of historical **type inventories** and commentary conversion rules, not a new source reading or a proof of medieval modal semantics.

## I. Why there are fifteen in the Tahdhīb but thirteen in the Shamsiyyah

The Cairo L11 Arabic expressly distinguishes **eight simple** (`فهذه بسائط`) and **seven compound** (`وهذه مركبات`) forms. That is **15 explicitly named classes** in this working source. This count should not be collapsed into the thirteen main propositions treated as the core inventory in al-Kātibī's *al-Risālah al-Shamsiyyah*.

Tony Street, *Najm al-Dīn al-Kātibī's al-Risālah al-Shamsiyyah* (NYU Press, 2024), commentary on §52 **pp.168–170**, and the thirteen-name **Appendix 1 p.329**, identifies the *customarily investigated thirteen*. His Appendix 2 distinguishes those thirteen from additional forms introduced as components, conclusions or intermediate conversions. Source: [critical bilingual edition](https://dhjhkxawhe8q4.cloudfront.net/library-of-arabic-literature-wp/assets/20240530034338/9781479827510_WEB-1.pdf).

**Item-by-item comparison yields a transparent difference:** Cairo L11 separately names the **absolute temporal** (`وقتية مطلقة`, LT1) and **absolute diffuse** (`منتشرة مطلقة`, LX1) among the *simple* forms. In the Shamsiyyah scheme their content appears as the **first component** of the compound `وقتية` and `منتشرة`, not as two additional entries in the *customarily investigated thirteen*. This explains **15 = 13 + 2 in this specific crosswalk**, not a claim that different authors' modal inventories are literally identical in every semantical respect.

**A crucial exclusion:** Street also quotes al-Rāzī's discussion of **fifteen** species and his decision to set aside *the strongest possible* and *the future possible* from systematic treatment (commentary §52, p.170). That is a **different enumeration criterion**. We must NOT claim those two omitted Rāzī species are LT1/LX1, or that every historical “fifteen” includes the identical types. Counts alone prove no taxonomy identity.

## III. The SIX classes “whose negative forms convert”

The key expression **`القضايا المنعكسة السوالب`** can characterize a *class of propositions* whose **negative forms** convert, even when a particular premise under discussion is **affirmative**. This was decisively clarified in Stage92 by Quṭb al-Dīn al-Rāzī, [*Taḥrīr al-Qawāʿid*, p.419](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/8199/419?lang=fa), and the 2019 edited early Shamsiyyah commentary by Özpilavcı–Öztop, p.89.

The six are:

1. **L — absolute necessary**: universal negative converts to **perpetual universal negative** (dāʾima).
2. **A — absolute perpetual**: converts to perpetual universal negative.
3. **LD1 — general conditional**: converts to **general customary** (`ʿurfiyya ʿāmma`) universal negative.
4. **AD1 — general customary**: converts to general customary universal negative.
5. **LD2 — special conditional**: converts to **general customary non-perpetual-for-some**, `ʿurfiyya lā-dāʾima li-l-baʿḍ`.
6. **AD2 — special customary**: converts to the same qualified universal-negative form.

This is the historical conversion list in Cairo L14:

> ومن السوالب تنعكس الدائمتان دائمة، والعامتان عرفية عامة، والخاصتان عرفية لا دائمة في البعض.

Tony Street's *Shamsiyyah* §§74–76, **pp.47–49**, specifies these universal-negative conversions and distinguishes the seven **other** forms of the standard thirteen whose negative universal forms do not convert in the corresponding way. The Cairo **additional** temporal-absolute components LT1 and LX1 are separately named components, and should not be silently counted as though they belonged to that exact seven-form standard inventory.

**Do not confuse:** whether a named *class's universal negative* converts, whether the currently supplied premise is negative, and whether a *particular negative* of the same class converts. These are three logically different claims.

## IV. The TWO exceptional particular-negative conversions

An even narrower rule, attested in the later commentary and al-Kātibī, permits a **particular negative** to convert only for the two special description-dependent types:

| Original type | Historical result of particular negative simple conversion | Positive particular by contraposition |
|---|---|---|
| **LD2, المشروطة الخاصة** | particular **AD2**, عرفية خاصة | negative particular **AD2**, with the negated predicate promoted as subject |
| **AD2, العرفية الخاصة** | particular **AD2**, عرفية خاصة | negative particular **AD2**, likewise |

See al-Yazdī's [p.83 individual assumption](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/12170/83?lang=ar) and [p.84 continuation](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/12170/84?lang=ar), and *Shamsiyyah* §§76.2 and 84.1. The distinction between **general customary non-perpetual-for-some** as the output of a *universal-negative* conversion and **special customary** as the output of the *particular* exception is important: these outputs should not receive the same unqualified English label.

The Stage93 finite-time verifier tests a **temporal shadow** of these exceptional particular conversions with all three assumptions present. It does not establish necessity or convertibility for every member of the six universal-negative classes. The Stage94 two-individual verifier shows why the particular subject and the nonpermanence witness must be the same person.

## V. Stage92 and the fourth-figure inference conditions

The 2019 expanded commentary (probably by al-Kātibī), p.89, distinguishes **six** modal conditions of fourth-figure productivity. The fourth condition requires the **sixth mood's negative minor** to be one of the two special forms **and** its **major** to belong to a *class whose negative form converts*. The fifth and sixth conditions analogously constrain moods seven and eight.

The 15/13 crosswalk now supplies a **typed reading** of that rule:

- `is_negative_minor` is a claim about **current polarity and term position**;
- `negative_minor_type ∈ {LD2,AD2}` licenses **special particular conversion** only with the full qualification;
- `major_type ∈ {L,A,LD1,AD1,LD2,AD2}` is a claim about the **negative-conversion class** of that major's modality, not about whether it currently carries a negative quality;
- any derived modality has to be checked separately using al-Kātibī §104 and the full commentary.

This resolves the Stage91 erroneous thought that “convertible negatives” necessarily describes an actually negative **major**. We do not emend the transmitted Arabic `الكبرى`.

## VI. New reusable source controls

The deterministic [modal-type crosswalk verifier](../../../../tools/tahdhib/verify_modal_type_crosswalk.py):

- reads the canonical bilingual `data.js`, not copied parallel source wording;
- asserts **15 unique** Cairo modal class names, exactly 8 simple + 7 compound;
- maps the defined Shamsiyyah thirteen and the two separately named Cairo temporal components;
- asserts exactly **six** universal-negative convertible classes and **seven** other standard classes;
- independently locks L14's exact printed conversion grouping and L15's special particular-conversion exception;
- writes **`verified_modal_type_crosswalk.json`** as an inspectable source-typing ledger.

This is a program for **terminological/source consistency**, not a formal theorem proving that the ancient modal combinations are valid. It is separate from the Stage89–94 logical test scripts and is included in GitHub's Tahdhīb reader/proof CI.
