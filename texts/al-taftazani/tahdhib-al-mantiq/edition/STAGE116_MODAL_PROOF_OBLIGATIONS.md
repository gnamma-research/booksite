## Stage116: formal bridge-insufficiency lemma (the precise obstruction)

**Lemma.** An inference system in which all antecedents and conclusions are assessed solely by actual-world person/time predicates does not, without an additional inter-world or essence-based condition, entail the **necessity across possible worlds of its actual-time conclusion**.

**Proof.** Let `M0` satisfy any one of the Stage101 temporal premises and its actual-time conclusion `∃x,t (S(x,t) ∧ ¬P(x,t))`. Form a two-world extension with actual world `w0 = M0`. At a second accessible world `w1`, set `S` empty while retaining a nonempty individual/time domain. All actual-world premises and actual-world conclusions retain exactly their valuations at `w0`, because none quantifies over `w1`. But at `w1` the existential `∃x,t(S∧¬P)` is false. Therefore `□∃x,t(S∧¬P)` is not a logical consequence of those *actual-only* premises. □

**Precisely limited meaning:** this is a theorem about the **inadequacy of the temporal shadow alone** as a necessity proof. The historical modal conclusions `O(A)` and `O(AD1)` are **not** stipulatively `□∃x,t(S∧¬P)`; they can mean reference-relative or description-relative permanence, and medieval source premises may carry full modal constraints. Thus the lemma is **not** a new countermodel to any of the 46 medieval claims. It explains rigorously why the 46 proofs remain zero-certified and which additional definitions must be supplied.
