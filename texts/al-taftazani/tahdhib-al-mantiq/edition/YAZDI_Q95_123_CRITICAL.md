# Al-Yazdī’s *Ḥāshiya* — working text from Qum 95 to the authentic ending at Q123
**Updated 7 November 2026. Status: source-linked Arabic and English translation, not a critically certified edition.**

## Primary text and attribution boundary
The searchable 400-page Qum volume is **not 400 pages of al-Yazdī's principal prose**. The final main `شرح` occurs on [printed Qum page 123](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/12170/123?lang=ar), concluding with an author's supplication; a **separate nonauthorial completion notice** then says `قد تمت النسخة المسماة ب « الحاشية في المنطق » ... ملا عبد الله اليزدي`. [Page 124](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/12170/124?lang=ar) has no main commentary, and [page 125](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/12170/125?lang=ar) is expressly headed **حواشي الحاشية**, the *later glosses on al-Yazdī’s gloss*. The former project register's Q114 endpoint was false and has been corrected everywhere relevant. Page 114 is only the beginning of the final conclusion on the parts of the sciences.

The present seven bilingual source files store each main `شرح` page in Qum **95–108, 110–123**: **29 consecutive page/topic blocks across 28 unique printed-page positions** (Q103 split into two nonoverlapping editorial sections, and Q109 contains only a matn heading, `الصناعات الخمس`). Each block has the page's principal Arabic, a newly translated English rendering, exact source page URL and explanatory notes. The source's printed footnote call numbers and separately signed `هامش` (Mīrzā Muḥammad ʿAlī, Mīrzā Riḍā, ʿAbd al-Raḥīm, or `al-Taqrīb`) are excluded from the authorial corpus. The Q123 completion notice is stored in **`colophon` metadata with `attribution="nonauthorial"`** and is displayed, if at all, as a clearly differentiated printed note.

All Arabic is normalized from the **digitally searchable Qum transcription**, not diplomatic from photographed pages. There has been NO comprehensive photo-based Qum/Gujarat/Tehran collation, no independently identified manuscript stemma, and no certified variants. Separate later annotators' conjectures are not silently imported.

## Critical apparatus for substantive issues

**CR-41 · Fourth-figure eight moods: Q95–98.** Al-Yazdī enumerates **six productive third-figure moods** and **eight productive fourth-figure moods**. Among the fourth figure's eight, the third yields **universal negative**, while most other negative moods yield particular negatives. He criticizes al-Taftāzānī’s wording for concealing this exception, and reports another mistake concerning a reductio of the sixth mood in *Sharḥ al-Risāla*. The several reductions (reductio, major conversion, minor conversion plus reordering, conversion of both premises) apply to **different bounded sets** of moods. Do not present one reduction as universally licensed.

**CR-42 · Claimed new general criterion: Q98–101.** The source’s `ḍābiṭa` describes syllogistic productivity through the middle's universal subjecthood or the major term's universal subjecthood, with actual connection of the minor and precise modal incompatibility. In the second figure, the modal relations of the middle with major/minor descriptions must conflict under the **same** intended intervals. The commentator's ending `وتحقيق هذا البحث على هذا الوجه الوجيه مما تفردت به` is an explicit claim to scholarly originality, which must be checked against earlier works. It is not a claim of originality by the modern editors.

**CR-43 · Exceptive logic: Q102–104.** Five ways of constructing a conditional conjunctive argument are shown. The exceptive argument can affirm/deny the antecedent or consequent, but only **modus ponens** and **modus tollens** work in the relation-grounded connected form; a truth-excluding disjunction and a falsity-excluding disjunction license different complementary exceptions. An exactly exclusive disjunction licenses all four. A proof by contradiction breaks down into **at least two** inferences, with further subordinate arguments sometimes required. The mixed-source Q103 page must be split into two editorial units **without inventing duplicate text**.

**CR-44 · Causal premises of analogy: Q105–108.** Analogy properly is a `ḥujja`, not simply the psychological act of comparison. The crucial premise asserts an actual operative causal property; it can be tested by `dawarān` (presence/absence of ruling tracking a characteristic) or `sabr wa-taqsīm` (enumeration and elimination). The legal-theory example concerns an intoxicating property. The Qum searchable p108 phrase `ثم يبطل ثانيا حكم عليه كل كل` is suspicious; retain the base text with an explicit photographic collation requirement rather than invent a smooth Arabic restoration.

**CR-45 · Epistemic types and demonstration: Q110–113.** Demonstration requires **each** premise to be certain, unlike material that generates dialectical assent, rhetorical probability, imaginative affect, or fallacy. The six foundational types of certain proposition are distinguished. For `burhān limmī`, the middle term must be both a **cause of knowledge** and the **real explanatory cause of the predicated relation**; for `burhān innī`, the middle gives knowledge of the fact without being its cause in reality. The humoral-medicine examples are historical and not modern clinical claims. Q113 criticizes later manuals for spending effort on conditional syllogistic refinements while neglecting the *five arts*; keep this as al-Yazdī's own evaluative position.

**CR-46 · Subject and parts of a science: Q114–118.** The three parts are **subject**, **problems/propositions**, and **conceptual and propositional principles**. Q114's `بالبرهان` is a reading said by Yazdī to be from scribes in **some unidentified copies**, while he also offers two interpretations that could make it acceptable; no manuscript basis is yet verified. Q115’s four-pronged objection questions whether the subject could be independent of the problems/principles; Q116 engages Dawwānī and the varying statuses of axioms, postulates and requested concessions; Q117–118 challenges narrow formulations of proper accidents (including problems from jurisprudence and grammar). Distinguish author's argument, quoted earlier thinkers and separately printed `هامش`.

**CR-47 · Formal object and ranking logic among sciences: Q119–121.** The eight preliminary headings of a discipline distinguish **purpose as motivating final cause**, subsequent **benefit**, name, author, subject area, relative ranking, division and pedagogical methods. Q120 asks whether logic is philosophy if philosophy concerns **external things as they are**, or if mental objects also fall within it. The conventional historic account of Aristotle/al-Fārābī/Avicenna is a reported intellectual history, not independently corroborated modern chronology. Q121 gives **nine parts of logic**, sometimes ten if words are separately counted; this must not be conflated with **our 19 modern editorial divisions of the matn**.

**CR-48 · Construction, analysis, definition, demonstration: Q122–123.** The `taqsīm` method builds a proof **backwards from a sought conclusion** through candidate middle terms and premise relations. `taḥlīl` analyzes an existing proof and its hidden arrangement. The other methods are constructing a defining account by genus/differentia/property and attaining certainty with appropriately grounded premises, rather than merely relying on reputation or acceptance. A historical link between **knowledge and practice** closes the main text. The Q123 copy completion notice is **not** part of the author's closing prayer.
