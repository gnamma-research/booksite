## Important interpretive findings and cruxes

1. **HWN-066–067:** advancing a constituent usually indicates emphasis/specification but need not universally assert exclusion. The discussion explicitly differentiates *ikhtiṣāṣ* from *ḥaṣr* in al-Subkī's account, then disputes that contrast; real/relative exclusivity is distinguished. The Imāmī background of the “following the Imams is following the Prophet” argument must remain attributed to its speaker.
2. **HWN-074–078:** *ṣidq* and *ḥaqq* are distinguished by the direction/aspect of correspondence. If truth in “proposition: statement capable of truth or falsehood” meant simply a *true report*, the proposed definition risks circularity. One response makes ṣidq signify the property of correspondence; its validity depends on conventional/literal usage and the relevant context. These are materially philosophical claims, not simply lexical synonyms.
3. **HWN-082** p149 is **PARTIAL** because the alternative use of `بالتحقيق` after `ويحتمل الاستقراء` is not reliably deciphered in the digital text. Do not silently replace with a more plausible Arabic phrase. The first causal attachment is understood, the second remains an open reading.
4. **HWN-084–085:** distinct theories of ordinary `ẓarf laghw` vs predicative `ẓarf mustaqarr`; one classifies by **expressed/omitted governing term**, another by **specific/general** action. The long note allows a specific action to be semantically understood in a grammatically predicative phrase, and warns against turning frequent patterns into exceptions-free rules.
5. **HWN-090** Q151–152 is **PARTIAL**: demonstrative “this” may point to a mentally ordered work, a verbal expression or externally inscribed signs, but an explicitly attributed al-Taqrīb text must be kept separate. Distinct schema of deictic reference should not be turned into invented codex variants.
6. **HWN-091–097:** seven putative referents (meanings, words, writing marks, pairwise and triple combinations), but predicating “refinement of speech” constrains the permitted referent. Dawwānī argues that book titles refer to a **kind of composed discourse**, not merely a single physical copy. `sawāʾ ... aw/am` is parsed differently by Abū ʿAlī, Ibn Hishām and al-Raḍī.
7. **HWN-098–103:** predicating abstract `tahdhīb` of a book requires rhetorical intensification or an omitted predicate; the dispute about `majāz al-ḥadhf` distinguishes omission from addition and grammatical from lexical transfer. These are historical rhetorical interpretations, not permission to fabricate Arabic manuscript text.
8. **HWN-104** p155 is a **complete first-pass English argument on logic as instrumental and canonical**: logic mediates between intellect and acquired conclusions; observance, not possession, prevents error; the definition is a descriptive **rasm**, not an essential **ḥadd**, because instrumentality is relational and preventing error is the final goal. A challenge concerning logic's self-application is answered. This extends the earlier Stage102 selection into a numbered source argument.
9. **HWN-106** p155–156: an especially important formal distinction: **not requiring** philosophical judgments to conform to Islam as a methodological criterion is not **requiring nonconformity**. It would be invalid to infer that philosophical inquiry is by definition anti-religious.
10. **HWN-107–110:** the grammatical ruler of coordination, the optional active/passive `muqarrib`, and genitive/min are examined as **competing historical parsings**, not certainties from a single printed digital source.
