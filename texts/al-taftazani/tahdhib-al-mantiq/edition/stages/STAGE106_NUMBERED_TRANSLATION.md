## Important textual and logical discoveries

1. **Note 24:** The relative syntactic force of explanatory `أي` is contested. Taftāzānī's observation on attached pronouns is taken to support the majority who regard the explanation as `ʿaṭf bayān`; a later objection says that the impossibility of adjectivally modifying a pronoun instead favours al-Sakkākī's treatment as coordination. Compare Ibn Hishām's objection to al-Zamakhsharī's analysis of Q 5:117. Neither conclusion is certified by an external independent commentary here.
2. **Note 25 is incomplete:** the Arabic searchable source has `ومن قال به ... وق.` with uncertain `به` and a suspicious terminal abbreviation. A note may be source-transcribed yet remain semantically/philologically incomplete. It is flagged; do not make up a reading.
3. **Notes 26–32:** Three different accounts of guidance are distinguished: showing the path; effectively bringing one to the sought goal; and the more general indication with grace that may or may not result in arrival. These are not one proposition. Biblical/Qurʾānic traditions and the opposing readings of Q 41:17 and Q 28:56 are cited as polemical tests. Track when a quote belongs to `al-Taqrīb`, Muḥammad ʿAlī, ʿAbd al-Raḥīm or a reported earlier authority. One commentator's assertion that apparent apostasy proves never to have had true faith is his own controversial premise.
4. **Notes 35–36:** The claim that `الاستعمال` appears singular in most copies versus a dual misreading is **the glossator's report**. It does NOT satisfy the project requirement of independently collated manuscript variants. `سواء الطريق` literally “middle of the road” can, by associated consequence, indicate the road that unfailingly leads to the destination; that consequence is not itself part of the bare lexicon.
5. **Notes 38–39:** Different rhetorical theories of `kināya` reverse the relationship of `lāzim` and `malzūm`; preserve the two orientations rather than forcing a single modern formal implication direction. Dawwānī's interpretation expresses the intended figurative referent, not necessarily an alternative dictionary meaning.
6. **Notes 41–42:** The `dīn/milla/sharīʿa` debate quotes al-Rāghib, al-Taftāzānī and later Shiʿi invocations; their authority and exact sources are not certified. One reading of the prologue foreshadows **both** logic and theology, and the other foreshadows theology more transparently while logic is its prerequisite; that is the `barāʿat al-istihlāl` claim.
7. **Notes 43–46:** The question of the attachment of `lanā` to `jaʿala` versus `rafīq` is grammatical and theological. One party objects that taking God to act *for our benefit* predicates human-style purposes; the glossator **disagrees with the ban on final causation** but accepts an interpretation common to opponents. Avoid treating the dispute as unanimous.
8. **Notes 47–50:** A separate unnumbered passage defines `ʿiṣma` and `luṭf`, and another unnumbered passage demonstrates the danger of turning the *usual* connotation of `duʿāʾ ʿalā` into a universally quantified major premise. These two are included outside the numbered count. The ensuing distinction between God 'requesting' and God 'bestowing mercy' explains `ṣalāt` by final effect rather than human passion.
9. **Notes 51–55:** The writer's theological account of the Prophet as the perfect individual and the identification of `risāla` as encompassing `nubuwwa` contain narrated claims and figures whose chains have not been authenticated. A reported alternative distinguishes direct encounter with the angel from dream, inspiration or audition; do not label this undisputed Sunni consensus.
