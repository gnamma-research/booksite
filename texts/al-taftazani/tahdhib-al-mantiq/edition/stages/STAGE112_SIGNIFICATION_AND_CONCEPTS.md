## Scholarly progress (new analysis with original-source boundaries)

### 1. Signification, Q191–196: whole / part / implied meaning

- [Q191–192](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/12170/192?lang=ar) divides conventional verbal signification into **`muṭābaqa`** (a word indicating its complete assigned meaning), **`taḍammun`** (a constituent of that assigned meaning) and **`iltizām`** (an external concomitant). The source insists that the same meaning can be signified differently *under different aspects or different assignments*. It warns against defining the species only by their object without the aspect `min ḥaythu`. DALALA notes21–22 remain partial.
- [Q193](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/12170/193?lang=ar) argues whether “sight” is contained in the idea of blindness or is a distinct *relatum* presupposed by the relation of privation. This is a precise distinction between a relation entering a definition and the other term of that relation remaining outside it.
- [Q193–195](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/12170/194?lang=ar) examines the controversial entailment **`taḍammun/iltizām `→` muṭābaqa`**: intended primary meaning, conventionally understood meaning, actual speaker intention, and a theoretical implied whole must not be treated as one relation. Finite verbs with implied subjects are used to stress-test the claim.
- [Q195–196](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/12170/196?lang=ar) rejects inferring a *real* case from a mere possibility and questions whether one necessarily conceives an unlimited sequence of a thing's concomitants. A mutually entailing pair does not necessarily constitute a vicious circle of epistemic causation. Source distinctions are preserved in PARTIAL notes32–33.

### 2. Signification, Q197–205: simple and compound expressions; intention

- [Q197–200](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/12170/199?lang=ar) analyses “compound expression” as a source part **intended to indicate** a constituent of its **corresponding** meaning. Merely having a visible/graphic constituent or potential use is not enough. The finite verb's material and form are disputed counterexamples, including implicit subjects. Long notes37–38 remain partial.
- [Q201](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/12170/201?lang=ar) reports Avicenna's *Shifāʾ* and al-Ṭūsī on lexical indication being tied to what the speaker intends, against later writers who treat apprehension of established senses as independent of the speaker's intention. The glossator proposes that much disagreement is over the definition of “signification,” not the existence of hearing-induced understanding.
- [Q202–203](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/12170/203?lang=ar) distinguishes a **report**, structurally apt for truth/falsehood, from an **inshāʾ**, which performs requests and speech acts. The necessity of the actual truth of “whole exceeds part” does not disqualify it from the report class. Restrictive vs nonrestrictive compounds differ by the interpreter's treatment of the second component as qualifying the first.

### 3. Signification, Q203–215: parts of speech, assignment, gradation, transfer

- [Q203–205](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/12170/204?lang=ar) compares logicians' independent noun, temporal verb-like `kalima`, and syntactic `adāt` with grammarians' `ism/fiʿl/ḥarf`, which do **not** perfectly coincide (defective verbs, verbal nouns, `asmāʾ al-afʿāl`). It then challenges whether a simple expression can be divided by one/many meanings without restricting the division to words with independently conceived nominal meanings. Long notes54,57,60,63,64 remain partial.
- [Q206–208](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/12170/208?lang=ar) distinguishes **specific assignment to a particular**, **general assignment to a universal**, and **general assignment to particular referents under a common concept**; these separate pronouns/demonstratives from proper names. Use (*istiʿmāl*) must not be conflated with original assignment (*waḍʿ*). Some attempted rescues require `istikhdām`, a deliberate change of referent.
- [Q209–210](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/12170/210?lang=ar) analyses *mutawāṭiʾ* universals (equal application) and *mushakkik* universals (priority, perfection or degree) **counterfactually** where actual members do not exist. Priority in cause is not identical with greater intensity. A new separately attributed gloss after note74 is stored outside numbered totals.
- [Q211–215](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/12170/214?lang=ar) distinguishes `mushtarak` (several independent assigned senses), `manqūl` (conventionally transferred), `ḥaqīqa` (literal within current convention) and `majāz` (figurative with blocking indicator). The same word may be literal in one disciplinary usage and transferred in another. The historical Q215 note91 mentions unverified variant spellings; no real independent manuscript witness is certified.

### 4. NEW Concepts chapter, Q216–221

- [Q216](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/12170/216?lang=ar) distinguishes the **individual mental form as a psychological occurrence** from the **nature represented and possessed in mental existence**; universality is considered primarily in the latter respect. This should be cross-referenced with al-Jurjānī, Avicenna and the user's broader form/intellect studies, but their writings have not yet been source-collated here.
- [Q217–218](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/12170/218?lang=ar) tests the universal/particular distinction against distant indistinct individuals, empty concepts, persons apparently thinkable under alternatives, and *farḍ* as intellectual admissibility rather than merely saying any arbitrary words.
- [Q219–220](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/12170/219?lang=ar) reports modal distinctions: removing necessity from existence vs nonexistence; general vs special possibility, and the claim that the concept “necessary existent” remains a universal *in signification* even though only one real instance can exist under sound demonstration. The al-Ahrī gloss is independently signed and has not been collapsed into Y.
- [Q220–221](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/12170/221?lang=ar) discusses actual simultaneously infinite collections vs a sequence that can continue without a last item, reporting a philosophical eternal-world view without endorsing it. The next source is **H-MAFAHIM local note12 at printed p221**, then notes13 onward.
