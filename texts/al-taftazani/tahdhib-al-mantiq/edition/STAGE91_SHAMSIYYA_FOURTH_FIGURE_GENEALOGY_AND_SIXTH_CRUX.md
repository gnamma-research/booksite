## 1. New genealogical discovery

In *Shamsiyyah* §95, al-Kātibī already enumerates the **same eight fourth-figure formal combinations** as those later explained in the al-Yazdī commentary. His §97 explicitly distinguishes:

- the **first five**, which older logicians regarded as productive in the categorical setting; and
- the **last three**, for which his repair requires the negative to be **one of two special compound modal forms**, not a simple negative proposition.

Thus the Stage89 outcome—five familiar nonmodal patterns, three not validated by simple existence—is *precisely the distinction the earlier source itself marks*. This is not an anomaly invented by modern Boolean formalization, and it is not a proof that al-Kātibī was mistaken. It is evidence that the logic being expounded is **historically modal**, and that the last three cannot be treated as unqualified A/E/I/O moods.

**Near-literal English summary of §97**: Earlier scholars limited productive modes to five because the last three allow divergent conclusions when built from simple premises. Al-Kātibī stipulates a special negative proposition to block that objection. The status of the proposed rescue must be checked under his definitions and actual conversion rules.

## 2. Al-Kātibī §103: five modal restrictions to carry into L16 commentary apparatus

| No. | Historical source condition, paraphrased | Formal role in the audit |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Actuality (*fiʿliyya*) in the fourth-figure premises | A merely possible identification of M with S or P does not support an actual predicative conclusion without an explicit bridge |
| 2 | Any negative premise used must be **convertible** under its full modal qualification | Ordinary O-propositions do not simply convert; a specific *khāṣṣa* negative may warrant a different rule |
| 3 | In mode 3, either the minor holds permanently or the major satisfies the general customary (*ʿurfiyya ʿāmma*) requirement | Quantifies a persistence condition needed for a universal-negative modal result |
| 4 | Source wording says **the major** in mode 6 must be a convertible negative | **INTERNAL SOURCE CRUX:** mode 6's negative is the **minor**, and §104 reduces the mode by converting that minor |
| 5 | In mode 8, the minor must be one of the two special forms and the major must meet general customary conditions | Constrains the reversal and the **conversion of the resulting conclusion** |

**Witness-level caution at #4.** The source's Arabic as printed by Street, p. 68, reads `كون الكبرى في السادس من المنعكسة السوالب`. A later note signed `سل` in the al-Yazdī tradition repeats `الكبرى`; these are **two textual occurrences** but not enough evidence for autographic authorial intent. The internal conflict is real; do **not** silently emend to `الصغرى` or attribute the conjecture to al-Kātibī.

It is possible that #4 is a mechanical transmission error, that a different enumeration is intended, or that it is an abbreviated reference with a specialized convention. The immediate context favors the **minor** as the target of conversion, but that supports an **editorial conjecture**, not a certified source reading.

## 3. The sixth-mode problem triangulated from independent passages

| Locus | What it explicitly asserts |
|---|---|
| al-Kātibī **§95.8**, Street p.65 | Mode 6: **particular negative minor**, **universal affirmative major** |
| al-Kātibī **§103**, Street p.68 | Fourth condition: **major** in mode 6 must belong to convertible negatives |
| al-Kātibī **§104**, Street p.69 | Result of mode 6 obtained through **conversion of the minor**, then reduction to figure II |
| al-Yazdī-associated signed `سل` gloss, p.145 | Repeats the apparently anomalous **major** designation |
| Ḥāshiyat `Tuḥfa` on the other printed *Sharḥ al-Tahdhīb*, online p.241 | Says explicitly that the sixth works by **conversion of the minor**, requiring it to be among the two special negative forms |

The fourth condition in its literal wording conflicts with both the sixth mood's listed premise qualities and the proposed proof. The controlled Taftāzānī Arabic says neither `الكبرى` nor `الصغرى` in this fine-grained source; therefore this dispute belongs **in the historical explanatory apparatus and its own variant inquiry**, never as a forced emendation to Cairo L16.

This is the first substantive **source-internal historical puzzle** uncovered by the proof-level audit after the original Stage90 Cairo phrase repair.

## 4. Source-authorized reduction plans for the three extra moods

Notation: S is minor term / desired conclusion's subject; P is major term / its predicate; M is middle term. Figure IV has major **P–M** and minor **M–S**. A/E/I/O here mean ordinary nonmodal quantified forms **only for displaying the skeleton**.

### Mode 6 — convert the special negative *minor*, then reduce to figure II

Premises in skeletal notation: `O(M,S)`, `A(P,M)`; target `O(S,P)`.

If the *historical special negative* allows conversion to `O(S,M)`, then the two premises become the second-figure `O(S,M)`, `A(P,M)`, which yields `O(S,P)`.

The explanatory Boolean fact `O(S,M) ∧ A(P,M) ⇒ O(S,P)` is valid. But the transition `O(M,S) ⇒ O(S,M)` is **not** generally valid for an unrestricted nonmodal particular negative. The *modal conversion license* must come from the source and cannot be assumed simply because the converted result would make a valid second-figure proof.

### Mode 7 — convert the special negative *major*, then reduce to figure III

Premises: `A(M,S)`, `O(P,M)`; target `O(S,P)`.

If the major is of the specifically convertible negative kind, obtain `O(M,P)`. Then `A(M,S)`, `O(M,P)` yield `O(S,P)` by the third figure.

Again, the final inference is valid in the **Boolean conditional skeleton**; its historic validity depends on justifying the conversion of `O(P,M)` under the relevant compound modality.

### Mode 8 — reverse the premise order and convert the *conclusion*

Premises: `E(M,S)`, `I(P,M)`; desired `O(S,P)`.

In ordinary Boolean logic, reversing the premise order into the first figure establishes **`O(P,S)`**: some P is M, and no M is S, so some P is not S. This is **not yet** `O(S,P)`.

The historical proposal is to convert that negative particular **result** under the specific modal qualifications supplied by §103 (#5), so that the sought direction follows. This is the *most revealing* demonstration that the special mode cannot be justified by mere subject-existence assumptions.

**What is proven:** a reduction from the premises to the reversed-direction intermediate; conditional validity if a licensed historical conversion exists.  
**What remains unproven:** that the particular derived modality actually meets all presuppositions of its special conversion, including its *time*, *subject-description* and *lā-dawām* scope.

The updated program `tools/tahdhib/verify_fourth_figure_scope.py` documents all three bridge requirements and verifies the **conditional nonmodal** inferential segments over 512 three-term assignments. A returned true flag for a bridge is **not** certification that the historical conversion license holds.

## 5. Modal conclusion inheritance: al-Kātibī §104

This source gives a valuable further dimension omitted from an ordinary eight-mood table: even if an inference holds, **what is the modality of the result?**

| Mode | Historical procedure / result, summarized from §104 |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | Converse of minor when permanence or the relevant convertible-negative class applies; otherwise general absolute |
| 3 | Perpetual if either premise has permanence; otherwise modality of converse of minor |
| 4–5 | Perpetual if major has permanence; otherwise modality of converse of minor, removing its nonpermanence qualification |
| 6 | Treat as second-figure result **after converting minor** |
| 7 | Treat as third-figure result **after converting major** |
| 8 | First reverse premise order, then convert the **conclusion** |

This shows why modern A/E/I/O proof success is **not sufficient**: the conclusion carries a historical modal classification that must be established separately.

## 6. Taftāzānī's own L16 remains source-limited

**Cairo p.12 (Stage90 corrected phrase):**

> وكليتهما مع الموجبة الجزئية موجبة جزئية إن لم يكن بسلب وإلا فسالبة

This directly photographed base does not itself express al-Kātibī's five additional modal constraints, and the source does not license their import as unmarked Taftāzānī words. The apparatus can and should say: the later *Sharḥ al-Tahdhīb* glosses explain how the compressed Taftāzānī list fits a technical system already laid out in al-Kātibī's earlier *Shamsiyyah*.

**Direct borrowing remains to be demonstrated:** common figures, mood numbering, and proof patterns strongly place both works within an interconnected post-Avicennan formal-logical tradition. An actual dependency claim would require verbal comparison of more passages, authorial citations, and chronological examination of intermediaries such as Quṭb al-Dīn al-Rāzī's *Sharḥ Maṭāliʿ*.
