## New example A — disjunctive row 6/6 (D/D)

**Working Arabic (normalized punctuation and vowel endings):**

> نحو: إمّا أن يكون هذا العدد زوجًا أو فردًا، وإمّا أن لا يكون زوجًا ولا فردًا.

**English:** For example, either this number is even or odd, or it is neither even nor odd.

**Source grade:** comparatively clear in both photographed impressions; working source-photo reading, **not** a manuscript-certified text. It illustrates a disjunction whose constituent alternatives are themselves disjunctions.

## New example B — disjunctive row 4/6 (K/K)

**Working Arabic; retain grammatical caution:**

> نحو: إمّا أن يكون «إن كانت الشمس طالعة فالنهار موجود»، وإمّا أن يكون «إن كانت طالعة لم يكن موجودًا».

**English:** For example, either the conditional “if the sun is risen, then daylight exists” holds, or the conditional “if it is risen, it does not exist” holds.

**Source grade:** the contrast between two connected conditionals is photographically evident, but the quotation boundaries, occurrence of **كان/كانت**, and ellipses in the second conditional need a further diplomatic pass. This is a provisional *sense-level* reading, **not** a fully translated verified cell.
