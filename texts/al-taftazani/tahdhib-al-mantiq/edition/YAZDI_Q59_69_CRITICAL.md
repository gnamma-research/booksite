## 1. Textual scope and authorship
Two source files are incorporated into the ordinary `Matn + sharḥ` reading state, with no third mode:
- `yazdi-continuous-q59-64-data.js`: six consecutive printed pages of al-Yazdī's **main شرح** on infinite-negated categorical terms and **simple and composite modalities** (editorial logic L.11).
- `yazdi-continuous-q65-69-data.js`: five consecutive printed pages of his **main شرح** on **connected conditionals, disjunctions and quantification over antecedent suppositions** (L.12). Qum p.65 opens with the last words of the modal discussion continued from p.64; the page 69 main commentary ends before its separately attributed `هامش`.

All the authorial main-text words were taken from the `شرح` section exposed by the Qum searchable web edition, with footnote-call numbers omitted and punctuation/spaces lightly normalized. Original source-page boundaries are retained at p.59→60, 63→64, 64→65, 65→66, 66→67 and 67→68. The printed **أقسام الشرطية** heading is a structural guide and has been removed from the authorial Arabic paragraph itself.

Extensive `هامش` material in the same volume, including the arguments by Mīrzā Muḥammad ʿAlī on coextensive modal conjuncts, triple disjunctions and quantification over all antecedent circumstances, has **not** been included or counted as al-Yazdī's main commentary. It remains a distinct reception/commentary layer eligible for separately attributed future translation.

## 3. Critical problems requiring separate apparatus

**CR-16 · Modal matter versus stated modality (Q59).** `مادة القضية` is the intrinsic condition actually obtaining in the relation. `جهة القضية` is the way necessity, permanence, possibility etc. is specified or represented in speech or thought. The assertion is true when its stated mode matches the real matter; conflating the two turns al-Yazdī's criterion into a tautology. Also differentiate a negation within a term (infinite-negated `maʿdūla`) from negation of the entire predicative relation.

**CR-17 · Necessity versus permanence and two durations (Q60–62).** `ḍarūra` denies that the subject/predicate relation can be separated; `dawām` says it is not in fact separated. Their subject conditioning may be by the subject **as such** throughout its duration, by the subject **while it bears a description**, by an **appointed time**, or by **an unspecified time**. The historical example of perpetual celestial motion must not be imported as contemporary natural science. Critical formal reconstruction must bind each temporal/modal operator to the correct subject life/description, rather than displaying the negative counterpart as an instantaneous contradiction.

**CR-18 · General possibility vs special possibility (Q61–64).** General `imkān` is denial that the opposite is necessary; special `imkān` combines nonnecessity on **both** sides. A composite contains a principal judgment plus a further qualification with opposite **quality** but preserved **quantity** of the subject. The conventional seven composites are distinguished from the eight simple modes. At Q63, al-Yazdī analyzes 4×4 = **16** candidate qualifications: **3 impossible**, **4 acceptable and named**, and **9 acceptable but not among the conventional recognized list**. This source explicitly refuses to identify historical nomenclature with the entire domain of logical possibility. The source does **not** enumerate all 16 in a fully explicit matrix here; do not claim any manufactured full matrix is authorial.

**CR-19 · The conditional connective and the intrinsic relation (Q65).** A `luzūmiyya` affirms a link on account of a relation or denies such a relation, whether a coincidental conjunction still happens or not. An `ittifāqiyya` deals with conjunction without its being founded on that relationship. The classical discussion therefore sharply distinguishes **connection** from **causally or otherwise relationally grounded connection**. This is not interchangeable with the truth-functional material implication of modern propositional calculus. A connected **affirmative** may have two internal **negative** categorical components.

**CR-20 · Three disjunction types, two interpretations of restriction, and intrinsic incompatibility (Q66–68).** `ḥaqīqiyya` excludes both jointly true and jointly false alternatives. `māniʿat al-jamʿ` excludes joint truth; `māniʿat al-khulū` excludes joint falsity. Both truth-only and falsity-only may be interpreted narrowly (the other case explicitly allowed) or broadly (the other case simply unspecified). The alternatives may be incompatible **from their own intelligible natures** (`ʿinādiyya`) or only within some particular material condition (`ittifāqiyya`). Confusing these senses makes several inference patterns unjustifiably stronger.

**CR-21 · Quantified antecedent circumstances (Q68–69).** The quantity of a conditional is established over `taqādīr al-muqaddam`, possible suppositions of the **antecedent**, not automatically over objects of a single categorical subject. The connective transforms independently complete assertoric components into dependently meaningful clauses of the whole conditional. A historian of logic must not identify this without argument with simple first-order quantification over individuals. Nor should the later printed marginal critique of a consequent that is an imperative be assigned to al-Yazdī.

**CR-22 · Diplomatic text still unverified.** The source reports a bracketed variant at Q59 (`جزئه/الجزء`) and another at Q65 (`الحمار ناهقا / الفرس صاهلا`). No manuscript siglum is identified from these searchable notices. The present display retains the Qum running text and records the alternatives in editorial notes. The photographed Qum and Gujarat prints and independently identified manuscript witness(es) must be compared before any emendation or new critical apparatus lemma is certified.

The new L.11 and L.12 Qum main-text sequences contain **eleven translated page-aligned blocks** and replace **39 overlapping excerpt cards** from those units in the reader. The display therefore contains **114 visible commentary blocks** (177−[11+10+9+18+23+29+10]+47). This count does not imply each visible block is a whole source page; Q55, for instance, contains only one line of main commentary.

The course of the original al-Taftāzānī **matn** and the independent Tahdhīb al-Kalām reader are untouched. The two reading states remain **Matn** and **Matn + sharḥ**, with no third commentary-only state. **Neither the entire al-Yazdī commentary nor a definitive manuscript critical edition has been completed.**
