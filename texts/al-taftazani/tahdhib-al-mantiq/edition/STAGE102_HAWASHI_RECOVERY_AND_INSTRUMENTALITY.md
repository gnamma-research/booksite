## Source-checked logical argument, Qum p.155, gloss no.104
[Arabic source p155](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/12170/155?lang=ar). The numbered gloss begins `قوله والمنطق آلة قانونية`. It unfolds a careful account of the definition of logic.

**Instrument:** Logic mediates between the rational faculty and new acquired conclusions. It does not supply the faculty or automatically yield conclusions. **Rule:** the art consists in universal canons governing particular inferential operations; the author gives conversion of a universal affirmative to a particular affirmative as an application, assuming the ordinary existent-term context of his example. **Protection:** error-prevention is ascribed to **observance of the rules**, not possession of the discipline. Otherwise every logician would be infallible, contrary to experience; one may neglect rules. These are three logically different predications, not interchangeable descriptive synonyms.

**Definition type:** The gloss concludes that this is `rasm`, not an essential `ḥadd`, because the art's instrumental relation is extrinsic and its protective benefit is a final end, not an intrinsic constitutive differentia. An objection notes that logical propositions can be instruments for investigating other logical propositions. The response distinguishes a part's service to another part from the whole being essentially defined by instrumentality; the underlying thesis needs only the relative/instrumental character rather than a blanket denial of reflexive use.

**Related page seam:** Q155 note 106 starts at the bottom of [p155](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/12170/155?lang=ar) and continues into [p156](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/12170/156). It distinguishes *not requiring* conformity to a criterion from *requiring nonconformity*: philosophy's not making an Islamic doctrinal rule a formal criterion is not the proposition that philosophy by definition must contradict it. Such distinctions matter for comparing the disciplines' formal objects.

**Attribution caution:** Q155 contains the tail of a prior note and different numbered glosses; Q157 visibly shifts from an ʿAbd al-Raḥīm signature to a separately introduced statement by Mīrzā Muḥammad ʿAlī. Signatures and cross-page continuations must be confirmed on the print image. See [p157](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/12170/157?lang=ar), [p178](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/12170/178?lang=ar).
