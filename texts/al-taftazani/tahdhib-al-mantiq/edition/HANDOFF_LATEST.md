## NEW INTELLECTUAL CONTENT (critical points, not modern claims of general consensus)

- **Muqaddima (notes1–15):** distinguish book's verbal introduction from science's intellectual prerequisites (its definition, subject, goal); why “an introduction” can be indefinite as a nominal subject; the literal “what is inside what” puzzle; the vanguard etymology vs technical re-assignment and metaphor; logical premise as part of an inference vs prerequisite condition. Separate speakers (Mīrzā Muḥammad ʿAlī, ʿAbd al-Raḥīm, quoted al-Taqrīb) must be identified. Note4 `وجه (خ ل)` is a *reported unverified copy variant*, NOT a newly certified manuscript variant.
- **Knowledge (notes16–19):** defining knowledge as a mental form, its reception or a relation; a separately quoted modern critic prefers disclosure of what really is. Whether knowledge needs definition before it is divided: difference between real definition, lexical explanation and direct introspective familiarity. Circularity objection misidentifies particular cognitions with general knowledge. Long note19 remains partial.
- **Conception and assent (notes20–34):** contrasts *simple intellectual act of judgment* with *compound four-element theory* (subject/predicate/relation/assent) and the newer theory (three elements with judgment as accident). Another distinct question concerns **parts of the proposition** (three vs four); do not conflate it with the psychological composition of assent. The complete/incomplete relation distinction matters for Q171–174. Notes20,23,30 partial. Note29's modern al-Karmī intervention remains separate. Eightfold taxonomy of imagined, doubtful, conjectural, probable, false, ignorant, imitative and certain cognition is the glossator's own classification.
- **Necessary/acquired knowledge (notes35–46):** every conception and every assent may be either immediate or discursively acquired. “Obtained without inquiry” is not strictly identical to “does not depend on inquiry” under intuitively acquired theoretical truths; same for `iqtisām` as actively dividing. Two theories of `kināya` depend on direction of `lāzim/malzūm`; inferring a narrower thing from a broader necessary consequence is invalid unless relation also goes back. Long circularity/regress argument note42 remains partial; critical assumption about cross-type acquisition explicitly registered.
- **Naẓar (notes47–49):** question why a definition uses *intelligible* rather than *known*, universals as middle terms and the authorial use of rhymed prose. Separate deliberated inquiry from intuition. These historical arguments do not by themselves demonstrate that particular things cannot be learned from signs.
