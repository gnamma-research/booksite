## Why the new arguments matter (and their limits)

- **Q178–179 local Intro50:** the difficulty of seeking what is not yet known (*Meno-style paradox*) and the requirement to identify the desired object under one description while remaining ignorant of another. The gloss reports al-Rāzī's objection, and defers more detailed objections by al-Marāghī. Keep Intro50 PARTIAL.
- **Q179–181 Intro52–64:** universal `qānūn` as an immediately applicable universal major premise, its actual vs potential particular applications, the distinction between mere possession of logic and attention to its rules, and contradictory outcomes across different persons or within one person over time. The need for logic is for the discursive thinker as such, not every metaphysical kind of cognition.
- **Q182–183 Intro66–70:** need for *both* parts of logic, definitional and inferential, requires showing both conceptions and assents may need acquisition. Apparent three-premise argument can unpack into four. Alleged regress from logic's being learned is met by immediate first-figure norms grounding other learned norms; distingue *needing a rule* from *needing to learn it*.
- **Q184–187 Intro71–82:** the subject of logic consists of known conception/assent *insofar as capable of leading to unknowns*. Long al-Taqrīb criticism says even an obvious “fire is hot” can be a major/minor in further inference. Natural sciences investigate intrinsic accidents of their subject, with disputed external, equal, broader and narrower intermediaries; a separately sourced gloss after Intro73 is not wrongly absorbed. Definiens and proof are logically meanings whose expressions serve as communicative signs.
- **Q188–191 H-DALALA 1–20:** first signification source rubric; three forms of **verbal conventional signification: muṭābaqa** (whole assigned sense), **taḍammun** (part), **iltizām** (outside concomitant). Historical language/logic preparatory studies are instrumental to cognition. The sixfold verbal/nonverbal crossed with conventional/natural/rational is reported as **inductively catalogued**, while the tripartite conventional division is argued conceptually. Natural signs may support rational causation but can be understood by habit absent explicit proof; the nonsense sound behind a wall indicates a speaker *rationally*, not through lexical designation.
