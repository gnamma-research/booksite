# Tahdhīb Stage 92 — al-Abharī's three fourth-figure moods and the six-condition expansion

**Date:** 2 November 2026. **Main project:** Taftāzānī's *Tahdhīb al-Manṭiq*, Cairo 1330/1912, L16 original pp.11–12. **Work type:** lineage of a logical doctrine and source-critical interpretation, not a fresh manuscript reading of the Tahdhīb matn.

## 1. New critical source and authorship level

Ferruh Özpilavcı and Abdullah Öztop, “er-Risâletü’ş-Şemsiyye’nin ... İlk Şerhi: Şerhu’l-mevâzi’i’l-müşkile ... İnceleme ve Tahkiki,” *Tahkik: Journal of Critical Editions of Islamic Manuscripts* 2/2 (2019): 1–114, DOI **10.5281/zenodo.3598210**; [full open-access article/critical text](https://isamveri.org/pdfdrg/D04349/2019_2/2019_2_OZPILAVCIF_OZTOPA.pdf).

The publication edits *Sharḥ al-Mawāḍiʿ al-Mushkila min al-Risāla al-Shamsiyya fī l-Qawāʿid al-Manṭiqiyya*, an early commentary on al-Kātibī's *al-Shamsiyya*. It is preserved in **Istanbul, Süleymaniye Library, Fazıl Ahmed Paşa 1612, fols. 3b–39a**, within a codex whose concluding colophon gives the year **691 AH / 1292 CE**, copied by ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz b. Ibrāhīm al-Mardānī. The researchers argue strongly that the commentary belongs to **Najm al-Dīn al-Kātibī himself**, using physical provenance and matching discussions in his other writings, but they explicitly acknowledge that attribution is not conclusively proven. Accordingly identify it as **probably/allegedly by al-Kātibī**.

**Methodological gain:** this is source-facing textual evidence from a near-contemporary copied tradition for how the eight fourth-figure cases were explained; it is **not** a 691 AH witness of Taftāzānī's later Tahdhīb.

## 2. A recorded attribution to Athīr al-Dīn al-Abharī (d. 663 AH/1265 CE)

The article's introduction §1.4 aligns the two works:

| Work as cited by 2019 editors | Original source locator | What is reported |
|---|---|---|
| al-Kātibī, *Baḥr al-Fawāʾid* | Ms. fol. **141a** | The three additional fourth-figure combinations were independently extracted by the teacher, **Athīr al-Dīn al-Abharī** |
| *Sharḥ al-Mawāḍiʿ al-Mushkila* | Ms. fols. **27b–28a**, in the copy of 691 AH | Credits **al-Abharī** with the extraction of those same three combinations |

The 2019 critical text near article p.83 includes the authorial ascription of the three additional moods to his teacher. Its near-literal sense is: “These are the three moods independently derived by the eminent teacher Athīr al-Dīn al-Abharī.”

**Scholarly status:** We have two **reported primary-source attributions in the Kātibī textual circle**, as edited and collated by Özpilavcı–Öztop. That supports an **Abharī → Kātibī → later Shamsiyya/Tahdhīb commentary tradition** genealogy. The literal history of discovery by al-Abharī is still an attribution by a near-contemporary logician, not an independently recovered personal autograph by al-Abharī. Direct textual copying by Taftāzānī from one exact source is not yet proven.

## 3. The five-condition Shamsiyya matn and expanded SIX-condition commentary

**Earlier short source:** *al-Kātibī, al-Risālah al-Shamsiyya*, §103, Tony Street 2024 bilingual edition, printed pp.68–69: [critical edition](https://dhjhkxawhe8q4.cloudfront.net/library-of-arabic-literature-wp/assets/20240530034338/9781479827510_WEB-1.pdf).

**Expanded source:** 2019 edition of *Sharḥ al-Mawāḍiʿ* [145] at printed article p.89; the footnotes **387–392** quote the five-condition Shamsiyya source against the six-condition explanation. The expanded commentary also explicitly disambiguates the final three moods.

| Condition | Short Shamsiyya §103 (five headings) | Early expanded commentary (six headings) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The syllogism uses actuality propositions | **Each** premise is an actuality proposition |
| 2 | The negative used is convertible | The negative premise is of a convertible form |
| 3 | Mode 3: permanence of minor OR general customary condition of major | Mode 3: a necessary/permanent minor OR appropriate convertibility condition of major |
| 4 | Mode 6: its major among `المنعكسة السوالب` | **Mode 6: its MINOR is one of the two special negatives; its MAJOR is among the proposition types whose negatives convert** |
| 5 | Mode 8: its minor a special form and its major of general customary range | **Mode 7: its MAJOR is a special negative; its MINOR is actual** |
| 6 | [No separate sixth heading] | **Mode 8: its MINOR is a special negative; its MAJOR is of the convertible-negative class** |

Thus the expansion extracts the extra condition for **mode 7**, and makes mode 6's **two separate modal demands** explicit. The differences are not a simple word-for-word equivalence: they are a fuller explanatory scheme. A proposed inference must preserve the exact technical definitions of **the two specials** and the **six convertible-negative classes** before claiming validity.

## 4. CORRECTION to Stage91: `المنعكسة السوالب` is a technical CLASS, not necessarily an actually negative premise

Stage91 prematurely described an apparent source-internal contradiction: since mode 6 has negative minor O and affirmative major A, how could §103 ask that “the major be a convertible negative”?

The expanded 2019 commentary, together with Quṭb al-Dīn al-Rāzī's explanatory wording, shows why **that diagnosis was too strong**. Consider these distinct assertions:

1. **Minor rule**: the actual **negative minor** is one of the two *khāṣṣa* modal propositions whose conversion is specially licensed.
2. **Major class**: the (affirmative) major is drawn from one of **six proposition classes whose universal NEGATIVE FORMS admit conversion**—`القضايا المنعكسة السوالب`. The statement concerns which modal **class** the affirmative major belongs to, not its present affirmative/negative *quality*.

In his [*Taḥrīr al-Qawāʿid*, p.419](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/8199/419?lang=fa), Quṭb al-Dīn al-Rāzī spells out the six classes as `القضايا الست المنعكسة السوالب`; the earlier expanded commentary (2019 p.86) makes the syntax still clearer as `القضايا الست التي تنعكس سوالبها الكلية`—“six proposition kinds whose **universal negative forms** convert.”

This is consistent with a mode-6 **negative minor** and **affirmative major**. Therefore do **not** emend the word `الكبرى` in §103 merely because the first reading seemed contradictory; preserve it and give the correct technical interpretation. The stated major condition may be independently required for the other Figure-II modal restrictions after the minor's conversion.

**What is still not certified:** the precise conversion theorem for each of the six modal classes under *actuality, permanence, subject descriptions, nonpermanence*; and which classes apply to each of the eight moods. Those demand a full source-based technical reconstruction.

## 5. The formal reduction directions remain valid *conditionally*

The Stage91 computational test (512 assignments of three sets including empty sets) still distinguishes:

- **Mode 6:** `O(M,S)` is the negative *minor*. If a historical special modal rule licenses `O(S,M)`, figure II yields `O(S,P)` together with `A(P,M)`. A separate condition can constrain the modal **class of the affirmative major**.
- **Mode 7:** `O(P,M)` is the negative *major*. If a historical special conversion licenses `O(M,P)`, figure III with `A(M,S)` yields `O(S,P)`. Source's extra condition is that the minor be actual.
- **Mode 8:** `E(M,S)`, `I(P,M)` first entail a **reverse-direction** `O(P,S)` through figure I; converting this *result* to `O(S,P)` needs precisely the extra qualified modal semantics. The requirement on the major's convertible-negative class must be shown to preserve the necessary specific form at the output.

These are **formal proof plans under conditional conversion licenses**, not verified medieval modal syllogisms. The source correction makes their **types of assumptions** intelligible; it does not license the final inference in an ordinary Boolean model.

## 6. A more substantial genealogy and independent comparison path

The source relation now has at least four layers:

1. **Athīr al-Dīn al-Abharī** — credited in the Kātibī textual circle as deriving modes 6–8.
2. **al-Kātibī, Shamsiyya §§95–104** — records five ordinary productive forms and three additional modally conditioned cases; supplies a short **five-part** list of modal conditions.
3. ***Sharḥ al-Mawāḍiʿ al-Mushkila*** (probably al-Kātibī; manuscript copied around 691 AH) — preserves a **six-part** expanded modal analysis and the explicit attribution to al-Abharī.
4. **Taftāzānī, Tahdhīb L16, and al-Yazdī-associated explanatory tradition** — compresses the figures in Cairo p.12 and later explicates them in connected technical register. The relationship is one of **shared prior technical tradition**, not proven direct author-to-author textual borrowing.

**Related technical witness:** Quṭb al-Dīn al-Rāzī's [*Sharḥ Maṭāliʿ al-Anwār*, pp.259 and 278](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/15419/278?lang=ar), which gives independent detailed conditions and counterexamples for modal figure IV. This is an explanatory/argumentative comparison, not a *Tahdhīb* textual witness.
