# Al-Yazdī’s Ḥāshiya, Qum pp.70–84: contradiction, simple conversion and contraposition
**6 November 2026 · source-linked Arabic and English translation · provisional working edition, NOT definitive.**

## Scope
The new reader files translate the continuous principal **شرح** in the searchable Qum 400-page edition in three editorial logical sections:
- `yazdi-continuous-q70-73-data.js` — **contradiction** (`tanāquḍ`), four source-page blocks, L.13.
- `yazdi-continuous-q74-80-data.js` — **simple conversion** (`ʿaks mustawī`), seven source-page blocks, L.14.
- `yazdi-continuous-q81-84-data.js` — **contraposition** (`ʿaks al-naqīḍ`), four source-page blocks, L.15.

Each block contains the page's main al-Yazdī explanation, English rendering, Qum source URL, explicit seam/boundary, and independently labelled editorial notes. The original Taftāzānī *matn* and section rubrics are NOT silently inserted into the attributed commentarial prose. Printed footnote call-numbers are dropped. A later note, even if long and fascinating, is explicitly **هامش**, commonly signed **Mīrzā Muḥammad ʿAlī** and not al-Yazdī; it is excluded from the principal bilingual commentary layer.

The main text concludes its contraposition proof at **Q84**. **Q85** is only the heading `باب الحجة وهيئة تأليفها`, not a translated page of commentary. The syllogistic discussion starts thereafter. Earlier source material on Qum 22–37, 39–51, and 54–69 is retained unchanged.

## Critical apparatus: problems to resolve

**CR-23 · Contradictory vs opposed (Q70–71).** The earlier matn requires two propositions to differ so that **the truth of each necessitates the falsity of the other, and the falsity of each necessitates the truth of the other**. Two particular forms can share truth; two universal forms can share falsity. Thus affirming vs denying alone does not create contradiction. Where quantifiers and modalities are explicit, quantity, quality and modality also differ. Persian pedagogical verse quoted by al-Yazdī lists **eight unities**: subject, predicate, place, condition, relation, part/whole, potency/act, and time. The original verse is quoted *as a cited mnemonic*, not introduced as a critical apparatus reading.

**CR-24 · Explicit contradictory or a consequence of the contradictory? (Q71–72).** General possibility is presented as an **explicit contradictory** of unrestricted necessity. General actuality, by contrast, is a *necessary consequence* of denying permanence, rather than simply the denial itself. Al-Yazdī notes that historical terminology treats the general actual proposition as the contradictory because nonpermanence is not an independently recognized proposition-form in their scheme. These differ logically unless extra conditions are imposed. Manuscript comparisons have not yet been made.

**CR-25 · Compound negation and quantifier scope (Q72–73).** A compound proposition is negated by the **inclusive** alternative that *at least one* constituent fails; both may fail. A common logical mistake is to negate a **particular compound assertion** by choosing one or other **universal** component-negation globally. Al-Yazdī explicitly requires the alternatives **for every individual**. In modern editorial notation, the distinction can be illustrated by `∀x(Subject(x) → (¬A(x) ∨ ¬B(x)))` versus `(∀x(Subject(x)→¬A(x))) ∨ (∀x(Subject(x)→¬B(x)))`. The formula is an **editorial diagnostic**, not an original symbolic notation or proof of temporal equivalence.

**CR-26 · Conversion as inference, not co-occurrence of truths (Q74–76).** The condition `مع بقاء الصدق` means that **if** the original holds, the conversion must hold—not that both are actually true in the world. The source's proof for universal negatives uses a reductio producing `بعض الحجر ليس بحجر`. Positive universal simple conversion to a positive particular, however, requires appropriate existence/import. Treating `Every C is B` as vacuously true of an empty C while claiming `Some B is C` follows would be a modern unsound inference. Identify the source's existence convention before formalizing it.

**CR-27 · al-Fārābī versus Ibn Sīnā: possible subjects (Q77–78).** Al-Yazdī presents two distinct conventions for the truth of `Every C may be B`: al-Fārābī can consider every **possibly C** to be possibly B, supporting a possibly-instantiated converted subject; Ibn Sīnā considers every **actually C** to be possibly B. The conversion ‘some **actually B** is possibly C’ does not follow from the latter. His example contrasts an actual animal ridden by Zayd (horses) with a possible donkey ridden by him. Al-Taftāzānī's `ولا عكس للممكنتين` follows the Avicennan convention. Do not merge the two philosophies into one interpretation.

**CR-28 · Proving a whole composite's conversion need not mean converting each constituent (Q79).** In the source al-Yazdī expressly adds `وفيه تأمل` to al-Taftāzānī's claim about converting the nonpermanence part of a special negative. His objection: conversion of a *compound as a compound* may hold even if a component, considered in isolation, lacks a recognized conversion. The source already provides comparable special affirmative conversions. This is a substantive methodological challenge, not merely a linguistic aside.

**CR-29 · The earlier and later contraposition methods (Q81–82).** The earlier method converts `Every C is B` to `Every non-B is non-C` **with the quality maintained**. The later method writes `No non-B is C`, **changing the quality** but keeping the subject C unchanged as the second term. These rules must be assessed under their historically appropriate treatment of *infinite-negative terms* and existential assumptions. It is an error to present the two as verbatim identical classical forms.

**CR-30 · The `iftirāḍ` proof and two modal intervals (Q83–84).** To prove special particular conversion, al-Yazdī explicitly **assumes an individual witness D** and studies which descriptions are true of that individual across differing times. He works through (i) the particular negative under simple conversion and (ii) the particular affirmative under contraposition. The substantive argument needs temporal semantics and a proper witness. The demonstration across Q83→84 is a single continuous argument, not separately authored two articles.

**CR-31 · Textual and layout limitations.** Qum's searchable transcription includes bracketed unspecified variants and numbered annotation calls without dated manuscript identifiers. It preserves examples and citations but not critical manuscript provenance. The full photographs of Qum, the separate Gujarat printed edition, Tehran printing and independently identified manuscript witnesses have **not** been collated for Q70–84. The page-seam transitions and the authorial/main vs post-`هامش` separation have been checked against the source web text, not yet against physical page geometry. The quoted Persian mnemonic on Q71 requires orthographic and verse-layout proofing before certification. A future definitive edition must establish exactly which readings are from the historical author's main text before publishing a new apparatus.
