## Object of the edition
The intended object is **al-Taftāzānī’s short logic matn plus the complete main ḥāshiya of Mullā ʿAbd Allāh al-Yazdī**, paragraph aligned without treating separately printed marginal annotators as al-Yazdī. The Qum printed book has 400 numbered pages but its main commentary does **not** end at p.114: the *Ajzāʾ al-ʿUlūm* commentary continues to an explicit closing prayer and completion notice on **printed p.123**. Printed p.124 is empty in the searchable source, and p.125 explicitly introduces **Ḥawāshī al-ḥāshiya**, commentary on the commentary, which must be excluded from the primary Yazdī layer. The first portion of the printed volume also contains publisher and biographical material. Therefore “400 pages of unabridged al-Yazdī commentary” is the wrong denominator. Establish the first and last lines of the ḥāshiya by photographic verification before certifying a continuous edition.

El-Rouayheb identifies this as a **ḥāshiya** (gloss), rather than a complete quotation-based **sharḥ**: it typically cites the first words (*qawluhu*) of a lemma and comments on them. This distinction must govern segmentation. The *matn* and *ḥāshiya* are separate sources with two original authors, so editorial bilingual headings and topic labels are clearly marked *editorial*.

**Historical manuscript note:** An Iranian textual-history survey reports that some copies date completion to **967 AH in Najaf**. This datum remains a reported colophon until the specific folio is verified. The same survey warns of a **second attributed Arabic gloss** and of a Persian translation with contested authorship (Yazdī, Hindī or Shahristānī); neither is automatically part of the ordinary ḥāshiya edited here.

**A historical correction:** al-Yazdī’s death is recorded as **981/1573–74** in a contemporary chronicle. The commonly repeated **1015/1606–07** is apparently a confusion with a different scholar; see El-Rouayheb, *The Development of Arabic Logic (1200–1800)* (2019), 115–16.

## Four key apparatus notes for review
**CR-01 · Matn vs ḥāshiya vs marginalia.** Introductory `فصل`, `خاتمة`, and quoted `قوله` lemmata are not all new prose by Yazdī; a `هوامش` section may quote `التقريب` or a named glossator. Each edited segment must store `speaker` and `source_layer` before publication.

**CR-02 · Q p.114, the reading `بالبرهان`.** Al-Yazdī reports `وأما ما وجد في بعض النسخ من التخصيص بقوله: بالبرهان فمن زيادة الناسخ`: he regards `بالبرهان` as a scribal addition in some copies. He **also allows a charitable construal**: the wording may reflect the usual case, or `burhān` could include `tanbīh` (drawing attention to a self-evident matter). Report both, distinguish `some copies` from independently identified witnesses, and do not silently delete `بالبرهان` from al-Taftāzānī’s matn.

**CR-03 · Q pp.105–107, the definition of analogy.** Yazdī sharply distinguishes *inspection/likening as an act* from the *premise-bearing inference* that qualifies as a `ḥujja`. It would be inaccurate to translate `tamthīl` consistently as “analogy” without occasionally specifying whether the operation or argument is meant. The asserted epistemic status of `dawarān` also belongs to the technical discussion of `ʿilla` in uṣūl al-fiqh.

**CR-04 · Q p.110, the five arts.** Distinguish matter (`mādda`) from figure/form (`ṣūra`). Poetry is classified by imaginative effect (`takhyīl`), rhetoric by probability (`ẓann`), demonstration by certainty (`yaqīn`), dialectic by recognized/conceded premises, and fallacy by inferior grounds. Never flatten all five to one modern inferential validity scheme.

## Added continuous Qum pp.30–37 — conceptual and textual editorial observations
The text file `edition/yazdi-continuous-universals-data.js` now contains **ten bilingual segments from eight distinct Qum pages**, immediately after the earlier eight-page Q22–29 continuous sequence. It follows source order while identifying editorial L.04–L.06 structure. The main print text includes the distinctions `مفهوم/معنى/مدلول` (Q30), possibility of a universal's predication on multiple things despite impossible or absent extensional instances (Q31), four relative dispositions of universals (Q31–32), the status of negated terms (Q32–34), relative versus real particularity and a reported teaching from one of al-Yazdī's teachers (Q35), and the derivation of the five predicables (Q35–37). The 'What is it?' argument for proximate and remote genera begins at Q36–37.

**CR-05 · Potential incompatibility of empty extensions and a fourfold exclusive classification.** On Q31 al-Yazdī admits universals with impossible or uninstantiated referents, such as the concept of a partner to God or a fabulous bird. On Q32 he classifies pairs of universals using quantified affirmative and negative propositions. In a purely extensional semantics, an empty concept-extension is simultaneously included in every concept-extension and disjoint from all of them, so equality/disjointness/inclusion cannot be a partition unless appropriate existence or intensional-possibility conditions are supplied. Critical editorial commentary must not project a modern default domain or suppress these conditions. No source Arabic has been changed.

**CR-06 · `تباين جزئي` is not simply ordinary complete disjointness.** Q33–34 explicitly says partial exclusion may obtain in both complete disjointness and partial overlap, and the complements of partially overlapping terms need not themselves overlap. Translate as partial exclusion rather than silently as ‘contrariety’ or ‘disjointness’.

**CR-07 · Q37 border.** The independently viewable Q37 source shows a block under `شرح` followed by `هامش`, but the first words after `هامش` may continue the preceding main argument due to the layout and transcription process. The current base stops before those words and marks the reading incomplete at that boundary; Q38 was not accessible in the source interface in this pass. A precise image-based check is required before claiming completeness of the author's genus explanation.

All newly displayed text is a working normalized Arabic text translated directly into English, **not** an image-verified diplomatic edition. The original 177 source-quoted archival excerpts remain intact, but the overlapping L.04–L.06 excerpts do not repeat in the reader.

## Third consecutive Arabic–English source pass: main ḥāshiya Qum 39–47
The dedicated source file `edition/yazdi-continuous-p39-47-data.js` contains nine source-page-aligned bilingual paragraphs covering the remainder of the five predicables and its accident/concomitant distinctions. The printed p38 is **solely a هامش page**. The main Q text resumes on p39 with `قوله وقد يقال على الماهية`; p48 begins a new chapter on the logical, natural and intellectual universal.

**CR-08 · Conceptual versus extended parts.** Q40–41 challenges the inference that the indivisible spatial point has no genus. Indivisibility in extension shows only the absence of spatial parts; genus is an intelligible component. The point’s possible intellectual composition is not refuted simply by denying geometric divisibility. This issue directly illuminates the earlier distinction between logical genus and an externally separate substantial form.

**CR-09 · Rāzī, al-Muḥākamāt, Ṭūsī and what a differentia answers.** At Q43 al-Yazdī reports the objection that a complete definition (e.g. `حيوان ناطق`) can answer an unrestricted `أي شيء` question and therefore the definition of a differentia would not exclude definitions. One proposed reply stipulates that the logician’s `أي` asks for a distinguishing characteristic not itself answering `ما هو`. Al-Ṭūsī’s reply is contextual: `أي شيء` presupposes prior identification of a genus, so that the reply must distinguish the species *within that already-known genus*. Preserve these three positions and do not merge the attributed writers' approaches into al-Yazdī’s single endorsed doctrine.

**CR-10 · Difference between `muqawwim` and `muqassim`.** Q44–46 explains two respects of one differentia: as a constitutive intelligible element of the species, and as a feature dividing the genus. A constitutive element of a higher kind belongs to lower kinds, while the corresponding converse fails in general; the rule for divisive characteristics proceeds in another direction. This is a rule of essential logical hierarchy, not a literal transference of spatial pieces.

**CR-11 · Three kinds of necessary concomitant and two evidential senses.** Q46–47 explicitly distinguishes what follows from quiddity, from external existence, and from mental existence (such as the universality of human nature, `maʿqūl thānī`). The strict `bayyin` is conceived with the subject alone; the broader `bayyin` produces immediate certitude after the two terms and their relation are understood. Translating both simply as ‘self-evident’ without qualification hides the difference.

The nine Q39–47 Arabic–English blocks remain a **working source-linked translation, not a definitive critical edition**. Source scans, alternate printed copies and manuscripts still need collating. Printed later marginal comments in the source are separately attributed and excluded from al-Yazdī’s main text.

**CR-12 · Whether the natural universal exists externally.** Q49 presents **three different standpoints**: the philosopher-majority's affirmation of natural universals in individuals, al-Taftāzānī's denial of one external common existent beyond the individuals, and al-Yazdī's qualification `وفيه تأمل`. ## Qum 70–84 continuous main commentary; contradiction and two conversions
The working edition now includes three further page-aligned source files: `yazdi-continuous-q70-73-data.js` (four Qum pages, L.13), `yazdi-continuous-q74-80-data.js` (seven pages, L.14), and `yazdi-continuous-q81-84-data.js` (four pages, L.15). See the full [Q70–84 critical dossier](YAZDI_Q70_84_CRITICAL.md) for fifteen source URLs, boundary and attribution checks and detailed arguments.

**CR-23–25:** contradiction is *bivalent opposition* in both directions, not bare positive/negative grammatical contrast; the eight unities protect the reference of the two statements. For compound modal propositions, a negation of the compound is an **inclusive disjunction** of the component negations. In a particular compound the alternatives must be tested **for each subject individual**; distributing a single global disjunction incorrectly over universal quantification changes the meaning.

**CR-26–28:** `ʿaks mustawī` preserves truth under the assumption that the original is true; a positive universal normally converts only to a particular, under appropriate existence commitments. The contrast between the *Farabian possible subject* and *Avicennan actually instantiated subject* determines why no general conversion of the two possibility forms is licensed. Al-Yazdī explicitly challenges using separate component-conversion rules to decide whether a *whole compound* converts.

**CR-29–30:** early contraposition retains quality and negates both terms; later contraposition reverses quality while negating only the predicate term. The exception for special particular forms is proved by hypothesizing a **witness** whose descriptions overlap across specified times, not by merely exchanging words.
