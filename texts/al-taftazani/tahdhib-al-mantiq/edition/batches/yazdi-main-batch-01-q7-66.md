### Basmala, praise, and the name of God

**Attribution:** al-Yazdī, principal ḥāshiya; printed hāmish excluded. **Certification:** working transcription, page photograph/manuscripts unchecked.

**العربية — Original**

هذا كتاب الحاشية. بسم الله الرحمن الرحيم. قوله «الحمد لله»: افتتح بحمد الله بعد البسملة ابتداء بخير الكلام واقتداء بحديث خير الأنام عليه وآله الصلاة والسلام. فإن قلت: حديث الابتداء مروي في كل من التسمية والتحميد فكيف التوفيق؟ قلت: الابتداء في حديث التسمية محمول على الحقيقي وفي حديث التحميد على الإضافي أو على العرفي أو في كليهما على العرفي. والحمد هو الثناء باللسان على الجميل الاختياري نعمة كان أو غيرها، والله علم على الأصح للذات الواجب الوجود المستجمع لجميع صفات الكمال، ولدلالته على هذا الاستجماع صار الكلام في قوة أن يقال:

**English — Translation**

This is the book of the Gloss. In the name of God, the Merciful, the Compassionate. On the words "Praise belongs to God": the author begins with praise following the basmala, opening with the best of words and following traditions transmitted from the best of humanity, upon him and his family be blessings and peace. An objection arises: traditions about beginning a work are related both for invoking God's name and for praising Him; how are they reconciled? The "beginning" in the tradition concerning the name can mean the beginning in the strict sense, while that in the tradition on praise is understood relatively or according to established custom; alternatively, both can be understood conventionally. Praise (*ḥamd*) is verbal commendation of voluntary excellence, whether it has benefited someone or not. The word "God" (*Allāh*) is, on the stronger view, the proper name of the Necessary Existent who possesses every attribute of perfection. Because the name signifies this comprehensiveness, the phrase amounts to saying ...

## Qum printed page 8 · L.01

### Guidance: arriving at the aim or being shown its way

**Arabic source:** https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/12170/8?lang=ar  
**Attribution:** al-Yazdī, principal ḥāshiya; printed hāmish excluded. **Certification:** working transcription, page photograph/manuscripts unchecked.  
**Page seam:** continues-to-9

**العربية — Original**

الحمد مطلقا منحصر في حق من هو مستجمع لجميع صفات الكمالات من حيث هو كذلك، فكان كدعوى الشيء ببينة وبرهان ولا يخفى لطفه. قوله «الذي هدانا»: الهداية قيل: هي الدلالة الموصلة، أي الإيصال إلى المطلوب، وقيل: هي إراءة الطريق الموصل إلى المطلوب. والفرق بين هذين المعنيين أن الأول يستلزم الوصول إلى المطلوب بخلاف الثاني، فإن الدلالة على ما يوصل إلى المطلوب لا يلزم أن تكون موصلة إلى ما يوصل فكيف توصل إلى المطلوب؟ والأول منقوض بقوله تعالى: «وأما ثمود فهديناهم فاستحبوا العمى على الهدى» إذ لا يتصور الضلال بعد الوصول إلى الحق. والثاني منقوض بقوله تعالى: «إنك لا تهدي من أحببت ولكن الله يهدي من يشاء» فإن النبي صلى الله عليه وآله كان شأنه إراءة الطريق. والذي يفهم من كلام المصنف في حاشية الكشاف هو أن الهداية لفظ مشترك بين هذين المعنيين وحينئذ يظهر اندفاع كلا النقضين ويرتفع الخلاف من البين. ومحصول كلام المصنف في تلك الحاشية أن الهداية لفظ يتعدى إلى المفعول الثاني تارة بنفسه نحو «اهدنا الصراط المستقيم»، وتارة بإلى نحو «والله يهدي من يشاء إلى صراط مستقيم»، وتارة باللام نحو «إن هذا القرآن يهدي للتي هي أقوم»، فمعناها على الاستعمال الأول هو الإيصال وعلى الثاني إراءة الطريق. قوله «سواء الطريق»: أي وسطه الذي يفضي سالكه إلى المطلوب البتة، وهذا كناية عن الطريق المستوي والصراط المستقيم إذ هما متلازمان، وهذا مراد من فسره بالطريق المستوي والصراط المستقيم. ثم المراد به إما نفس الأمر عموما أو خصوص ملة الإسلام، والأول أولى لحصول البراعة الظاهرة بالقياس إلى قسمي الكتاب. قوله «جعل لنا»: الظرف إما متعلق بجعل واللام للانتفاع كما قيل في قوله تعالى «وجعل لكم الأرض فراشا»، وإما برفيق ويكون تقديم معمول المضاف

**English — Translation**

... praise without qualification belongs only to the one who possesses every perfection precisely as such. The expression is thus almost a claim supported by its evidence, and its elegance is apparent. On "who guided us": guidance (*hidāya*) has been defined as a direction that actually brings one to the sought goal, or as showing the path by which that goal may be reached. The first entails arrival; the second does not, since pointing out the way to a destination need not itself carry one even to the route, much less the destination. The first definition faces the Qur'anic example of Thamūd, to whom God showed guidance but who preferred blindness, for someone who has already arrived at the truth could not in that sense fail to reach it. The second faces the verse "You cannot guide whom you love; God guides whom He wills", since the Prophet plainly showed people the route. The resolution, according to al-Taftāzānī's gloss on al-Kashshāf, is that *guidance* is used in both senses, so neither purported counterexample undermines the other sense. The author observes three verbal constructions: guiding a direct object, as in "Guide us the straight path"; guiding **to** a path; and guiding **toward/for** what is most upright. On one construction the sense is bringing to the goal, on another displaying the route. On "the middle of the road": the central way is the one whose follower reaches the goal; it signifies the straight path. This may denote reality as it is generally, or the Islamic religious path specifically. The general meaning better anticipates the two divisions of this book. On "made for us": the prepositional expression may depend on "made", where *for* expresses benefit, as in "made the earth a resting-place for you"; alternatively it belongs with the word "companion", in which case a dependent expression has been advanced before its governing phrase ...

**Editorial notes**

1. The two meanings of hidaya—arriving and showing a path—are a lexical/exegetical discussion, not a single philosophical definition.

2. The final phrase crosses into the grammatical discussion at p9; the original Qum pagination is retained.

3. Qur'anic wording is quoted as part of the historical Arabic source, not supplied as an additional authorial proof.

### Syntax of the preface, divine concurrence and the sending of the Prophet

**Attribution:** al-Yazdī, principal ḥāshiya; printed hāmish excluded. **Certification:** working transcription, page photograph/manuscripts unchecked.

**English — Translation**

The first interpretation is more straightforward in wording, the second more appropriate in meaning. On "divine assistance" (*tawfīq*): it is the directing of causes toward the desired good. On "blessing" (*ṣalāt*): originally it signifies prayer, the request for mercy; when predicated of God, the meaning of asking is removed and the word refers figuratively to mercy itself. On "upon the one whom He sent": the Prophet is not named explicitly, out of reverence and to suggest that these attributes identify him uniquely. This description was selected because it entails the other attributes of perfection and expressly indicates his being sent. The rank of messengership is here described as above prophethood, since a messenger is a prophet sent with a religion and a scripture. On "guidance" (*hudā*): this can be an accusative expressing the purpose of "He sent him", referring to God's guiding act so that the purpose and the act have the same agent. ## Qum printed page 10 · L.01

### Guidance as an attributive state; inward and outward truth

**Arabic source:** https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/12170/10?lang=ar  
**Attribution:** al-Yazdī, principal ḥāshiya; printed hāmish excluded. **Certification:** working transcription, page photograph/manuscripts unchecked.  
**Page seam:** complete-at-page

**العربية — Original**

المفعول به وحينئذ فالمصدر بمعنى اسم الفاعل أو يقال: أطلق على ذي الحال مبالغة نحو «زيد عدل». قوله «هو بالاهتداء حقيق»: مصدر مبني للمفعول، أي بأن يهتدى به، والجملة صفة لقوله «هدى»، أو يكونان حالين مترادفين أو متداخلين ويحتمل الاستيناف أيضا، وقس على هذا قوله «نورا» مع الجملة التالية له. قوله «به»: ظرف متعلق بالاقتداء لا ب «يليق»، فإن اقتداءنا به إنما يليق بنا لا به، فإنه كمال لنا لا له، وحينئذ تقديم الظرف لقصد الحصر والإشارة إلى أن ملته ناسخة لملل سائر الأنبياء. وأما الاقتداء بالأئمة عليهم السلام فيقال إنه اقتداء به حقيقة أو يقال: الحصر إضافي بالنسبة إلى سائر الأنبياء. قوله «وعلى آله»: أصله أهل بدليل تصغيره على أهيل، خص استعماله في الأشراف والأهل أعم منه، وآل النبي عترته المعصومون. قوله «وأصحابه»: هم المؤمنون الذين أدركوا صحبة النبي مع الإيمان. قوله «مناهج»: جمع منهج وهو الطريق الواضح. قوله «الصدق»: الخبر والاعتقاد إذا طابق الواقع كان الواقع أيضا مطابقا له، فإن المفاعلة من الطرفين؛ فمن حيث إنه مطابق للواقع بالكسر يسمى صدقا ومن حيث إنه مطابق له بالفتح يسمى حقا، وقد يطلق الصدق والحق على نفس المطابقية والمطابقية أيضا.

**English — Translation**

... the object of the verb. On that reading the verbal noun "guidance" means "one who guides"; alternatively it is predicated of the bearer of the state by an emphatic figure, as one says "Zayd is justice itself". On "he deserves to be followed in guidance": the verbal noun is understood passively—"it is fitting that he should be followed in guidance". The clause may qualify the preceding word "guidance", or both may be simultaneous or nested accusatives of state; it could also be a new independent sentence. The same alternatives apply to "a light by following whom it is fitting ..." On "him": the prepositional phrase depends on "following", not on "befits", since following him befits **us**, not him; it is a perfection on our part, not an addition to his. The preposed pronoun limits the relevant act of following and alludes to the supersession of earlier religious ordinances by his mission. Following the Imams may be described as genuinely following him, or the restriction may be only relative to the other prophets. On "his family": the word is related to *ahl*, as its diminutive form indicates; *āl* is especially used of persons of elevated standing, whereas *ahl* is wider. The Prophet's family is here identified as his protected descendants. On "his companions": they are those believers who encountered the Prophet in his lifetime. "Paths" is the plural of a clear road. On "truth": when a report or belief corresponds to reality, reality may be said reciprocally to correspond to it. Considered as correspondence of the report **to** reality it is termed *ṣidq* (truthfulness); considered from the other aspect it is *ḥaqq* (truth). Both words may also designate the relation of correspondence itself.

**Editorial notes**

1. The distinctive identification of the Prophet's family is the commentator's doctrinal wording and should not be silently neutralized or attributed to the matn.

2. The printed p10 هامش offers an extensive grammatical objection from al-Taqrīb. It is not part of this author's main شرح.

3. The Arabic last formulation has a possible repetitive printing at «المطابقية والمطابقية»; retained as a provisional searchable reading.

## Qum printed page 11 · L.01

### Logic and kalām: the book's stated scope

**Arabic source:** https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/12170/11?lang=ar  
**Attribution:** al-Yazdī, principal ḥāshiya; printed hāmish excluded. **Certification:** working transcription, page photograph/manuscripts unchecked.  
**Page seam:** complete-at-page

**العربية — Original**

قوله «بالتصديق»: متعلق بقوله «سعدوا» أي بسبب التصديق والإيمان بما جاء به النبي صلى الله عليه وآله. قوله «وصعدوا معارج الحق»: يعني بلغوا أقصى مراتب الحق، فإن الصعود على جميع مراتبه يستلزم ذلك. قوله «بالتحقيق»: ظرف لغو متعلق بصعدوا كما مر، أو مستقر خبر لمبتدأ محذوف، أي هذا الحكم متلبس بالتحقيق، أي متحقق. قوله «وبعد»: هو من الغايات ولها حالات ثلاث، فإنها إما أن يذكر معها المضاف إليه أو لا، وعلى الثاني إما أن يكون نسيا منسيا أو منويا، فهي على الأولين معربة وعلى الثالث مبنية على الضم. قوله «فهذا»: هذه الفاء إما على توهم «أما» أو على تقديرها في نظم الكلام، وهذا إشارة إلى المرتب الحاضر في الذهن من المعاني المخصوصة المعبر عنها بالألفاظ المخصوصة، أو تلك الألفاظ الدالة على المعاني المخصوصة، سواء كان وضع الديباجة قبل التصنيف أو بعده إذ لا وجود للألفاظ المرتبة ولا للمعاني في الخارج. فإن كانت الإشارة إلى الألفاظ فالمراد بالكلام الكلام اللفظي، وإن كانت إلى المعاني فالمراد به الكلام النفسي أي المعنوي الذي يدل عليه الكلام اللفظي. قوله «غاية تهذيب الكلام»: حمله على هذا إما على المبالغة نحو «زيد عدل» أو بناء على أن التقدير «هذا الكلام مهذب غاية التهذيب»، فحذف الخبر وأقيم المفعول المطلق مقامه وأعرب بإعرابه على طريقة مجاز الحذف. قوله «في تحرير المنطق والكلام»: ولم يقل في بيانهما لما في لفظ التحرير من الإشارة إلى أن هذا البيان خال عن الحشو والزوائد. والمنطق آلة قانونية تعصم مراعاتها الذهن عن الخطأ في الفكر. والكلام هو العلم الباحث عن أحوال المبدأ والمعاد على نهج قانون الإسلام.

**English — Translation**

On "through assent": the expression depends on the preceding "they attained happiness": they did so through assent and belief in what the Prophet brought. "They ascended the stairs of truth" means that they reached its highest stages. "Through verification" can be an adverbial modifier of "ascended", or a phrase serving as the predicate of an implied subject: the statement itself is established and verified. On "now then" (*wa-baʿd*): this is a temporal expression with three grammatical treatments, depending on whether its genitive complement is expressed, forgotten, or merely understood. Under the first two it is inflected; under the third it takes fixed final *ḍamma*. On "this": the prefixed *fa-* may imply an unspoken "as for", and the demonstrative may point to determinate meanings ordered in the mind and expressed by particular words, or to the ordered words signifying those meanings. This interpretation applies whether the preface was composed before or after the rest of the book: neither the ordered words nor the ordered meanings are physically present as such outside the mind. If it points to words, "discourse" means verbal discourse; if to meanings, it refers to mental or meaningful discourse that speech signifies. On "the utmost refinement of discourse": the wording can be an emphatic metaphor, as when one says "Zayd is justice", or it can be understood through an omitted predicate: "this discourse has been refined to the utmost degree". On "in presenting logic and theology in precise form": the verb means critical preparation rather than merely stating them and implies freedom from irrelevant prolixity. Logic is a rule-governed instrument whose observance preserves the intellect from error in thinking. Kalām is the science investigating the conditions of the First Origin and the Return according to the governing principles of Islam.

**Editorial notes**

1. The distinction between *kalām lafẓī* (verbal discourse) and *kalām nafsī* (mental/meaning discourse) is introduced in an interpretive discussion of a demonstrative.

2. This commentary covers both logic and theology as the two parts of the original Taftāzānī work.

3. The statement defining logic is introduced as the source's own summary rather than a separate reconstructed philosophical argument.

## Qum printed page 12 · L.01

### The prologue's pedagogical dedication and grammatical choices

**Arabic source:** https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/12170/12?lang=ar  
**Attribution:** al-Yazdī, principal ḥāshiya; printed hāmish excluded. **Certification:** working transcription, page photograph/manuscripts unchecked.  
**Page seam:** complete-at-page

**العربية — Original**

قوله «وتقريب المرام»: بالجر عطف على التهذيب، أي هذا غاية تقريب المقصود إلى الطبائع والأفهام، والحمل إما على طريقة المبالغة أو التقدير: هذا الكلام مقرب غاية التقريب. قوله «من تقرير عقائد الإسلام»: بيان للمرام، والإضافة في عقائد الإسلام بيانية إن كان الإسلام عبارة عن نفس الاعتقادات، وإن كان عبارة عن مجموع الإقرار باللسان والتصديق بالجنان والعمل بالأركان أو كان عبارة عن مجرد الإقرار باللسان فالإضافة لامية. قوله «جعلته تبصرة»: أي مبصرا، ويحتمل التجوز في الإسناد، وكذا قوله «تذكرة». قوله «لدى الإفهام»: بالكسر، أي تفهيم الغير إياه أو تفهيمه للغير، والأول للمتعلم والثاني للمعلم. قوله «من ذوي الأفهام»: بفتح الهمزة، جمع الفهم، والظرف إما في موضع الحال من فاعل «يتذكر» أو متعلق ب «يتذكر» بتضمين معنى الأخذ أو التعلم، أي يتذكر آخذا أو متعلما من ذوي الأفهام، وهذا أيضا يحتمل الوجهين. قوله «سيما»: السي بمعنى المثل، يقال: هما سيان أي مثلان، وأصل «سيما» «لا سيما» حذفت «لا» في اللفظ لكنه مراد، و«ما» زائدة أو موصولة أو موصوفة، هذا أصله ثم استعمل بمعنى خصوصا، وفيما بعده ثلاثة أوجه. قوله «الحفي»: الشفيق. قوله «الحري»: اللائق.

**English — Translation**

On "and bringing the goal near": the genitive construction joins it to "refinement", meaning that the work brings its intended subject as close as possible to different minds and capacities. The expression is either emphatic or stands for "this discourse renders the subject as accessible as it can be". On "the exposition of Islamic beliefs": the phrase explains the "goal". If Islam denotes the beliefs themselves, the genitive is explanatory; if it denotes the joint practice of verbal profession, inward assent and prescribed acts, or simply verbal profession, the genitive instead expresses belonging or association. On "I made it an instrument of insight": the sense is "something causing one to see", with figurative attribution; the same applies to "a reminder". On "at the time of giving understanding": the form with short initial vowel refers to making another person understand, whether a student is being instructed or a teacher instructs another. On "among those who understand": the form with an open initial vowel is a plural of understanding. The phrase may qualify the person who remembers or depend on the verb "remember", with an implied sense of learning or taking instruction from the knowledgeable. On "especially": this is historically derived from the construction *lā siyya-mā*, literally "not the like of", but is used to mean "particularly"; the following words admit more than one grammatical analysis. The adjective *ḥafī* means solicitous or affectionate; *ḥarī* means deserving or fitting.

**Editorial notes**

1. The Arabic distinguishes two vocalizations of *afhām/ifhām*; there is no basis to impose a single modern grammatical paraphrase.

2. The printed «هامش» on this page explains ḥafī through an alternative cited source, which is excluded.

3. The named dedication to a cherished son is in Taftāzānī's matn and the commentary's lexical explanation, not a new dedication composed by Yazdī.

On "support": it means that through which a person's affairs are sustained. "Assistance" signifies strengthening, from a root meaning strength; "safeguard" means what protects one from slipping. **English — Translation**

... one item alone, then the question asks after the entire quiddity peculiar to it. If the item is an individual, a species can serve as the answer; if the item is itself a universal nature, the complete definition can do so. If the question names several things, it asks instead for the entire quiddity shared among them. When their natures are the same, this common whole is a single nature and a species again answers the question. When their natures differ, the question seeks the whole essential element common to their different natures. That element, as we have already learned, is the genus. **English — Translation**

... remote genus, namely growing body. On ‘when related’: differentia has one relation to the quiddity whose distinctive part it is, and another to the genus from whose other members it differentiates that quiddity. Under the first aspect it is **constitutive** (*muqawwim*), because it is an essential component that makes the quiddity determinate; under the second it is **divisive** (*muqassim*), because the presence of this differentia divides the genus into a class possessing it, and its absence into another class—as animal is divided into rational and nonrational animals. 2. Neither the original Arabic nor the English identifies these essential differentiae with independently existing parts of a material individual.

Thus the first division yields three kinds: concomitant of quiddity, like the evenness of four; concomitant of external existence, like the burning effected by fire; and concomitant of mental existence, like universality ascribed to human nature. The last belongs to what is called a second intelligible. Second, a concomitant is evident or nonevident, and ‘evident’ has two senses. In the stricter sense the conception of the concomitant follows merely from conceiving what entails it, as conceiving sight is involved in conceiving blindness. A nonevident concomitant on this account does not follow from merely conceiving the subject, as the capacity for writing does not follow directly from conceiving human. In the broader sense a concomitant is evident if conceiving the subject, conceiving the concomitant and considering the relation between them necessarily leads to assenting that they are connected: for example, the evenness of four. The mind, having grasped four, evenness and their relation, judges with certainty that evenness belongs to four. A nonevident concomitant under this broader criterion is one for which those three conceptions alone do not compel assent, as with the world's being originated. In substance these are two different dichotomies, although each employs ‘evident’ and ‘nonevident’. On ‘lasting’: the celestial sphere's motion is said to be lasting even though separation from it is not impossible in the sphere's own nature. On ‘quickly’: blushing from embarrassment and pallor from fear. On ‘slowly’: youth.

**Editorial notes**

1. Three ways of dependence are expressly distinguished: quiddity, external existence and mental existence. ‘Second intelligible’ (maʿqūl thānī) here is tied to the mental mode of existence, not a separate material attribute.

2. The stricter and wider senses of self-evidence (‘bayyin’) have different thresholds: implication from one conception alone versus certainty resulting from the two conceptions plus their relation.

3. The source examples of celestial motion and psychology are historical illustrations, not factual assertions of current physics or medicine.

4. The wording in the Qum searchable text includes a bracketed alternative «وتصور» at the broader conception; it has been normalized into a readable sentence without priority being assigned to a distinct manuscript. The photo-diplomatic reading remains unverified.

### Logical, natural and intellectual universals; the dispute over extramental existence

**English — Translation**

It is called a **natural universal**, because it is found in natures—that is, in extramental reality, as the subsequent discussion will explain. The combination of that subject with universality as its attribute—‘universal human’ or ‘universal animal’—is called an **intellectual universal**, because this composite exists only in the intellect. On ‘likewise the five kinds’: the three considerations—logical, natural and intellectual—also apply to each of the five predicables: genus, species, differentia, property and common accident. Thus the concept of *species*, ‘a universal predicated in answer to “What is it?” of many agreeing in essence’, is a logical species; a nature to which this concept applies, such as human or horse, is a natural species; and the composition of species-predicability with a nature, such as ‘human-as-species’, is an intellectual species. The other predicables are analogous. Indeed, these three considerations extend even to the particular. In ‘Zayd is particular’, the concept of particularity—the impossibility of supposing that the concept be predicated of many—is a logical particular; Zayd, to whom it applies, is a natural particular; and ‘Zayd-as-particular’, the compound of attribute and subject, is an intellectual particular. On ‘the truth is that the existence of the natural universal means the existence of its individuals’: there should be no doubt that the logical universal does not exist as such outside the intellect, since universality accrues to concepts in the intellect, and so belongs among second intelligibles. The intellectual universal likewise has no extramental existence: if one of its components is not there, neither is the composite. The dispute concerns the **natural universal**, for example human nature considered simply as human, which becomes universal under an intellectual consideration. Does it exist in external things through the existence of its individuals, or are only the individuals externally existent? The former is the position of most philosophers; the latter that of certain later thinkers, including al-Taftāzānī, who therefore says that the latter is correct. His reason is that, if one and the same universal existed externally in its many individuals, a single thing would bear opposed attributes and occupy several places. To say that the natural universal exists would then mean no more than that its individuals exist. **There is room for reconsideration here**, al-Yazdī remarks, referring the fuller establishment of the correct position to glosses on *al-Tajrīd*.

1. Al-Yazdī explicitly distinguishes **the position of the majority of philosophers**, **al-Taftāzānī's preferred denial** of the common natural universal’s extramental presence, and **his own reservation** «وفيه تأمل». These voices must not be conflated.

2. The phrase «وجود الطبيعي بمعنى وجود أشخاصه» must not be flattened into a denial that external humans exist. Its issue is whether the nature common to individuals possesses a numerically shared extramental existence.

3. Qum's searchable main text contains variant brackets at «الثلاث» and «قياس» and «أفراده». This working reading follows the running text rather than pretending those marginal indicators identify collated manuscript sigla.

### What makes a defining account adequate: extension, clarity and essentiality

**Arabic source:** https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/12170/50?lang=ar  
**Attribution:** al-Yazdī, principal ḥāshiya; printed hāmish excluded. **Certification:** working transcription, page photograph/manuscripts unchecked.

**العربية — Original**

قوله «معرف الشيء»: بعد الفراغ عن بيان ما يتركب منه المعرف شرع في البحث عنه، وقد علمت أن المقصود بالذات في هذا الفن هو البحث عنه وعن الحجة، وعرفه بأنه ما يحمل على الشيء، أي المعرف، ليفيد تصور هذا الشيء إما بكنهه أو بوجه يمتاز عن جميع ما عداه. ولهذا لم يجز أن يكون أعم، لأن الأعم لا يفيد شيئا منهما كالحيوان في تعريف الإنسان؛ فإن الحيوان ليس كنه الإنسان لأن الإنسان هو الحيوان مع الناطق، وأيضا لا يميز الإنسان عن جميع ما عداه لأن بعض الحيوان هو الفرس، وكذا الحال في الأعم من وجه. وأما الأخص أعني مطلقا فهو وإن جاز أن يفيد تصوره تصور الأعم بالكنه أو بوجه يمتاز عما عداه كما إذا تصورت الإنسان بأنه حيوان ناطق فقد تصورت في ضمنه الحيوان بأحد الوجهين، لكن لما كان الأخص أقل وجودا في العقل وأخفى في نظره، وشأن المعرف أن يكون أعرف من المعرف، لم يجز أن يكون أخص أيضا. وقد علم من تعريف المعرف بما يحمل على الشيء أنه لا يجوز أن يكون المعرف مباينا للمعرف، فتعين أن يكون مساويا له في الصدق. ثم ينبغي أن يكون المعرف أعرف من المعرف في نظر العقل لأنه معلوم موصل إلى تصور مجهول هو المعرف، لا أخفى منه ولا مساويا له في الخفاء والظهور. قوله «بالفصل القريب حد»: التعريف لا بد أن يشتمل على أمر يخص المعرف ويساويه بناء على ما سبق من اشتراط المساواة، فهذا الأمر إن كان ذاتيا كان فصلا قريبا وإن كان عرضيا كان خاصة لا محالة، فعلى الأول المعرف يسمى حدا، وعلى الثاني يسمى رسما. ثم كل منهما إن اشتمل على الجنس القريب يسمى حدا تاما ورسما تاما، وإن لم يشتمل على الجنس القريب سواء اشتمل على الجنس البعيد أو كان هناك فصل قريب وحده أو خاصة وحدها يسمى حدا ناقصا ورسما ناقصا. هذا محصل كلامهم وفيه أبحاث لا يسعها المقام.

**English — Translation**

On ‘a defining account of a thing’: having finished discussing the components from which a defining account is constructed, the author turns to the account itself. You have already learned that the two proper objects of this discipline are the defining account and the argument. A defining account is something predicated of the thing to be defined in order to bring about a conception of it—either in what it is essentially or through a feature that distinguishes it from everything else. This is why a definition cannot be **wider** than what it defines: a wider concept supplies neither its entire essence nor a distinguishing feature. ‘Animal’ cannot define ‘human’, since human is animal qualified by rational, and the class of animals contains horses as well as humans. A term which is wider in one respect and narrower in another likewise fails. A term **unrestrictedly narrower** than its object could, in principle, cause one to conceive the wider thing along with its essence: grasping human as rational animal also makes one grasp animal. Nevertheless, the narrower term is less available to the intellect and less evident to it, while a defining expression should be better known than what is to be defined; so it cannot properly be narrower either. Nor can a completely disjoint term define it, since the defining expression must be predicable of its object. Thus the two must have **the same range of predication**. Moreover the defining expression must be more evident to the intellect, because it is something already known that brings about a conception not yet known—not something equally obscure or more obscure. On ‘definition by a proximate differentia is a ḥadd’: given coextensiveness, the account must contain a distinguishing feature coextensive with what is defined. If this feature belongs to the essence, it is a proximate differentia, and the account is an **essential definition** (*ḥadd*); if it is accidental, it is a property and the account is a **description** (*rasm*). When the proximate genus accompanies the differentiating constituent, the ḥadd or rasm is complete; when it does not, whether a remote genus is present or only the differentia/property alone, the account is incomplete. Such is the gist of their account—though a number of questions about it cannot be pursued here.

**Editorial notes**

1. The source relies on a distinction of *defining expression* (muʿarrif) from *the defined object* (muʿarraf); both can appear as unvowelled «المعرف». English explicitly disambiguates roles, without altering the Arabic consonants.

2. Two separate criteria are required: **coextension** and **greater intellectual clarity**. Neither alone suffices.

3. The differentia/property distinction determines *ḥadd* versus *rasm*, while the presence of proximate genus determines *complete* versus *incomplete*. These are logically separate axes.

4. The Qum searchable text contains footnote numbers inside the main layer; these editorial call numbers are not treated as part of al-Yazdī’s transmitted Arabic.

**English — Translation**

On ‘they did not take the common accident into consideration’: the stated purpose of definition is either to discover the thing’s essence or to distinguish it from everything else; a common accident, taken **by itself**, supplies neither. That is why they do not employ it alone in definition. But a conjunction of features, each independently a common accident, may together mark off the thing: ‘walking upright’ for human, or ‘a flying creature that gives birth’ for bat. Such a composite is treated as a **compound property**, and a definition formed from it is accepted, as later writers explicitly note. On ‘the incomplete account may be permitted’: earlier logicians allowed an incomplete definition to employ a wider essential predicate—such as ‘animal’ for human—so that it is an incomplete *ḥadd*, or a wider accidental predicate—‘walking’ for human—so that it is an incomplete *rasm*. They even allowed an accidental predicate **narrower** than what is defined, such as using ‘laughing’ to define animal. Al-Taftāzānī does not accept this: he regards it as an account by what is more obscure, and therefore inadmissible. On ‘as with a verbal [definition]’: a **lexical explanation** may likewise be more general, as in explaining an unfamiliar plant-name with the word ‘plant’. On ‘explanation of what the word signifies’: it identifies among meanings already stored in the hearer’s mind which object the expression designates. Unlike a real definition, it does not produce a previously unknown conception from something already known.

**Editorial notes**

1. Al-Yazdī explicitly reports a permissive older view and al-Taftāzānī's stricter criterion. These must not be merged into a single undifferentiated doctrine.

2. The compound-property examples describe an extensional working definition in the historical classification, not the claim that upright walking is exclusive to humans in current biological taxonomy.

3. The Arabic example «سعدانة نبت» is a lexical gloss of the plant-name *saʿdāna* by the broader class ‘plant’.

On ‘what signifies the relation’: the expression in a spoken proposition that indicates the predicative relation is called the **copula** (*rābiṭa*), the signifier being called by the name of what it signifies, since strictly the copula is the predicative relation itself. Its designation as ‘what indicates the relation’ shows that the copula functions as an instrumental term, because it signifies a relational meaning that is not independent in the manner of a complete name. The copula may be stated or omitted: the former yields an expressed proposition of **three** components, the latter of **two**. On ‘*huwa* was borrowed for it’: copulae may be temporal, signifying that the predicative relation is conjoined to one of the three times, or nontemporal. Al-Yazdī reports al-Fārābī's explanation that, when philosophical learning was translated from Greek into Arabic, the translators found Arabic **defective verbs** to express temporal predication, but no ready nontemporal copula corresponding to Persian *ast* and Greek *estin*. They therefore borrowed words such as *huwa* and *hiya*, although these were originally pronouns rather than instrumental particles. Hence the author says that *huwa* was borrowed for the copula. Other expressions, such as derivatives of defective verbs—*kāʾin* and *mawjūd*—can also serve: ‘Zayd is standing’ or ‘Mīrs is a poet’. On ‘otherwise, conditional’: a proposition is conditional if its judgment is not simply the affirmation or denial of one predicate of a subject, but rather the holding of a relation on the supposition of another, the denial of that holding, the incompatibility between two relations, or the denial of such incompatibility. The first kind is a **connected conditional**, the second a **disjunctive conditional**. The division of propositions into categorical and conditional is, as presented by the author, an **intellectual division by affirmation and negation**; the division of the conditional into connected and disjunctive kinds, by contrast, is established through **induction**.

**Editorial notes**

1. The distinction between ‘copula’ as a verbal sign and the predicative *nisba* it signifies is especially significant for relation and logic; the text says the former receives the name of the latter by metonymy.

2. The Arabic main text reports a historical claim by al-Fārābī about the Greek/Arabic copula; it should be presented as his report, not as a modern comparative-linguistic finding.

3. Qum includes a parenthetical printed variant for the personal example «ميرس / ميرسي». The main running reading is kept; the personal name remains uncertain without page images.

4. The last sentence contrasts *ḥaṣr ʿaqlī* and *ḥaṣr istiqrāʾī*. These are a priori exhaustive classification versus an observed/classificatory generalization, not simply ‘deductive logic’ versus ‘inductive science’.

**English — Translation**

On ‘or by its actuality’: the relation obtains in actuality. The **general unqualified actual** proposition (*muṭlaqa ʿāmma*) asserts that the relation is realized at some point in one of the three times. It is called *unqualified* because that is what a proposition normally conveys without express mention of necessity, permanence, or another modality. It is *general* because it is broader than the special existential forms qualified by nonpermanence or nonnecessity. On ‘or by the absence of the necessity of its contrary’: when what is asserted is that the opposite of the stated relation is not necessary—‘Zayd can be a writer’, meaning writing is not impossible for him, and its negation is not necessary—the proposition is called **possible** (*mumkina*). Possibility here is the removal of the necessity of the opposite; the form is called general because it includes the narrower *special possible*. On ‘these are simple’: the eight preceding modalized kinds are **simple modal propositions**. A modal proposition is either simple, in its content solely affirmative or solely negative, or **composite**, in its content combining affirmation and negation without expressing the second component as a separately worded proposition. A composite may be visibly qualified in words, such as ‘Every human laughs actually, but not permanently’: ‘not permanently’ points to an opposed negative judgment. Or the composite content may be implicit, as in ‘Every human can be a writer with special possibility’, which contains both general possibility of predication and general possibility of nonpredication. In classifying a composite as affirmative or negative, one looks to the **first**, principal component of the proposition. A composite is produced by qualifying a simple proposition with a condition such as nonpermanence (*lā-dawām*) or nonnecessity (*lā-ḍarūra*).

**Editorial notes**

1. The expression «الإمكان» here means denial of the necessity of the opposite, not always an equally open two-sided possibility.

2. The source's examples of composite modalities are traditional schemata. Formal quantifier and time scope matter: the implicit negative component should not be read as an instantaneous contradictory of the original affirmative.

3. ‘Simple’ and ‘composite’ here concern the underlying affirmative/negative content, not a count of words or grammatical clauses.

On ‘the incompatibility of the two relations’: the component relations may both be affirmative, both negative, or one of each. When incompatibility is **affirmed**, the proposition is an **affirmative disjunction**; when it is **denied**, a **negative disjunction**. On ‘the genuine disjunction’ (*ḥaqīqiyya*): this is a disjunctive judgment whose two component relations are incompatible in both **truth and falsity**, as in ‘This number is either even or odd’. A corresponding negative disjunction denies that mutual incompatibility, as in ‘It is not invariably the case that this number is either even or divisible into two equal parts’. A **truth-excluding** disjunction (*māniʿat al-jamʿ*) asserts, or in its negative form denies, incompatibility of the two component relations with respect to **being true together only** ...

**Editorial notes**

1. Al-Yazdī distinguishes the quality of the disjunction **as a whole** from the qualities of its constituent judgments.

2. The printed «هامش» contains an extensive discussion by Mīrzā Muḥammad ʿAlī about whether a trichotomy of word classes can count as a logical disjunction. That argument is NOT attributed to al-Yazdī in this reader.

3. The phrase «في الصدق فقط» continues at Q67 with examples of the truth-excluding kind.
