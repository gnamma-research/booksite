## C. Inference under exact source constraints

The simple presentation of an **exceptive connected conditional** is:

> الاستثنائي ينتج من المتصلة وضع المقدم ورفع التالي

Let `P` be antecedent (`muqaddam`) and `Q` consequent (`tālī`). In a **modern truth-functional explanatory model**, `P→Q` and `P` license `Q` (affirming antecedent), and `P→Q` and `¬Q` license `¬P` (denying consequent). The invalid forms `P→Q,Q ⟹ P` and `P→Q,¬P ⟹ ¬Q` have simple counterexamples. These modern facts do **not** establish the traditional distinction between `luzūmiyya` and `ittifāqiyya`, which concern the *basis of the connection* and require commentary and modal/temporal scope.

The printed wording **`الحقيقية وضع كل كمانعة الجمع ورفعه كمانعة الخلو`** refers to the two sides of a real disjunction: on asserting a member, derive the denial of the other (*preventing conjunction*); on denying a member, derive affirmation of the other (*preventing joint falsity*).

| Premise type (modern schematic) | From P | From not-P |
|---|---|---|
| `ḥaqīqiyya`: precisely one of P,Q | infer not-Q | infer Q |
| `māniʿat al-jamʿ`: not both | infer not-Q | no inference to Q |
| `māniʿat al-khulū`: at least one | no inference to not-Q | infer Q |

These conditional inferences are useful *logical probes* and do not settle whether the historically defined propositions were intrinsic (`ʿinādiyya`) or accidental (`ittifāqiyya`). Recall the distinct Cairo L12 printed wording `وكل منها عنادية` from Stage 82, which treats intrinsic opposition as a further possible classification across the three forms.
