### What constitutes a “page” here?
This is the **Qum PRINTED page position in the principal ḥāshiya**, not a standard-length English typeset page. A printed page can contain a single line of al-Yazdī (e.g. Q55, Q91) and many paragraphs by another scholar in the separately marked **هامش**. We have translated every page-position's searchable **main شرح text**, but we have NOT counted the later glossator's pages or filled a main-text position with invented prose.

Seven printed positions within Q7–123 do not have an independently delineated principal-commentary paragraph in the available searchable edition: **20, 21, 38, 52, 53, 85 and 109**. These are empty, heading-only or marked marginal-only. They are not falsely included among the 110. Q103 crosses two editorial topics and has **two nonoverlapping translated blocks** on one printed page; Q31 and Q35 similarly cross editorial divisions. This accounts for **113 blocks versus 110 unique printed positions**.

The bound volume's contents continue into later **ḥawāshī al-ḥāshiya** after Q123. The visible Q123 closing prayer belongs to al-Yazdī's main discourse, but the printed `قد تمت النسخة المسماة بالحاشية في المنطق` completion statement is a **separate printing/copying notice**. The secondary ḥawāshī explicitly begin at **Q125**, not Q114.

## Editorial controls that prevent inflated claims

1. **Principal author vs later annotation:** Each page's source was restricted to what the searchable source labels **شرح**. Named glosses by Mīrzā Muḥammad ʿAlī, ʿAbd al-Raḥīm, Dawwānī and others marked **هامش** are not silently included. They are a future **distinct** translation project.
2. **Original matn preserved:** All **19** existing Taftāzānī logic divisions remain in their prior source files, and the independent Kalām corpus of **1,044** clauses is untouched. Bilingual headings and the two 55-page translation units are editorial reading aids, not forged historical authorial structure.
3. **Exactly two text states in the main book reader:** **Matn** and **Matn + sharḥ**, with both 55-page batches accessible via one nested compact menu. No third “sharḥ only” control and no proliferation of the visible Darūrī toolbar.
4. **Historical excerpt archive preserved:** The original **177 separately source-linked bilingual selections** remain in `yazdi-layer-data.js` for provenance and printed Q/I13 variant comparisons. All are now superseded **visually** by 113 consecutive source blocks, not duplicated in the main reading flow. The nine Qum/Gujarat printed comparison dossiers and seventeen further Gujarat source links remain available at the relevant Q14–19 pages.
5. **Source authority and variants:** The working base is the digitally searchable Qum printed text, with Q18 cross-checked in the Najaf Desert Library representation. The Gujarat 2013 and other printings have only been compared for selected earlier loci. **Photographic Qum/Gujarat/Tehran line collation and an independently verified manuscript witness have NOT been achieved for all 110 positions.**
6. **Existing cruxes are retained:** Q24's `ولا لازم ذهني` versus two printed readings `له لازم`; Q37's possible main-text continuation after a printed **هامش** boundary; Q71's Persian mnemonic; Q95–101 fourth-figure modal rules; Q108's suspicious searchable `حكم عليه كل كل`; Q114's reported `بالبرهان` variant; Q117's reported `لذواتها` alternative. No unsupported text has been quietly emended.
7. **Textual scope remains honestly labelled:** These are **first-pass translations of the entire principal commentary as represented in the SEARCHABLE Qum text**, not a definitive or independently certified critical edition. A 110-page source-position count does **not** certify an independently collated 110-page author's transcript.

## Scholarly milestones represented in the full translation
- **Q7–19:** Dedication, guidance, scope, conception versus assent, and formal subject of logic.
- **Q22–47:** Signification, four relations of universals, five predicables, genus and differentia.
- **Q48–69:** The three senses of universal; definition and description; categorical, modal and conditional propositions.
- **Q70–84:** Contradiction; simple conversion; early and later contraposition.
- **Q86–104:** Categorical syllogistic figures and conditional/exceptive inference, including the commentator's disagreement with Taftāzānī's fourth-figure formulation.
- **Q105–113:** Induction, analogy and the five logical arts, including causal demonstration.
- **Q114–123:** Parts of sciences, proper accidents, scientific principles, pedagogical methods and the author's closing counsel.
