Heading, verses, and the governing construction [IV:800–801]

Meccan; four verses. [The DKA heading additionally places its revelation after al-Tīn.] In the name of God, the All-Merciful, the Merciful.

For the accustomed security of Quraysh—(1) their accustomed winter and summer journey—(2) let them worship the Lord of this House, (3) who has fed them against hunger and secured them against fear. (4)

“For the accustomed security of Quraysh” (li-īlāfi (لإيلاف) Quraysh) depends grammatically on “let them worship”: He commands them to worship Him because He has accustomed them to the two journeys. If you ask why fāʾ (فاء (ف)) then enters, I reply: because the statement carries a conditional meaning. Its sense is: if for nothing else, let them worship Him for their accustomed security. God’s blessings upon them are innumerable; if they do not worship Him for His other blessings, let them worship Him for this one conspicuous blessing.

It is also said to mean: “Marvel at Quraysh’s accustomed security.” Another interpretation connects it to what precedes: “He made them like eaten stalks for the accustomed security of Quraysh.” This resembles enjambment (taḍmīn) in poetry: the meaning of one verse depends upon the preceding verse in such a way that it cannot stand correctly without it. In Ubayy’s codex the two are one sūrah, with no division. It is related of ʿUmar that he recited them both in the second unit of the sunset prayer, having recited “By the fig” in the first.

The meaning is that He destroyed the Abyssinians who intended to attack them, so that people would hear of this, stand in greater awe of them, and accord them increased respect. Thus security would be established for their two journeys and no one would dare attack them.

The fāʾ and the implied condition [XVI:585–586]

Arabic lemma: فلم دخلت الفاء

Meccan; four verses. In the name of God, the All-Merciful, the Merciful.

On “why, then, does fāʾ (فاء (ف)) enter?”: the fāʾ (فاء (ف)) [in the question fa-lima] indicates objection. That is: if li-īlāf (لإيلاف) depends on fa-l-yaʿbudū (فليعبدوا), why does the fāʾ (فاء (ف)) of succession stand between the governing expression and what it governs? He answers that fāʾ (فاء (ف)) introduces the consequence of an omitted condition. This must be supplied, because if one reconstructs simply “let them worship Him for the accustomed security of Quraysh,” the fāʾ (فاء (ف)) remains without anything to which it relates.

It may also be understood as reinforcement, with fāʾ (فاء (ف)) marking succession: “For Quraysh’s accustomed security, let them worship Him—so let them worship.” Likewise His saying “so let them rejoice” [10:58]. Its permissibility has already been transmitted from al-Zubayr, from al-Zajjāj. His saying “and your Lord—magnify Him” [74:3] follows this pattern. He says: “Fāʾ (فاء (ف)) enters because of the conditional meaning, as though it said, ‘Whatever happens, do not cease to magnify Him.’”

On “the meaning is: immā (إمّا) lā (لا), let them worship Him”: the author is reported to have said, “The Arabs say ‘Do this, immā (إمّا) lā (لا),’ meaning ‘If you will not do something else, do this.’ Mā (ما) is additional, compensating for the omitted kāna. They pronounce lā (لا) with imāla (إمالة) because it stands in place of the verb, like balā, both taking the verb’s place. One also says, ‘Give me this, immā (إمّا) lā (لا).’”

The journeys and variant forms [IV:801]

Quraysh had two journeys: they travelled to Yemen in winter and Syria in summer, obtaining provisions and trading. They were secure on both journeys because they were the people of God’s sanctuary and custodians of His House; no one molested them, whereas other people were seized and raided.

Īlāf (إيلاف) comes from ālaftu l-makāna (آلفت المكان), ūlifuhu īlāfan (أولفه إيلافًا): I became accustomed to a place; hence I am muʾlif (مؤلف). The poet says: “Among those accustomed to the [herbage / easy journey], not those grazing arāk (أراك).” [The DKA transcription reads al-rahw, “easy travelling”; Ṭībī’s printed base reads al-zahw and explains both forms below.]

The reading li-ilāfi (لإلاف) Quraysh also occurs, meaning “because of Quraysh’s becoming accustomed.” It is said that one says aliftuhu ilfan wa-ilāfan (وإلافًا). Abū Jaʿfar reads li-ilfi Quraysh. Both forms occur in the poet’s words: “You claim that your brothers are Quraysh; they have ilf (إلف), while you have no ilāf (إلاف).”

The camel verse and the verbal noun [XVI:587]

Arabic lemma: من المؤلفات

On “among those accustomed”: one says ālaftu l-makāna (آلفت المكان), ūlifuhu īlāfan (أولفه إيلافًا) when one becomes accustomed to a place; hence muʾlif (مؤلف). [The cited wording is] al-zahw ghayra l-awārik. Zahw (زهو) denotes being short of ripeness; zahw (زهو) also means herbage and dates beginning to colour. One says zahat al-ibilu zahwan when camels travel for a night or more after watering. Zahawtuhā, “I drove them,” is also used: the verb is both transitive and intransitive. Zāhiya (زاهية) camels do not graze saline plants. Some transmit al-rahw with rāʾ (راء (ر)): easy travelling. One says, “The horses came at an easy pace.” Awārik (أوارك) is the plural of ārika (آركة): camels eating arāk (أراك). Al-Jawharī says: “Arakat means that she stayed among the arāk (أراك), which belongs to the saline vegetation; she is ārika (آركة), plural awārik (أوارك).”

On “that is, because of Quraysh’s becoming accustomed”: according to this interpretation, ilāf (إلاف) is the verbal noun of fāʿala (فَاعَلَ), and means muʾālafa (مؤالفة), like ḍāraba (ضارب), muḍārabatan (مضاربةً) and ḍirāban (ضرابًا).

The name Quraysh and the syntax of “journey” [IV:802]

ʿIkrima reads li-yaʾlafa (ليألف) Qurayshun, ilfahum riḥlata l-shitāʾi wa-l-ṣayf (رحلة الشتاء والصيف). Quraysh are the descendants of al-Naḍr ibn Kināna. They were named with the diminutive of al-qirsh, a great sea creature that attacks ships and can be overcome only with fire. Muʿāwiya is reported to have asked Ibn ʿAbbās, “Why was Quraysh so named?” He replied, “After a sea creature that eats but is not eaten and overcomes but is not overcome,” and recited: “Quraysh is the creature dwelling in the sea; after it Quraysh was named Quraysh.”

The diminutive conveys magnification. Another explanation derives it from qarsh, earning, because they earned through trade and travelling through the lands.

Īlāf (إيلاف) is first stated without qualification, then replaced by an appositional expression specifying the two journeys, to magnify this accustomed security and remind them of the greatness of its blessing. Riḥla (رِحْلة) is accusative as the object of īlāfihim (إيلافهم), just as yatīman (يتيمًا) is made accusative by iṭʿām (إطعام) [90:14–15]. The two journeys of winter and summer are meant. The singular is used because confusion is impossible, as in: “Eat into part of your belly [rather than fill your bellies].”

Two poems [XVI:588–589]

Arabic lemma: وقيل

On “it is also said”: this indicates a verbal noun of faʿala (فَعَلَ), like kataba kitāban (كتب كتابًا).

After the verse beginning “You claim” comes: “Those people were secured against hunger and fear, whereas the sons of Asad have hungered and feared.”

Its author is Musāwir ibn Hind, satirising Banū Asad. He says: you are not of Quraysh, nor are Quraysh of you; your claim to their brotherhood is false, for they were fed against hunger and secured against fear, whereas you were not. The author says: “This belongs to the verses with subtle meanings: the first hemistich reports their claim, while the second argues against them and binds them to its consequence.”

On the verse beginning “Quraysh is the creature”: al-Wāḥidī and Muḥyī l-Sunna transmit the following as al-Jumaḥī’s: “Quraysh is the creature dwelling in the sea; after it Quraysh was named Quraysh. It eats the lean and the fat, and never leaves a feather to anything with two wings. So too, throughout the lands, the tribe Quraysh devours the lands in vigorous fashion. In the latter age they shall have a prophet who multiplies killing and scratching wounds among them.”

وقرئ: رحلة، بالضم: وهي الجهة التي يرحل إليها: والتنكير في جوع وخوف لشدتهما، يعنى: أطعمهم بالرحلتين من جوع شديد كانوا فيه قبلهما، وآمنهم من خوف عظيم وهو خوف أصحاب الفيل، أو خوف التخطف في بلدهم ومسايرهم. وقيل: كانوا قد أصابتهم شدة حتى أكلوا الجيف والعظام المحرقة، وآمنهم من خوف الجذام فلا يصيبهم ببلدهم. وقيل ذلك كله بدعاء إبراهيم صلوات الله عليه. ومن بدع التفاسير: وآمنهم من خوف، من أن تكون الخلافة في غيرهم. وقرئ: من خوف، بإخفاء النون.

عن رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم: «من قرأ سورة لإيلاف قريش أعطاه الله عشر حسنات بعدد من طاف بالكعبة واعتكف بها» .

Hunger, fear, and the concluding report [IV:803]

Ruḥla (رُحْلة) is also read, with ḍamma (ضَمَّة (ـُ)): the destination to which one travels. The indefiniteness of “hunger” and “fear” indicates their severity. Through the two journeys He fed them against the severe hunger they had previously suffered and secured them against great fear: fear of the People of the Elephant, or fear of being seized in their own country and on their routes.

It is also said that hardship had afflicted them until they ate carrion and burnt bones; and that He secured them against fear of leprosy, so that it does not afflict them in their country. All this, it is said, came through Abraham’s prayer. Among novel interpretations is: “He secured them against fear” that the caliphate might belong to others. Min khawf is also read with concealment of the nūn (نون (ن)) [ikhfāʾ (إخفاء)].

From God’s Messenger: “Whoever recites the sūrah ‘For the accustomed security of Quraysh,’ God gives him ten good deeds for every person who circumambulates the Kaʿba and stays there in devotional retreat.”

TEXTUAL AND REVIEW NOTES K106-U1 / T106-U1: The camel verse has zahw in the printed Dubai base and rahw in the DKA transcription. Both are preserved and explained. The difficult printed phrase الزهو غير الإدراك appears in the transcription as well; it is not securely established as a transcription error. The bracketed reconstruction of the cited verse and its relation to the adjacent lexical explanations require further fine collation before final publication. The provisional account above retains all the lexical alternatives rather than suppressing the difficulty. T106-U2: The reading “al-Zubayr, from al-Zajjāj” occurs in the print; the modern apparatus notes a witness omission. It remains attributed as printed, without asserting a resolved identification.
