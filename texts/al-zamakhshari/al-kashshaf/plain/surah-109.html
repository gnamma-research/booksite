Q 109:1–6

بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ ٱلرَّحْمَـٰنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ
﴿ قُلْ يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلْكَـٰفِرُونَ ۝١ لَآ أَعْبُدُ مَا تَعْبُدُونَ ۝٢ وَلَآ أَنتُمْ عَـٰبِدُونَ مَآ أَعْبُدُ ۝٣ وَلَآ أَنَا۠ عَابِدٌ مَّا عَبَدتُّمْ ۝٤ وَلَآ أَنتُمْ عَـٰبِدُونَ مَآ أَعْبُدُ ۝٥ لَكُمْ دِينُكُمْ وَلِىَ دِينِ ۝٦ ﴾

In the name of Allah, the Beneficent, the Merciful.

1 Say: O disbelievers

2 I worship not that which ye worship

3 Nor worship ye that which I worship

4 And I shall not worship that which ye worship

5 Nor will ye worship that which I worship

6 Unto you your religion, and unto me my religion

Verse translation: Marmaduke Pickthall (1930).

Zamakhsharī · الكشافK109.01

مكية، وهي ست آيات «نزلت بعد الماعون» ويقال لها ولسورة الإخلاص: المقشقشتان، أى المبرئتان من النفاق بسم الله الرحمن الرحيم

Heading and verses

Meccan; six verses. [K-DKA adds: revealed after al-Māʿūn.] This sūrah and Sūrat al-Ikhlāṣ are called al-Muqashqishatān: the two that clear one of hypocrisy. In the name of God, the All-Merciful, the Merciful. “Say: O unbelievers! I do not worship what you worship, nor are you worshippers of what I worship. Nor am I a worshipper of what you have worshipped, nor are you worshippers of what I worship. To you your religion, and to me my religion.”

Zamakhsharī · الكشافK109.02

المخاطبون كفرة مخصوصون قد علم الله منهم أنهم لا يؤمنون. روى أن رهطا من قريش قالوا: يا محمد، هلم فاتبع ديننا ونتبع دينك: تعبد آلهتنا سنة ونعبد إلهك سنة، فقال معاذ الله أن أشرك بالله غيره: فقالوا: فاستلم بعض آلهتنا نصدقك ونعبد إلهك، فنزلت، فغدا إلى المسجد الحرام وفيه الملأ من قريش فقام على رؤوسهم فقرأها عليهم، فأيسوا.

Those addressed and the occasion of revelation

Those addressed are particular unbelievers whom God knew would not believe. It is reported that a group of Quraysh said: “Muḥammad, come: follow our religion and we will follow yours. You worship our gods for a year and we will worship your God for a year.” He replied: “God forbid that I associate anything else with God!” They said: “Then touch some of our gods in reverence; we will believe you and worship your God.” The sūrah was then revealed. In the morning he went to the Sacred Mosque, where the leaders of Quraysh were gathered, stood before them, and recited it to them. They lost hope.

Ṭībī · فتوح الغيبT109.01

قولُه: (ونَتَّبعُ)، عن بعضِهم: هو عطفٌ على محلِّ "[فاتَّبعْ]"، لأنه لو كان مضارعًا لكان مجزومًا، أنه جوابُ "هَلُمَّ". وقولُه: "نعبدُ" إلى آخره، تفسير.

قولُه: (فاستلمْ)، أَيْ: قَبِّلْ؛ يقال: استلمِ الحجرَ، أي: صافحْه، ثُمّ عَمَّ في كلِّ مُماسّة.

Grammar of the proposed bargain; istilām (XVI, 606–607)

On “and we will follow”: according to one explanation, this is coordinated with the syntactic position of “then follow,” because if that expression were an imperfect verb, it would be jussive as the response to “come” (halumma (هلمّ)). “We will worship,” and what follows, explains the proposal. On “then touch in reverence” (fa-stalim): that is, kiss. One says istalama al-ḥajara, meaning that he touched the Stone with his hand; the expression was then extended to every kind of contact.

Q 109:2–5

﴿ لَآ أَعْبُدُ مَا تَعْبُدُونَ ۝٢ وَلَآ أَنتُمْ عَـٰبِدُونَ مَآ أَعْبُدُ ۝٣ وَلَآ أَنَا۠ عَابِدٌ مَّا عَبَدتُّمْ ۝٤ وَلَآ أَنتُمْ عَـٰبِدُونَ مَآ أَعْبُدُ ۝٥ ﴾

2 I worship not that which ye worship

3 Nor worship ye that which I worship

4 And I shall not worship that which ye worship

5 Nor will ye worship that which I worship

Verse translation: Marmaduke Pickthall (1930).

Zamakhsharī · الكشافK109.03

لا أعبد أريدت به العبادة فيما يستقبل، لأن «لا» لا تدخل إلا على مضارع في معنى الاستقبال، كما أن «ما» لا تدخل إلا على مضارع في معنى الحال، ألا ترى أن «لن» تأكيد فيما تنفيه «لا» وقال الخليل في «لن»: أن أصله «لا أن» والمعنى: لا أفعل في المستقبل ما تطلبونه منى من عبادة آلهتكم، ولا أنتم فاعلون فيه ما أطلب منكم من عبادة إلهى ولا أنا عابد ما عبدتم أى: وما كنت قط عابدا فيما سلف ما عبدتم فيه، يعنى لم تعهد منى عبادة صنم في الجاهلية، فكيف ترجى منى في الإسلام ولا أنتم عابدون ما أعبد أى: وما عبدتم في وقت ما أنا على عبادته. فإن قلت: فهلا قيل: ما عبدت، كما قيل: ما عبدتم؟ قلت: لأنهم كانوا يعبدون الأصنام قبل المبعث، وهو لم يكن يعبد الله تعالى في ذلك الوقت. فإن قلت: فلم جاء على «ما» دون «من» ؟ قلت، لأن المراد الصفة، كأنه قال: لا أعبد الباطل، ولا تعبدون الحق. وقيل: إن «ما» مصدرية، أى: لا أعبد عبادتكم، ولا تعبدون عبادتي

Future and past; the use of mā

“I do not worship” intends worship in the future, because lā (لا) is used only with an imperfect verb having a future meaning, just as mā (ما) is used only with an imperfect having a present meaning. Do you not see that lan emphatically negates what lā (لا) negates? Al-Khalīl said that lan originally consists of lā (لا) an. The meaning is: in the future I will not do what you ask of me, namely worship your gods; nor will you do in the future what I ask of you, namely worship my God. “Nor am I a worshipper of what you have worshipped” means: at no time in the past was I a worshipper of what you worshipped then. You never knew me to worship an idol in the age of ignorance; how, then, can this be expected of me in Islam? “Nor are you worshippers of what I worship” means: at no time have you worshipped that which I now worship. If you ask why He did not say “what I worshipped” (mā (ما) ʿabadtu (عبدت)), as He said “what you worshipped” (mā (ما) ʿabadtum (عبدتم)), I answer: they used to worship idols before the prophetic mission, whereas he was not worshipping God at that time. [Ṭībī's sustained objection to this statement is translated below.] If you ask why mā (ما), “what,” is used rather than man, “who,” I answer: the intended consideration is the description, as though He said, “I do not worship the false, and you do not worship the true.” It is also said that mā (ما) forms a verbal noun: I do not perform your worship, and you do not perform my worship.

Ṭībī · فتوح الغيبT109.02

قولُه: (فَهلّا قيل)، يعني: قولُه: ﴿وَلَا أَنْتُمْ عَابِدُونَ مَا أَعْبُدُ﴾، قرينةٌ لقوله: ﴿وَلَا أَنَا عَابِدٌ مَا عَبَدْتُمْ﴾، فلِمَ خولِفَ في الثانية إلى ﴿مَا أَعْبُدُ﴾، وكان الظاهر ﴿مَا عَبَدْتُ﴾، كما قيل في الأولى ﴿مَا عَبَدْتُمْ﴾؟

قولُه: (وهو لم يكنْ يعبدُ اللهَ تعالى في [ذلك] الوقت)، الانتصاف: «هذا القولُ خطأٌ أصلًا وفرعًا، أما أصلُه فإنّ القدريّ يعتقدُ أنَّ النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم، لم يكنْ قبلَ البعثِ على دينِ نبيٍّ قبلَه، لأن ذلك غَميزةٌ في حقّه ومنفّرٌ عن اتّباعه، ويعتقدون أن الناسَ كلَّهم متعبّدون بمقتضى العقلِ بوجوبِ النظرِ في آياتِ الله وأدلّةِ توحيدِه ومعرفتِه، وأنّ وجوبَ النظرِ بالعقلِ لا بالسمع؛

فتلك عبادةُ قبل المبعث، يجبُ أن لا يظنوا به عليه السلام الإخلالَ بها فأصلُهم حينئذٍ يقتضي أنه صلى الله عليه وسلم كانَ قبلَ المبعثِ يعبدُ اللهَ عز وجل، فحافظَ الزمخشريُّ [على] هذا الأصلِ في عدمِ اتْباعِه لنبيٍّ سابق، فأخلَّ بالتفريعِ على أصلِه الآخرِ في وجوبِ العبادةِ بالعقل.

والحقُّ أنه صلى الله عليه وسلم كانَ متعبدًا قبلَ الوحي ويَتَحنّثُ في غارِ حراء؛ فإنْ كانَ مجئُ قولِه ﴿أَعْبُدُ﴾، لأن الماضي لم تحصلْ فيه العبادةُ المرادةُ في الآية، فيحملُ الأمرُ فيما عَبَدْتُ، على [مجموعِ] العبادةِ الحاصلةِ التي لم تُعلمْ إلّا بالشرع، لا على مجرّدِ توحيدِ الله ومعرفتِه؛ فإنّ ذلك لم يَزلْ ثابتًا له عليه السلام قبل البعثة.

وأما مجيئُه مضارعًا، فلتصوير عبادتِه في نفسِ السامعِ وتَمكُّنِها، كقولِه: ﴿أَلَمْ تَرَ أَنَّ اللَّهَ أَنْزَلَ مِنَ السَّمَاءِ مَاءً فَتُصْبِحُ الْأَرْضُ مُخْضَرَّةً﴾ [الحج: 63]، والأصلُ: أصبحتْ؛ عُدلَ عنه للمعنى المذكور». وقلتُ: يجوزُ أن يُحملَ على الاستمرارِ في الماضي والآتي بقرينةِ التقابل، كما في قوله تعالى: ﴿إِنَّ الَّذِينَ يَتْلُونَ كِتَابَ اللَّهِ وَأَقَامُوا الصَّلَاةَ﴾ [فاطر: 29]، بعطفِ الماضي على المستقبل. والصحيحُ أنه صلواتُ الله عليه كانَ [قبلَ] المبعثِ متعبدًا بشرعٍ.

Why “what I worship” rather than “what I worshipped”? (607–608)

On “why did He not say…?”: “Nor are you worshippers of what I worship” is paired with “nor am I a worshipper of what you have worshipped.” Why, then, is the second altered to “what I worship,” when “what I worshipped” would appear to be expected, corresponding to “what you worshipped” in the first? On “he was not worshipping God at that time”: al-Intiṣāf says: “This statement is mistaken both in its underlying principle and in its consequence. As for the principle, the Qadarī holds that before his mission the Prophet was not following the religion of a previous prophet, because that would be a blemish in his standing and discourage people from following him. They also hold that all people are obliged, by the requirements of reason, to reflect upon God's signs and the proofs of His oneness and knowledge of Him, and that reflection is obligatory through reason rather than revealed instruction. That is worship before the mission, and they must not suppose that he failed to perform it. Their own principle thus entails that he worshipped God before his mission. “Al-Zamakhsharī preserved his principle concerning the absence of adherence to an earlier prophet, but failed to draw the consequence of his other principle: the rational obligation of worship. The truth is that he worshipped before revelation and devoted himself to religious observance in the cave of Ḥirāʾ. If the use of ‘I worship’ is because the worship intended by the verse had not occurred in the past, the matter concerning ‘what I worshipped’ should be understood as the full body of actual worship known only through revealed law, not the mere affirmation of God's oneness and knowledge of Him, which were always established for him before the mission. “As for its imperfect form, this vividly presents his worship to the hearer's mind and fixes it there. Compare: ‘Have you not seen that God sent down water from the sky, and the earth becomes green?’ (22:63). The expected form is ‘became’; it is altered for the reason just stated.” I say: it may be understood as continuity in both past and future, as indicated by the parallel expressions. Compare: “Those who recite God's Book and established prayer” (35:29), where a past verb is coordinated with an imperfect. The correct position is that before the mission he practiced worship according to a revealed law.

Ṭībī · فتوح الغيبT109.03

روى ابنُ الجوزي في كتابِ "الوفا"، عن الإمامِ أحمدَ بنِ حنبلٍ رحمه اللهُ تعالى: «مَن قالَ: إن رسولَ الله صلى الله عليه وسلم كانَ على دينِ قومِه، فهو قولُ سوء، أليسَ [كانَ] لا يأكلُ ما ذُبحَ على النُّصب؟ وقالَ أبو الوفاءِ عليٌّ بنُ عقيل: كان رسولُ الله صلى الله عليه وسلم متدينًا قبلَ بعثتِه، بما يَصحُّ عنده أنه مِن شريعةِ إبراهيم عليه السلام، وأما بعدَ بعثتِه، فهل كانَ يتعبّدُ بشريعةِ مَن قبلَه؟

فيه روايتان: إحداهما: أنه كانَ متعبدًا بما صَحَّ مِن شرائعِ مَن قبلَه بطريقِ الوحيِ إليه،

لا من جهتِهم ولا نَقلِهم ولا كتبهم المنزلة، واختارها أبو الحسن التميميُّ، وهو قولُ أصحابِ أبي حنيفةَ رحمهم الله.

والروايةُ الثانية: أنه لم [يكنْ] متعبدًا بشئٍ من الشرائعِ، إلّا ما أُوحيَ إليه من شريعتِه، وهو قولُ المعتزلةِ والأشعرية. ولأصحابِ الشافعيّ وجهانِ كالروايتين. واختلفَ القائلون بأنه متعبدٌ بشرعِ مَن قبلَه: بأيِّ شريعةٍ كان متعبدًا؟ قالَ بعضُهم: بشريعةِ موسى عليه السلام إلّا ما نُسخَ في شرعنا. وظاهرُ كلامِ أحمدَ رحمه الله تعالى، أنه كانَ متعبدًا بكلِّ ما صَحَّ أنه شريعةٌ لنبيٍّ قبلَه، ما لم يثبتْ نسخْه، لقوله تعالى: ﴿أُولَئِكَ الَّذِينَ هَدَى اللَّهُ فَبِهُدَاهُمُ اقْتَدِهِ﴾ [الأنعام: 90].

وقالَ ابنُ قتيبة: لم تَزلِ العربُ على بقايا من دينِ إسماعيلَ عليه السلام، من ذلك: حجُّ البيت، والخِتان، وإيقاعُ الطلاقِ إذا كان ثلاثًا، وأن للزوجِ الرّجعةَ في الواحدةِ والاثنين، ودِيّةُ النفسِ [مئةٌ] من الإبل، والغُسلُ من الجنابة، وتحريمُ ذواتِ المحارمِ بالقرابةِ والصِّهر، فكانَ رسولُ الله صلى الله عليه وسلم على ما كانوا عليه من الإيمانِ بالله والعملِ بشرائعهم.

وأما قولِه تعالى: ﴿مَا كُنْتَ تَدْرِي مَا الْكِتَابُ وَلَا الْإِيمَانُ﴾ [الشورى: 52]، يُعنى به: شرائعُ الإيمان، ولم يُردْ به الإيمانُ الذي هو الإقرارُ بالله». تَمَّ كلامُ ابن الجوزي.

Earlier revealed law before and after the mission (608–609)

Ibn al-Jawzī transmits in al-Wafāʾ from Imām Aḥmad ibn Ḥanbal: “To say that God's Messenger followed his people's religion is an evil statement. Did he not refrain from eating what was slaughtered on the sacrificial stones?” Abū al-Wafāʾ ʿAlī ibn ʿAqīl said: “Before his mission God's Messenger practiced as religion whatever he knew to be soundly established of Abraham's revealed law. After his mission, did he practice according to the law of those before him? There are two transmitted positions. “The first is that he practiced whatever was soundly established of the laws of those before him through revelation to him, not through their own accounts, transmissions, or revealed books. Abū al-Ḥasan al-Tamīmī preferred this position; it is also the position of Abū Ḥanīfa's followers. “The second is that he was bound by none of the earlier laws, but only by what was revealed to him of his own law. This is the position of the Muʿtazila and the Ashʿarīs. Al-Shāfiʿī's followers have two views corresponding to these two transmitted positions. “Those who say he practiced according to an earlier law differ over which law it was. Some say Moses' law, apart from what was abrogated in our law. The apparent sense of Aḥmad's words is that he practiced everything soundly established as the law of an earlier prophet, unless its abrogation was established, because God says: ‘Those are the ones God guided; follow, then, their guidance’ (6:90). “Ibn Qutayba said: The Arabs continued to observe remnants of Ishmael's religion. These included pilgrimage to the House, circumcision, making divorce final when it reached three, the husband's right to take his wife back after one or two divorces, a blood-money payment of one hundred camels for a life, washing after major ritual impurity, and the prohibition of marriage to those forbidden through blood relationship or marriage affinity. God's Messenger thus shared their belief in God and their practice of these religious ordinances. As for ‘You did not know what the Book was, nor faith’ (42:52), this means the ordinances of faith, not faith in the sense of acknowledging God.” Here ends Ibn al-Jawzī's discussion.

وقلتُ: غرضُ المصنفِ من ارتكابِ هذا المحظور، دَفعُ التكرارِ من الكلام باختلافِ الزمانينِ المستقبلِ والماضي؛ فإنه جَعلَ القرينتينِ الأوليينِ للاستقبال والأخُريين للماضي، ولذلك توجّهَ عليه السؤال. والأوجَهُ أن يقال: إن الكلامَ ما وقع في عبادةِ رسولِ الله صلى الله عليه وسلم، وأنه أيُّ شئٍ عبد فيما مضى من الزمان، بل وقع فيما يُستقبل، كما يشهدُ له سَببُ النزولِ بقوله: ﴿مَا أَعْبُدُ﴾، على ظاهره. وأما قولُه: ﴿مَا عَبَدْتُمْ﴾ على الماضي، فللمبالغةِ مِن التبرّي عنهم وعن عبادتهم، فهو على خلافِ الظاهر.

Ṭībī's objection to the distribution of past and future (609)

I say: the author's purpose in venturing this objectionable statement was to remove repetition from the discourse by distinguishing the two times, future and past. He made the first pair refer to the future and the latter pair to the past; hence the question arose against him. The sounder explanation is that the discourse does not concern God's Messenger's past worship or what he worshipped in earlier times. It concerns the future, as the occasion of revelation attests. “What I worship” therefore remains in its apparent sense. As for the past form “what you worshipped,” it intensifies his repudiation of them and their worship, and is thus understood beyond the immediately apparent temporal sense.

Ṭībī · فتوح الغيبT109.05

قالَ الإمام: «في الآيةِ قولان: الأولُ: أنه لا تكرارَ فيها، وفيه وجوه:

[أحدُها] أنّ الأولَ للاستقبال، لأن "لا" لا تدخلُ إلّا على مضارعٍ في معنى الاستقبال، أي: لا أفعلُ في المستقبلِ ما تطلبونه مني من عبادةِ آلهتِكم، ولا أنتم فاعلون في المستقبلِ ما أطلبُ منكم من عبادةِ إلهي، ثمّ قالَ: ﴿وَلَا أَنَا عَابِدٌ مَا عَبَدْتُمْ﴾، أي: لستُ في الحالِ بعابدٍ معبوديكم، ولا أنتم في الحالِ بعابدين معبودي.

وثانيها: أن يُقلب، فيجعلَ الأولُ للحالِ والثاني للاستقبال، وعليه كلامُ الزجاجِ والواحدي ومحيي السُّنة؛ قال الواحدي: «وإنما جئَ بـ"ما" بدلَ "من" ليقابلَ قولَه ﴿مَا تَعْبُدُونَ﴾ حملًا للثاني على الأول». وقال الزجاجُ ومحيي السُّنة: «هذا خطابٌ لمن سبقَ في علمِ الله أن لا يؤمن».

وثالثُها: قولُ أبي مسلم: المقصودُ من الأُولَيينِ المعبود، و"ما" بمعنى "الذي"، أي: لا أعبدُ الأصنامَ ولا تعبدونَ الله، وفي الأُخريَيْنِ "ما" مصدرية، أي: ولا أنا عابدٌ مثلَ عبادتكم المبنيّةِ على الشك، ولا أنتم عابدون مثلَ عبادتي المبنيّةِ على اليقين.

ورابعُها: أن تُحملَ الأولى على نفي الاعتبارِ الذي ذكروه، والثانيةُ على العامِ بجميعِ الجهات، أي: لا أعبدُ ما تعبدون رجاءَ أن تعبدوا الله، ولا أنتم عابدون رجاءَ أن أعبدَ صنمَكم، ثم قال: ولا أنا عابدٌ صنمَكم لغرضٍ من الأغراض، بوجهٍ من الوجوه، وكذا أنتم لا تعبدون اللهَ لغرضٍ من الأغراض؛ مثالُه: مَن يدعو غيرَه إلى الظلمِ لغرضِ التنعّم، فيقول: لا أظلمُ لغرضِ التنعّم، بل لا أظلمُ أصلًا، سواءٌ كان للتنعّمِ أو غيرِه.

Four explanations that avoid repetition (610)

The Imām [Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī] says: “There are two positions concerning the passage. The first is that it contains no repetition, and this has several explanations. “First: the opening pair concerns the future, since lā (لا) enters only upon an imperfect verb with future meaning: in the future I will not do what you ask of me, worshipping your gods; nor will you do in the future what I ask of you, worshipping my God. Then He says, ‘Nor am I a worshipper of what you worshipped’: I am not at present worshipping your objects of worship, nor are you at present worshipping my object of worship. “Second: reverse this, making the first pair concern the present and the second the future. This is the position of al-Zajjāj, al-Wāḥidī, and Muḥyī al-Sunna. Al-Wāḥidī says: ‘Mā is used instead of man to correspond to “what you worship,” making the second expression conform to the first.’ Al-Zajjāj and Muḥyī al-Sunna say: ‘This addresses those whom God already knew would not believe.’ “Third: Abū Muslim's position. The first two statements concern the object of worship, and mā (ما) means ‘that which’: I do not worship idols, and you do not worship God. In the latter two, mā (ما) forms a verbal noun: I do not worship with a worship like yours, founded on doubt; nor do you worship with a worship like mine, founded on certainty. “Fourth: understand the first pair as denying worship on the particular consideration they proposed, and the second as a general denial in every respect. I do not worship what you worship in the hope that you will worship God, nor do you worship in the hope that I will worship your idol. He then says: I will not worship your idol for any purpose whatsoever, in any manner whatsoever; similarly, you do not worship God for any purpose. It is like someone inviting another to commit injustice to obtain pleasure, who replies: ‘I do not commit injustice for pleasure; indeed, I do not commit injustice at all, whether for pleasure or anything else.’”

وقلتُ: هذا الوجهُ هو الذي اخترناه لطباقِه المقام، ثُمّ المختارُ الوجهُ الرابعُ من القولِ الأول.

وثانيهما: أنهم ذكروا تلك الكلمةَ مرتين، يعني: تعبدُ آلهتَنا شهرًا ونعبدُ إلهك شهرًا، وتعبد [آلهتَنا] سنةً ونعبدُ إلهك سنة، فأتى الجوابُ على التكرار على وفِق قولِهم، وفيه ضَربُ من التهكُّم؛ فإنّ مَن كرّرَ الكلمةَ الواحدةَ لغرضٍ فاسد، فإنه يُجازى لدفعِ تلك الكلمةِ على سبيلِ التكرارِ استخفافًا». نقل هذا الوجهَ محيي السُّنةِ عن القُتَيْبي، أخصر منه.

Two explanations accepting repetition; Ṭībī's preference (611)

“The second position accepts that repetition occurs, for two reasons. First, repetition conveys emphasis, and the greater the need for emphasis, the more fitting the repetition. No situation needs emphasis more than this one: they returned to him repeatedly with this proposal and entertained hopes because they saw his eagerness for their faith.” Muḥyī al-Sunna says: “Most scholars say that the Qurʾān was revealed in the Arabs' language and according to their ways of addressing one another. Their usages include repetition for emphasis and explanation, just as they include abbreviation for lightness and concision.” I say: this is the explanation we have chosen because it fits the situation. Next in preference is the fourth explanation under the first position. “Second: they stated the proposal twice: you worship our gods for a month and we will worship your God for a month; you worship our gods for a year and we will worship your God for a year. The response therefore repeats itself in conformity with their speech. There is a kind of mockery in this: someone who repeats the same statement for a corrupt purpose is answered with repeated rejection of that statement, in disdain.” Muḥyī al-Sunna transmits this explanation more briefly from al-Qutaybī.

al-Kāfirūn · “To you your religion” and the closing report

K IV:808–809 · T XVI:606–612

Q 109:6

6 Unto you your religion, and unto me my religion

Verse translation: Marmaduke Pickthall (1930).

Zamakhsharī · الكشافK109.04

لكم دينكم ولي دين لكم شرككم، ولى توحيدي. والمعنى: أنى نبى مبعوث إليكم لأدعوكم إلى الحق والنجاة، فإذا لم تقبلوا منى ولم تتبعوني، فدعوني كفافا ولا تدعوني إلى الشرك. عن رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم: «من قرأ سورة الكافرين فكأنما قرأ ربع القرآن وتباعدت منه مردة الشياطين، وبريء من الشرك ويعافى من الفزع الأكبر».

“To you your religion” and the closing report

“To you your religion, and to me my religion”: to you your association of partners with God, and to me my affirmation of His oneness. The meaning is: I am a prophet sent to you to call you to truth and salvation. If you will not accept what I bring or follow me, leave me alone without injury and do not invite me to associate partners with God. God's Messenger is reported to have said: “Whoever recites Sūrat al-Kāfirūn, it is as though he recited a quarter of the Qurʾān. The rebellious devils withdraw far from him, he is cleared of associating partners with God, and he is spared the greatest terror.”

قولُه: (فَدَعوني كَفافًا)، النهاية: «الكَفافُ هو الذي لا يفضُلُ عن الشئ، ويكونُ بقَدرِ الحاجةِ إليه، وهو نصبٌ على الحال. وقيل: أرادَ به مكفوفًا عنِّي شَرُّهم. وقيل: أن لا تنالوا منّي ولا أنا أنالُ منكم، أي: تكفّونَ عني وأكفُّ عنكم». فإذن، في قولِه ﴿لَكُمْ دِينُكُمْ وَلِيَ دِينِ﴾ معنى المُتاركةِ وتقريرُ كلِّ من الفريقين الآخرَ على دينه، فيكون منسوخًا بآيةِ القتال.

وقال القاضي: «ولي ديني الذي أنا عليه لا أرفضُه، فليس فيه إذنٌ في الكفرِ ولا مَنعٌ عن الجهاد، فلا يكونُ منسوخًا». وقد فُسِّرَ "الدِّينُ" بالحسابِ والجزاءِ والدعاءِ والعبادة.

Kafāf; the disagreement over abrogation (611–612)

On “leave me alone without injury” (fa-daʿūnī kafāfan (فدعوني كفافًا)): al-Nihāya says: “Kafāf is what does not exceed a thing and is proportionate to the need for it. It is accusative as a circumstantial expression. It is also said that the meaning is: with their harm held back from me. Or: you do not injure me, and I do not injure you—you refrain from me, and I refrain from you.” On this understanding, “To you your religion, and to me my religion” conveys mutual disengagement, each party allowing the other to remain upon its religion; it would then be abrogated by the verse commanding fighting. The Qāḍī says: “‘To me my religion’ means the religion I follow and will not abandon. It neither grants permission for unbelief nor prohibits fighting, and therefore is not abrogated.” Dīn has also been explained as reckoning, recompense, supplication, and worship.

On “it is as though he recited a quarter of the Qurʾān”: we have transmitted from al-Tirmidhī, from Ibn ʿAbbās and Anas, that God's Messenger said: “Whoever recites ‘Say: O unbelievers,’ it is equivalent for him to a quarter of the Qurʾān.” The sūrah is complete.
