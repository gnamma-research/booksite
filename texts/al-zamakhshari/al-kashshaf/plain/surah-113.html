الفلق والفرق: الصبح، لأن الليل يفلق عنه ويفرق: فعل بمعنى مفعول. يقال في المثل:

هو أبين من فلق الصبح، ومن فرق الصبح. ومنه قولهم: سطع الفرقان، إذا طلع الفجر.

وقيل: هو كل ما يفلقه الله، كالأرض عن النبات، والجبال عن العيون، والسحاب عن المطر، والأرحام عن الأولاد، والحب والنوى وغير ذلك. وقيل: هو واد في جهنم أوجب فيها من قولهم لما اطمأن من الأرض: الفلق. والجمع: فلقان. وعن بعض الصحابة أنه قدم الشأم فرأى دور أهل الذمة وما هم فيه من خفض العيش وما وسع عليهم من دنياهم، فقال: لا أبالى، أليس من ورائهم الفلق؟ فقيل: وما الفلق؟ قال: بيت في جهنم إذا فتح صاح جميع أهل النار من شدة حره

Text and explanation

Al-falaq (الفلق) and al-faraq (الفرق) mean morning, because night is split and parted away from it: faʿal (فَعَل) has the meaning of mafʿūl (مفعول). The proverb says, “It is clearer than the breaking of morning,” or “than the parting of morning.” Related to this is their expression saṭaʿa al-furqān (سطع الفرقان) when dawn rises. It is also said to mean everything God splits open: the earth for plants, mountains for springs, clouds for rain, wombs for children, grain and date-stones, and other things. It is also said to be a valley in Hell or a pit within it, from their use of falaq (فلق) for a depression in the ground; the plural is fulqān (فلقان). It is reported that one of the Companions came to Syria and saw the houses of the protected non-Muslim communities (ahl al-dhimma), their comfortable lives and the worldly plenty granted them. He said, “I do not mind. Is al-falaq (الفلق) not awaiting them?” He was asked, “What is al-falaq (الفلق)?” He answered: “A chamber in Hell which, when opened, makes all the inhabitants of the Fire cry out at the intensity of its heat.”

“Because night is split away from it”

Arabic lemma: لأن الليل يفلق عنه

Sūrat al-Falaq (الفلق). Meccan; also said to be Medinan. Five verses. In the name of God, the All-Merciful, the Merciful. His words, “Because night is split away from it”: that is, night splits away from morning and morning emerges. Faʿal (فَعَل) has the meaning of mafʿūl (مفعول): night is what is split away from it. His words, “It is also said to mean everything He splits open.” Al-Qāḍī says: “It includes all possible beings, for He split the darkness of nonexistence away from them with the light of bringing into existence—particularly what emerges from an origin, such as springs, rains, plants and children. In conventional use it is especially morning, which is why it was explained that way. Morning is singled out because it changes the condition, replaces the loneliness of night with the joy of light, evokes good through the Day of Resurrection, and signals that the one able to remove the darkness of night from this world can remove from the person seeking refuge what he fears. The word Lord is more fitting here than the other names, because protection from harms is an act of nurture.” [REVIEW T113-U1: The scanned quotation at p.645 has difficult wording محاكاة الخير بيوم القيامة and a final word read provisionally as تربية. “I do not mind. Is al-falaq not awaiting them?”

Arabic lemma: لا أبالي، أليس من ورائهم الفلق؟

His words, “I do not mind. Is al-falaq (الفلق) not awaiting them?”: that is, I do not mind the beauty of their houses and the ease of their lives. He then begins a new interrogative sentence to elicit assent: “Is al-falaq (الفلق) not awaiting them?” Comparable is what we transmitted from al-Bukhārī, Muslim, Aḥmad, al-Tirmidhī and al-Nasāʾī, from Ibn ʿAbbās in a long report from ʿUmar—may God be pleased with him: “I entered upon the Messenger of God ﷺ and greeted him. He was reclining upon the woven strands of a mat which had left marks upon his side. I sat down and lifted my head to look about the house. By God, I saw nothing there to catch the eye except three hides. I said: ‘Messenger of God, pray that God grant plenty to your community: He has granted plenty to Persia and Rome, although they do not worship God.’ He sat upright and said, ‘Are you in doubt, son of al-Khaṭṭāb? These are people whose good things have been given them in advance in this worldly life.’ I said: ‘Ask forgiveness for me, Messenger of God.’” The report [continues]. As for interpreting al-falaq (الفلق) as a valley in Hell, Muḥyī al-Sunna transmits one report from Ibn ʿAbbās that al-falaq (الفلق) is a prison in Hell, and from al-Kalbī that it is a valley in Hell. من شر ما خلق من شر خلقه. وشرهم : ما يفعله المكلفون من الحيوان من المعاصي والمآثم، ومضارة بعضهم بعضا من ظلم وبغى وقتل وضرب وشتم وغير ذلك، وما يفعله غير المكلفين منه عن الأكل والنهس واللدغ والعض كالسباع والحشرات، وما وضعه الله في الموات من أنواع الضرر كالإحراق في النار والقتل في السم. و

Text and explanation

“From the evil of what He created”: from the evil of His creation. Their evil consists in the disobedience and sins committed by morally responsible living beings, and the harm they do one another through injustice, aggression, killing, striking, abuse and so forth; in what the non-responsible among living beings do by devouring, tearing with the teeth, stinging and biting, such as predatory animals and creeping creatures; and in the kinds of harm God has placed in inanimate things, such as burning in fire and killing in poison.

“Their evil is what morally responsible living beings do”

Arabic lemma: وشرهم ما يفعله المكلفون من الحيوان

His words, “Their evil is what morally responsible living beings do.” Perhaps “among living beings” is supplied as an explanation of “the morally responsible” in order to exclude the angels. Al-Qāḍī says: “The world of creation is singled out as that from which refuge is sought because evil is confined to it: the world of command is wholly good. Its evil is either voluntary—confined to its agent or extending to others, as in unbelief and injustice—or natural, as in fire's burning and poisons' destruction.”

Text and explanation

Al-ghāsiq (الغاسق) is night when its darkness becomes dense, from His statement “… until the darkness of night” [17:78]. Related expressions are “the eye ghasaqat”—filled with tears—and “the wound ghasaqat”—filled with blood. Its wuqūb (وقوب) is the entry of its darkness into everything. One says waqabat al-shams when the sun sets. In the report, when he saw that the sun had set, he said, “This is the time when it becomes lawful”—meaning the sunset prayer. It is also said to be the moon when full. ʿĀʾisha—may God be pleased with her—reports: the Messenger of God ﷺ took my hand, pointed to the moon, and said, “Seek refuge in God from the evil of this, for it is the darkening one when it enters.” Its wuqūb (وقوب) is its entering eclipse and becoming dark. Al-ghāsiq (الغاسق) may also mean a black snake; its waqb is its striking and piercing. Waqb means a hole, as in waqbat al-tharīd (الثريد), the hollow in a dish of bread soaked in broth. Refuge is sought from the evil of night because evil spreads more widely in it and is harder to guard against. Hence their sayings, “Night conceals misfortune better,” and “Night has become treacherous.” When it grows dark, treachery increases. Evil is attributed to night because it accompanies it, occurring within it. His words, “When its darkness becomes dense.” Al-Jawharī: “Iʿtakara al-ẓalām (اعتكر الظلام): it became intermingled, as though part of it returned upon another because of the slowness of its clearing.” His words, “One says waqabat al-shams when it sets.” Al-Rāghib: “Waqb is like a hollow in something; hence waqabat al-shams. His words, “From ʿĀʾisha—may God be pleased with her.” The report was transmitted by Imam Aḥmad and al-Tirmidhī; it does not contain “he took my hand.” The Imam transmits from Ibn Qutayba: “The moon was called ghāsiq (غاسق) because it is eclipsed and darkens: its light departs and it becomes black. Text and explanation

Al-naffāthāt (النفّاثات) are women, souls, or groups of female sorcerers who tie knots in threads, blow upon them and recite incantations. Nafth is blowing with saliva. This has no effect—unless, indeed, something harmful is administered as food, drink or something to smell, or the bewitched person is brought into contact with it in some way. But God—mighty and majestic—may perform an act at that moment as a test by which those steadfast upon truth are distinguished from the Ḥashwiyya and the ignorant common people. The Ḥashwiyya and the rabble attribute it to the women and their blowing; those steadfast in the firm word pay it no attention and attach no weight to it. If you ask, “What, then, does seeking refuge from their evil mean?”, I answer: there are three interpretations. First, refuge is sought from their activity, the practice of sorcery, and their sin in doing it. Second, refuge is sought from their leading people astray through their sorcery and deceiving them by their false practices. Third, refuge is sought from whatever evil God causes to befall someone at the moment of their blowing. [TEXT CORRECTION K113-C2: Both electronic Arabic texts have النفخ من ريق. “By which those steadfast upon truth are distinguished from the Ḥashwiyya”

Arabic lemma: يتميز به الثبت على الحق من الحشوية

One day, while with me, he prayed to God repeatedly, then said: ‘Have you realized, ʿĀʾisha, that God has given me an answer concerning what I asked Him?’ I said, ‘What is that, Messenger of God?’ He said: ‘Two men came to me. الراغب: «تأثير السحر في النبي -صلى الله عليه وسلم-، لم يكن من حيث إنه نبي، وإنما كان في بدنه من حيث إنه إنسان أو بشر، كما كان يأكل ويتغوط ويغضب ويشتهي ويمرض، فيصح من حيث هو نبي، وإنما يكون ذلك قادحا في النبوة. أو وجد للسحر تأثير في أمر يرجع إلى النبوة،

Commentary

Arabic lemma: والله يعصمك من الناس

Al-Rāghib: “The effect of sorcery upon the Prophet ﷺ did not concern him inasmuch as he was a prophet; it concerned his body inasmuch as he was a human being or mortal, just as he ate, excreted, became angry, desired and fell ill, while remaining sound in his prophetic capacity. It would impair prophethood only [if] sorcery were found to affect something pertaining to prophethood. Just so, his being wounded and having his front teeth broken on the day of Uḥud did not impair the protection God guaranteed him in ‘God will protect you from people’ [5:67]. Likewise, the polytheists' gaining control over certain regions within Islam is not taken as invalidating the perfection of Islam stated in ‘Today I have perfected your religion for you’ [5:3].” Al-Qāḍī says: “This does not make the unbelievers truthful in calling him bewitched, for they meant that he was mad through sorcery.” [REVIEW T113-U2: The scan p.648 reads فيصح من حيث نبي and then أو وجد للسحر تأثير. The first is provisionally read as preservation of prophetic soundness; “[if]” expresses the apparently intended conditional of the second, whose printed أو is problematic. Check al-Rāghib's source before removing the flag. At p.649 the scan has بما يقع, correcting OpenITI's بما لا يقع, which reverses the argument.]

“The rabble”

Arabic lemma: الرعاع

Al-Nihāya: “He was bewitched with a comb and combings”: these are the hairs that fall from the head and beard when combed. It is also transmitted as mushāqa (مشاقة): “what breaks off from silk and flax when cleaned and combed. Mashq is pulling something to lengthen it.” “Juff is the casing of the spathe, the covering over it.” His words, “The rabble”: immature youths and the base multitude. His words, “Scheming women”: he likens their scheming to sorcery. The author of al-Intiṣāf abridged it and then said: “Had anyone other than al-Zamakhsharī offered this interpretation, it would have been counted among the innovations of exegesis.”

al-Falaq · They may also mean scheming women, from His statement “Your scheming is indeed great” [12:28], liken

4 And from the evil of malignant witchcraft

5 And from the evil of the envier when he envieth

English verse translation: Marmaduke Pickthall (1930).

Zamakhsharī · الكشافK113.06

ويجوز أن يراد

بهن النساء الكيادات، من قوله إن كيدكن عظيم تشبيها لكيدهن بالسحر والنفث في العقد.

أو اللاتي يفتن الرجال بتعرضهن لهم وعرضهن محاسنهن، كأنهن يسحرنهم بذلك إذا حسد إذا ظهر حسده وعمل بمقتضاه: من بغى الغوائل للمحسود، لأنه إذا لم يظهر أثر ما أضمره فلا ضرر يعود منه على من حسده، بل هو الضار لنفسه لاغتمامه بسرور غيره. وعن عمر بن عبد العزيز: لم أر ظالما أشبه بالمظلوم من حاسد. ويجوز أن يراد بشر الحاسد: إثمه وسماجة حاله في وقت حسده، وإظهاره أثره.

Text and explanation

They may also mean scheming women, from His statement “Your scheming is indeed great” [12:28], likening their scheming to sorcery and blowing on knots; or women who tempt men by presenting themselves to them and displaying their attractions, as though bewitching them through that. “When he envies”: when his envy becomes manifest and he acts as it dictates, seeking misfortunes for the person envied. If he does not manifest the effect of what he conceals, no harm from him reaches the person he envies. Rather, he harms himself by grieving at another's happiness. ʿUmar ibn ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz is reported to have said: “I have never seen a wrongdoer who more resembles someone wronged than an envier.” The envier's evil may also mean his sin and the ugliness of his condition at the time of his envy and his displaying its effect.

al-Falaq · If you ask, “‘From the evil of what He created’ includes everything from which refuge is sought

Zamakhsharī · الكشافK113.07

فإن قلت: قوله من شر ما خلق تعميم في كل ما يستعاذ منه، فما معنى الاستعاذة بعده من الغاسق والنفاثات والحاسد؟ قلت: قد خص شر هؤلاء من كل شر لخفاء أمره، وأنه يلحق الإنسان من حيث لا يعلم، كأنما يغتال به. وقالوا: شر العداة المداجى الذي يكيدك من حيث لا تشعر. فإن قلت: فلم عرف بعض المستعاذ منه ونكر بعضه؟ قلت: عرفت النفاثات، لأن كل نفاثة شريرة، ونكر غاسق، لأن كل غاسق لا يكون فيه الشر، إنما يكون في بعض دون بعض، وكذلك كل حاسد لا يضر. ورب حسد محمود، وهو الحسد في الخيرات. ومنه قوله عليه الصلاة والسلام: «لا حسد إلا في اثنتين » وقال أبو تمام:

وما حاسد في المكرمات بحاسد

وقال:

إن العلا حسن في مثلها الحسد

عن رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم: «من قرأ المعوذتين فكأنما قرأ الكتب التي أنزلها الله تعالى كلها» .

Text and explanation

If you ask, “‘From the evil of what He created’ includes everything from which refuge is sought. What is the point, then, of subsequently seeking refuge from the darkening one, the women who blow and the envier?”, I answer: their evil is singled out from all evil because its operation is hidden and reaches a person from where he does not know, as though he were treacherously struck by it. They say: “The worst enemy is the one who conceals his hostility and plots against you without your perceiving it.” If you ask, “Why are some of those from whom refuge is sought made definite and others indefinite?”, I answer: al-naffāthāt (النفّاثات) is made definite because every such blower is evil. Ghāsiq (غاسق) is indefinite because evil is not present in every darkening night, but in some and not others. Likewise, not every envier causes harm. There is even praiseworthy envy: envy in good things. Hence his statement—blessings and peace be upon him—“There is no envy except in two cases.” Abū Tammām said: “One who envies noble accomplishments is no [blameworthy] envier.” He also said: “Loftiness is such that envy of its like is fair.” It is transmitted from the Messenger of God ﷺ: “Whoever recites the two sūrahs of refuge (al-Muʿawwidhatān) is as though he had recited all the books God Most High has sent down.” [TEXT VARIANT K113-C3: Dubai p.650 omits شر العداة, “the worst enemy,” before المداجي. It is present in the independent four-volume transcription and the Turkish Arabic/Turkish passage; it is included here and the difference recorded.]

“As though he were treacherously struck by it”

Arabic lemma: كأنما يغتال به

His words, “As though he were treacherously struck by it.” Al-Asās: “So-and-so treacherously attacks whoever passes him; he killed him by stealth; I fear his ghāʾila (غائلة), that is, the eventual consequence of his evil.” His words, “There is no envy except in two cases.” We transmitted from al-Bukhārī, from Abū Hurayra, that the Messenger of God ﷺ said: “There is no envy except concerning two: a man to whom God has given the Qurʾān, who recites it at times throughout night and day. His neighbour hears him and says, ‘Would that I had been given what this man was given, so that I might do as he does!’ And a man to whom God has given wealth, who spends it as it should be spent. Someone says, ‘Would that I had been given what this man was given, so that I might do as he does!’” Al-Nihāya: “Ḥasad is that a man sees a blessing enjoyed by his brother and wishes it would leave him and become his own instead. Ghabṭ (غبط) is to wish to have its like without wishing it to leave him. The report means: there is no harmless envy except in two cases.”
