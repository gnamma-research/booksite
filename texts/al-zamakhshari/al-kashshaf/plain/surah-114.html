1 Say: I seek refuge in the Lord of mankind

English verse translation: Marmaduke Pickthall (1930).

Zamakhsharī · الكشافK114.02

قرئ: قل أعوذ، بحذف الهمزة ونقل حركتها إلى اللام، ونحوه. فخذ أربعة. فإن قلت: لم قيل برب الناس مضافا إليهم خاصة؟ قلت: لأن الاستعاذة وقعت من شر الموسوس في صدور الناس. فكأنه قيل، أعوذ من شر الموسوس إلى الناس بربهم الذي يملك عليهم أمورهم، وهو إلههم ومعبودهم، كما يستغيث بعض الموالي إذا اعتراهم خطب بسيدهم ومخدومهم ووالى أمرهم.

Text and explanation

“Qul aʿūdhu (قل أعوذ)” has been read with the hamza (همزة (ء)) omitted and its vowel transferred to the lām (لام (ل)). Comparable is “fa-khudh arbaʿata (أربعة)” [2:260]. If you ask, “Why say ‘in the Lord of humankind,’ annexing Lord specifically to them?”, I answer: because refuge is sought from the evil of the one who whispers in human breasts. It is as though one had said: I seek refuge from the evil of the one who whispers to people, in their Lord who controls their affairs, who is their God and the one they worship—just as some dependants, when a calamity befalls them, seek help from their master, the one they serve, and the governor of their affairs.

“Why say ‘in the Lord of humankind’?”

Arabic lemma: لم قيل: {برب الناس}

Sūrat al-Nās (الناس). Meccan; also said to be Medinan. Six verses. In the name of God, the All-Merciful, the Merciful. His words, “Why say ‘in the Lord of humankind’?”: that is, He is the Lord of all worlds, so why single out humankind here? He answers: it is humankind alone who seek help from their Lord, owner and object of worship against the affliction that befalls them.

وفي بعض التفاسير: إن دفع شر الشيطان ووسوسته بأحد أمور ثلاثة، إما بأن لا يمكنه من الوسوسة من حيث كونه ربا، أو بأن يمكنه، لكن يمنعه قهرا من حيث المالكية، أو بأن ينهاه عن الوسوسة زجرا، لكن يريدها اختيارا من حيث كونه إلها، أو يقال: إن العبد استعاذ بالله من الشيطان. وعلل الاستعاذة بأوصاف مناسبة على الترقي: وصفه عز وجل أولا بأنه الرب، لأن أول ما يعرف العبد من ربه، كونه منعما عليه ظاهره وباطنه، ثم ينتقل منه إلى المعرفة بأنه متصرف فيه ومالكه، ثم ينتقل إلى المعرفة بأنه هو المعبود على الإطلاق، وأن لا مصير إلا إليه.

“Just as some dependants, when a calamity befalls them, seek help from their master, the one they serve, and the governor of their affairs”

Arabic lemma: كما يستغيث بعض الموالي إذا اعتراهم خطب بسيدهم ومخدومهم ووالي أمرهم

His words, “Just as some dependants, when a calamity befalls them, seek help from their master, the one they serve, and the governor of their affairs.” He observes an ascent in the help sought: protection through governance is stronger than through being served, while protection through mastery is weaker than through being served. Likewise, the sense of irresistible dominion in divinity is higher than in ownership, and [that is higher] than in lordship. Some commentaries say: “Repelling Satan's evil and whispering is through one of three things: either not enabling him to whisper, inasmuch as He is Lord; or enabling him but forcibly preventing him, inasmuch as He is King; or forbidding him to whisper as a rebuke, yet willing it by choice, inasmuch as He is God.” Alternatively, it may be said: the servant seeks refuge in God from Satan, grounding the request in appropriate attributes in ascending order. He first describes Him—mighty and majestic—as Lord, because the first thing the servant knows of his Lord is that He bestows blessings upon his outward and inward being. He then advances to knowing that He governs him and owns him, and then to knowing that He alone is the object of worship without qualification and that there is no final resort except to Him. [REVIEW T114-U2: The difficult third alternative reads لكن يريدها اختيارا. “Yet willing it by choice” preserves the wording; the subject and the intended theological distinction between prohibition and willing need checking in the unnamed source. Do not replace it with “the devil chooses it” without evidence.]

al-Nās · If you ask, “What is the relation of ‘King of humankind, God of humankind’ to ‘Lord of humankind’?”,

K IV:823–825 · T XVI:652–656

Q 114:1–3

بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ ٱلرَّحْمَـٰنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ قُلْ أَعُوذُ بِرَبِّ ٱلنَّاسِ ﴿١﴾ مَلِكِ ٱلنَّاسِ ﴿٢﴾ إِلَـٰهِ ٱلنَّاسِ ﴿٣﴾

1 Say: I seek refuge in the Lord of mankind

2 The King of mankind

3 The god of mankind

English verse translation: Marmaduke Pickthall (1930).

Zamakhsharī · الكشافK114.03

فإن قلت: ملك الناس إله الناس ما هما من رب الناس؟ قلت: هما عطف بيان، كقولك:

سيرة أبى حفص عمر الفاروق. بين بملك الناس، ثم زيد بيانا بإله الناس، لأنه قد يقال لغيره:

رب الناس، كقوله اتخذوا أحبارهم ورهبانهم أربابا من دون الله وقد يقال: ملك الناس. وأما إله الناس فخاص لا شركة فيه، فجعل غاية للبيان. فإن قلت: فهلا اكتفى بإظهار المضاف إليه الذي هو الناس مرة واحدة؟ قلت: لأن عطف البيان للبيان، فكان مظنة للإظهار دون الإضمار

Text and explanation

If you ask, “What is the relation of ‘King of humankind, God of humankind’ to ‘Lord of humankind’?”, I answer: both are explanatory apposition (ʿaṭf bayān (عطف بيان)), as in “the conduct of Abū Ḥafṣ, ʿUmar, al-Fārūq.” He is clarified by “King of humankind,” then further clarified by “God of humankind.” For someone other than Him may be called “lord of humankind,” as in “They took their rabbis and monks as lords apart from God” [9:31]; someone may also be called “king of humankind.” But “God of humankind” is exclusive, admitting no partnership, and is therefore made the culmination of the clarification. If you ask, “Why not be content with explicitly mentioning the annexed noun ‘humankind’ once?”, I answer: because explanatory apposition serves clarification, making it a suitable place for explicit mention rather than substitution by a pronoun.

Ṭībī · فتوح الغيبT114.03

قوله: (وقد يقال: ملك الناس)، الراغب: «الملك: هو المتصرف بالأمر والنهي في الجمهور، وذلك مختص بسياسة الناطقين؛ ولذلك يقال: ملك الناس، ولا يقال: ملك الأشياء».

“Someone may also be called king of humankind”

Arabic lemma: وقد يقال: ملك الناس

His words, “Someone may also be called king of humankind.” Al-Rāghib: “A king is one who directs the populace through command and prohibition. This is peculiar to governing rational beings; hence ‘king of humankind’ is said, but not ‘king of things.’”

al-Nās · Al-waswās is a noun meaning whispering (waswasa), as zalzāl means shaking (zalzala)

K IV:823–825 · T XVI:652–656

Q 114:4–5

مِن شَرِّ ٱلْوَسْوَاسِ ٱلْخَنَّاسِ ﴿٤﴾ ٱلَّذِى يُوَسْوِسُ فِى صُدُورِ ٱلنَّاسِ ﴿٥﴾

4 From the evil of the sneaking whisperer

5 Who whispereth in the hearts of mankind

English verse translation: Marmaduke Pickthall (1930).

Zamakhsharī · الكشافK114.04

الوسواس اسم بمعنى الوسوسة، كالزلزال بمعنى الزلزلة. وأما المصدر فوسواس بالكسر كزلزال. والمراد به الشيطان، سمى بالمصدر كأنه وسوسة في نفسه، لأنها صنعته وشغله الذي هو عاكف عليه. أو أريد ذو الوسواس. والوسوسة: الصوت الخفي. ومنه: وسواس الحلي.

والخناس الذي عادته أن يخنس، منسوب إلى الخنوس وهو التأخر كالعواج والبتات لما روى عن سعيد بن جبير: إذا ذكر الإنسان ربه خنس الشيطان وولى، فإذا غفل وسوس إليه الذي يوسوس يجوز في محله الحركات الثلاث، فالجر على الصفة، والرفع والنصب على الشتم، ويحسن أن يقف القارئ على الخناس ويبتدئ الذي يوسوس على أحد هذين الوجهين

Text and explanation

Al-waswās (الوسواس) is a noun meaning whispering (waswasa (وسوسة)), as zalzāl (زَلْزال) means shaking (zalzala (زلزلة)). The verbal noun, however, is wiswās (وِسْواس), with kasra (كَسْرَة (ـِ)), like zilzāl (زِلْزال). What is meant is Satan: he is named with the verbal noun as though he were whispering itself, since that is his craft and the occupation to which he constantly devotes himself. Alternatively, “the possessor of whispering” is intended. Waswasa (وسوسة) is a faint sound; hence waswās (وَسْواس) al-ḥuly, the rustling of jewellery. Al-khannās (الخنّاس) is one whose habit is to withdraw. It is a form associating him with khunūs (خنوس), retreating, like al-ʿawwāj (العوّاج) and al-battāt (البتّات) [occupational forms]. For it is related from Saʿīd ibn Jubayr that when a person remembers his Lord, Satan withdraws and turns away; when he is heedless, Satan whispers to him. “Alladhī (الذي) yuwaswisu” [who whispers] may occupy a position corresponding to any of the three case vowels: genitive as a description, or nominative or accusative as an expression of invective. It is fitting for the reciter to stop at al-khannās (الخنّاس) and begin with alladhī (الذي) yuwaswisu on either of these two interpretations [nominative or accusative]. [REVIEW K114-U1: “These two interpretations” naturally refers to nominative/accusative in the base passage. Ṭībī explicitly glosses it as “description and invective” below; the Turkish translation follows that gloss. The disagreement is retained rather than silently resolved.]

Ṭībī · فتوح الغيبT114.04

قوله: (وأما المصدر فوسواس)، عن بعضهم: أراد بالوسواس الاسم الذي هو بمعنى الوسوسة وهو المصدر. وقال المغاربة: الفرق بين المصدر واسم المصدر هو أن المعنى الذي يعبر

عنه بالفعل الحقيقي، الذي هو مبتدأ الفعل الصناعي، إذا اعتبر فيه تلبس الفاعل به وصدوره منه وتجدده؛ فاللفظ الموضوع بإزائه مقيدا بهذا القيد، سمي مصدرا وإن لم يعتبر فيه ذلك، فاللفظ الموضوع بإزاء ذلك مطلقا عن هذا القيد المذكور، هو اسم المصدر.

قوله: (صنعته)، ويروى: ضيعته. النهاية: «ضيعة الرجل: ما يكون منه معاشه كالصنعة والتجارة والصناعة وغير ذلك».

قوله: (منسوب إلى الخنوس)، قال: منسوب من حيث إنه جعل الخنوس عادة له.

قوله: (إذا ذكر الإنسان ربه خنس)، روينا في "صحيح البخاري" تعليقا عن ابن عباس قال: قال رسول الله -صلى الله عليه وسلم-: «الشيطان جاثم على قلب ابن آدم؛ فإذا ذكر الله خنس، وإذا غفل وسوس».

“The verbal noun, however, is wiswās”

Arabic lemma: وأما المصدر فوسواس

His words, “The verbal noun, however, is wiswās (وِسْواس).” According to one explanation, by waswās (وَسْواس) he meant the noun which has the meaning of waswasa (وسوسة), itself the verbal noun. The Maghribī grammarians say: the difference between a verbal noun (maṣdar (مصدر)) and a noun of the verbal action (ism (اسم) al-maṣdar (المصدر)) is this. The meaning expressed by the real act—the origin of the grammatical verb—may be considered together with the agent's engagement in it, its proceeding from him, and its renewed occurrence. The expression assigned to that meaning, restricted by this qualification, is called a maṣdar (مصدر). If that qualification is not considered, the expression assigned to the meaning without the stated restriction is an ism (اسم) al-maṣdar (المصدر). His words, “His craft”: another reading is “his livelihood” (ḍayʿatuhu (ضيعته)). Al-Nihāya: “A man's ḍayʿa (ضيعة) is that from which he earns his living, such as a craft, trade, an industry and so on.” His words, “Associated with khunūs (خنوس)”: he says “associated” because withdrawing has been made his habit. His words, “When a person remembers his Lord, he withdraws.” We transmitted in Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī, as a suspended report (taʿlīqan (تعليقًا)), from Ibn ʿAbbās: the Messenger of God ﷺ said, “Satan crouches upon the heart of the son of Adam. When he remembers God, he withdraws; when he is heedless, he whispers.”

لخلو المجرور، أعنى: "مالك يوم الدين"، من العامل، والفصل بين النعت والمنعوت، وكذا الوقف على "المستقيم" جائز وليس بحسن، وإنما جوز لأنه آخر الآية».

Commentary

Arabic lemma: ويحسن أن يقف القارئ

His words from “It is fitting for the reciter to stop” to “on either of these two interpretations”: that is, description and invective. In al-Kawāshī: “A stop at al-khannās (الخنّاس) is sufficient if you make [what follows] nominative or accusative in blame; it is not permissible if you make it genitive as a description of al-khannās (الخنّاس).” I say: its impermissibility is questionable, considering the verse ending. The author of al-Murshid says: “When you have said ‘the All-Merciful, the Merciful,’ the stop is sufficient because it is the end of a verse. It is not a complete stop, because the genitive expression—I mean ‘Master of the Day of Judgment’—is left without its governor, and the epithet is separated from what it qualifies. Likewise a stop at ‘the straight [path]’ is permissible but not a good stop; it is permitted only because it is the end of the verse.” [TEXT CORRECTION T114-C1: OpenITI reads وفي عدم الجواز نظرا للفاصلة. The scan p.654 reads وفي عدم الجواز نظر، نظرا للفاصلة: the missing نظر is restored in this translation. “Sufficient,” “complete” and “good” preserve distinct stop categories, not interchangeable praise.]

al-Nās · “From among jinn and humankind” explains “who whispers,” on the basis that Satan is of two kinds, ji

K IV:823–825 · T XVI:652–656

Q 114:6

مِنَ ٱلْجِنَّةِ وَٱلنَّاسِ ﴿٦﴾

6 Of the jinn and of mankind

English verse translation: Marmaduke Pickthall (1930).

Zamakhsharī · الكشافK114.05

من الجنة والناس بيان الذي يوسوس، على أن الشيطان ضربان: جنى وإنسى، كما قال شياطين الإنس والجن. وعن أبى ذر رضى الله عنه قال لرجل: هل تعوذت بالله من شيطان الإنس؟ ويجوز أن يكون من متعلقا بيوسوس، ومعناه: ابتداء الغاية، أى:

يوسوس في صدورهم من جهة الجن ومن جهة الناس، وقيل: من الجنة والناس بيان للناس، وأن اسم الناس ينطلق على الجنة، واستدلوا بنفر ورجال: في سورة الجن. وما أحقه، لأن الجن سموا «جنا» لاجتنانهم، والناس «ناسا» لظهورهم، من الإيناس وهو الإبصار، كما سموا بشرا، ولو كان يقع الناس على القبيلين، وصح ذلك وثبت: لم يكن مناسبا لفصاحة القرآن وبعده من التصنع.

Text and explanation

“From among jinn and humankind” explains “who whispers,” on the basis that Satan is of two kinds, jinn and human, as He says: “devils of humankind and jinn” [6:112]. Abū Dharr—may God be pleased with him—is reported to have asked a man: “Have you sought refuge in God from human Satan?” Min may also attach to yuwaswisu, denoting a starting point: that is, he whispers in their breasts from the direction of the jinn and from the direction of human beings. It is also said that “from among jinn and humankind” explains al-nās (الناس), and that the name al-nās (الناس) applies to jinn. They adduce nafar (نفر) [a group] and rijāl (رجال) [men] in Sūrat al-Jinn as evidence. I do not accept it as established: jinn are called jinn because they are concealed, and people nās (ناس) because they are manifest, from īnās (إيناس), seeing, just as they are called bashar. Even if nās (ناس) applied to both classes, and that were sound and established, it would not suit the Qurʾān's eloquence and its freedom from affectation.

“From the direction of human beings”

Arabic lemma: ومن جهة الناس

His words, “From the direction of human beings”: for example, he whispers into a Muslim's heart, concerning astrologers and soothsayers, that they know the unseen; and concerning jinn, that they harm and benefit. In al-Muṭṭalaʿ: “According to one explanation, if the phrase is taken as clarification, ‘from among jinn and humankind’ is a circumstantial expression (ḥāl) relating to the pronoun in ‘who whispers.’”

“I do not accept it as established”

Arabic lemma: وما أحقه

His words, “I do not accept it as established”: that is, I do not establish it, from their saying ḥaqqaqtu al-shayʾa aḥuqquhu (حقّقت الشيء أحقّه), “I established the thing.” The Imam says: “It is said that ‘from among jinn and humankind’ gives two divisions included under ‘in the breasts of al-nās (الناس).’ It is as though the common element shared by jinn and humans were called insān, while the human being were also called insān; the expression insān would thus apply equivocally to both genus and species. The evidence is the report that a group of jinn came and were asked, ‘Who are you?’ They answered, ‘Nās (ناس) from among the jinn.’ God has also called them rijāl (رجال) in ‘There were men of humankind who sought refuge with men of the jinn, and they increased them in distress’ [72:6]. Thus it would be permissible to call them nās (ناس) here. This strained statement—he does not mean that it is weak—for making insān the name of a genus containing both jinn and humans is remote from the language.” [REVIEW T114-U3: The final sentence is problematic in the print itself: وهذا القول المتعسف لا يريد أنه ضعيف. Its wording is retained, even though its denial of weakness conflicts with the following criticism. The phrase لا يريد may be displaced from the next gloss; that is a hypothesis, not an adopted correction. Check the Imam's source before repairing it. Mā (ما) in وما أحقه is negative, not an exclamation; the scan, Ṭībī's gloss and Turkish p.1480 agree.]

Zamakhsharī · الكشافK114.06

وأجود منه أن يراد بالناس: الناسي، كقوله يوم يدع الداع كما قرئ من حيث أفاض الناس ثم يبين بالجنة والناس، لأن الثقلين هما النوعان الموصوفان بنسيان حق الله عز وجل.

عن رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم «لقد أنزلت على سورتان ما أنزل مثلهما، وإنك لن تقرأ سورتين أحب ولا أرضى عند الله منهما » يعنى المعوذتين. ويقال للمعوذتين: المقشقشتان.

Text and explanation

Better than that is to understand al-nās (الناس) as al-nāsī (الناسي) [the forgetful one], like “the day the caller calls” [54:6], and as “from where al-nās (الناس) departed” [2:199] has been read. It is then explained by “jinn and humankind,” since the two weighty kinds (al-thaqalān (الثقلان)) are the two species characterized by forgetting God's due—mighty and majestic is He. It is related from the Messenger of God ﷺ: “Two sūrahs have been sent down to me the like of which has never been sent down. You will never recite two sūrahs more beloved or more pleasing to God than these”—meaning the two sūrahs of refuge. The two sūrahs of refuge are also called al-muqashqishatān (المقشقشتان). [TRANSLATOR'S NOTE: The phonological argument is the omission of final yāʾ (ياء (ي)) in al-nāsī (الناسي), parallel to al-dāʿī (الداعي)/al-dāʿ (الداع). No claim that the ordinary reading means “forgetful” is being added.]

“Better than that”

Arabic lemma: وأجود منه

His words, “Better than that”: that is, better than this strained statement; he does not mean that it is an interpretation possessing merit. It is to take al-nās (الناس) in “the breasts of al-nās (الناس)” as “the forgetful one.” It can then be divided into jinn and humans, for they are two classes characterized by forgetting God's due. [TEXT NOTE: The scan/transcription has صفتان موصوفان, literally “two attributes described.” The contextual “two classes” follows the preceding discussion and needs verification against another witness.]

تمت السورة

“Al-muqashqishatān”

Arabic lemma: المقشقشتان

His words, “Al-muqashqishatān (المقشقشتان).” Al-Nihāya: “In the report, the sūrahs ‘Say: O unbelievers’ and ‘Say: He is God, One’ are called al-muqashqishatān (المقشقشتان): the two that free one from hypocrisy and association of partners with God, as a sick person is freed from his illness. One says taqashqasha al-marīḍ (المريض) when the sick person recovers and becomes well.” [TRANSLATOR'S NOTE: The base text applies the term to al-Falaq (الفلق) and al-Nās (الناس); this quotation applies it to al-Kāfirūn and al-Ikhlāṣ. The differing identifications are preserved.] The sūrah is complete.
