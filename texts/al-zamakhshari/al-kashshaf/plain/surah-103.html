Zamakhsharī · الكشافK103.02

أقسم بصلاة العصر لفضلها، بدليل قوله تعالى: والصلاة الوسطى صلاة العصر، في مصحف

حفصة. وقوله عليه الصلاة والسلام «من فاتته صلاة العصر فكأنما وتر أهله وماله» ولأن التكليف في أدائها أشق لتهافت الناس في تجاراتهم ومكاسبهم آخر النهار، واشتغالهم بمعايشهم.

أو أقسم بالعشي كما أقسم بالضحى لما فيهما جميعا من دلائل القدرة. أو أقسم بالزمان لما في مروره من أصناف العجائب. والإنسان: للجنس. والخسر: الخسران، كما قيل: الكفر في الكفران. والمعنى: أن الناس في خسران من تجارتهم إلا الصالحين وحدهم، لأنهم اشتروا الآخرة بالدنيا، فربحوا وسعدوا، ومن عداهم تجروا خلاف تجارتهم، فوقعوا في الخسارة والشقاوة وتواصوا بالحق بالأمر الثابت الذي لا يسوغ إنكاره، وهو الخير كله: من توحيد الله وطاعته، واتباع كتبه ورسله، والزهد في الدنيا، والرغبة في الآخرة وتواصوا بالصبر عن المعاصي وعلى الطاعات، وعلى ما يبلو الله به عباده.

The oath and the exception [IV:793–794]

He swears by the afternoon prayer because of its excellence. Evidence is His saying, “and the middle prayer—the afternoon prayer,” in Ḥafṣa’s codex [cf. 2:238], and the Prophet’s saying, “Whoever misses the afternoon prayer is as though he had been deprived of his family and property.” Moreover, the obligation to perform it is more demanding because people rush into their trading and earning toward the end of the day and are occupied with their livelihoods.

Or He swears by the late afternoon, just as He swears by the morning brightness, because both contain signs of divine power. Or He swears by time because of the many kinds of wonders in its passage.

“The human being” denotes the species. Khusr (خسر) means khusrān (خسران), loss, just as kufr is used for kufrān (كفران). The meaning is that people lose in their trading, except the righteous alone: they purchase the hereafter with this world, and so profit and attain felicity. Everyone else trades in the opposite way and falls into loss and wretchedness.

“And counsel one another to the truth”: to the established reality that cannot rightfully be denied. It encompasses all good: affirming God’s oneness and obeying Him, following His books and messengers, renouncing attachment to this world, and desiring the hereafter.

“And counsel one another to patience”: in refraining from acts of disobedience, performing acts of obedience, and bearing the trials with which God tests His servants.

Ṭībī · فتوح الغيبT103.01

سورة {والعصر}

مكية، ثلاث آيات

بسم الله الرحمن الرحيم

قوله: (فكأنما وتر أهله وماله)، النهاية: "وتر: أي نقص، يقال: وترته إذا نقصته، فكأنك جعلته وترا بعد أن كان كثيرا. وقيل: هو من الوتر: الجناية؛ فشبه من فاتته صلاة العصر بمن قتل حميمه، أو سلب أهله وماله. ويروى بنصب الأهل ورفعه فمن نصب جعله مفعولا ثانيا لوتر، وأضمر فيها مفعولا لم يسم فاعله عائدا إلى الذي فاتته الصلاة، ومن رفع لم يضمر وأقام الأهل مقام ما لم يسم فاعله، لأنهم المصابون المأخوذون؛ فمن رد النقص إلى الرجل نصبهما، ومن رده إلى الأهل والمال رفعهما".

“As though deprived of his family and property” [XVI:565]

Arabic lemma: فكأنما وتر أهله وماله

Meccan; three verses. In the name of God, the All-Merciful, the Merciful.

On “as though he had been deprived of his family and property,” al-Nihāya says: “Wutira (وُتِرَ) means diminished. You say watartuhu when you diminish someone, as though making him solitary after he had been many. It is also said to derive from watr, an injury: the person who misses the afternoon prayer is likened to someone whose intimate companion has been killed or whose family and property have been taken from him.

“The report is transmitted with ‘family’ both accusative and nominative. Those who put it in the accusative make it the second object of wutira (وُتِرَ), and understand within the verb an unexpressed passive subject referring to the person who missed the prayer. Those who put it in the nominative posit no such pronoun, but make ‘family’ the passive subject, since they are the ones struck by the loss and taken away. Whoever attributes the deprivation to the man puts both nouns in the accusative; whoever attributes it to family and property puts them in the nominative.”

Rushing, time, and mutual counsel [XVI:566]

Arabic lemma: لتهافت

On “because they rush” (li-tahāfut (لتهافت)): it means falling down piece by piece. Tahāfut al-farāsh fī l-nār (تهافت الفراش في النار) means the moths’ falling into the fire.

On “or He swears by time,” al-Zajjāj says: “Al-ʿaṣr (العصر) means time; al-ʿaṣr (العصر) also means day, and al-ʿaṣr (العصر) means night.” Ḥumayd ibn Thawr says: “The two times—day and night—do not delay, when they seek something, in overtaking what they aim at.”

On “‘and counsel one another to the truth’: to the established reality,” and what follows, al-Rāghib says: “Waṣiyya (وصية) is giving another person advance instructions about something to be done, accompanied by admonition and sincere advice. It derives from their expression arḍ wāṣiya (أرض واصية): land whose vegetation is continuous. One says awṣāhu (أوصاه) and waṣṣāhu (وصّاه); a people tawāṣaw (تواصوا) when they counsel one another.” One says qaddamtu ilayhi bi-kadhā (بكذا) when one commands someone before the time comes for the action to be needed.

[تمت السورة]

The four requirements of deliverance [XVI:567]

The Imām [Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī] says: “The verse contains a severe threat, for it judges all people to be in loss except whoever possesses faith, righteous action, mutual counsel to the truth, and mutual counsel to patience. This indicates that deliverance depends on these things together. Just as the morally responsible person must acquire what concerns his own self, he must also, regarding others, call to religion, give sincere advice, command right and forbid wrong, and love for another what he loves for himself. Mutual counsel is then repeated so that the first includes calling to God and the second remaining steadfast in it.”

End of the sūrah.
