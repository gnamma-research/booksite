Zamakhsharī · الكشافK097.02

عظم القرآن من ثلاثة أوجه: أحدها: أن أسند إنزاله إليه وجعله مختصا به دون غيره:

والثاني. أنه جاء بضميره دون اسمه الظاهر شهادة له بالنباهة والاستغناء عن التنبيه عليه، والثالث: الرفع من مقدار الوقت الذي أنزل فيه. روى أنه أنزل جملة واحدة في ليلة القدر من اللوح المحفوظ إلى السماء الدنيا. وأملاه جبريل على السفرة، ثم كان ينزله على رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم نجوما في ثلاث وعشرين سنة. وعن الشعبي: المعنى إنا ابتدأنا إنزاله في ليلة القدر

The Qurʾān’s greatness and its descent [IV:780]

He magnifies the Qurʾān in three ways. First, He attributes its sending down to Himself and makes it His exclusive act, to the exclusion of anyone else. Second, He uses its pronoun instead of its explicit name, attesting its prominence and its having no need for attention to be called to it. Third, He elevates the rank of the time in which it was sent down.

It is related that it was sent down all at once on the Night of Decree, from the Preserved Tablet to the lowest heaven. Gabriel dictated it to the scribes; thereafter he brought it down to God’s Messenger in instalments over twenty-three years. From al-Shaʿbī: the meaning is, “We began sending it down on the Night of Decree.”

Exclusive agency and the night’s reflected honour [XVI:522–523]

Arabic lemma: وجعله مختصا به

Meccan; five verses. In the name of God, the All-Merciful, the Merciful. [The embedded Kashshāf heading instead records disagreement about its place of revelation.]

On “and makes it His exclusive act”: he means that the construction belongs to the category of placing the semantic agent first, as in “I took care of your concern,” and “I fulfilled your need.” The choice of the plural form conveys a magnification beneath which every other magnification falls.

On “elevates the rank of the time in which it was sent down”: there is a subtle point here. He first says, “He magnifies the Qurʾān in three ways,” then says, “elevates the rank of the time.” The expected expression would be “elevates its rank by sending it down on this night.” He departs from that expression to indicate that the night was honoured by its descent within it, becoming possessed of importance and distinction. The Qurʾān’s own distinction and importance therefore follow all the more. He then rises further in elevating the night’s rank by saying, “And what will make you know what the Night of Decree is?”; higher still with “The Night of Decree is better than a thousand months”; and higher yet with “The angels and the Spirit descend in it.”

Ṭībī · فتوح الغيبT097.02

How “sending down” applies to speech [XVI:523]

Arabic lemma: روي أنه أنزل جملة واحدة

On “it is related that it was sent down all at once”: if you ask, “In your explanation of the preface you said that sending down means moving a thing from above to below, and that this belongs to bodies and is not literally realized in speech. Speech is therefore described by an attribute of its bearer because of its connection with him. This figurative use works when Gabriel brings the Qurʾān down to the Prophet, but how does it work for its descent from the Tablet to heaven, since that is without an intermediary?”—I answer: in that case sending down is borrowed from bodies and applied to meanings. The transfer of the Qurʾān from the Tablet to heaven, and its being established there, is likened to a body’s descent from a higher place to a lower, and it is said: “Surely We sent it down on the Night of Decree.”

On this basis, its manifestation in the visible world—that is, the Tablet—from the unseen world, which is the higher world, can also be explained as a descent. On the first interpretation it is a transferred expression without comparison, a majāz (مجاز) mursal; on the second it is a figurative expression preceded by a comparison.

When the night occurs and why it is concealed [IV:780]

They differ about its time. Most hold that it falls in Ramaḍān, on one of the odd nights of the final ten; the most frequent view is that it is the seventh of those nights. Perhaps the reason for concealing it is that the person seeking it will keep many nights alive in worship, hoping to coincide with it, so that his worship increases and his reward is multiplied; and that, if it were made known, people should not rely on attaining its excellence and then neglect the other nights.

Ubayy’s testimony concerning Ramaḍān [XVI:523–524]

Arabic lemma: على أنها في شهر رمضان

On “that it falls in Ramaḍān”: we have transmitted from Muslim, al-Tirmidhī, and Abū Dāwūd, from Zirr ibn Ḥubaysh, who said: “I heard Ubayy ibn Kaʿb being told, ‘ʿAbd Allāh (الله) ibn Masʿūd says: whoever stands in prayer throughout the year will encounter the Night of Decree.’ Ubayy replied, ‘By God, besides whom there is no god, it is certainly in Ramaḍān’—he swore and made no exception—‘and, by God, I know which night it is. It is the night on which God’s Messenger commanded us to stand in prayer: the twenty-seventh night.’” The report continues.

Decree, distinction, and a thousand months [IV:780–781]

The meaning of “the Night of Decree” is the night on which affairs are determined and decided, from His saying, “In it every wise affair is distinguished” [44:4]. It is also said to have been named thus because of its importance and distinction above all other nights.

“And what will make you know what the Night of Decree is?” means that your knowledge has not reached the utmost of its excellence or the limit of its exalted rank. He then explains this by saying that it is “better than a thousand months.” Its excellence reaches this height because of the religious benefits He mentions as occurring in it: the descent of the angels and the Spirit, and the determination of every wise affair.

Concerning the specification of this period, it is mentioned that God’s Messenger spoke of a man of the Children of Israel who bore arms in God’s path for a thousand months. The believers marvelled at this and their own deeds seemed paltry to them; so they were given a night better than that warrior’s entire period. It is also said that, in former times, a man was not called a worshipper until he had worshipped God for a thousand months. They were therefore given a night which, if they kept it alive in worship, would entitle them to be called worshippers more than those worshippers of old.

قوله: (وقيل: سميت بذلك لخطرها)، نقل الإمام عن الزهري أنه قال: "ليلة القدر ليلة العظمة والشرف؛ من قولهم: لفلان قدر عند فلان، أي: منزلة وشرف، ويدل عليه قوله تعالى: {ليلة القدر خير من ألف شهر}. وهو يحتمل أن يراد منه، أن من أتى بفعل الطاعات صار ذا قدر وشرف، أو أن الطاعات لها في تلك الليلة قدر زائد وشرف. وعن أبي بكر الوراق: سميت ليلة القدر، لأنه نزل فيها كتاب ذو قدر، على لسان ملك ذي قدر، على أمة لها قدر".

Eternal determination and the meaning of qadr [XVI:524]

Arabic lemma: ليلة تقدير الأمور

On “the night on which affairs are determined”: the Imām [Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī] transmits from al-Wāḥidī that qadr (قدر) in the language means taqdīr (تقدير): making something according to the measure of another, without increase or diminution. He says: “It is named thus because it is the night for the determination of affairs and judgements. From Ibn ʿAbbās: God Most High determined in it everything that would occur in that year—rain, provision, bringing to life, and causing to die—until the following year, as in His saying, ‘In it every wise affair is distinguished’ [44:4]. The meaning is not that God’s determination comes into being only on that night. He determined all measures in pre-eternity, before creating the heavens and earth. Rather, what is meant is making those determinations manifest to the angels.”

On “it is also said to have been named thus because of its importance”: the Imām transmits from al-Zuhrī: “The Night of Qadr (قدر) is the night of greatness and distinction, from the expression ‘So-and-so has qadr (قدر) with so-and-so,’ meaning standing and honour. His saying, ‘The Night of Decree is better than a thousand months,’ supports this. It may mean that whoever performs acts of obedience becomes possessed of rank and distinction, or that acts of obedience themselves possess additional worth and distinction on that night.” From Abū Bakr al-Warrāq: “It is called the Night of Qadr (قدر) because in it a book possessing qadr (قدر) descended, through the tongue of an angel possessing qadr (قدر), upon a community possessing qadr (قدر).”

The descent and the peace of the night [IV:781]

“Descend”: to the lowest heaven; or, according to another view, to the earth. “The Spirit” is Gabriel. It is also said to be a kind of angelic creature which the angels see only on that night.

“For every matter”: they descend on account of every matter God has decreed for that year until the next. The reading min kulli imriʾ (امرئ), “for every person,” is also transmitted: on account of every human being. It is said that, on that night, they encounter no believing man or woman without greeting them with peace.

“Peace it is”: it is nothing but safety; that is, God decrees in it only safety and good, whereas on other nights He decrees both affliction and safety. Or it is nothing but greetings of peace, because of the many greetings they address to believers.

Maṭlaʿ (مَطْلَع) is read both with fatḥa (فَتْحَة (ـَ)) and with kasra (كَسْرَة (ـِ)) on the lām (لام (ل)): maṭlaʿ (مَطْلَع) and maṭliʿ (مَطْلِع).

Ṭībī · فتوح الغيبT097.05

قوله: (ما هي إلا سلامة)، يريد أن {هي} مبتدأ و {سلام} الخبر، فقدم وجعل نفس السلام لإعطاء معنى الاختصاص. قال صاحب "الكشف": {هي} ابتداء و {سلام} خبر مقدم، وهو بمعنى الفاعل، أي: هي مسلمة. ولا بد من هذا التقدير ليصح تعليق {حتى} به؛ لأنه إذا حمل على المصدر لم يجز تعليق {حتى} به؛ لأنه لا يفصل بين الصلة والموصول. ويجوز تعليقه بقوله: {تنزل الملائكة}، ولا يجوز أن تكون {هي} مبتدأ، و {حتى} في موضع الخبر، لأنه لا فائدة فيه؛ إذ كل ليلة بهذه الصفة.

The syntax of “Peace it is” [XVI:525]

Arabic lemma: ما هي إلا سلامة

On “it is nothing but safety”: he means that hiya (هي), “it,” is the subject and salām (سلام), “peace,” the predicate. The predicate is placed first, and the night is made peace itself, to convey exclusivity.

The author of al-Kashf says: “Hiya (هي) is a subject and salām (سلام) a preceding predicate. Salām (سلام) has the sense of an active participle: it brings peace. This interpretation is necessary if ḥattā (حتى), ‘until,’ is to depend on it. If salām (سلام) were taken as a verbal noun, ḥattā (حتى) could not depend on it, because one may not separate a governing expression from its complement. It may instead depend on ‘the angels descend.’ Hiya (هي) cannot be the subject with the phrase beginning ḥattā (حتى) as its predicate, for that would convey no useful information: every night has this characteristic [of lasting until dawn].”

The two readings of maṭlaʿ [XVI:525]

Arabic lemma: وقرئ: {مطلع}

On “maṭlaʿ (مَطْلَع) is read”: al-Kisāʾī reads maṭliʿ (مَطْلِع), with kasra (كَسْرَة (ـِ)) on the lām (لام (ل)); the others read it with fatḥa (فَتْحَة (ـَ)). Al-Zajjāj says: “With fatḥa (فَتْحَة (ـَ)) it is the verbal noun, meaning rising: one says ṭalaʿa al-fajru ṭulūʿan wa-maṭlaʿan (طلع الفجر طلوعًا ومطلعًا), ‘dawn rose.’ With kasra (كَسْرَة (ـِ)) it is a noun for the time of rising.” According to some, it cannot here mean the place of rising. God knows best.

The sūrah is complete, praise be to God Most High.

Source and review notes

al-Qadr · source and review notes
TEXTUAL NOTES K097-U1: The first interpretative statement makes God the exclusive agent of revelation; Ṭībī’s explicit grammatical explanation confirms this. The Turkish comparator’s wording appears to make the Qurʾān itself the agent; it has not been followed. K097-U2: The DKA text supplies the two distinct explanations of the thousand months. The embedded base in the Ṭībī transcription telescopes part of their wording; the complete DKA passage is retained. T097-U1: Ṣila and mawṣūl in the discussion of the verbal noun concern the relation of a governing expression to its dependent material; the intervening hiya is the difficulty. The English renders the grammatical relation rather than implying an ordinary relative pronoun.
