Zamakhsharī · الكشافK112.02

هو ضمير الشأن، والله أحد هو الشأن، كقولك: هو زيد منطلق، كأنه قيل:

الشأن هذا، وهو أن الله واحد لا ثانى له. فإن قلت: ما محل هو؟ قلت: الرفع على الابتداء والخبر الجملة. فإن قلت: فالجملة الواقعة خبرا لا بد فيها من راجع إلى المبتدإ، فأين الراجع؟ قلت:

حكم هذه الجملة حكم المفرد في قولك «زيد غلامك» في أنه هو المبتدأ في المعنى، وذلك أن قوله الله أحد هو الشأن الذي هو عبارة عنه، وليس كذلك «زيد أبوه منطلق» فإن زيدا والجملة يدلان على معنيين مختلفين، فلا بد مما يصل بينهما. وعن ابن عباس: قالت قريش: يا محمد، صف لنا ربك الذي تدعونا إليه، فنزلت: يعنى: الذي سألتمونى وصفه هو الله، وأحد: بدل من قوله، الله. أو على: هو أحد، وهو بمعنى واحد، وأصله وحد.

Text and explanation

Huwa (هو) [He/it] is the pronoun of the matter (ḍamīr al-shaʾn (ضمير الشأن)), and “God is One” is that matter, as when you say, “It is: Zayd is departing.” It is as though one had said: the matter is this—namely, that God is one, with no second beside Him. If you ask, “What is the syntactic position of huwa (هو)?”, I answer: nominative, as the subject (mubtadaʾ (مبتدأ)); its predicate is the sentence. If you ask, “A sentence functioning as a predicate must contain something referring back to the subject. Where, then, is that reference?”, I answer: this sentence has the same status as the single predicate in your statement “Zayd is your servant-boy”: in meaning, it is the subject itself. For “God is One” is precisely the matter expressed by huwa (هو). This is not the case with “Zayd—his father is departing,” since Zayd and the sentence denote two different meanings; something connecting them is therefore indispensable. Ibn ʿAbbās reports: Quraysh said, “Muḥammad, describe to us your Lord, to whom you summon us,” and the sūrah was revealed. That is: the one whose description you have asked me for is God. Aḥad (أحد) is a substitution (badal) for “God”; alternatively, [it is construed with an understood subject] “He is One.” It has the meaning of wāḥid (واحد), and its original form is waḥad (وَحَد).

“The one whose description you asked me for is God, and aḥad is a substitution”

Arabic lemma: الذي سألتموني وصفه هو الله، و {أحد

Sūrat al-Ikhlāṣ. Meccan; it is also said to be Medinan. Four verses. In the name of God, the All-Merciful, the Merciful. His words, “The one whose description you asked me for is God, and aḥad (أحد) is a substitution.” Abū al-Baqāʾ says: “Huwa (هو) is a subject meaning ‘the one asked about,’ for they said, ‘Is your Lord made of copper or gold?’ On this interpretation, Allāh (الله) may be the predicate of the subject, with aḥad (أحد) a substitution, or the predicate of an omitted subject. Allāh (الله) may also be a substitution, with aḥad (أحد) the predicate. The hamza (همزة (ء)) of aḥad (أحد) replaces wāw (واو (و)), since it means al-wāḥid (الواحد).” Substitution of hamza (همزة (ء)) for a wāw (واو (و)) carrying fatḥa (فَتْحَة (ـَ)) is rare. It is also said that the hamza (همزة (ء)) is original, like the hamza (همزة (ء)) in aḥad (أحد) used for generality.

إذا علم هذا، فنقول: إنهم لما قالوا: صف لنا ربك الذي تدعونا إليه، قيل لهم: المسؤول عنه الله، وهو واحد متفرد بالذات في عدم المثل والنظير؛ فإجراء الكلام للتمييز، والصفة فارقة. وإن استلزم التعظيم؛ لأنه ابتداء أمر الرسول -صلى الله عليه وسلم-، إرشادا للقوم، وتنبيها على معبود عظيم الشأن قاهر السلطان، فكأنه قيل: قل يا محمد: الشأن والأمر أن الله أحد لا ثاني له، فدل بقوله: {الله}، على جميع صفات الكمال، وبالأحد على جميع صفات الجلال؛ فالمناسب أن يقال: واحد لا ثاني له، لأنه دال لنفي ما يذكر معه. والاحتمال الثاني، وهو أن يتعلق بالوجهين كليهما، أي: {هو} ضمير الشأن، أو {هو} بمعنى المسؤول؛ فحينئذ لا فرق بين أحد وواحد، قال الجوهري: «الأحد بمعنى الواحد، وهو أول العدد»، وقال صاحب "النهاية": «الواحد هو الفرد الذي لم يزل وحده، ولم يكن معه آخر».

“It has the meaning of wāḥid”

Arabic lemma: وهو بمعنى واحد

His words, “It has the meaning of wāḥid (واحد).” There are two possibilities. First, this may attach to the second interpretation: huwa (هو) answers their request, “Describe your Lord to us,” and is the pronoun referring to the one asked about. In that case, a distinction must be drawn between wāḥid (واحد) and aḥad (أحد). He says in al-Aḥzāb: “Aḥad (أحد) originally means waḥad (وَحَد), that is, al-wāḥid (الواحد); then it was assigned to general negation, applying equally to masculine and feminine, one and more than one.” The author of al-Nihāya transmits from al-Azharī: “The difference between al-wāḥid (الواحد) and al-aḥad (الأحد) is that aḥad (أحد) was formed to negate whatever number is mentioned with it: you say, ‘No one came to me.’ Wāḥid (واحد) is a name formed for the beginning of number: you say, ‘One person came to me,’ but do not say [in this affirmative construction], ‘Aḥad (أحد) came to me.’ Al-wāḥid (الواحد) is unique in essence, without likeness or counterpart; al-aḥad (الأحد) is unique in meaning. It is also said: al-wāḥid (الواحد) is that which is not divisible, is not doubled, does not admit division, and has neither counterpart nor likeness. Only God Most High combines these two descriptions.” Al-Azharī says in Tafsīr asmāʾ (أسماء) Allāh (الله) al-ḥusnā: “Al-aḥad (الأحد) is among the attributes God has reserved to Himself: nothing shares it with Him, and nothing other than God is described as al-aḥad (الأحد). One does not say ‘an aḥad (أحد) man’ or ‘an aḥad (أحد) dirham’; one says ‘a wāḥid (واحد) man.’” Knowing this, we say: when they said, “Describe to us your Lord, to whom you summon us,” they were told: the one asked about is God, one, unique in essence, without likeness or counterpart. The discourse thus serves to distinguish, and the description differentiates. Although it entails magnification, [when] huwa (هو) is taken as the pronoun of the matter, the descriptions serve magnification alone. For this is the beginning of the Messenger's ﷺ mission, guiding the people and drawing attention to an object of worship of immense dignity and overwhelming dominion. It is as though one had said: Say, Muḥammad, the matter and affair is that God is One, with no second beside Him. Allāh (الله) indicates all the attributes of perfection; aḥad (أحد), all the attributes of majesty. Accordingly, it is appropriate to say “One, with no second beside Him,” because aḥad (أحد) indicates the negation of whatever is mentioned with it. The second possibility is that [his explanation] attaches to both interpretations together: either huwa (هو) is the pronoun of the matter, or huwa (هو) means the one asked about. There would then be no distinction between aḥad (أحد) and wāḥid (واحد). Al-Jawharī says, “Al-aḥad (الأحد) means al-wāḥid (الواحد), and is the beginning of number.” The author of al-Nihāya says, “Al-wāḥid (الواحد) is the solitary one who has always been alone, with no other beside Him.” [TEXT CORRECTIONS T112-C1: The scan at p.633 reads لا يتجزأ and إلا الله, correcting the transcription. وقرأ عبد الله وأبى: هو الله أحد، بغير قل وفي قراءة النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم: الله أحد، بغير قل هو وقال من

قرأ: الله أحد، كان بعدل القرآن. وقرأ الأعمش: قل هو الله الواحد. وقرئ: أحد الله، بغير تنوين: أسقط لملاقاته لام التعريف. ونحوه

ولا ذاكر الله إلا قليلا

والجيد هو التنوين، وكسره لالتقاء الساكنين. والصمد فعل بمعنى مفعول، من صمد إليه إذا قصده، وهو السيد المصمود إليه في الحوائج.

Text and explanation

ʿAbd Allāh (الله) and Ubayy read “Huwa (هو) Allāhu (اللَّهُ) aḥad (أحد),” without “Say.” In the Prophet's ﷺ reading it is “Allāhu (اللَّهُ) aḥad (أحد),” without “Say: He”; and he said, “Whoever recites ‘Allāhu (اللَّهُ) aḥad (أحد)’—it is equivalent to the Qurʾān.” Al-Aʿmash read “Qul huwa (هو) Allāhu (اللَّهُ) al-wāḥid (الواحد).” It has also been read “aḥadu (أحدُ) llāhu,” without tanwīn (تنوين (ـً ـٍ ـٌ)): the tanwīn (تنوين (ـً ـٍ ـٌ)) is dropped because it meets the lām (لام (ل)) of the definite article. Comparable to this is: “Nor remembering God, except a little.” The sounder usage is to retain the tanwīn (تنوين (ـً ـٍ ـٌ)), giving it a kasra (كَسْرَة (ـِ)) because of the meeting of two unvocalized consonants. Al-Ṣamad (الصمد) is on the pattern faʿal (فَعَل) with the meaning of a passive participle (mafʿūl (مفعول)), from ṣamada (صمد) ilayhi, “he directed himself towards him.” It is the master to whom people turn with their needs. [REVIEW K112-B: The report about equivalence is translated as given; Ṭībī's correction to “one third” follows in T112.12. Its presence here is not a fresh authentication.]

“It was equivalent to the Qurʾān”

Arabic lemma: كان يعدل القرآن

His words, “It was equivalent to the Qurʾān.” It has been said: its recitation was equivalent to reciting the Qurʾān. The report is cited as evidence for this reading. Perhaps the meaning is that “Say: He” functions as an introduction and preparation for “God is One.” This works only if the pronoun is taken as the pronoun of the matter.

“Nor remembering God, except a little”

Arabic lemma: ولا ذاكر الله إلا قليلا

His words, “Nor remembering God, except a little.” Its opening is: “I found him not returning to goodwill under reproach.” That is: I reminded him. “Nor remembering” is intended with tanwīn (تنوين (ـً ـٍ ـٌ)); it was dropped because two unvocalized consonants met. Allāh (الله) consequently remains accusative, not genitive through annexation (iḍāfa (إضافة)). Dhākir (ذاكر) is genitive, coordinated with mustaʿtib (مستعتب): “nor remembering.” That is: I reminded him of the affection formerly between us, but he was found not to turn back, through reproach, from the ugliness of what he had done. His words, “The sounder usage is tanwīn (تنوين (ـً ـٍ ـٌ))”: this is the well-known reading. His words, “The master to whom people turn with their needs.” Al-Zajjāj cited al-Asadī: “Early came the death-announcer with news of the two best of Banū Asad: ʿAmr ibn Masʿūd and the master to whom all turn.” Al-Ṣamad (الصمد): that is, the one to whom everything turns—the one who created all things, and whom nothing can do without. Muḥyī al-Sunna transmitted from Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid, al-Ḥasan and Saʿīd ibn Jubayr: “Al-Ṣamad (الصمد) is the one with no interior cavity.” Al-Shaʿbī said: “The one who neither eats nor drinks.”

Zamakhsharī · الكشافK112.04

والمعنى: هو الله الذي تعرفونه وتقرون بأنه خالق السماوات والأرض وخالقكم، وهو واحد متوحد بالإلهية لا يشارك فيها، وهو الذي يصمد إليه كل مخلوق لا يستغنون عنه، وهو الغنى عنهم لم يلد لأنه لا يجانس، حتى تكون له من جنسه صاحبة فيتوالدا. وقد دل على هذا المعنى بقوله أنى يكون له ولد ولم تكن له صاحبة. ولم يولد لأن كل مولود محدث وجسم، وهو قديم لا أول لوجوده وليس يجسم ولم يكافئه أحد، أى: لم يماثله ولم يشاكله. ويجوز أن يكون من الكفاءة في النكاح، نفيا للصاحبة:

Text and explanation

The meaning is: He is the God whom you know, and whom you acknowledge as the creator of the heavens and the earth and your own creator. He is one, alone in divinity, with no partner in it. He is the one to whom every creature turns; they cannot dispense with Him, whereas He has no need of them. “He did not beget,” because He shares no genus with anything, such that He might have a consort of His own genus and the two might produce offspring. His statement “How could He have a child, when He has no consort?” [6:101] indicates this meaning. “Nor was He begotten,” because everything begotten is originated and a body; He, however, is pre-eternal (qadīm (قديم)), with no beginning to His existence, and is not a body. No one equals Him: that is, no one resembles Him or is like Him. It is also possible to take this from suitability between spouses (kafāʾa (كفاءة)) in marriage, thereby denying Him a consort.

His words, “Thus His statement ‘He is God.’” The fāʾ (فاء (ف)) introduces a detailed exposition; the general statement being expounded is “what encompasses His attributes.” Since Allāh (الله) names the essence—and in the Opening of the Book he established the impossibility of its being a descriptive epithet—it nevertheless has in each context a meaning appropriate to that context. The particular character of the polytheists' question required its explanation as “the Creator,” because of His statement, “If you ask them who created the heavens and the earth, they will certainly say: God” [31:25]. Thus Allāh (الله) here, as an answer, points them to the one who creates things. You know that knowledge and power are what make creatorship possible: these two attributes are therefore included under it. This is what “implicit in that is His description as powerful and knowing” indicates. He cannot be powerful and knowing without being knowing, living, hearing and seeing. Then these descriptions were followed by the meaning of oneness in “One.” Since uniqueness excludes recourse to another, He was affirmed to possess ṣamadiyya (صمدية), so that recourse would be to Him. Once this established an essence entailing the attributes of creatorship, knowledge, power, life and divinity, their perfection was to be explained, together with their difference from creatures' attributes in past and future. At this point it was said: “He neither begot nor was begotten, and no one is equal to Him.”

Text and explanation

If you ask, “Why is this sūrah the equivalent of the whole Qurʾān, despite its shortness and the closeness of its two ends?”, I answer: “It is for some great cause that whoever becomes a master becomes one.” That is solely because it encompasses God's attributes, His justice and His oneness. For anyone who acknowledges its excellence and believes the Messenger of God's ﷺ statement about it, this is sufficient proof that the science of divine oneness (ʿilm al-tawḥīd) has an exalted standing with God. How could it be otherwise? Knowledge follows its object: it is ennobled by the nobility of what is known, and lowered by its lowliness. The object of this science is God Most High and His attributes, and what is and is not admissible concerning Him. What, then, do you think of the nobility of its rank, the grandeur of its position, its eminence over every other science, and its winning the prize ahead of them? Whoever disparages it does so because of the weakness of his knowledge of its object, the smallness of his reverence for Him, his lack of fear of Him, and his remoteness from considering his own ultimate fate. O God, gather us among those who know You, act for You, affirm Your justice and Your oneness, and fear Your threat. It is called “The Sūrah of the Foundation,” because it encompasses the foundations of religion. Ubayy and Anas transmitted from the Prophet ﷺ: “The seven heavens and the seven earths were founded upon ‘Say: He is God, One.’” That is: they were created solely to serve as signs of God's oneness and of the knowledge of His attributes expressed by this sūrah. It is related from the Messenger of God ﷺ that he heard a man reciting “Say: He is God, One,” and said, “It has become assured.” He was asked, “Messenger of God, what has become assured?” He answered, “Paradise has become assured for him.”

“The equivalent of the whole Qurʾān”

Arabic lemma: عدل القرآن كله

His words, “The equivalent of the whole Qurʾān.” It is transmitted with fatḥa (فَتْحَة (ـَ)) or kasra (كَسْرَة (ـِ)) on the ʿayn (عين (ع)). Al-Akhfash says: “ʿIdl (عِدْل), with kasra (كَسْرَة (ـِ)), means an equivalent. ʿAdl (عَدْل), with fatḥa (فَتْحَة (ـَ)), is originally the verbal noun in ‘I made this nicely equal [to that]’; you turn it into a noun for an equivalent to distinguish it from a ʿidl (عِدْل), a load of goods.” Al-Farrāʾ says: “ʿAdl (عَدْل), with fatḥa (فَتْحَة (ـَ)), is what equals something while belonging to a different kind. ʿIdl (عِدْل), with kasra (كَسْرَة (ـِ)), is its like. You say, ‘I have the ʿidl (عِدْل) of your servant-boy, or of your sheep,’ when it is a servant-boy equal to a servant-boy or a sheep equal to a sheep. If you mean its value in something of another kind, you pronounce the ʿayn (عين (ع)) with fatḥa (فَتْحَة (ـَ)). Some Arabs sometimes pronounce it with kasra (كَسْرَة (ـِ)); that was an error on their part.”

“It is for some cause that whoever becomes a master becomes one”

Arabic lemma: لأمر ما يسود من يسود

The correct version is “one third of the Qurʾān.” We have transmitted from al-Bukhārī, Muslim, Mālik (مَالِك), Abū Dāwūd and al-Nasāʾī, from Abū Saʿīd, that a man heard another reciting “Say: He is God, One” repeatedly. In the morning he came to the Prophet ﷺ and mentioned it, apparently regarding it as little. The Messenger of God ﷺ said: “By Him in whose hand is my soul, it is indeed equivalent to one third of the Qurʾān.” Al-Qāḍī says: “Because this sūrah, short as it is, encompasses all knowledge of divinity and refutes those who deviate concerning it, the report says that it equals one third of the Qurʾān. The Qurʾān's aims are confined to explaining beliefs, rulings and narratives. Whoever equates it with the whole takes into account what is intended for its own sake among these.” His words, “It is for some cause that whoever becomes a master becomes one”: the opening is: “I resolved to stay for a morning.” Mā (ما) is an additional particle giving indefiniteness and magnification: that is, it is for a great cause that whoever becomes a master becomes one. “It suffices as proof for whoever acknowledges”

Arabic lemma: وكفى دليلا من اعترف

His words, “It suffices as proof for whoever acknowledges.” Man iʿtarafa (من اعترف) [whoever acknowledges] is the object of kafā (كفى) [suffices]. Its subject is what is indicated by “because it encompasses God's attributes.” The pronoun in “its excellence” refers to the sūrah. “Believes” is coordinated with “acknowledges”; “the Messenger of God's ﷺ statement” attaches to “believes.” “That the science of divine oneness” attaches to “as proof,” which is a specification (tamyīz (تمييز)). That is: for whoever acknowledges the sūrah's excellence and believes the Messenger's statement, this suffices as proof that the science of divine oneness has an exalted standing with God. The Prophet's ﷺ statement intended here is the one he transmits at the end of the sūrah: “The seven heavens were founded …,” to the end. I have not found this report in the recognized source collections. Al-Tirmidhī, Abū Dāwūd and Ibn Māja transmit from Burayda that the Messenger of God ﷺ heard a man saying: “O God, I ask You by my testimony that You are God—there is no god but You—the One, al-Ṣamad (الصمد), who neither begot nor was begotten, and to whom no one is equal.” The Prophet ﷺ said: “By Him in whose hand is my soul, he has asked God by His greatest name: when invoked by it, He answers; when asked by it, He gives.”

Commentary

A conclusion from the words of Shaykh Faṣīḥ al-Dīn—may God have mercy on him: “‘God, al-Ṣamad (الصمد)’ is not joined by a conjunction to the preceding sentence, because it confirms and explains its content. The same applies to ‘He did not beget,’ because it confirms the content of ‘God, al-Ṣamad (الصمد)’: the absolutely self-sufficient one, whom everything needs, cannot be a begetter or begotten, since that necessarily entails need. “‘Nor was He begotten’ is joined to ‘He did not beget,’ because ‘was not begotten’ does not express the meaning of ‘did not beget,’ and thus does not confirm its meaning; rather, the two sentences confirm the content of the preceding sentence. ‘And no one is equal to Him’ is joined [by a conjunction] because its content does not confirm the content of the two preceding sentences: it expresses that He cannot have any equal in anything mentioned concerning essence and attributes. He is therefore one, with no partner—exalted, holy and supremely great. “The predicate is definite in ‘God, al-Ṣamad (الصمد)’ to negate [the claim of] anyone who asserts and names another as ṣamad (صمد). It is indefinite in ‘God, One,’ because they did not call things aḥad (أحد) in this sense.” [REVIEW T112-U4: The scan reads نفيا لنفي من زعم وسمى غيره صمدا, literally “to negate the negation of whoever asserted and named another ṣamad (صمد).” This double negation is problematic. The bracketed rendering gives the apparent intended argument, not a verified emendation. Retain the flag pending another witness or the quoted source.] The sūrah is complete.
