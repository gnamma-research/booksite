AL-KAWTHAR (108) — ABUNDANT GOOD Complete first-pass translation of both authorial layers • C05 • 27 October 2026

SOURCE AND STATUS Kashshāf: Dār al-Kitāb al-ʿArabī, 1407 AH, IV:806–808, OpenITI Shamela0023627-ara1, collated with the embedded base text in Ṭībī. Ṭībī: Futūḥ al-Ghayb, Dubai, 1434/2013, XVI:600–605, OpenITI Sham19Y0020891-ara1, with the omitted report wording restored from printed XVI:603. Both authorial layers extend through their closing reports. Modern editorial footnotes are excluded. Report attribution is preserved without independent authentication. This is a complete first draft, with unresolved readings explicitly marked below.

Al-Zamakhsharī · al-KashshāfK108.01

Heading, verses, and the meaning of al-kawthar [IV:806]

Meccan; three verses. [The DKA heading additionally places its revelation after al-ʿĀdiyāt.] In the name of God, the All-Merciful, the Merciful.

We have indeed given you abundant good. (1) So pray to your Lord and sacrifice. (2) Surely it is the one who hates you who is cut off. (3)

In the recitation of God’s Messenger: innā anṭaynāka, with nūn [instead of the ʿayn in aʿṭaynāka]. In his saying: “And give the middling portion” (wa-anṭū l-thabaja).

Al-kawthar has the pattern fawʿal, from kathra, abundance: it means what is exceedingly abundant. A Bedouin woman whose son had returned from a journey was asked, “What did your son bring back?” She replied, “He returned with kawthar.” The poet says: “You are abundant in good, son of Marwān, and wholesome; your father, the son of noble women, was abundant in good.”

Al-Ṭībī · Futūḥ al-GhaybT108.01

The dialectal verb and the poem [XVI:600–601]

On K108.01

Meccan; three verses. In the name of God, the All-Merciful, the Merciful.

On “and give the middling portion,” al-Nihāya says: “This is the language of Yemen. He wrote to Wāʾil: ‘Anṭū l-thabaja,’ meaning: give the middle quality as alms, neither the choicest property nor its poorest. The feminine ending was attached because the word shifted from noun to adjective.”

On “son of noble women”: women of the choicest kind; the ʿaqīla of anything is its noblest part. A kawthar among men is one abundant in good and giving. The verse is by al-Kumayt.

Al-Zamakhsharī · al-KashshāfK108.02

The river and its first visitors [IV:807]

It is also said that al-Kawthar is a river in Paradise. From the Prophet: when this sūrah was revealed, he recited it and said, “Do you know what al-Kawthar is? It is a river in Paradise that my Lord has promised me, containing abundant good.” Its description is transmitted as follows: “Sweeter than honey, whiter than milk, colder than snow, and softer than butter; its banks are chrysolite, and its vessels are silver, as numerous as the stars of heaven.”

It is also transmitted: “Whoever drinks from it never thirsts again. The first to reach it are the poor Emigrants: dirty in clothing, dishevelled in hair, who are not given pampered women in marriage and for whom the doors of the entrance halls are not opened.” One of them dies with his need still turning within his breast; were he to adjure God, God would fulfil his oath.

From Ibn ʿAbbās: he interpreted al-kawthar as abundant good. Saʿīd ibn Jubayr said to him, “Some people say it is a river in Paradise!” He replied, “That is part of the abundant good.” [The attribution differs in the report that Ṭībī quotes below; both authors’ formulations are retained.]

Al-Ṭībī · Futūḥ al-GhaybT108.02

Reports concerning the river [XVI:601]

On K108.02

On “it is a river in Paradise”: we transmit in Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī, from Saʿīd ibn Jubayr, from Ibn ʿAbbās, who said concerning al-kawthar: “It is abundant good.” Ibn Jubayr was asked, “But people maintain that it is a river in Paradise?” Saʿīd replied, “The river in Paradise is part of the good that God Most High gave him.”

From Aḥmad ibn Ḥanbal, al-Tirmidhī, Ibn Mājah and al-Dārimī, from Ibn ʿUmar: God’s Messenger said, “Al-Kawthar is a river in Paradise. Its banks are gold, and its course runs over pearls and rubies. Its soil is more fragrant than musk, and its water sweeter than honey and whiter than snow.”

In ʿĀʾisha’s report: “Its banks are hollowed pearls, and its vessels as numerous as the stars of heaven.” Al-Bukhārī transmitted it.

Al-Ṭībī · Futūḥ al-GhaybT108.03

Those denied entry at the doors [XVI:602]

On K108.02

On “for whom the doors of the entrance halls are not opened”: the report is transmitted by al-Tirmidhī from Thawbān. God’s Messenger said: “My pool is as extensive as the distance between Aden and ʿAmmān of al-Balqāʾ. Its water is whiter than snow and sweeter than honey, and its cups are as numerous as the stars of heaven. Whoever drinks from it never thirsts afterward. The first people to reach me there are the poor Emigrants, dishevelled in hair and dirty in clothing, who do not marry pampered women and for whom the doors of the entrance halls are not opened.”

Al-Tirmidhī says: ʿUmar ibn ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz said, “I have married a pampered woman, Fāṭima daughter of ʿAbd al-Malik, and the doors of the entrance halls have been opened for me. Therefore I shall not wash my head until it becomes dishevelled, nor the garment next to my body until it becomes dirty.”

In al-Jāmiʿ: “Sudad is the plural of sudda, which here means door.” In al-Nihāya: “A sudda is like an awning over a doorway, protecting it from rain. It is also said to be the open space before the door, or the door itself: that is, doors are not opened for them. In Abū l-Dardāʾ’s report, he came to Muʿāwiya’s door and was not admitted. He said, ‘Whoever frequents the ruler’s entrance is made to stand and sit.’”

I say: the more fitting interpretation is to take the genitive construction ‘doors of the sudad’ as explanatory, making it an indirect expression for the doors of kings and great men, with sudda understood as the awning or forecourt.

Al-Ṭībī · Futūḥ al-GhaybT108.04

The report concerning al-Rubayyiʿ [XVI:602–603]

On K108.02

On “were he to adjure God, God would fulfil his oath”: he said this in the report concerning al-Rubayyiʿ. We transmit from al-Bukhārī, Muslim, Abū Dāwūd and al-Nasāʾī, from Anas ibn Mālik: his paternal aunt al-Rubayyiʿ broke a girl’s incisor. They sought a pardon from her people, but they refused. They offered compensation, but they refused. They came to God’s Messenger and insisted on retaliation. God’s Messenger ordered retaliation.

Anas ibn al-Naḍr said, “Messenger of God, will al-Rubayyiʿ’s incisor be broken? No! By Him who sent you with the truth, her incisor shall not be broken.” God’s Messenger said, “Anas, does not God’s Book prescribe retaliation?” Then the people were satisfied and pardoned her. God’s Messenger said, “Among God’s servants are those who, were they to adjure God, He would fulfil their oath.” It means: were such a person to ask God, He would answer him. Adjuration here means an appeal for favour (istiʿṭāf).

Al-Zamakhsharī · al-KashshāfK108.03

Prayer, sacrifice, and the double gift [IV:807]

Naḥr means slaughtering sacrificial camels. According to ʿAṭiyya, the prayer is the dawn prayer at Jamʿ [Muzdalifa], and the slaughter is at Minā. It is also said to mean the festival prayer and sacrifice. Another interpretation takes prayer generically and naḥr as placing the right hand over the left.

The meaning is: you have been given limitless good in both abodes, such as no one else has been given, and the giver of all this is I, the God of the worlds. Thus the two exalted grounds for being envied without ill will have come together for you: receiving the noblest and most abundant gift from the most generous giver and greatest benefactor. So worship your Lord, who has honoured you through His giving, ennobled you, and protected you from dependence on the favours of created beings, thereby defying your people who worship other than God. When you slaughter, slaughter for His sake and in His name, opposing their slaughter for idols.

Al-Ṭībī · Futūḥ al-GhaybT108.05

The Giver and the gift [XVI:603]

On K108.03

On “the giver of all this is I, the God of the worlds”: this signals a preference for Ibn ʿAbbās’s interpretation, that al-kawthar means abundant good. It also draws out what the plural pronoun conveys of greatness and majesty, for its speaker is none other than the God of the worlds; and [the text is difficult here] “even if the gift were not great, the Giver is great.”

Because of these two correspondences, “So pray to your Lord” follows, with an explicit noun replacing a pronoun. That is: since both gift and Giver are great, perform the greatest bodily and financial acts of worship that you can. Slaughter is preferred as the expression so as to incorporate the meaning of cutting the soul off from immediate pleasures. “Surely it is the one who hates you who is cut off” is then joined to this, completing the good news. The Imām says: “Having given him good news of immense blessings, and knowing that their completion consists in the subjugation of enemies, He said, ‘Surely it is the one who hates you who is cut off.’”

Al-Sulamī transmits from Jaʿfar al-Ṣādiq: “We have given you a light in your heart that guided you to Me and cut you off from everything besides Me.” From al-Qāsim: “Your hater is the one cut off from the good things of both abodes.” God knows best.

Al-Zamakhsharī · al-KashshāfK108.04

The one truly cut off [IV:807–808]

It is the person among your people who hates you because you oppose them who is cut off, not you. Every believer born until the Day of Resurrection is among your children and descendants. Your remembrance is raised on pulpits and minarets and on the tongue of every scholar and person who remembers God until the end of time: God is mentioned first and you second. In the hereafter you have what description cannot encompass. Someone such as you is not called cut off. The one cut off is your hater, forgotten in this world and the hereafter; if mentioned, he is mentioned with a curse.

They used to say, “Muḥammad is a ṣunbūr: when he dies, his remembrance dies.” It is said that the verse was revealed concerning al-ʿĀṣ ibn Wāʾil, who called him al-abtar. Al-abtar means someone without offspring; hence a tailless donkey is called al-ḥimār al-abtar.

Al-Ṭībī · Futūḥ al-GhaybT108.06

Minarets, indirect expression, and ṣunbūr [XVI:604]

On K108.04

On “and the manār,” al-Nihāya says: “Manār is the plural of manāra: the marker between two boundaries. Hence Abū Hurayra’s report, ‘Islam has waymarks and manār’: signs and ordinances by which it is recognised.” It is also said that manāʾir is the plural of the manāra from which the call to prayer is made. Its original form is manāwir, since it derives from nūr, light; hamza replaces wāw. A root letter may be treated like an added one, as in maṣāʾib, originally maṣāwib.

On “someone such as you is not called cut off”: this resembles “someone such as you is not miserly” as an indirect expression (kināya). It means: someone with your attribute—every believer born until the end of time being his child—is not called cut off.

On ṣunbūr, al-Nihāya says: “A person cut off, without offspring. Originally ṣunbūr is a palm shoot growing on the trunk rather than in the ground. It is also said to be a solitary palm whose lower part is slender. They meant that when he was uprooted his remembrance would cease, just as the trace of a ṣunbūr disappears, because he has no offspring.”

Al-Zamakhsharī · al-KashshāfK108.05

Closing report [IV:808]

From God’s Messenger: “Whoever recites Sūrat al-Kawthar, God gives him drink from every river in Paradise, and ten good deeds are recorded for him for every offering the servants have offered on the Day of Sacrifice or will offer.”

Al-Ṭībī · Futūḥ al-GhaybT108.07

“Or will offer” [XVI:605]

On K108.05

On “or will offer”: according to some, “or” distinguishes the kinds [past and future offerings].

End of the sūrah.

Textual and review notes

TEXTUAL AND REVIEW NOTES K108-U1: The first electronic Kashshāf witness prints أعطيناك despite immediately saying “with nūn.” The embedded Kashshāf in Ṭībī has أنطيناك, which supplies the intended dialectal form. T108-U1: The electronic text drops the oath and the Prophet’s reply in the al-Rubayyiʿ report. The full words above are restored from printed XVI:603, visually inspected. T108-U2: The printed phrase وأن المعطى لم يكن عظيما، إلا أن المعطي عظيم remains syntactically difficult. The provisional concessive rendering is retained with an explicit flag; do not silently rewrite it as an unqualified claim about the gift’s size. The following sentence expressly describes both gift and Giver as great. T108-U3: The phrase ليدمج معنى معطى قطع النفس in the scan/transcription is also awkward. “So as to incorporate the meaning of cutting the soul off” renders the evident relation of sacrifice to self-denial, but the extra معطى needs further textual collation. K108-U2: Jamʿ is Muzdalifa in the Arabic. The Turkish comparison’s phrasing places the dawn prayer at Minā; the English follows the Arabic distinction between Jamʿ and Minā.
